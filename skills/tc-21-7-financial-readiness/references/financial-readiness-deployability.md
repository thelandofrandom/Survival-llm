# Financial Readiness and Deployability — TC 21-7 (Chapter 5)

The preface calls this "probably the most important chapter of this circular." Thesis: **set finances up so you can deploy without making changes, and do it now** — not at deployment notification, when the finance office will be swamped and changes may take weeks.

## Paying bills while deployed

Pay continues through the same system, deposited by SURE PAY. Recommended setup: a checking account with an ATM card — pay bills by check, access money worldwide.

- **Allotments** for fixed debts (car payments, mortgages, installment loans) — each has a beginning and end date. Only AC soldiers can establish them; limit **six discretionary**.
- **Electronic fund transfer (EFT)** through the financial institution for bills beyond the six — nominal fee, bills paid on time, no late charges, no credit damage from mail delays.
- **Draft payments** arranged directly with the creditor — normally no fee.
- Keep a list of every bill's mailing address and amount, plus each financial institution's address, phone, and account numbers. Take the checkbook and ATM card on deployment. Start allotments **right away**.

## Deployment entitlements

- **BAS** may terminate on deployment (field rations replace it).
- **Foreign Duty Pay (FDP):** enlisted, rate by grade.
- **Imminent Danger Pay (IDP):** all soldiers in a specified geographic area, flat monthly rate.
- **Family Separation Allowance (FSA):** soldiers with dependents away from family **more than 30 consecutive days**; paid on the **31st day, retroactive to day 1**, until redeployment.

## Taxes in a combat zone

In a CZ or qualified hazardous duty area (QHDA) established by Presidential Executive Order: all military pay for enlisted soldiers and warrant officers is exempt from federal and state income-tax withholding; officers are exempt on pay up to the maximum rate for the Sergeant Major of the Army **plus $150**. State laws vary.

## Married soldiers and families

- **Family care plan** — up to date, the best financial preparation for a spouse.
- **Separate checking accounts** for soldier and spouse (plus a joint account for use when not deployed); a single joint account causes problems during separation.
- **DD Form 1337** — spouse holds it for noncombatant evacuation: eligible for **2 months' advance pay**, repayable, for immediate expenses (apartment, rental car).
- **Family support group** — chartered volunteer group of unit family members; information and support during separations.
- Spouse can get an LES copy via the rear detachment commander (finance + battalion PAC); a power of attorney should authorize the spouse to pick it up.
- **Current will** — JAG prepares wills free. Do it immediately, not at deployment notification; without one, a family can have problems claiming belongings if the soldier is MIA or a casualty.
- **Family deployability checklists** — use them now, not at deployment time.
