# Banking and Credit — TC 21-7 (Chapter 6)

## Credit

Credit is borrowing money now and promising to pay it back later. **Compare interest rates and get the lowest rate possible** — the interest rate is the price of the loan. A bank or credit union should be the **first stop** for a car loan, but compare rates elsewhere too.

**Loan application:** loan information, personal information, employment information, financial information, co-applicant/co-signer information, plus a copy of the purchase agreement. The institution verifies the information and runs a credit check. A down payment is usually needed on a big purchase; the larger the down payment, the less must be borrowed.

**Co-signers:** a co-applicant applies with the borrower (e.g., a spouse). A co-signer (often a parent for a young soldier) is responsible for the loan if the borrower fails to pay; a co-signer with good credit history can help get the loan.

**Leasing vs. buying:** leasing is presented as an option when a soldier cannot save enough to buy in a year, but the manual's through-line is that the only other way to buy expensive things without saving in advance is credit — and credit costs interest. Saving first is preferred.

**Credit cards:** every card has a spending limit set by the company; exceeding it means the company can refuse the purchase. The monthly statement shows purchases, balance, and minimum payment; companies charge different interest rates and some charge a yearly membership fee. Know the rate, know the fee, pay more than the minimum.

**Government Travel Charge Card — not a credit card:** the Army Travel Charge Card (Government Travel Charge Card program) cannot carry a balance forward. The account **must be paid in full when the monthly bill from Bank of America is received**. Individual Billing Accounts (IBAs) are issued to individual government travelers.

**Note:** the manual contains no section on payday lending or predatory lending by name; its credit guidance is general (compare rates, co-signer liability, minimum payments).

## Checking accounts

Three basic types: **standard** (full service — unlimited check writing, ATM deposits and withdrawals); **interest-bearing** (interest on average daily balance, minimum balance varies by institution); **premium** (minimum balance $10,000 across savings, money-market, and time deposits; adds a no-fee credit card with reduced interest rate, free checks, reduced personal-loan rates). Any type can be joint (both can write checks — advantage in an emergency, disadvantage that two writers make the account harder to manage and balance) or individual (only the opener writes checks — easier to manage). Discuss options with an account representative; personal financial situation decides.

**Writing checks and reconciling:** checks pay bills in person or by mail, and "checks are like cash" — no one untrusted should have access to the checkbook. Reconcile the monthly statement against personal records; re-add if balances do not match; visit or call the institution if the discrepancy persists.
