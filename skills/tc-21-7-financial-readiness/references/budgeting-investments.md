# Budgeting and Investments — TC 21-7 (Chapter 7)

## How to build a budget

"The best way to track your money is to develop a budget." Write down the goal, list **fixed expenses** (same every month) and **variable expenses** (vary month to month — clothing, phone bills, etc.), add **savings as a line item**, total expenses plus savings, subtract from monthly income. Review after **3 months** to compare actual costs with estimates; adjust as income, debts, and priorities change. The budget should be flexible — "your budget will fail unless you invest the time and effort to make it work."

**Single-soldier example:** goal, a new stereo; savings $100/month for the stereo and $50/month for other savings; expenses and savings totaling $700.

**Family example** (married soldier, wife and daughter; goal, a $300 vacation to visit parents — $75/month reaches it in 4 months): rent $450, utilities $70, food $300 (commissary is the tip for cheaper food), car payment $290, gas/oil/repairs $100, car insurance $60, clothing $75, cable TV $25, babysitting $40, telephone $45, church $40, school $130, recreation $50, incidentals/pocket money $120, vacation savings $75, other savings $50. **Total $1,920** against income of $1,428 after taxes + $520 spouse part-time = **$1,948**, remaining balance **$28**. Go over the budget with the spouse — both have to plan together; if it is not working, change it, but stick to some kind of budget.

## PCS moves

The Army pays for the move, but budget for out-of-pocket costs: hotel/motel bills at the old station, while traveling, and at the new station until housing is found; security deposits for housing and utilities; transportation, meals, laundry, and other unreimbursed incidentals. Talk to the finance office before the move about entitlements and per diem rates. On a PCS, pay is a flat per diem rate — no travel records needed for the voucher. **Never close the old SURE PAY account until the first SURE PAY deposit has been made to the new one.** No obligation to switch financial institutions on a PCS.

## Emergencies

First answer: come and see the leader. **Army Emergency Relief (AER)** and the **American Red Cross (ARC)** help with stolen or lost money, funeral expenses, travel for valid emergency leave, emergency car repairs, fire or other disaster, clothing replacement, and other money problems. Out of town, any installation's family assistance office coordinates with AER. Assistance is often an **interest-free loan that must be paid back**, but both organizations also offer help that does not have to be repaid, plus non-financial help (food baskets, commissary vouchers). The first sergeant and commander can point to other sources.

## Investments — brief survey (manual's terms)

- **Savings accounts:** low risk.
- **Certificates of Deposit (CDs):** money left for a set period; higher interest than savings; longer period, higher yield; penalty for early withdrawal; low risk.
- **Money Market Deposit Accounts (MMDAs):** rates vary with market conditions; minimum balance required; usually only six pre-authorized withdrawals a month (three payable to a third party); the manual calls them risky because the money is invested in the money market.
- **Stocks:** part ownership of a company; value rises or falls with the company; dividends possible; fairly risky.
- **Government bonds:** a loan to a government entity; U.S. Treasury bonds the safest; less risky than stocks because of guaranteed payback at maturity.
- **Corporate bonds:** a loan to a corporation at a fixed interest rate; similar in risk to stocks.
- **Mutual funds:** many investors pooling money into a diversified list of stocks, bonds, and bills, managed by knowledgeable people; generally less risky than stocks and bonds through diversification; common types Growth, Growth and Income, International/Global.
- **Money market mutual funds:** pooled investment in short-term items (Treasury bills, CDs, banker's acceptances, commercial paper); income and liquidity; fairly risky because money markets fluctuate.
- **Individual Retirement Accounts (IRAs):** no taxes until withdrawal; Traditional and Roth forms; the manual notes Roth IRAs offer no withdrawal penalties if the account has been open five years and the taxpayer is at least 59½ or is using the withdrawal for college or a first home, with no requirement to start taking money by 70½ and no tax-deductible contributions; fairly safe as long as the institution is sound.
- **Tangible investments:** things that can be seen and touched.

The chapter describes the vehicles and their relative risk and leaves the choice to the soldier.
