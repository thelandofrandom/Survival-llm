# Combat Skills and Fieldcraft — TC 3-21.75, Chapters 5–8

## Chapter 5 — Cover, concealment, and camouflage: outline

Section I. Cover: natural cover; man-made cover.
Section II. Concealment: natural concealment; actions as concealment.
Section III. Camouflage: movement; positions; outlines and shadows; shine; shape; colors; dispersion; preparation; individual techniques.

## Cover

- **Natural cover:** rocks, folds in the ground, trees thick enough to stop a bullet, ditches, berms.
- **Man-made cover:** walls, buildings, vehicles, sandbags, fighting positions.
- Cover is protection from **fire**. If it will not stop a bullet, it is concealment, not cover — and confusing the two gets people killed.

## Concealment

- **Natural concealment:** vegetation, shadows, ground folds, darkness.
- **Actions as concealment:** staying still, staying low, staying in shadow, moving only when the enemy is not looking.
- Concealment is protection from **observation**. It hides you; it does not protect you.

## Camouflage — the rules

- **Movement:** move slowly and deliberately in the open; move fast between cover. Movement draws the eye more than any other thing.
- **Positions:** build the position to blend with the surroundings, not to stand out from them.
- **Outlines and shadows:** break up the human silhouette — the most recognizable shape on a battlefield. Mind your shadow; it gives you away from the air.
- **Shine:** skin, metal, glass, and optics reflect light. Cover them — face paint, tape, cloth, mud.
- **Shape:** the regular shapes of military equipment (straight lines, right angles) stand out against nature's irregular shapes. Break them up with vegetation and netting.
- **Colors:** match the surroundings; change camouflage when the surroundings change.
- **Dispersion:** a group in one place is a target. Spread out — the distance between Soldiers is the distance that keeps one burst from killing two men.
- **Preparation:** camouflage is done before the position is occupied, not after the enemy arrives.
- **Individual techniques:** face paint (the face is the most recognizable part of the body); helmet cover with vegetation; uniform discipline (sleeves down, collar up, no bright colors).

## Chapter 6 — Fighting positions: outline

- Cover; concealment; camouflage; sectors and fields of fire; hasty and deliberate fighting positions; two-man fighting position; one-man fighting position; close combat missile fighting positions; range cards.

## Sectors and fields of fire

Assigned before the position is dug, and the position is built to serve them. Each Soldier knows his sector, his primary and secondary sectors, and the limits of his fire — and the sectors of adjacent positions interlock, so there is no dead space between them.

## Hasty and deliberate positions

- **Hasty fighting position:** dug fast when contact is imminent — a prone scrape with a parapet, enough to get below ground level and return fire.
- **Deliberate fighting position:** dug to standard when time allows — overhead cover, a parapet, a grenade sump, and a cleared field of fire.
- **Two-man fighting position:** the standard. Two Soldiers, interlocking sectors; one digs while the other provides security, then they switch.
- **One-man fighting position:** the fallback, when there is no partner.
- **Close combat missile fighting positions:** built for anti-armor weapons, with the backblast area cleared — a missile fired from a position with an obstructed backblast kills the firer.

## Range cards (DA Form 5517)

The record of the position: the sector sketch, the ranges to reference points, and the data that lets a replacement gunner fight from the position without re-learning it. A position without a range card is a position that dies with its occupant.

## Chapter 7 — Movement: outline

- Individual movement techniques; immediate actions while moving; fire and movement; movement on vehicles.

## Individual movement techniques

- **Low crawl:** the body flat, the weapon carried low, movement by the arms and legs — the slowest, most concealed movement.
- **High crawl:** the body raised on the elbows and knees — faster, less concealed.
- **Rush:** 3–5 seconds, then down. Never longer than the enemy needs to aim. Pick the next covered position before you stand up.
- **Movement between covered positions:** planned in advance — know where you are going before you move.

## Immediate actions while moving

- **React to flares:** drop, freeze, and do not move until the flare burns out.
- **React to indirect fire:** drop, take cover, and move out of the impact area.
- **React to direct fire:** return fire, seek the nearest covered and concealed position, report, maneuver. The same pattern as the battle drills.

## Fire and movement

One element fires while the other moves, alternating, so the enemy is always suppressed and the moving element is always covered. The fire element's job is to keep the enemy's head down; the moving element's job is to get to the next position without getting hit. The two jobs are equally important and equally lethal if done badly.

## Movement on vehicles

Mounting, riding, and dismounting under fire. The vehicle is transport, not cover — and the dismount is the most dangerous moment, because the enemy watches the vehicle and shoots the men who get out of it. Dismount fast, disperse, and get to cover.

## Chapter 8 — Urban areas: outline

Section I. Movement techniques: avoiding open areas; moving parallel to buildings; moving past windows; crossing a wall; moving around corners; moving within a building.
Section II. Other procedures: entering a building; clearing a room.
Section III. Fighting positions: hasty fighting position; prepared fighting position.

## Movement techniques

- **Avoid open areas:** streets, plazas, and intersections are kill zones. Move through buildings and alleys where possible.
- **Move parallel to buildings:** stay close to the wall, in its shadow, below the windows.
- **Move past windows:** below the sill, fast, without silhouetting. A window is a firing port for the enemy inside.
- **Cross a wall:** over the top, not around the end — the enemy expects you at the end.
- **Move around corners:** pie the corner — clear it in slices, weapon ready, without stepping into the open.
- **Move within a building:** along the walls, clearing as you go, watching the stairwells and the rooftops.

## Entering a building and clearing a room

- **Entering a building:** the doorway is the fatal funnel — the most dangerous place in urban combat. Enter fast, dominate the room, and clear the corners first.
- **Clearing a room:** the manual's room-clearing drill, with the four-man entry as the standard and the adaptations for fewer men. Each man has a corner; the team clears the room as a unit, not as four individuals. Speed, surprise, and violence of action are the principles — a slow entry is a dead entry.

## Fighting positions in urban areas

- **Hasty fighting position:** built fast in or around a building, using the building as the parapet.
- **Prepared fighting position:** built to standard, with the walls as cover and the windows and doors as firing ports. The urban fighting position uses the building as its protection — and the building's weakness (its openings) as its fields of fire.
