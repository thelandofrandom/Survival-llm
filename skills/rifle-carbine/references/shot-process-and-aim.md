# Shot Process and Aim — Reference (TC 3-22.9, Chapters 5–7)

Distilled from TC 3-22.9 *Rifle and Carbine* (Army, May 2016, C3 Nov 2019). Paraphrased; short quotes only.

## Chapter outline

- Ch. 5 — Employment: shot process, functional elements, target acquisition.
- Ch. 6 — Stability: support, muscle relaxation, natural point of aim, recoil management, shooter–gun angle, field of view, carry positions, stabilized firing, firing positions.
- Ch. 7 — Aim: common engagements, common aiming errors, complex engagements, target conditions, environmental conditions.

## The shot process

| Phase | Actions |
|---|---|
| Pre-shot | Target acquisition (detect, identify, locate), weapon presentation, establish stability |
| Shot | Aim (sight alignment, sight picture, hold), trigger control, follow-through |
| Post-shot | Call the shot, evaluate, re-engage or move |

The process is the same for every engagement. Speed changes with target size, target distance, and shooter capability — the process does not.

## The four functional elements

| Element | What it is | Failure mode |
|---|---|---|
| Stability | A consistent base, maintained through the shot until the recoil pulse ends — hold, support, posture | Wobble, drift, inconsistent base |
| Aim | Orienting the weapon, aligning sights, aligning on target, lead and elevation (hold) | Cant, bad sight picture, wrong hold |
| Control | Conscious actions before, during, and after the shot — whether, when, how to engage; trigger control first | Jerked trigger, no follow-through |
| Movement | Moving during the engagement — forward, retrograde, lateral, turning — while maintaining the other three | Shooting while moving without training |

The elements are interdependent. A failure in any one is a miss.

## Stability procedures

- **Support:** use the ground, a wall, a sandbag, a bipod, or a barricade. Support is the single greatest accuracy multiplier. Barricade shooting: brace the handguard or a bipod against cover.
- **Muscle relaxation:** relax every muscle not needed to hold the position; let bone support (elbows on the ground, skeleton carrying the weight) do the work.
- **Natural point of aim (NPA):** close the eyes, relax, open the eyes, see where the sights are. Adjust the *body*, not the muscles, to bring NPA onto the target. A shooter who muscles the weapon onto the target drifts off it the moment they relax or fire.
- **Recoil management:** body position and grip absorb recoil and return the sights to target. Position, not strength.
- **Carry positions:** low ready, high ready, patrol carry — balancing readiness against safety and fatigue.

## Firing positions (summary)

| Position | Stability | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Prone supported | Highest | Deliberate long-range fire |
| Prone unsupported | High | When no support is available |
| Kneeling supported | Medium-high | Quick stable position with cover |
| Kneeling unsupported | Medium | Fast, mobile |
| Standing supported | Medium | Barricade or wall available |
| Standing unsupported | Lowest | Close, fast engagements; movement |

Stabilized firing — using any available support — is always preferred when time allows.

## Common aiming errors

| Error | Symptom | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Cant (weapon tilted) | Shots drift laterally, worse at distance | Level the weapon; check the optic's level |
| Improper sight alignment | Front and rear sights misaligned | Align sights before aligning on target |
| Improper sight picture | Sights aligned but not on the right point | Confirm the sight picture on the target, not near it |
| Flinch | Anticipating recoil; shots low | Dry-fire practice; ball-and-dummy drill |
| Improper hold | Wrong elevation or windage hold | Confirm range and wind; use the correct hold |
| Poor trigger control | Shots scattered horizontally | Smooth, straight-to-the-rear press |

## Complex engagements

| Engagement | Technique |
|---|---|
| Moving target | Lead — aim ahead of the target by an amount based on speed, distance, and time of flight |
| Multiple targets | Engagement order (greatest threat first), rapid transitions, follow-through on each |
| Angled shot (uphill/downhill) | Gravity affects drop by horizontal distance, not slope distance — hold for the horizontal range |
| Limited visibility (night, smoke) | Thermal sights, illuminators, lasers; slower pace, closer ranges |
| Shooting while moving | Shorten the engagement distance or stop to shoot when precision matters |

## Environmental conditions

| Factor | Effect | Response |
|---|---|---|
| Wind | Dominant factor; pushes the bullet laterally, more at distance | Read by mirage, vegetation, and feel; hold into the wind |
| Temperature / altitude | Air density changes trajectory | Confirm zero at the operating altitude when possible |
| Rain | Degrades visibility and optics | Protect the optic; accept reduced effective range |
| Light (glare, shadow, backlight) | Changes what the shooter sees through the optic | Adjust position or use a sunshade; never shoot into glare blind |
