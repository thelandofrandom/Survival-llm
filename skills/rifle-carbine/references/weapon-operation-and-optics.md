# Weapon Operation and Optics — Reference (TC 3-22.9, Chapters 2–4)

Distilled from TC 3-22.9 *Rifle and Carbine* (Army, May 2016, C3 Nov 2019). Paraphrased; short quotes only.

## Chapter outline

- Ch. 2 — Rifle and carbine principles of operation: Army standard service rifle, upper receiver, lower receiver, cycle of function, cooling.
- Ch. 3 — Aiming devices: units of angular measurement, optics, thermal sights, pointers/illuminators/lasers.
- Ch. 4 — Mountable equipment: adaptive rail system, mountable weapons, aiming devices, accessories.

## The weapon

- **Army standard service rifle:** the M4-series carbine (M16-series rifle as its longer-barreled counterpart).
- **Upper receiver:** barrel, bolt carrier group, charging handle, handguards/rail. This is where the firing happens.
- **Lower receiver:** trigger group, magazine well, stock, pistol grip. This is where the shooter controls the weapon.
- Know every part by name, function, and failure mode — diagnosis starts with the part that failed.

## Cycle of function

| Step | What happens | Failure symptom if it stops here |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Feeding | Round stripped from the magazine, moved toward the chamber | Failure to feed — bolt does not return fully forward; visible gap at the ejection port |
| 2. Chambering | Round seated in the chamber | Failure to chamber — bolt does not fully seat and lock |
| 3. Locking | Bolt locks into the barrel extension | Bolt not locked; weapon will not fire |
| 4. Firing | Firing pin strikes the primer; round fires | Failure to fire — hammer falls, audible "click," no bang |
| 5. Unlocking | Gas cycles the bolt carrier rearward; bolt unlocks | Bolt seizes; case stays in the chamber |
| 6. Extracting | Extractor pulls the spent case from the chamber | Failure to extract — case partially or fully seated |
| 7. Ejecting | Ejector throws the case out of the ejection port | Failure to eject — case extracted but not thrown clear |
| 8. Cocking | Hammer reset for the next shot | Hammer not cocked; trigger has no resistance ("mush") |

The phase where the cycle stopped identifies the general problem. Diagnose by symptom, not by theory.

## Cooling rules

- Sustained fire heats the barrel; a hot barrel shifts point of impact, degrades accuracy, and can cook off a chambered round.
- Manage the rate of fire; let the barrel cool between sustained engagements.
- Never leave a round chambered in a hot weapon longer than necessary.

## Angular measurement

| Unit | Definition | Field value |
|---|---|---|
| Minute of angle (MOA) | 1/60th of a degree | **1 MOA = 1.047 inches at 100 yards** — treat as 1 inch per 100 yards (≈2 in at 200 yd, ≈3 in at 300 yd) |
| Mil (milliradian) | 1/1000th of a radian | 1 mil = 1 meter at 1,000 meters (≈3.6 in at 100 yd) |

- MOA is the language of sight adjustment: a sight that moves 1 MOA per click moves point of impact ~1 inch at 100 yards.
- Mils are the language of range estimation and holdover with mil-dot reticles.
- Optics may adjust in MOA while the reticle is graduated in mils — the shooter must convert without hesitation.

## Optics

| Device | Role | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Close combat optic (CCO) | Reflex/red-dot, close range | Fast target acquisition; no magnification |
| Rifle combat optic (RCO) | Magnified, medium range | Reticle with range and holdover marks; know what each mark means |
| Magnified optics | Precision at distance | Adjustment values, eye relief, and failure modes matter |

- An optic does not make a bad shooter good; it makes a good shooter faster.
- Know the reticle, the adjustment values, and the failure modes (dead battery, fogged lens, loose mount — and the iron sights underneath).

## Thermal sights

- Detect heat, not light: see through darkness, smoke, and light foliage.
- Defeated by glass, water, and heavy rain; present a digital image the shooter must learn to read.
- **Stadia reticle:** vertical stadia (lower left) estimates range to a standing dismounted threat by height; horizontal stadia (upper right) estimates range to an exposed threat by width — both using standard threat dimensions.

## Pointers, illuminators, lasers

- Visible and infrared laser aiming devices and illuminators.
- **Safety:** never point a laser at friendly forces or aircraft. Infrared lasers are invisible to the naked eye and therefore more dangerous, not less.

## Mountable equipment

- **Adaptive rail system:** the handguard rail is the mounting platform.
- Mountable weapons (grenade launchers, shotguns), aiming devices (optics, lasers, illuminators), accessories (vertical grips, bipods, lights).
- **Rule:** every mounted item adds weight and changes balance and point of impact. Mount only what the mission requires, confirm zero after mounting, and know how to remove a failed accessory under pressure.
