## Trigger and breathing control

- **Trigger control:** a smooth, straight-to-the-rear press, finger placed so the press does not pull the muzzle off target. Hold the trigger to the rear until the recoil pulse ends (follow-through).
- **Breathing control:** fire at the natural respiratory pause — the brief moment at the end of an exhalation when the body is still. Do not hold the breath more than a few seconds; oxygen deprivation degrades vision and judgment.
- **Workspace management:** the area in front of the body where loading, reloading, and malfunction clearance happen — kept clear, efficient, and consistent so the shooter can work without looking.
- **Calling the shot:** predict where the shot went from the sight picture at the moment of firing, before looking at the target. A shooter who can call the shot can diagnose their own errors.
- **Rate of fire:** deliberate, rapid, and sustained rates — faster is not better if it is not accurate. Manage barrel heat at sustained rates.
- **Follow-through:** hold the position, hold the trigger, reacquire the sight picture, be ready for the next shot.

## Malfunction symptom table

| Symptom | Diagnosis | Corrective action |
|---|---|---|
| Trigger squeezed, hammer falls, audible "click," no bang | Failure to fire | **Immediate action** |
| Bolt does not return fully forward; visible gap at the ejection port | Failure to feed (may cause stovepipe or double feed) | **Immediate action** |
| Round feeds but bolt does not fully seat and lock | Failure to chamber | **Immediate action** |
| Case stays partially or fully seated; extractor lost its grip | Failure to extract | **Immediate action** |
| Case extracted but not thrown clear of the ejection port | Failure to eject | **Immediate action** |
| Trigger has little resistance ("mush") or cannot be squeezed | Cycle interrupted beyond immediate action | **Remedial action** |
| Immediate action fails after two cycles | Persistent stoppage | **Remedial action** |
| Case lodged sideways, stopping the bolt | Stovepipe | **Remedial action** |
| Two rounds competing for the chamber | Double feed | **Remedial action** |
| Bolt override or other hand-clearing stoppage | Complex stoppage | **Remedial action** |

## Immediate action

- Simple, rapid actions taken when the trigger is squeezed, the hammer falls with an audible "click," and the weapon does not fire.
- **Rule: do not over-analyze.** Execute corrective action immediately, without hesitation or investigation, during combat conditions.
- The end state of any corrective action is a properly functioning weapon.

## Remedial action

- A skilled technique applied to a specific problem that immediate action will not fix.
- Taken when the trigger has little resistance ("mush") or cannot be squeezed.
- **Minimum of two cycles of immediate action first** — the first without a magazine change, the second with a magazine change — before concluding remedial action is needed.
- Remedial action requires the soldier to identify one of four issues (immediate action failed, stovepipe, double feed, or other specific stoppage) and apply the specific technique for it.

## Transition to secondary weapon

| Situation | Action |
|---|---|
| Primary weapon cannot be brought back quickly | Transition to secondary weapon and continue the engagement |
| Secondary weapon cannot defeat the threat | Move to cover, announce status to the team, execute corrective action |
| No secondary weapon | Move to cover, announce status to the team, execute corrective action |

Announce your status to the team. A silent malfunction is a gap in the team's fire.

## Zeroing procedure (Appendix E)

1. **Mechanical zero:** set the sights to their mechanical zero as the starting point.
2. **Laser borelight:** boresight with the laser borelight to get on paper.
3. **25-meter grouping:** fire groups at 25 meters on the 25-meter zero target to confirm consistency before adjusting.
4. **25-meter zero:** adjust the sights to center the group on the target at 25 meters.
5. **Confirmation:** verify the zero with live fire. **Borelight procedures must be verified with live-fire zero at 25 meters** — borelight alone is not a zero.

## Zeroing notes

- Grouping comes before zeroing: a shooter who cannot group cannot zero, because the adjustment is meaningless on a scattered group.
- A coach observes the shooter's errors (stability, trigger, breathing, position) and notes them for discussion after the group — not during it.
- Peer coaches are useful when an experienced coach is unavailable, and coaching itself deepens the peer coach's own understanding — but a peer coach is limited by their level of training.
- The coach cannot observe aiming errors directly; coach and firer must have an open dialogue, and the firer must not hesitate to ask questions.

## Boresight offsets (Appendix F)

- **10-meter boresight offsets:** the table for boresighting at 10 meters — used when a 25-meter range is unavailable.
- **25-meter zero offsets:** the table for zeroing at 25 meters with various sight systems.
- Offsets exist because the sight sits above the bore: at short distances the bullet strikes below the line of sight, and the offset compensates. Always use the offset table for the specific sight system mounted.

## Drills (Appendix D)

- **Dry-fire drills:** trigger control, sight alignment, position, and malfunction clearance without ammunition.
- **Live-fire drills:** grouping, zeroing, and engagement drills at increasing complexity.
- **Combat drills:** movement, multiple targets, transitions, and malfunction clearance under time pressure.
- Training progresses from the simplest shot to the most complex — never the reverse.
