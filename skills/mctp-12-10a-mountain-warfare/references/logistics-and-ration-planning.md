# Logistics and Ration Planning — MCTP 12-10A

Source: MCTP 12-10A, Chapter 6 (Logistics and Sustainment). Distilled from the manual; see `full-text.md` for the verbatim source.

## Calorie Requirements

- Mountain operations can demand **6,000 or more calories per day**.
- Despite the greater caloric requirement, high altitude **reduces the appetite** — leaders must enforce eating.
- Plan calories for the environment with the right balance of fat, carbohydrate, and protein.

## Ration Values

| Ration | Calories | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Standard MRE | ~1,250 calories each | Insufficient for mountain operations; at high altitude an individual needs more than the standard three MREs per day. Each case: 12 meals, ~22 lb. |
| Meal, cold weather (MCW) | ~1,540-calorie menu bags | Main meal is dehydrated; designed for cold weather. |
| First strike ration (FSR) | 2,850 calories in one ration | Needs supplemental items (fruit, vegetables, milk) or one additional MRE at high altitude to meet calorie needs and add menu variety. |
| Modular operational ration enhancement | Supplemental calories and nutrients | Augments the MRE or FSR; **not a replacement** for either. |

## Ration Planning Rules

- Keep food warm where possible, such as by carrying it inside the clothing. Items not kept warm freeze and cannot be eaten.
- A diet high in carbohydrates is often the best way to maintain the calories necessary to function at altitude.
- When comparing rations: three MREs (~3,750 calories) fall well short of the 6,000+ calorie mountain requirement — plan the MCW or FSR plus supplements, not MREs alone.
- Plan on adding **one MRE per day** to increase calories and menu variety at altitude.

## Water

| Condition | Planning factor |
|---|---|
| Stationary | 4 quarts per day per Marine |
| Moving | Up to 8 quarts per day per Marine |
| High mountains | Add about 2 quarts per individual |

- Water sources are often frozen, contaminated, or far below the position.
- Melting snow for water costs fuel and time — plan for both.

## Acclimatization

- Personnel must be acclimatized and in a high state of physical fitness, with specialized equipment maintained, before operations at altitude.
- No shortcuts exist; the schedule governs the mission, not the reverse.
- Reference timeline (ATP 3-90.97, Appendix A): 80% of respiratory acclimatization in 7–10 days; 80–90% overall in two weeks to one month; maximum acclimatization may take months to years.
- **Work high, sleep low** — the altitude at which troops sleep is critical.
- Training for mountain operations takes two to three weeks and runs concurrent with acclimatization.

## Cold-Weather Injury Prevention

- History's lesson: mountain campaigns have repeatedly produced large numbers of casualties from frostbite and other preventable cold-weather injuries.
- Prevention: layering discipline, buddy system, warming rotations, dry socks, and command emphasis.
- Downwash from helicopters in freezing conditions can cause frostbite to exposed skin — keep skin covered during helicopter operations.
