# Infiltration and Area Development — Reference (FM 31-21, Chapters 6 and 7)

Distilled from FM 31-21 *Special Forces Operations* (Army, Feb 1969, Ch. 1, 1971). Paraphrased; short quotes only.

> **Historic doctrine.** Infiltration means and communications hardware are 1960s-era. The planning factors and the seven-phase model are the durable content.

## Chapter outline

- Ch. 6 — Infiltration and exfiltration: methods, planning, execution.
- Ch. 7 — Organization and development of the UWOA: area command, SF–resistance relations, PSYOP, communications, logistics, administration, training program.

## Infiltration methods

| Method | Means | Strengths | Limits |
|---|---|---|---|
| Air — parachute | Static-line / free-fall into a reception area | Classic UW entry; reaches deep denied areas | Weather, reception party, enemy air defense, drop-zone security |
| Air — helicopter | Landing or rappel into an LZ | Precise, can carry equipment | Range, weather, noise, enemy air defense |
| Air — fixed-wing landing | Landing strip | Largest loads | Needs a usable strip and security |
| Water — surface craft | Boats, small craft | Coastal access | Tides, weather, enemy coastal surveillance |
| Water — subsurface | Submarine / swimmer delivery | Stealth | Slow, limited loads, specialized |
| Land | Foot or vehicle through gaps | No air/water signature | Slowest, most vulnerable, depends on guides and population |

## Infiltration planning checklist

- [ ] Reception arrangements confirmed (party, signals, security)
- [ ] Alternate infiltration method and alternate DZ/LZ selected
- [ ] Abort criteria defined before departure
- [ ] Communications plan and check-in schedule set
- [ ] Equipment and resupply loads staged for the first days in-country
- [ ] Evasion and escape plan briefed
- [ ] **Exfiltration plan written before infiltration begins**

## Exfiltration — the harder half

- Exfiltration mirrors infiltration, with casualties, prisoners, and equipment to move and an enemy now alert.
- Plan exfiltration routes, reception, and alternate means at the same time as infiltration.
- A team that knows how it got in but not how it gets out is trapped, not deployed.

## The seven phases of UW area development

| Phase | Name | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| I | Psychological Preparation | Condition the population to support resistance and resist enemy control. Political work before military work. |
| II | Initial Contact | SF elements contact resistance leaders, assess potential, establish the relationship. |
| III | Infiltration | The detachment enters the area. |
| IV | Organization | Build the area command, sector commands, guerrilla units, underground, auxiliary. Establish communications, security, logistics. |
| V | Buildup | Train, equip, expand the force; stockpile supplies; conduct limited operations to test the force and the enemy. |
| VI | Combat Employment | Full guerrilla operations in support of theater objectives. |
| VII | Demobilization | Disband or transition the force when the need ends or a legitimate government assumes control. Planned, not improvised. |

## UWOA development checklist

- [ ] **Area command** established: combined command controlling subordinate sector commands
- [ ] **Sector commands** stood up under the area command
- [ ] **Guerrilla units** organized, trained, equipped
- [ ] **Underground** (clandestine political organization) functioning
- [ ] **Auxiliary** (civilian support network: food, shelter, intelligence, couriers) functioning
- [ ] **Communications:** nets linking area command to sectors and to the SFOB, with courier backup
- [ ] **Logistics:** caches established, airdrop/resupply plan running, captured-supply procedures set
- [ ] **Security:** compartmentation, vetting, physical security of caches and HQ
- [ ] **PSYOP:** messaging to the population, the enemy, and the resistance itself
- [ ] **Administration:** records, discipline, pay or equivalent
- [ ] **Training program:** structured, scheduled, covering weapons, tactics, demolitions, communications, medicine, leadership

## SF–resistance relations — rules

1. The detachment commander advises; he does not command the resistance. Resistance leaders must own the movement.
2. Trust is the currency: cultural respect, language, shared hardship, kept promises.
3. The movement that collapses when the detachment leaves was never a movement — it was a dependency.

## Logistics in the UWOA

- The UWOA lives on caches, airdrops, captured supplies, and population support.
- Logistics planning precedes operations. A guerrilla force without ammunition is a crowd.
- Cache discipline: dispersed, concealed, compartmented — one compromised cache must not unravel the network.
