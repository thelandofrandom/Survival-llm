# Guerrilla Warfare and Security — Reference (FM 31-21, Chapters 3, 8, 9)

Distilled from FM 31-21 *Special Forces Operations* (Army, Feb 1969, Ch. 1, 1971). Paraphrased; short quotes only.

> **Historic doctrine.** Principles below are the manual's durable core. Specific organizations, communications means, and references to 1960s command structures are dated.

## Chapter outline

- Ch. 3 — Fundamentals of UW: indigenous resistance forces, guerrilla warfare, evasion and escape, subversion.
- Ch. 8 — Intelligence and security: production, counterintelligence, security within the UWOA.
- Ch. 9 — Combat employment of guerrilla forces: operations, demobilization.

## Guerrilla warfare principles

| Principle | What it means in practice |
|---|---|
| Surprise | Attack only with local advantage and detailed, timely intelligence; rehearse; disappear before the enemy reacts |
| Elusiveness | Never accept a stand-up fight against regular forces; trade ground for time and lives |
| Mobility | Move faster than the enemy can react; difficult terrain is the shield |
| Population support | The population provides intelligence, security, food, shelter, and recruits — and is the force's early-warning system |
| Security | Protective surveillance; civilian supporters augment local security and report enemy movement |
| Avoid static defense | Difficult terrain restricts enemy observation and movement; static positions invite destruction |

## Offensive operations

- Depend on detailed, timely intelligence and a well-conceived, rehearsed plan.
- Raid, ambush, interdiction, harassment: hit lines of communication, isolated outposts, supply columns.
- In support of conventional operations: interdict LOCs, seize key terrain ahead of the advance, disrupt enemy reserves.
- Coordination with conventional forces in the same area is a command problem — plan it, do not improvise it.

## Defensive posture

- The guerrilla is always prepared for rapid withdrawal to alternate areas.
- Security and area control come more easily in terrain that limits the enemy's ability to mount coordinated operations quickly.
- A force that tries to hold ground like a regular unit will be destroyed like one.

## Subversion

Actions — violent or nonviolent — designed to reduce the military, moral, political, economic, or psychological strength of the enemy or the regime it supports. A resistance organization undermines or overthrows a government or occupying power by the systematic application of subversion. SF personnel must be trained to conduct and support subversive operations; it is the political war that makes the military war possible.

**Warning from the manual:** a resistance movement has a political character that may not be apparent when support is first extended. Evaluate the long-range as well as the short-range effects of any support rendered. Arming the wrong movement creates the next enemy.

## Evasion and escape (E&E)

- Purpose: return to friendly control military personnel and selected individuals isolated in hostile areas or escaped from captivity.
- SF maintains the capability to support or conduct E&E inside UWOAs; personnel require training in evasion methods and techniques.
- The Air Force holds responsibility for joint E&E tactics, techniques, and equipment; the Army provides support and the in-area capability.

## Intelligence in the UWOA

| Function | Practice |
|---|---|
| Collection | The population is the greatest asset — enemy patrols, checkpoints, supply movements, morale |
| Production | Evaluate and disseminate fast enough to act on; tactical and immediate |
| Counterintelligence | The underground and auxiliary are penetrated as a matter of course; compartmentation, need-to-know, constant vetting |
| Security | Physical security of detachment, area command, caches; communications security; protection of the supporting population |

## Security rules

1. **Compartmentation:** no one knows more than they need to do their job.
2. **Vetting is continuous:** a trusted courier yesterday is a suspect today if the pattern changes.
3. **One compromise can unravel months:** a captured courier or a compromised cache is treated as a network emergency, not a local incident.
4. **Communications security is survival:** enemy direction-finding and interception locate a detachment faster than any patrol.

## Demobilization (Phase VII)

- Trigger: the need ends, or a legitimate government exists that can assume control.
- **Courses of action:** disband the force in part or in whole; integrate personnel into legitimate security forces; or transition to a political role — decided by the sponsoring authority, not by the detachment.
- **Tasks:** collect or account for weapons; return personnel to civilian life or integrate them; settle pay and obligations; honor the political settlement.
- **Failure mode:** a guerrilla force abandoned armed and unpaid becomes the next war's problem. Demobilization is a planned operation with its own resources and timeline.

## Stability operations (Ch. 10) — quick reference

- Mission: advise, assist, and train host-nation forces; support civic action and PSYOP.
- Distinct logistics and personnel selection: language, cultural, and political skills at least as much as combat skills.
- Selection and training of personnel for stability operations is a separate, deliberate process — do not assume a UW-trained detachment is automatically a stability-operations detachment.
