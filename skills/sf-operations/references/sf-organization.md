# SF Organization — Reference (FM 31-21, Chapters 2 and 4)

Distilled from FM 31-21 *Special Forces Operations* (Army, Feb 1969, Ch. 1, 1971). Paraphrased; short quotes only.

> **Historic doctrine.** Organizations below are the 1969 structure. Current SF organization differs (the modern ODA/ODB/ODC and SF company/battalion/group structure evolved from this lineage but is not identical). Use for lineage and principles.

## Chapter outline

- Ch. 1 — General: role of SF, mission and capabilities.
- Ch. 2 — Organization and general employment: group, battalion, company, detachments.
- Ch. 4 — Command and control of UW forces: external and internal organization, communications.

## Organization table (1969)

| Element | Commander | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Special Forces Group | Colonel | Parent organization; trains, deploys, sustains detachments; group HQ and staff (personnel, intelligence, operations, logistics, communications, medical, CA/PSYOP) |
| Special Forces Battalion | Lieutenant colonel | Intermediate HQ controlling several companies |
| Special Forces Company | Major | Controls multiple operational detachments |
| Operational Detachment C (ODC) | — | Control detachment for a wide area; controls several B detachments |
| Operational Detachment B (ODB) | — | Control detachment for a sector; controls several A detachments |
| Operational Detachment A (ODA, "A-team") | Captain | Basic operational unit; deploys into denied territory, contacts and builds the resistance |
| Special Forces Operational Base (SFOB) | — | Rear base commanding, controlling, sustaining, and communicating with deployed detachments |

## The A-team (Operational Detachment A)

The basic operational unit — a small team built around a detachment commander and specialists:

- **Commander** (captain) and **executive officer** — leadership, planning, liaison with resistance leaders.
- **Operations/intelligence sergeant** — planning, intelligence, area assessment.
- **Weapons sergeants** — small arms, crew-served weapons, training indigenous fighters.
- **Demolitions/engineering sergeant** — demolitions, obstacles, field engineering.
- **Communications sergeants** — the detachment's link to the SFOB; radio operation and maintenance.
- **Medical sergeants** — casualty care and medical training of indigenous forces; often the detachment's most valuable civil-affairs asset.

The team is designed to live, move, and fight with indigenous forces for extended periods with minimal outside support. Its protection is security, mobility, and the population — not firepower.

## The SFOB (Special Forces Operational Base)

- Commands and controls deployed detachments, directly or through B/C control detachments.
- Sustains them: resupply (airdrop, infiltration), replacements, medical evacuation.
- Links the UW effort to the theater commander and the unified command.
- In a large theater, multiple SFOBs may exist, each under the theater UW command.

## Command relationships

| Situation | Who controls the guerrilla force |
|---|---|
| Early UWOA development | SFOB, directly or through B/C detachments |
| UWOA inside an advancing tactical command's area of influence | Operational control transfers to that tactical command; SF personnel attach as liaison/advisors |
| Delegation below division level | Generally not done — guerrilla forces are a theater asset |

## Mission-type orders

- Distances, topography, and enemy counter-guerrilla activity preclude detailed control from the base.
- Subordinate commanders receive mission-type orders, time-phased to support stated objectives, and operate freely within the scope of assigned tasks.
- Response to orders depends on the relationship between the deployed SF element and the resistance force — a relationship built on trust, not authority.

## Command principles

1. **Flexibility:** the structure bends to the mission, the area, and the resistance potential. The table of organization is a starting point, not scripture.
2. **Unity through the SFOB:** all detachments answer through one chain to the theater UW command.
3. **Liaison on transfer:** when control passes to a tactical command, SF personnel go with the force as advisors — the guerrilla force is never handed over without its SF link.
4. **Communications are command:** without secure, redundant communications, the detachment is isolated and the resistance is uncontrollable.
