# Planning and March Math — FM 21-18 (Chapters 1–3, ¶¶1-1–3-5)

## Introduction (Chapter 1)

- **Definition:** movement of troops and equipment mainly by foot, limited vehicle support; combat readiness, ease of control, terrain adaptability, slow rate, increased fatigue; does not depend on roads.
- **Historical examples:** 3/30th Infantry, Sicily, 20–21 Jul 1943 — 54-mile cross-country march in 33 hours, in the attack 2 hours after arrival; 3d U.S. Army, Ardennes, Dec 1944 — ~250,000 men, 25,000 vehicles shifted 50–150 miles; 1,254,042 truck-miles 18–31 Dec; 42,000 tons moved 17–23 Dec.
- **Mission:** arrive at the prescribed time **and** physically able to execute the tactical mission immediately. Equipment, rate, and rests must equate with the men's endurance.
- **Classification:** tactical (combat zone, security, combat-ready formations, contact expected) vs. administrative (COMMZ/zone of the interior, best method, contact remote, secure routes).
- **Types:** day (preferred absent enemy threat; faster, less tiring, more vulnerable); limited visibility/night (closed formations, slow, hard control/reconnaissance, good concealment, surprise, avoids heat); forced (more hours, not more rate; only when needed; tell troops why); shuttle (alternate riding/marching, round-trips, too few vehicles).

## Factors (Chapter 2)

- **Tactical (¶2-1):** do not walk soldiers until they enter battle; then carry only what they wear, weapons, ammunition, water, minimal rations. All-round security always: covering force (large units), advance guard, flank and rear guards. Reconnoiter and mark routes; check bridge capacities and underpass dimensions; reduce obstacles. Combat support forward for quick availability; sustainment loads may trail. Grouping for movement = organization for combat.
- **March discipline (¶2-3):** formation, distances, speed, concealment/cover; water, light, noise, communication disciplines.
- **Water discipline (¶2-4):** drink plenty before; only treated water from approved sources; often, before/during/after; small quantities, slowly, even when not thirsty; refill at every opportunity; avoid spilling. Dehydration as prevalent in cold as hot (perspiration hidden by clothing). Salt in food suffices; extra salt only under medical direction. Purification tablets if pure water unavailable (FM 21-10). NBC areas: consumption rises, forced hydration needed.
- **Acclimatization/morale (¶¶2-5–2-6):** mountain ops need 10–14 days high-altitude training; psychological adjustment by gradual education; self-confidence from leadership and progressive training. Morale: advance warning; do not form too early; avoid standing delays; steady rate; reasonable passing-vehicle speed; full break time for the rear; leaders march with soldiers and walk the column.
- **Load limits (¶2-7):** fighting load ≤ **48 lb**; approach march load (incl. fighting load) < **72 lb**. Carry what can be carried without impaired combat effectiveness; one truck + one trailer per rifle company or smaller for additional gear.

## Planning (Chapter 3)

- **Sequence (¶3-1):** receive mission → warning order → estimate of the situation (S3) → detailed movement plans/OPORD → road movement orders (OPORD or annex, with table/overlay/strip map) → reconnaissance party (ground recon; map/air recon are not substitutes) → quartering party (HHC commander; marks areas, plans RP-to-area movement, guides rehearse).
- **Organization:** march unit (single commander; squad–company) → march serial (one+ march units, same characteristics; usually battalion+) → march column (one+ serials, same route; usually brigade+). Order of march: tanks/infantry interspersed; artillery/mortars forward and throughout; air defense throughout or by bounds; engineers well forward; antitank throughout.
- **Rates (App. A ¶3f; Ch. 3 table not recovered in extraction):** roads day 4.0 kph, night 3.2 kph; cross-country day 2.4 kph, night 1.6 kph. Normal pace 30 in. at 106 steps/min. Normal day: 20–32 km in 5–8 h at 4 kph. Formation column of twos; 2–5 m between soldiers (1–3 m night), 50 m platoons (25 m night), 100 m companies (50 m night).
- **Halts (App. A ¶3j):** 15 min after first 45 min; then 10 min every 50 min; halfway, 15-min rest with sock change; security posted; A-rations 1 h, MREs 30 min.
- **Math (¶¶3-2–3-3):** R = D/T; D = R×T; T = D/R. TDIS = distance ÷ rate (excludes long halts). PST (min) = column length × factor (0.0150 @ 4.0 kph; 0.0187 @ 3.2; 0.0250 @ 2.4; 0.0375 @ 1.6) — e.g., 1,500 m at 4 kph = 22.5 min. Arrival = SP + TDIS + scheduled halts (0800 + 6h45 + 1h = 1545). Completion = arrival + PST, or SP + TDIS + PST + scheduled halts (0800 + 6h45 + 41 min + 1h = 1626). Road clearance = TDIS + PST. SP: recognizable, never in defile/on hill/at sharp curve, far enough out for units to be at speed and interval. RP: recognizable, guides meet units, units do not stay. Column/road gap, time gap (no prescribed values), traffic density (vehicles per mile/km).
- **Products:** road movement table (annex; serials, times at checkpoints, vehicles, load class, routes, remarks), operation overlay, strip map (SP, RP, restrictions, critical points, distances).
