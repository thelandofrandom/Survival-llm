# Execution and Duties — FM 21-18 (Chapter 4, ¶¶4-1–4-20; Appendix A)

## Conduct of the March (¶4-1)

- **Reconnaissance party:** route travel time, underpass/bridge capacity, culverts, ferries, fords, critical points, obstacles.
- **Main body:** constant designated speed; maintain interval and column gap; erratic speed (esp. at hills) creates an accordion/whipping effect forcing unsafe tail speeds; behind schedule → designated catch-up speed only. SP arrival on time is critical; report crossing each control point; air and ground security throughout.
- **Trail party:** last march unit in a battalion serial, usually BMO-led with maintenance and medical personnel; recovers disabled vehicles and stragglers.

## Security, Halts, Night, Safety (¶¶4-2–4-8)

- Continuous all-round security on the march and at halts; passive/active measures vs. air attack and long-range weapons; more security at critical points.
- **Night marches:** close intervals; connecting files and messengers; unit colors on flashlights for recognition; flashlights, lanterns, luminous markers, pyrotechnics per light discipline. Safety: off-road trails where possible; marked/equipped guards front and rear (flanks if vehicles may approach) with reflective/luminous material; guards carry reflective fabric/tape, vests, caps, mittens, hat bands, signs, red lights, flashlights, batons, flags, lanterns; warn vehicle operators, restrict speeds, teach luminous-device meanings; enforce safety even on foot-exclusive routes.

## Duties (¶¶4-9–4-18)

- **Company commander:** warning order in time; reconnoiter route to SP; march plan and road movement order (enemy/friendly situation, purpose, units, objective, order/rate/route, SP/time, RP, disciplines, formation time, uniform/equipment incl. water/rations, collecting posts, straggler control, C2); inspect before marching; head of company to SP, then supervise; check rate, formation, stragglers; rapid movement into assembly areas after.
- **Executive officer:** leads when commander is not at the head; supervises pace setter; assists at halts; posts/supervises traffic guards and guides; supervises CP and latrines on arrival.
- **First sergeant:** NCO inspections; formation, pace, intervals, noise/light/water/rest discipline.
- **Platoon leader:** briefs platoon; inspects clothing/equipment; maintains distances; enforces discipline; at halts clears the road, relaxes men (sit/lie, loosen equipment), checks condition, enforces water/food discipline and sanitation; reforms ~1 min before marching; leads into assembly area from the RP.
- **Platoon sergeant:** halt inspections; straggler control (examines casualties with aidman before recommending fall-out to wheel transport); resupply of water, rations, medical supplies at rest stops.
- **Squad leader:** inspects boots (fit, serviceability) and socks (clean, no holes/bad mends); extra pair of socks per soldier; foot kit (powder, gauze, adhesive tape); full canteens; marches where he can control the squad; shifts heavy loads from tired to rested soldiers; foot inspection after the march, reports to platoon leader.
- **Medical aidman:** assesses/treats march casualties; advises on evacuation.
- **Guides:** lead route and area entry/exit; posted at critical points (esp. junctions with direction changes) with instructions and strip map; join the tail or are picked up by transport.
- **Road guards:** ~50 m front and rear of column; at intersections while crossing (acting as guides); flashlights/lanterns at night.
- **Pace setter:** experienced soldier, same load as the majority, 4–10 m at the head; maintains ordered rate by pace and cadence; medium height for average strides; pace governed by the most heavily loaded element; supervised by the officer/NCO at the head.

## Special Operations (¶¶4-19–4-20)

- **Limited visibility:** disadvantages — navigation, slower speed, checkpoint recognition, control, intervals; advantages — security, tighter formations, less traffic, surprise/stealth. Increase control: shorter distances, more guides, connecting files, visual comms.
- **Forced marches:** normal day 8 h / 32 km at 4 kph. Maxima: **56 km in 24 h; 96 km in 48 h; 128 km in 72 h**. Increase hours, not rate, where possible; rest out of the hottest time of day; consider faster rate when troops are most rested. Worked 52-km schedule from first light: 20 km @ 4 kph (5.0 h) → noon meal/rest 2.0 h → 20 km @ 4 kph (5.0 h) → supper/rest 6.0 h → 12 km @ 3.2 kph (3.84 h) → total 21.84 h.

## Battalion SOP Extract (Appendix A)

Standardizes foot-march procedures for tactical marches on improved roads when no enemy attack is indicated: water discipline (treated water only, drink often, slowly), light discipline (no unshielded light, no smoking at night), noise discipline (inspect every soldier to eliminate noise), communications discipline (minimum radio; arm-and-hand signals, messengers), code words for 5-minutes-to-break / begin break / 3-minutes-to-march; organization (reconnaissance, quartering parties; companies as march units; frontline ambulance per company; motorized elements integrated or following by bounds); control measures (SP/RP, critical points, times, order/route of march, distances, column gap, pace setter, road movement graph/table, assembly/bivouac areas, CP, comms, lead/trail chemical-light markers); security (continuous all-round, passive/active vs. aircraft and long-range weapons); formation (staggered column of twos, one file each side of the road); straggler control (chain of command owns fall-outs; name lines and medical disposition relayed; mission-essential equipment transferred to others).
