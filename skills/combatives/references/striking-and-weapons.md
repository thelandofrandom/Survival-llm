# Striking and Contact Weapons — TC 3-25.150, Chapters 5–6

## Chapter 5 — Advanced ground fighting techniques: outline

- Advanced ground fighting techniques; striking skills; defense; advanced clinch positions; pummeling; knee strikes.

## Advanced ground fighting

- **Advanced finishing moves** — the submissions that require the intermediate positions as a platform.
- **Side control escapes** — opponent in standard side control; opponent with near-side hip control; opponent with head and far arm underhooked; opponent with both hands in near-side control. Four variations because side control is not one position — it is four, and each needs its own escape.
- **North/south escapes** — the north/south position (head-to-head, chest-to-chest, opposite directions); opponent rested on forearms; opponent with both elbows in armpits.
- **Far-side arm bar**; **paper cutter from modified knee mount to belly scarf hold**; **reverse arm bar with leg from the guard**; **Achilles lock defense**.

## Advanced clinch positions

- **Inside control** — hands inside the opponent's arms, controlling the center.
- **Neck and biceps** — one hand on the neck, one on the biceps, the classic control tie-up.
- **Double underhooks** — both arms under, the strongest clinch control.
- **Front headlock** — the head trapped under the arm, the standing guillotine position.
- **Counter to inside control** — the answer when the opponent wins the inside.

## Pummeling

Pummeling is the fight for the inside position — the arms swimming for underhooks and control. The manual's pummeling set: **50/50 pummeling** (even position, both fighters swimming); **modified seatbelt counter**; **overhook counter**; **outside-to-inside wedge**; **over-to-under lever**; **under-to-over lever**; **push the elbow inward**; **double elbow lift**. Pummeling is the clinch equivalent of drilling — it is trained until the arms find the underhook without thinking.

## Knee strikes

- **Attack:** long knee (from distance); up knee (from the clinch, driving upward); round knee (across the body).
- **Defense:** hip check; pull toward the knee; pull away from the knee; hand check; knee lift and grab; tilt the head; hip check, turn down; pull toward the knee, inside hook; pull away from the knee, sweep; pull away from the knee, throw; knee strike, snap-down; hip bump from modified seatbelt clinch; 50/50, catch and lift; counter to inside control, catch and lift; hip check entry for hip toss.

The knee is the clinch's finishing weapon — the defense is not to block it (you cannot block a knee with your hands) but to change the distance or the angle so it misses, or to catch it and turn it into a takedown.

## Striking skills — movement

- **Basic footwork** — the stance and the step that keep the fighter balanced and mobile.
- **Weight shift drill** — transferring weight between the feet to generate power without telegraphing.

## Arm strikes — attack

| Strike | Description |
|---|---|
| Jab | The lead-hand straight punch — the range-finder and the setup |
| Cross | The rear-hand straight punch — the power punch |
| Hook | The bent-arm punch around the guard |
| Uppercut | The rising punch up the middle |

## Arm strikes — defense

- **Cross, front cover** — the front hand covers against the cross.
- **Hook, rear cover** — the rear hand covers against the hook.
The covers are the striking equivalent of the frame — a barrier that keeps the opponent's weapons off you while you close the distance.

## Kicks — attack

| Kick | Description |
|---|---|
| Round kick | The shin or instep across the body, to the leg, body, or head |
| Switch kick | The stance switches, then the round kick fires — faster, less telegraphed |
| Push kick (teep) | The ball of the foot driving straight into the opponent — a range weapon, not a damage weapon |

## Kicks — defense

Evading a kick (move out of range); beating a kick (intercept it); stopping a kick (meet it with a shin or a knee); catching a kick (trap the leg and turn it into a takedown); checking a kick (shin-to-shin block).

## Break fall

- **Forward rolling break fall** — the roll that lets a thrown fighter land without injury. Trained before the throw takedowns, because a fighter who cannot fall cannot be thrown in training.

## Throw takedowns and double-leg attacks

- **Arch and turn** — the throw entry.
- **Double-leg attacks:** double leg; snatch (the low single-leg variant).
- **Defense:** sprawl (the hips drop, the legs shoot back, the opponent's head is driven down); cross-face; quarter nelson; shoulder pressure roll to rear mount — the defense that turns a failed takedown into the most dominant position in the system.

## Chapter 6 — Contact weapons: outline

- Thrusting weapons (attack, defense, grappling with weapons).
- Short-range contact weapons — knives (grips, defending, disarms).
- Grappling with weapons (primary weapons).

## Thrusting weapons — the rifle and bayonet

**Grips:** the rifle is held to present the bayonet as the point and the butt as the hammer. **Primary weapons:** the rifle as a contact weapon — it is a weapon at every range, not just at the muzzle.

**Attack — body positioning:** position first, as in grappling. **Attacking with force:**
- **Thrust** — the bayonet driven straight into the target.
- **Butt stroke to the head** — the butt swung upward into the head.
- **Butt stroke to the groin** — the butt driven into the groin.
- **Slash** — the bayonet drawn across the target.
- **Smash** — the rifle body driven into the target.
- **Modified movements:** modified thrust; modified slash — the same attacks adapted to confined space or a compromised grip.

**Sample sequence from the manual:** parry right, thrust, butt stroke to the groin, slash, attack position. The sequence is the point: the techniques are chained, not used one at a time.

**Defense — parry movements:** parry right; parry left. **Blocks:** high block; low block; side block. The parry deflects the thrust; the block stops it. The counter follows immediately — a parry without a counter is a delay, not a defense.

## Short-range contact weapons — knives

**Grips:** straight grip (blade forward, edge out); reverse grip (blade back, edge out). **Defending — disarms:** strikes (hit the knife hand); leverage (bend the wrist past its range); extension (extend the arm to break the grip). The manual is honest about knives: the defense is not to win a knife fight — it is to survive one long enough to get to a weapon or get away.

## Grappling with weapons

- **Muzzle strike** — the rifle muzzle driven into the opponent.
- **Tug of war** — both fighters gripping the same weapon; the fight is over who controls it.
- **With the rifle:** palm strike; clear primary weapon elbow strike; clear primary weapon front kick; clear primary weapon head butt. "Clear primary weapon" means the technique's purpose is to clear the opponent's hands off your rifle so you can use it.
- **Opponent reaches for your weapon with one hand** — the retention defense: control the reaching hand, strike, and re-establish control of the weapon.

**The through-line of the chapter:** every fight is over weapons. The fight is won by the fighter who controls the weapon, and the techniques exist to take one, keep one, or clear one. A Soldier who loses his weapon in a grapple has lost the fight even if he wins the grapple.
