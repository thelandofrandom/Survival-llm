# Ground Fighting — TC 3-25.150, Chapters 3–4

## Chapter 3 — Basic ground fighting techniques: outline

- Basic fight strategy; ground grappling; clinch fighting.
- Dominant body positions; basic body positioning moves; basic finishing moves; basic sweeps; basic range-controlling techniques.

## The basic fight strategy (paras 3-1 to 3-8)

**The four phases of a fight (para 3-2):** projectile, striking, clinching, grappling. The number-one element of a fight is **range**; the other elements are angle and level.

**The four decisions:**
- **Create space (para 3-5):** the primary goal — establish space to transition to the primary weapon.
- **Maintain space (para 3-6):** if the primary weapon is out of reach, maintain space to transition to the secondary weapon, or close the distance.
- **Close the distance (para 3-7):** the willingness to close with the enemy is a defining characteristic of a Warrior; the ability to do it against an aggressive opponent is the first step in using range to control a fight.
- **Gain dominant position (para 3-8):** appreciation for dominant position ties together what would otherwise be a list of unrelated techniques.

## Dominant body positions (Chapter 3)

In order of control, most dominant first:

1. **Rear mount** — behind the opponent, both feet hooked around his thighs. The most dominant position in the system: the opponent cannot see you, cannot strike you effectively, and you have both arms free to choke.
2. **Mount** — astride the face-up opponent's chest or abdomen, both knees and feet on the ground, free from entanglement with the opponent's legs.
3. **Side control** — perpendicular across the opponent's torso, chest-to-chest, controlling his head and hips.
4. **Knee mount** — one knee in the opponent's abdomen, the other off the ground and away from the opponent, stabilized.
5. **Guard** — legs locked around the opponent (at least one leg above the knee), controlling him from the bottom. A defensive position that is also a platform for sweeps and submissions.

## Basic ground grappling — the fundamentals

- **Stand in base** — prone and supine, with and without the primary weapon. A stable base that cannot be easily moved, with the weapon protected. Everything starts here.
- **Fighting stance** — the standing base for the clinch and takedown entries.
- **Grips** — opposing-thumb grip; wrestler's grip; thumb/thumbless grip. The grip is the connection that makes every technique possible; a lost grip is a lost technique.

## Basic body positioning moves

| Move | From → To | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Arm trap and roll | Guard/bottom → top | Trap the arm, roll the opponent, take the top |
| Passing the guard | In the guard → side control/mount | Clear the legs and gain a dominant position |
| Achieve the mount from side control | Side control → mount | Climb the hierarchy |
| Arm push and roll to the rear mount | Side control/guard → rear mount | The highest climb — to the most dominant position |
| Escape the rear mount | Rear mount (bottom) → neutral | Survive the worst position |
| Escape the mount, shrimp to the guard | Mount (bottom) → guard | The fundamental escape: shrimp (hip escape) to recover the guard |

## Basic finishing moves

| Finish | From | Mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| Rear naked choke | Rear mount | Forearm across the throat, blood choke — the highest-percentage finish in the system |
| Cross-collar choke | Mount and guard | Both hands in the collar, crossed, cutting blood flow |
| Bent arm bar | Mount and side control | Elbow bent past its range — a joint lock on the elbow/shoulder |
| Straight arm bar | Mount | Arm straightened and hyperextended at the elbow |
| Straight arm bar | Guard | Same lock, applied from the bottom |
| Guillotine choke | Standing/guard | Head trapped under the arm, forearm across the throat |

**Rule:** position first, then finish. A choke from a bad position is a gift to the opponent.

## Basic sweeps

- **Scissors sweep** — from the guard: legs scissor the opponent's base out from under him, reversing the position.
- **Sweep from the attempted straight arm bar** — when the opponent defends the arm bar by posting, use his defense as the sweep.

## Basic range-controlling techniques

- **Frame** — arms and legs creating a barrier that keeps the opponent at a distance.
- **Hook and head control** — the hook controls the leg, the head control controls the posture.
- **Three options from the bottom:** Option 1 — create space, engage with the primary weapon; Option 2 — maintain space, employ the secondary weapon; Option 3 — achieve the clinch.

## Clinch fighting — achieve the clinch

- **Close the distance** — the entry: get inside striking range without getting hit.
- **Modified seatbelt clinch** — one arm over the shoulder, one under the arm, hands clasped — the control position of the clinch.
- **Double underhooks** — both arms under the opponent's arms, controlling his upper body.
- **Rear clinch** — behind the opponent, the standing version of the rear mount.
- **Basic takedowns:** front takedown; rear takedown.

## Chapter 4 — Intermediate ground fighting techniques: outline

- Intermediate fight strategy; clinch fighting; grappling with strikes; regaining the initiative.
- Intermediate detainee procedures (single/two-man cuffing, escort, vehicle extraction).

## Regaining the initiative — defense

- **Against chokes** — the defenses that stop the choke and return you to a neutral or better position.
- **Against the standing headlock** — escape the headlock from the bottom: form the frame; follow the leg; lift and turn; block and lift; arch over. Five variations, one principle: the headlock is a bad position for the man applying it if you know the escape.
- **Defend against the attempt to mount** — arch defense; backdoor defense.
- **Escape the half-guard** — the intermediate position between guard and side control.
- **Reverse bent arm bar from side control** — turn a bad position into a submission.

## Intermediate body positioning — pass the guard

Five entries, one goal — clear the legs and gain side control or mount:
- Pass the guard with the knee in the tailbone.
- Pass the guard under one leg.
- Pass the guard under both legs.
- Pass the guard, near-side leg through.
- Pass the guard, far-side leg through.

## Attacks from the mount

Papercutter choke; nutcracker choke; leaning choke; sleeve choke; collar choke; single wing choke; straight arm bar from the rear mount. The mount is the platform; the chokes are the tools. The papercutter (forearm across the throat, slicing) and the collar chokes (using the opponent's own uniform against him) are the highest-percentage attacks from the top.

## Attacks from the guard

- **Triangle choke** — legs forming a triangle around the opponent's head and arm, blood choke from the bottom.
- **Elevator sweeps** (option one and option two) — the legs lift the opponent's base and reverse the position.
- **Elevator sweep escape from the mount, shrimp to the guard** — the escape that turns a bad position into a sweep.
- **Reverse bent arm bar from the guard**; **hip heist sweep**; **guillotine choke from the guard**.

## Knee mount and leg attacks

- **Knee mount from side control**; near-arm side bar from the knee mount; reverse bent arm bar from the knee mount.
- **Leg attacks:** straight ankle lock; straight knee bar. Leg attacks are the intermediate level's new threat — they attack a joint the opponent is not defending while he worries about his neck and arms.

## Intermediate takedowns

Drop to single leg; double leg; tree top; block the far knee; hook the foot; hip throw. The double leg is the workhorse — the same takedown at every level, refined with entries and finishes.

## Grappling with strikes

- **Pass the guard with strikes** — strikes open the guard; the pass follows the opening.
- **Strikes from side control** — the dominant position used to punish.
- **Against strikes in the guard** — close, middle, and far range defenses. The guard is a defensive position against strikes: control the posture, control the distance, and the strikes lose their power.

## Regaining the initiative — against chokes

- Against the standing guillotine; against the standing headlock — knee strikes; step in front; step behind. The theme: a choke defense that only stops the choke is half a defense. The good ones end with you in control.

## Intermediate detainee procedures

- **Single-man cuffing techniques** — including the wall variant (cuffing a detainee against a wall for control).
- **Two-man cuffing techniques** — front takedown; rear takedown; wheelbarrow.
- **Detainee escort** — moving a cuffed detainee without losing control.
- **Vehicle extraction** — door variant; window variant. Pulling a non-compliant person from a vehicle, with a partner, without getting hurt.

These are the techniques for taking a person into custody and moving him without killing him — the manual treats them with the same step-by-step precision as the chokes, because in stability operations they are used more often than the chokes are.
