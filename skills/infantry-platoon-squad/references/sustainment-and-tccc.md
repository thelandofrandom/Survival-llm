# Sustainment and TCCC — ATP 3-21.8

## Sustaining the Platoon

The platoon sergeant owns sustainment. The cycle: **plan, prepare, execute, assess**.

### Classes of Supply

| Class | Contents |
|---|---|
| I | Subsistence (food, water) |
| II | Clothing, individual equipment, tools |
| III | Petroleum, oils, lubricants |
| IV | Construction and barrier materials |
| V | Ammunition |
| VI | Personal demand items |
| VII | Major end items (weapons, vehicles) |
| VIII | Medical supplies |
| IX | Repair parts |

### Distribution and Resupply

- **Logistics packages (LOGPACs):** preconfigured resupply by ground or air, delivered to a logistics release point.
- Resupply is planned in the OPORD (Sustainment paragraph) — what, when, where, and how the platoon gets it.
- Water and ammunition are the two resupply items that stop a platoon fastest. Plan for both before the mission, not during it.

### Maintenance

- PMCS (preventive maintenance checks and services) is a daily discipline for weapons, optics, vehicles, and communications — not a pre-inspection scramble.
- The platoon sergeant determines Soldier load and load echeloning (fighting load, approach-march load, sustainment load) per ATP 3-21.18.

## Tactical Combat Casualty Care (TCCC)

This section is current (2024) and can be followed. Full detail is in ATP 4-02.11; this is the platoon's employment of it.

### Phase 1 — Care Under Fire

- Return fire. Move the casualty to cover.
- Stop life-threatening bleeding with a tourniquet.
- Do not expose yourself or the casualty to further fire to provide care.

### Phase 2 — Tactical Field Care (MARCH)

| Letter | Priority | Action |
|---|---|---|
| **M** — Massive hemorrhage | First | Tourniquets, wound packing, junctional control; reassess all bleeding |
| **A** — Airway | Second | Position, suction, nasopharyngeal airway; surgical airway only if trained and indicated |
| **R** — Respirations | Third | Seal open chest wounds (vented chest seal), needle decompression for tension pneumothorax |
| **C** — Circulation | Fourth | IV/IO access, tranexamic acid per protocol, blood products when available, reassess for shock |
| **H** — Hypothermia / Head injury | Fifth | Hypothermia prevention kit, blanket; monitor consciousness, protect the head-injured casualty |

Plus: pain control, antibiotics, splinting, eye care, documentation.

### Phase 3 — Tactical Evacuation Care

- Continue MARCH care during evacuation.
- The casualty is not "done" until handed to medical personnel.
- Document everything on the **DD Form 1380 TCCC Card** — treatments given, times, and trends. The card travels with the casualty and is the receiving medic's briefing.

### Who Does What

- Every rifleman: tourniquet and care under fire.
- Combat lifesavers: tactical field care bridge between the rifleman and the medic.
- Platoon medic: advanced field care and evacuation coordination.
- Platoon sergeant: casualty collection point, evacuation assets, and accountability.
