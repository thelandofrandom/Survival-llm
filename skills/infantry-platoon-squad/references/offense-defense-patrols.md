# Offense, Defense, and Patrols — ATP 3-21.8

## Forms of Maneuver (Offense)

| Form | Idea | When to use |
|---|---|---|
| Envelopment | Attack the flank or rear, avoid the enemy's strength | Flank is accessible — preferred form |
| Turning movement | Deep envelopment that forces the enemy to abandon his position | To turn him out of his defenses rather than assault them |
| Infiltration | Small elements move through gaps to attack from the rear | Slow, stealthy, high payoff |
| Penetration | Concentrated attack through a narrow front, rupture and roll up the defense | When the defense must be broken at a point |
| Frontal attack | Direct attack on the enemy's front | Least preferred — attacks his strength; used only when no other form is feasible or to fix the enemy |

## The Attack

- **Hasty attack:** limited planning, exploiting a fleeting opportunity.
- **Deliberate attack:** full planning, rehearsals, preparation.
- Organization: assault element, support (base-of-fire) element, breach element when obstacles must be crossed.
- Control measures: phase lines, objectives, boundaries, assault positions, line of departure.
- Sequence: move to the assault position → establish the base of fire → breach → assault through the objective → consolidate → reorganize.
- Limited visibility: same sequence, tighter control, more rehearsal.

## Movement to Contact

- Organize for contact: lead element, main body, reserve.
- On contact, execute the battle drill: return fire, seek cover, report, maneuver.
- Develop the situation, then decide: attack or bypass.

## Area Defense

- Primary, alternate, and supplementary positions; interlocking sectors of fire.
- **Final protective line (FPL):** the machine-gun line that is the last barrier before the position is overrun.
- Sequence: engage at maximum effective range, shift fires as the enemy closes, fight the close battle with grenades and bayonets if necessary.

## Retrograde

| Form | Idea |
|---|---|
| Delay | Trade space for time; inflict maximum damage while falling back |
| Withdrawal | Planned disengagement, usually at night |
| Retirement | Move away from the enemy when not in contact |

Retrograde is the hardest operation — it looks like retreat and demands the most discipline.

## Engagement-Area Development — 7 Steps

1. Identify likely enemy avenues of approach.
2. Determine the enemy's scheme of maneuver.
3. Determine where to kill the enemy.
4. Emplace weapons systems.
5. Plan and integrate obstacles.
6. Plan and integrate indirect fires.
7. Rehearse.

The engagement area is where fires, obstacles, and maneuver converge: the enemy is canalized into it, fixed in it, and destroyed in it.

## Fighting Positions

- Must provide **cover** (protection from fire) and **concealment** (protection from observation), sectors of fire, and mutual support.
- **Hasty:** foxhole dug in minutes, using natural cover.
- **Deliberate:** dug in, revetted, overhead cover, grenade sumps.
- Machine-gun positions: firing platforms, sited for grazing fire. Javelin positions: long-range engagement, clear backblast.

## Combat Patrols

**Raid:** surprise attack on a fixed objective with a defined withdrawal — get in, destroy the target, get out.

**Ambush:** surprise attack from concealed positions on a moving or temporarily halted enemy.

| Type | Kill zone |
|---|---|
| Point ambush | Single kill zone |
| Area ambush | Multiple related ambushes |
| Linear ambush | Long kill zone along a route |
| L-shaped / V-shaped | Kill zones trapping the enemy in interlocking fire |

- Organization: assault element, support element, security element.
- Sequence: occupy the ambush site → initiate on the leader's signal → assault through the kill zone → collect intelligence and equipment → withdraw.
- **Security patrols** protect the main body by operating forward of it.

## Reconnaissance Patrols

| Type | Mission |
|---|---|
| Area reconnaissance | Learn about a specific area or objective |
| Route reconnaissance | Learn about a route — trafficability, obstacles, enemy presence |
| Zone reconnaissance | Learn about a larger area |

- Actions on the objective: observe from concealment, record (SALT reports), withdraw undetected.
- A reconnaissance patrol that fights has usually failed. Its product is the report.
