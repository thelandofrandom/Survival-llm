# Organization and Leaders — ATP 3-21.8

## The Rifle Platoon

| Element | Composition |
|---|---|
| Platoon headquarters | Platoon leader, platoon sergeant, RTO, forward observer, medic |
| Rifle squads (×3) | 9 Soldiers each |
| Weapons squad | M240 machine guns, Javelin missiles |

## The Rifle Squad — 9 Soldiers

- **Squad leader** — commands the squad; positions himself between the two fire teams where he can best control it.
- **Fire team Alpha (4):** team leader, rifleman, grenadier (M320), automatic rifleman (M249).
- **Fire team Bravo (4):** team leader, rifleman, grenadier (M320), automatic rifleman (M249).

## Fire Team Roles

| Position | Weapon | Job |
|---|---|---|
| Team leader | Rifle | Controls the team's fires and movement |
| Automatic rifleman | M249 light machine gun | The team's base of fire |
| Grenadier | M320 grenade launcher | High-explosive reach; engages area targets |
| Rifleman | Rifle | Flexible man — security, ammunition, second set of eyes |

## The Weapons Squad

- **M240B / M240L medium machine guns** — bipod- and tripod-mounted; the platoon's sustained-fire base.
- **Javelin close-combat missile system** — anti-tank; sited for long-range engagement with clear backblast.
- Employment: as a whole (base of fire for the rifle squads) or attached by element to rifle squads. Machine guns are positioned for interlocking fires, grazing fire across the engagement area, and a final protective line.

## Key Leaders

| Leader | Primary job |
|---|---|
| Platoon leader | Primary planner; receives WARNORD/OPORD/FRAGORD from the company; maneuvers squads; synchronizes efforts; looks ahead to the platoon's next move |
| Platoon sergeant | Second-in-command; sustainment and discipline; assists with the OPORD; determines Soldier load and load echeloning; prepares rehearsals and priorities of work |
| Squad leader | Fights the squad as two mutually supporting fire teams |
| Team leader | Fights the fire team; controls fires and movement |
| Forward observer | Calls and adjusts indirect fires |
| RTO | Platoon communications |

## Habitual Attachments

The platoon may receive engineers, mortars, snipers, additional medics, and forward observers. Attachments are integrated into the plan and rehearsals — never bolted on at the line of departure.

## Command and Control

- Control through orders, hand-and-arm signals, radio, and — when those fail — by example and position.
- The platoon leader positions himself where he can see and influence the decisive point.
- Every Soldier knows his battle position, his sector of fire, and his buddy.
