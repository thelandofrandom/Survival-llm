# Planning and Movement — ATP 3-21.8

## Troop Leading Procedures (TLP) — 8 Steps

| Step | What happens |
|---|---|
| 1. Receive the mission | From company WARNORD, OPORD, or FRAGORD; analyze task, purpose, and higher intent (two levels up) |
| 2. Issue a warning order | Five-paragraph OPORD format, minimal information; alerts the platoon and starts parallel planning |
| 3. Make a tentative plan | Mission analysis (METT-TC), course-of-action development and analysis — the bulk of the work |
| 4. Initiate movement | Start moving/preparing while planning continues; time is the scarcest resource |
| 5. Conduct reconnaissance | Leader's recon of objective, routes, positions; confirm or change the tentative plan |
| 6. Complete the plan | Finalize course of action, control measures, coordination |
| 7. Issue the operation order | Five-paragraph OPORD, delivered so every leader can repeat it back |
| 8. Supervise and refine | Rehearsals, inspections, adjustments until execution |

TLP runs in parallel with higher headquarters' planning (parallel planning) — the platoon does not wait for the company order to start.

## METT-TC (I) — Mission Variables

- **M**ission — task and purpose.
- **E**nemy — composition, disposition, capabilities, most likely and most dangerous courses of action.
- **T**errain and weather — OAKOC: Observation and fields of fire, Avenues of approach, Key terrain, Obstacles, Cover and concealment.
- **T**roops and support available — what you actually have, including attachments.
- **T**ime available — planning, rehearsal, movement, execution.
- **C**ivil considerations — civilians, structures, and their effect on the mission.
- **(I)**nformational considerations — integrated into the other variables.

## The Five-Paragraph OPORD

1. **Situation** — enemy, friendly, attachments/detachments, civil considerations.
2. **Mission** — task and purpose, one sentence.
3. **Execution** — commander's intent, concept of the operation, scheme of maneuver, tasks to subordinate units, coordinating instructions.
4. **Sustainment** — logistics, medical, personnel.
5. **Command and Signal** — command, control, communications.

WARNORD follows the same format with minimal information. FRAGORD changes the OPORD without rewriting it.

## Movement Formations

| Formation | Use | Trade-off |
|---|---|---|
| Column / file | Restricted terrain, night, dense vegetation | Most control, least firepower forward |
| Line | Contact imminent, enemy to the front | Maximum firepower forward |
| Wedge | Fire team's default (two forward, two back) | Firepower forward and to the flanks |
| Echelon | Threat from one flank | Firepower weighted to that flank |
| Vee | Platoon level | Firepower forward and to both flanks |

Squad default: column with fire teams in wedge.

## Movement Techniques

| Technique | When | How |
|---|---|---|
| Traveling | Contact not likely | All elements move continuously; fastest, least secure |
| Traveling overwatch | Contact possible | Lead element moves continuously; trail element moves as needed to maintain overwatch |
| Bounding overwatch | Contact expected | One element always stationary and overwatching while the other bounds; successive bounds (same element leads) or alternate bounds (leapfrog); slowest, most secure |

**Limited visibility:** tighter intervals, frequent halts, compass and pace-count navigation, absolute noise and light discipline.

## Maneuver

- **Base-of-fire element:** fixes the enemy with fire.
- **Bounding/maneuver element:** moves to the flank or rear to destroy him.
- **Reserve:** held back to exploit success or reinforce failure.
- Rule: fire without maneuver is suppression without decision; maneuver without fire is a target.

## Route Selection Checklist

- [ ] Covered and concealed where possible
- [ ] Avoids likely enemy observation and obstacles
- [ ] Rally points and checkpoints set
- [ ] Azimuths and distances recorded; route briefed so any man can lead it
- [ ] Navigation by map, compass, and terrain association
