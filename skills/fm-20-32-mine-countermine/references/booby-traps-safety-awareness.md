# Booby Traps, Safety, and Mine Awareness — FM 20-32

## Booby Traps and Expedient Devices (Chapter 13)

**Purpose and control:** booby traps do not distinguish between friend and foe. Setting them is a dangerous task and must be carefully controlled. Exact drills cannot be prescribed, but the procedures below should be followed as closely as possible.

**Planning, setting, and recording:**

- **Timeliness:** the setting-party commander must be given all information and sufficient time to carry out a reconnaissance, so his plan and the soldiers' briefing are as complete as possible.
- **Orders and briefing:** after reconnaissance the commander makes a detailed plan, prepares the order, and briefs soldiers on enemy intelligence; number and types of traps; location and design (precise or general); tasking and allocation of areas; recording, marking, and arming procedures; control measures; time requirements; material and equipment availability; rehearsal and equipment preparation.
- **Rehearsal:** whenever possible, rehearse laying procedures; thoroughly exercise control and safety measures so flaws are discovered; lay practice traps and carry out arming and recording procedures. If rehearsal cannot be held at the trap location, hold it in a similar area.
- **Control point:** the commander establishes a control point on arrival at the trap area — a headquarters and material holding area from which safe routes start. If rehearsal was elsewhere, assemble all troops at the control point before setting starts; relate the ground to the plan, confirm control measures, and check firing devices and equipment for serviceability and adequacy.
- **Control of parties:** each setting party consists of one or two men, working in a clearly defined area (use tape if necessary), positioned so accidental detonation will not cause friendly casualties. Entry to areas where traps are being set is strictly controlled — in most situations only the commander, setting parties, and a recorder enter. Troops not assigned tasks remain at the control point unless otherwise authorized.
- **Marking:** as with nuisance minefields, mark booby-trapped areas until they are evacuated. When possible use standard booby-trap signs (Figure 13-4: 28 × 20 cm, white with red markings). When signs are not available use temporary markings (any barrier or fence) to clearly indicate a booby-trapped area. Remove temporary marking after evacuation and before the enemy reconnoiters the area.
- **Setting:** setting parties transport required explosives and material to the site. They do not arm firing devices or traps until ordered. In a one-man party the man remains with the trap until it is armed or the commander directs him to leave. In a two-man party one man returns to the control point and reports completion while the other remains with the trap. A party enters and leaves its area by the same route; if the route is not clearly apparent, lay tapes to guide the commander and recorder.
- **Inspection and arming:** the setting-party commander inspects traps for safety and camouflage before giving the order to arm them, and devises an arming plan that lets parties withdraw safely — in open areas, arm from the furthest mine from the control point; in buildings, arm from the top down. **DANGER: do not attempt to further camouflage the area after the trap is armed.**

**Assembly (examples from the manual):** a nonelectric, directly connected trap using an M142 firing device — design the trap and collect materials; test the M142; prepare and position the charge for coupling and prepare the explosive to receive a blasting cap; prepare the coupler and insert it into the charge; prepare the M142, set it to operate in the desired manner, and remove the round- or square-headed pin; remove all materials and signs of laying and fully camouflage the area; arm the trap by removing the positive safety pin. A detonating-cord-connected trap is armed by connecting the two detonating cords and then removing the positive safety pin.

**Reporting and recording:** correct reporting and recording must be observed to avoid friendly casualties. Procedures for recording booby traps are the same as for nuisance mines (Chapter 8). When traps are set in areas occupied by the enemy, they are not recorded; it is sufficient to note their location and type in after-action reports.

## Clearing Booby Traps

- **Pulling with a grapnel:** carefully select the pull site — it might itself be mined or trapped. When a booby trap is pulled and does not explode, wait at least 30 seconds before approaching, in case delay devices were used. Disposal of unexploded traps depends on their condition when inspected. Pulling procedure is similar to that for pulling mines (Chapter 11).
- **Trip wires:** check the area for AP devices before proceeding. Place a grapnel hook as close as possible to the trip wire. Do not touch the trip wire until the pulling party is in a covered area.
- **Pull and release:** pull away objects that conceal and operate pull-and-release mechanisms.
- **Pressure mechanisms:** pull pressure mechanisms from under objects that conceal and operate them. If impossible, blow them in place. In many cases it may be easier to pull the charge than the firing device — take extreme care, because additional mechanisms are often concealed in or under the main charge. Actuate pressure mechanisms by suspending one-half pound of explosive above the pressure plate.
- **Destroying in place:** explode a small charge near the booby trap's charge; use this method only if damage from the explosion is acceptable. When it is impossible to place the explosive close enough to ensure detonation of the main charge, carefully place it alongside the mechanism. Do not assume the main charge is safe to handle just because the mechanism has been destroyed.
- **Clearing by hand:** neutralizing, disarming, removing, and disposing of traps without causing damage. Extremely hazardous; only when pulling or destruction in place is impossible or unacceptable. Clearance should only be conducted by EOD personnel or experienced engineers. Carefully examine all aspects of the trap before deciding how to clear it.
- **Explosive line charge:** quick results where only a narrow path is required through a booby-trapped area; gives clearance for the same distance to either side, only where it is in contact with the ground.
- **Armor:** where traps with small charges (designed as AP devices) are in open areas, armored vehicles track back and forth over the area, shortening clearing time with little risk of casualties.
- **Fire:** if traps are set in grass or dense vegetation, fire can burn away camouflage and expose traps; in most cases sufficient heat is generated to burn or explode them. Unexploded traps after a fire are considered extremely sensitive and must not be cleared by hand.

**Improvised traps:** in low-intensity conflicts there is a high incidence of improvised mines and booby traps. A complete list of firing devices and improvised demolition material is impossible; most improvised traps are variations on the types described in the chapter. An electric firing device requires a current to be passed between two contacts; the ways to achieve this are limited only by imagination, and simple electric firing devices can be manufactured from household items or appliances (Figure 13-6). Examples of improvised mechanical firing devices are in Figures 13-7 and 13-8. The simplest and most accurate method of incorporating delay is a clock or timer; any mechanism or chemical reaction that takes a measurable time to complete its function can be used.

## Safety and Training (Appendix E)

Appendix E governs safety and training for mine operations, including safe handling, emplacement, and reduction procedures; MOPMS safety and fragment-hazard zones and fencing; and the training program for installation and removal of US mines and firing devices (Appendix A procedures) and for special-purpose munitions (Appendix B). Training must cover mine recognition, arming and safing procedures, marking, and reporting — untrained handling of mines and firing devices kills.

## Mine Awareness (Appendix F)

Mine awareness covers recognition of mines and minefields, the mine risk assessment prepared before operations (example in Appendix F), and reporting of suspected mines and minefields through the chain of command. Any soldier who encounters a suspected mine or minefield marks it, reports it, and does not attempt to clear it without training and orders.

## Threat Mine/Countermine Operations (Appendix C)

Threat forces employ mines differently from US forces — including the use of antihandling devices on AP mines, which US forces do not do — and use their own breaching techniques and expedient devices. Detailed doctrine on threat employment is provided so US forces recognize how the enemy can employ these weapons. Appendix D covers Air Volcano employment; Appendix G gives countermine data for planning reduction; Appendix H is a metric conversion chart.
