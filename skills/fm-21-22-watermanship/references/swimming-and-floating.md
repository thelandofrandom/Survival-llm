# Swimming and Floating — FM 21-22

## Military Swimming

Military swimming means swimming in full clothing and equipment to reach an objective in fit condition for action. If shipwrecked far from shore, the soldier does **not** try to swim to shore — he tries to keep afloat until rescued. Military swimming therefore includes: submerging and floating; dog paddle and breast stroke; treading water; side stroke; elementary back stroke; jumping; underwater swimming. Detailed instruction is in FM 21-20, chapter 5. If a man does the scissors kick more naturally than the frog kick, or vice versa, do not change his style.

## Submerging and Bobbing

- Submerge first in shallow water with eyes open. It is hard to stay under because air trapped in the chest forces the body up; expelling part of the air through the nose lets a man submerge without effort. Above water, gulp air through the mouth.
- **Bobbing:** raise the arms abruptly, sideward and upward, to go down; lower them abruptly, sideward and downward, to rise. Bobbing conceals the swimmer and protects him from enemy fire.

## The Three Floats

Floating is the best way to conserve energy. Anyone can float, motionless or with slight arm or leg movement. Expand the chest as much as possible to increase buoyancy; after a deep breath, further expansion can be had without sucking in air by pulling up the stomach with the stomach muscles.

- **Back float** — the best relaxing float. Legs together or apart, arms extended to the side or overhead. With legs together and arms by the side, the legs tend to sink and drag the body under.
- **Jelly-fish float** — body doubled up, knees pulled to the chest, head dropped on the chest, arms clasping the knees. The body rolls forward until only the back shows above water. Used mainly when removing clothing.
- **Prone float** — face down, arms and legs extended. The basic position for all prone swimming strokes; face submerged with eyes open to see under water, or head high to observe above it.

## Strokes

- **Dog paddle** — from the prone float. Hands slightly cupped, fingers joined, thumb along the forefinger, reach forward and pull back on the water; legs doubled up and extended to the rear. Left leg and left arm, right leg and right arm, work together. Arms and legs do not break the surface, so this is the silent-swimming stroke.
- **Breast stroke** — extremely easy and relaxed, usable in all types of water, calm or choppy, with the head held high for better observation. Also used for underwater swimming and when carrying equipment on the back. By splashing with hands and arms on the forward movement, it can push away debris and thin surface oil and flame.
- **Treading water** — stand upright with the feet going through the motions of climbing a ladder, hands pushing down on the water. The position for observing while swimming.
- **Side stroke and elementary back stroke** — covered in FM 21-20.

## Blast Protection in the Water

If depth charges, torpedoes, or aerial bombs are exploding nearby: float on the back, **cross the legs, tense the body, tighten the anus, and keep as near the surface of the water as possible**. Get out of the danger area and out of the water as soon as possible. The same crossed-legs, tensed-body back float is prescribed to avoid blast injury in the water generally.
