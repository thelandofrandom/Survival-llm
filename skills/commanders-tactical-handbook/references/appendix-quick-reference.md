## Machine gun employment (Appendix A)

- Machine guns are the commander's base of fire: they provide the suppression that lets maneuver happen.
- Employment principles: position for grazing fire and interlocking sectors; protect the gun team (it is the enemy's priority target); plan ammunition supply — a machine gun without ammunition is a heavy rifle.
- The gun's sector of fire, principal direction of fire, and final protective line are set in the defense order and rehearsed.

## Weapons handling (Appendix B)

- Safe handling rules for the unit's weapons; clearing procedures; weapons conditions and statuses.
- The handbook's weapons-handling appendix is a reference for the armorer and the small-unit leader: every weapon has a prescribed condition for each situation (patrol, defense, movement), and the condition is checked, not assumed.

## NBC defense basics (Appendix C)

- **Detection and warning:** recognize NBC attack indicators (unusual smoke, odors, casualties with no visible wound, dead animals/insects).
- **Protection:** mission-oriented protective posture (MOPP) levels — the level is set by the commander based on the threat, and the trade is protection versus heat and fatigue.
- **Decontamination:** immediate (skin, personal equipment), operational (unit-level), and thorough (support-level). Decontaminate early — the longer a contaminant stays, the worse the casualty.
- **Note:** specific agents, protective equipment, and detection means in this 1988 appendix are dated. Current CBRN doctrine and equipment supersede it.

## Landing zone brief and markings (Appendix E)

- **LZ brief:** location, size, surface, slope, obstacles, approach and departure directions, wind, and the marking method.
- **Markings:** panels, smoke, lights (including infrared for night operations), and the signals the landing force uses to identify the LZ.
- **Safety:** the LZ must be secure before the aircraft arrives; the marking party is vulnerable and needs its own security.

## Roadblocks and searches (Appendix G)

- **Roadblocks:** sited to stop and control traffic; covered by fire; with a search area, a holding area, and an escape route for the roadblock party.
- **Searches:** systematic, by the book — persons, vehicles, and buildings searched in a set sequence, with the search team and the security team as separate elements.
- **Discipline:** a roadblock that is not covered by fire is a target; a search that is not systematic misses what it is looking for.

## Enemy prisoners of war (Appendix H)

- **Handling:** search, segregate (by rank, sex, and category), silence, speed, safeguard — the five S's of EPW handling.
- **Evacuation:** EPWs are moved to the rear quickly; the capturing unit does not hold them.
- **Treatment:** humane treatment is a legal and a practical requirement — a force that mistreats prisoners loses the intelligence they provide and the legitimacy it needs.

## Patrol coordination (Appendix I) and overlay (Appendix J)

- **Coordination:** adjacent units, supporting arms, and higher headquarters are informed of patrol routes, times, and recognition signals before the patrol departs.
- **Overlay:** the example patrol overlay shows routes, checkpoints, rally points, and objectives on a single graphic — the patrol's plan in picture form, issued with the order.

## Antiterrorism procedures (Appendix N)

- Awareness, physical security, and personal protective measures for personnel and installations.
- The appendix's specific threat picture is 1980s-era; the principles (vary routines, harden targets, report suspicious activity) remain sound.

## Common military symbology (Appendix O) and terms (Appendix P)

- Standard symbols for friendly and enemy units, weapons, and control measures — the graphic language every leader must read fluently.
- Common tactical terms and definitions — the shared vocabulary that makes orders short and unambiguous.

## Metric conversion (Appendix Q)

|---|---|
| Inches → centimeters | 2.54 |
| Feet → meters | 0.3048 |
| Yards → meters | 0.9144 |
| Miles → kilometers | 1.609 |
