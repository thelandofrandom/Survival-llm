# Fire Support — Reference (MCRP 3-11.1A, Part II)

Distilled from MCRP 3-11.1A *Commander's Tactical Handbook* (USMC, Jul 1988). Paraphrased; short quotes only.

> **Note on vintage.** 1988 publication. Fire support procedures and formats are durable; specific aircraft, munitions, and weapons-capability data in the appendices are dated — verify against current references.

## Chapter outline

- General fire support: general information, company fire support planning, suggested fire support coordinator SOP, fire support techniques, company fire support board.
- Quick fire support plan.
- Call for fire elements, procedures, format card.
- Close air support: control procedures, briefing form.
- Appendices F, K, L: sample fire plan sketches; CAS aircraft capabilities and munitions; weapons capabilities.

## Company fire support planning checklist

- [ ] Fire support coordinator designated and SOP understood
- [ ] Available supporting arms identified: 60mm mortars, 81mm mortars, artillery, naval gunfire, close air support
- [ ] Targets planned and numbered; target list distributed
- [ ] Fire support board maintained (who fires what, when, on which target)
- [ ] Suppression, smoke, and SEAD (suppression of enemy air defenses) planned where needed
- [ ] Final attack heading (magnetic azimuth) set for CAS
- [ ] Fire support coordinated with the scheme of maneuver — fires support the maneuver, not the reverse

## Quick fire support plan ("APPLE" format)

| Element | Content |
|---|---|
| Target information | Line number, target number, description, location (grid), altitude (ASL, meters/feet), remarks |
| Schedule | Firing unit, organization, fire timings, remarks |
| Front | The forward line of own troops and the scheme of maneuver it supports |

The quick fire support plan is a pre-formatted plan for rapid use: targets listed, timings scheduled, and the whole plan fittable on a single sheet. It is the company commander's fire plan when there is no time for a deliberate one.

## Call for fire — elements

1. **Observer identification** — who is calling.
2. **Warning order** — type of mission, size of element, method of target location.
3. **Target location** — grid, shift from a known point, or polar plot.
4. **Target description** — what the target is, its size, and its activity.
5. **Method of engagement** — type of adjustment, ammunition, distribution.
6. **Method of fire and control** — when to fire, and who controls it.

## Call for fire — procedures

1. **Initial call:** observer identification, warning order, target location.
2. **Adjustment:** observer corrects the fall of shot ("add/drop, left/right") until rounds are on target.
3. **Fire for effect:** the full weight of the requested fires on the adjusted target.
4. **End of mission:** observer reports effects and ends the mission, freeing the firing unit.

The handbook's **call for fire format card** (pages 183–184) is a pocket card carrying the format in full. A forward observer carries it; the format is followed in order, every time, because a garbled call for fire is a danger to friendly troops.

## Close air support — control procedures

- CAS is controlled by the forward air controller (FAC) or the terminal controller on the ground.
- Control procedures: the controller briefs the aircrew, clears the attack, and controls the final run.
- Safety: friendly positions, the final attack heading, and the target location must be known to both the controller and the aircrew before weapons release.
- The **CAS briefing form** standardizes what the aircrew needs: target description and location, friendly positions, enemy threats (especially air defense), attack heading, and any restrictions.

## CAS briefing form — fields

- Target: description, location (grid), altitude.
- Friendlies: location, markings, and the closest friendly position to the target.
- Threats: enemy air defense, small arms, and any other hazard to the aircraft.
- Attack heading: final attack heading (magnetic azimuth), and whether it is mandatory or recommended.
- Restrictions: no-fire areas, collateral concerns, weather, and time constraints.

## Fire support techniques

- **Suppression (ISUP):** fires that degrade the enemy's ability to fight, even without destroying him.
- **Smoke (ISMK):** obscuration to conceal movement or blind the enemy.
- **Fire for effect (FFE):** the full weight of fires on a target.
- **Air support (AF):** CAS integrated with ground fires.
- **SEAD:** suppression of enemy air defenses, continuous or interrupted, to protect aircraft.
- Each supporting arm (60mm, 81mm, artillery, naval gunfire) has its own entry on the fire support board: ISUP / ISMK / FFE / AF / SUPP / SEAD, and whether the support is continuous, interrupted, or other.

## Sample fire plan sketch (Appendix F)

A fire plan sketch shows: the objective and key terrain, friendly positions, target locations with numbers, sectors of fire, and the sequence of fires. It is drawn on the map or overlay and issued with the order, so every leader can see the fire plan at a glance.
