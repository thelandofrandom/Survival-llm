# Offense, Defense, and Patrolling — Reference (MCRP 3-11.1A)

Distilled from MCRP 3-11.1A *Commander's Tactical Handbook* (USMC, Jul 1988). Paraphrased; short quotes only.

> **Note on vintage.** 1988 publication. Planning procedures and tactical fundamentals are durable; equipment, communications, and threat data are dated — verify against current references.

## Troop-leading steps (quick card)

1. Begin planning — METT-T estimate; warning order.
2. Arrange — movement, reconnaissance, issue of order, coordination.
3. Make reconnaissance — update METT-T; confirm enemy vulnerabilities.
4. Complete the plan — concept of operations driven by METT-T.
5. Issue the order — five-paragraph format.
6. Supervise — brief-backs, rehearsals, inspections.

## METT-T / SALUTE / DRAW-D / KOCOA-W

| Framework | Elements |
|---|---|
| METT-T | Mission, Enemy, Terrain and weather, Troops and support available, Time available |
| SALUTE (enemy) | Size, Activity, Location, Unit, Time, Equipment — plus strength |
| DRAW-D (enemy capabilities) | Defend, Reinforce, Attack, Withdraw, Delay |
| KOCOA-W (terrain/weather) | Key terrain, Observation and fields of fire, Cover and concealment, Obstacles, Avenues of approach, Weather/astronomical data |

## Five-paragraph order (quick card)

1. **Situation** — friendly forces (higher, adjacent, supporting; attachments/detachments), enemy forces (SALUTE, DRAW-D, most probable course of action).
2. **Mission** — who, what, when, where, why — one sentence.
3. **Execution** — commander's intent, concept of operations (scheme of maneuver + fire support plan), subordinate missions (main effort, supporting efforts, reserve), coordinating instructions.
4. **Administration and Logistics** — "beans, bullets, bandages, bad guys, and batteries."
5. **Command and Signal** — signal plan; location of key leaders; chain of command and succession. Close: "Any questions? The time is now ______."

## Attack — general coordinating instructions checklist

- [ ] Order of movement and formations
- [ ] Consolidation instructions: security, automatic weapons, fields of fire, entrench — **SAFE**
- [ ] Displacement instructions
- [ ] Fire support plan coordinated with the scheme of maneuver
- [ ] Rehearsal conducted (especially for night attack, fortified position, and MOUT)

## Offensive operation types

| Operation | Key demand |
|---|---|
| Movement to contact | Readiness to fight the moment contact is made |
| Attack of a fortified position | Deliberate preparation, suppression, breaching, assault; more fires, more time, more rehearsal |
| Mechanized attack | Infantry-armor integration; speed and shock |
| Night attack | Rehearsal, control measures, lighting, recognition signals |
| Helicopterborne | LZ, air movement, and ground plan as one operation |
| MOUT | Room-by-room clearance; fundamentals of securing a room |
| Amphibious assault (helo/surface) | Ship-to-shore movement as a tactical operation |

## Engagement-area build sequence

1. **Enemy:** size and configuration; distribution between echelons and composition of each; time available to prepare.
2. **Terrain and weather:** identify restricted avenues of approach; find the avenue with the best cover/concealment, egress routes, and maximum-range weapon employment; decide massed surprise vs. concentric fires.
3. **Troops and support available:** overwhelm the enemy with different threats to put him in a dilemma.
4. **Time available:** enemy movement speed across the terrain; number of shots into the engagement area; disengagement and egress speed; ammunition required — pre-staged at subsequent positions.
5. **Refinement:** obstacles to stop, split, or divert the enemy column and narrow the engagement area; direct-fire weapons positioned knowing the enemy will target ideal positions first.

## Defense checklist

- [ ] Positions mutually supporting; obstacles integrated with fires
- [ ] Reserve designated, located, with commitment criteria decided in the plan
- [ ] Counterattack planned before the battle: triggers, routes, fire support
- [ ] Engagement areas built per the sequence above
- [ ] Ammunition pre-staged at subsequent positions
- [ ] Rear area security assigned

## Retrograde operations

| Type | Purpose | Key risk |
|---|---|---|
| Delay | Trade space for time | Losing control of the delaying force |
| Withdrawal | Break contact | Enemy pursuit during the break |
| Retirement | Move away to a new mission | Being fixed while moving |

All three demand more control measures than an attack.

## Patrol steps

1. Plan (patrol order, route, contingencies).
2. Rehearse (actions on contact, actions at the objective, rally points).
3. Move to the objective (movement technique, security).
4. Actions on the objective (reconnaissance or ambush).
5. Withdraw (planned route, rally points).
6. Debrief (report, lessons, follow-on tasking).

## Ambush patrol — essentials

- Site selection: a kill zone the enemy must enter and cannot easily leave.
- Elements: security, support (fires), assault — each with a defined position and task.
- Control: initiation signal, cease-fire signal, withdrawal signal — all rehearsed.
- Withdrawal planned before the ambush is sprung; the ambush that cannot disengage is a trap for the ambusher.

## Urban patrolling tips

- Vary routes and times — never become predictable.
- Watch rooftops and windows; control the high ground.
- Move along walls, not down the center of streets.
- Keep intervals; maintain all-around security.
- Expect the enemy at intersections, in upper floors, and in basements.
