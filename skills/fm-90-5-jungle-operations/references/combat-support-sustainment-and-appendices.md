# Combat Support, Sustainment, and Appendices — FM 90-5

## Helicopters, Armor, Mechanized Infantry, Combat Support (Chapter 6)

- **Helicopters:** the jungle's answer to the absence of roads, but landing zones are scarce and must be cut or found.
- **Armor and mechanized infantry:** limited to ground that will carry them and to targets that can be definitely located.
- **Combat support:** planned around the jungle's restrictions on movement, observation, and communications rather than in spite of them.

## Combat Service Support (Chapter 7)

- **Supply:** the weight and bulk problem; high fuel consumption rates; anticipate requirements well in advance.
- **Maintenance:** a climate that rots equipment — jungle maintenance and supply techniques are a training subject in their own right.
- **Medical:** treatment of wounds and prevention of infection in a hot, wet environment; evacuation planning from the start.
- The jungle logistics problem is the same one the earlier manuals stated — anticipate well in advance, use waterways and air resupply, expect rapid deterioration of clothing and equipment — stated here as a sustainment problem for a force already in contact.

## The Ten Appendices

The appendices are FM 90-5's most useful durable content:

| Appendix | Subject |
|---|---|
| A | References |
| B | Navigation and tracking — compass, map, and pace count; resection using artillery marking rounds; four soldiers keeping a pace count |
| C | Jungle obstacles |
| D | Waterborne operations |
| E | Fighting positions and shelters |
| F | NBC warfare in jungle areas |
| G | Communication techniques in a jungle environment |
| H | The individual soldier's combat load |
| I | Adjustment of fire by sound |
| J | Defensive formations |

**Navigation (Appendix B):** the answer to the jungle's core navigation problem — no landmarks, no visibility, no reliable resection — is azimuth and pace count, with redundancy (four soldiers keeping a pace count) and resection by artillery marking rounds when available.

**Adjustment of fire by sound (Appendix I):** when limited visibility prevents observed adjustment of indirect fire, fire is adjusted by sound — a technique the main text flags as essential and the appendix details.

**Fighting positions and shelters (Appendix E):** positions built for the jungle's soil and drainage conditions, where extensive ground-level roots can make digging difficult and positions may have to be built above ground level.
