# Tactical Operations — FM 90-5

## The Shape of Jungle Combat

"Combat in the jungle is characterized by long periods of developing the situation and looking for the enemy; and short periods of violent, and sometimes unexpected, combat." Jungle operations follow the basic guides of FM 7-8, FM 7-10, FM 7-20, FM 90-4, and FM 100-5, with special techniques resulting from restricted maneuver, slow tempo, close combat, and limited visibility.

## The Four Requirements

To meet jungle conditions, units must have:

1. **Aggressive intelligence-gathering procedures.**
2. **Disciplined soldiers.**
3. **Solid SOPs proven in training and updated on a continuous basis.**
4. **Aggressive and tough-minded leadership.**

Discipline matters because of the extended periods of looking, often fruitlessly, for the enemy; when contact is made, maximum advantage comes only through aggressive and violent action predicated on solid SOPs. Aggressive leadership at the small-unit (squad and platoon) level is the element that ties discipline and training together.

## What the Jungle Does to Weapons

- Lack of line-of-sight and clearance may prevent visual contact between units, interlocking fires, and the use of TOW or Dragon missiles.
- Tree limbs may block mortars, flame weapons, 40-mm grenades, and hand grenades.
- Machineguns may not be able to attain grazing fire.
- Adjustment of indirect fire support is difficult due to limited visibility and may have to be accomplished by sound (Appendix I).
- Noise conditions differ: large numbers of animals, and their noise — or lack of it — can indicate something out of the norm. Sounds do not carry as far as on the conventional battlefield, so noises are closer than first believed.
- Movement is very difficult: heat, thick vegetation, and rugged terrain tire troops rapidly, especially those carrying heavy weapons or radios.

## The Threat

Chapter 4 is devoted entirely to the threat — the only one of the four jungle manuals with a dedicated enemy chapter. The enemy is a jungle fighter in his own right, trained to exploit the jungle. Do not assume superior firepower or technology removes the threat: his advantages are knowledge of the terrain, ability to live off the land, and willingness to fight at close quarters on ground of his choosing. The chapter is a Cold War product and its enemy picture is dated, but its premise is durable: potential jungle enemies train to exploit the jungle, "so must the US Army."

## Reconnaissance, Offense, Defense, Retrograde

- **Reconnaissance, surveillance, and security:** develop the situation before contact; patrolling is the primary collection means.
- **Offensive operations:** limited objectives, close control, violent action on contact.
- **Defensive operations:** all-round security, defensive formations (Appendix J), fighting positions and shelters (Appendix E).
- **Retrograde:** a deliberate operation with security throughout.
- **Other combat operations:** covered in the same chapter, with the same emphasis on SOPs that let small units fight without orders when communications fail.
