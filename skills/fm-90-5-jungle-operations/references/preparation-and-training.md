# Preparation and Training to Deploy — FM 90-5

## The Commander's Questions

When a unit is alerted for jungle training or combat, the commander must first consider:

- Where will the unit train or operate?
- What are the climatic and terrain conditions of the objective area?
- How much time does the unit have to prepare?
- What available training areas resemble the objective area?
- What type of operations — conventional or counterguerrilla?
- Will the unit take its own equipment?
- Does any equipment require modification (including camouflage painting)?
- What special equipment is required?
- Does the unit have jungle warfare instructors, soldiers with jungle experience, or linguists?
- What training assistance is available — outside instructors, training aids?
- Does higher headquarters have special SOPs for jungle war?
- Are all soldiers physically fit?
- What information is available about the enemy, local civilians, and allied forces in the objective area?

Once these questions are answered, the commander can develop a program to prepare the unit to operate in the jungle.

## Training Tips

- Units committed to jungle operations may have to fight as soon as they arrive — make the best use of preparation time.
- **Use garrison time:** teach jungle subjects by classroom instruction as soon as possible, so time in jungle training areas can go to advanced techniques.
- **Use local training areas:** even if they do not resemble jungle terrain, some jungle techniques can be introduced in them, providing a training base to expand on deployment.
- **Integrate individual training into unit training:** introduce individual jungle-living skills early in classes, then practice them during unit exercises, rather than devoting field time to them.
- **Begin physical training early** in the local training area, before deployment.
- Emphasis on the mental, physical, and psychological aspects of operating in a jungle environment. The key lies with the chain of command in developing a "will to win" as well as a "will to train to win."

## Training Subjects

- **Individual training:** jungle environment and acclimation; jungle maintenance and supply techniques; communications; and the individual skills of living in the jungle.
- **Staff and leader subjects:** the same subjects at the planning level, plus jungle maintenance and supply techniques.

## Acclimation

Allow time to adjust (acclimate) to the jungle before hard operations. The acclimation process is automatic and begins on arrival; physical conditioning and gradual exposure are the commander's tools. (For the full acclimation numbers — well developed by the fourth day, body remaining acclimated one to two weeks after departure — see FM 31-30 and FM 31-35 in this vault.)
