# Jungle Operations and Training

Distilled from MCTP 12-10C (ATP 3-90.98), Chapters 2–7 and Appendix D (Sep 2020).

## Foundations (Chapter 2)

- The jungle compresses the battlefield: engagements at tens of meters; the side that sees first usually shoots first.
- The deep fight is fought by patrols and aircraft, not massed maneuver; the close fight by small units that may be out of contact for hours.
- The thick canopy degrades GPS and satellite comms — the jungle force must operate when networks are degraded or absent. Joint operations are the norm.
- **Fundamentals:** security (360-degree, close-range — the jungle hides the enemy until he is close), movement (slow, deliberate, noisy if done wrong), surprise (concealment works both ways), sustainment (everything carried in; the jungle provides almost nothing usable without treatment).

## Combat Power (Chapter 3)

- **Leadership:** disease, heat, and navigation discipline enforced by example.
- **Information:** scarce and hard-won; the canopy defeats aerial observation. Human intelligence and patrol reporting become primary.
- **Command and control:** units close together and still out of contact — vegetation blocks radio, terrain blocks line of sight.
- **Movement and maneuver:** the jungle's decisive limitation; the force that can move and sustain movement chooses the fight.
- **Intelligence:** canopy defeats most collection.
- **Fires:** short fields of fire, poor observation; canopy defeats air observation and degrades air support.
- **Sustainment:** everything carried by foot or helicopter; the helicopter needs a landing zone the jungle does not provide. Air evacuation emphasis is necessary; where the jungle is too dense for a landing zone, plan other means.
- **Protection:** camouflage is easy and concealment nearly total — the problem is the enemy's concealment, and the environment: disease, heat, animals.

## Offensive Operations (Chapter 4)

- Close fight: movement to contact, hasty/deliberate attacks, exploitations, pursuits — all at short range, limited visibility.
- Reconnaissance and patrols are the decisive element: find the enemy before he finds you.
- Surprise is achievable precisely because observation is limited — a silent force can appear at close range.
- Favor **infiltration** and small-scale envelopment around a flank the enemy cannot observe. Frontal attack in the jungle is a close-range brawl with no room to maneuver — a last resort.
- Consolidate: secure ground and trails, reorganize, prepare for the counterattack through the same concealment.

## Defensive Operations (Chapter 5)

- Defense of positions dominating **trails, rivers, and clearings** — the jungle's movement corridors. Depth from successive positions, not a continuous line.
- **Defending encircled** is a distinct jungle problem: a cut-off position can be sustained by air and hold out far longer than in open ground, because the enemy cannot mass against it without exposing himself on the same trails.
- Plan the transition to the offense from the start — disease, heat, and enemy infiltration all work against a static position over time.

## Stability Operations (Chapter 6)

- Population scattered in villages along rivers and roads. Stability tasks (security, governance, development) adapted to places the government may never have reached and the insurgent may have lived in for years.

## Training (Chapter 7)

- **Re-learn every skill for the jungle:** rifle wet, muddy, corroded; radio under triple canopy; march through secondary jungle at 500 m/hr.
- **Camouflage and concealment:** the jungle provides it; the problem is discipline — movement, noise, light, and position camouflage.
- **Survival, evasion, and escape; water survival:** jungle survival is a water problem and a disease problem before a food problem. River crossings and swamp movement are distinct skills.
- **Land navigation:** the skill the whole operation depends on and the one most degraded by the environment (see navigation reference).
- **Jungle field craft:** living in the wet, keeping equipment working, moving silently, sleeping without becoming an insect or animal casualty.
- **Medical training:** malaria, diarrheal disease, immersion foot, jungle rot, heat injury — and self-aid/buddy aid for all of them.
- **Physical conditioning and acclimatization:** heat, humidity, load carriage over broken ground; ~one week to acclimatize.
- **March discipline:** slow, frequent halts, water discipline, security at every halt. A unit that marches in the jungle like it marches on a road arrives exhausted, dehydrated, and ambushed.
- **Adjustment and conduct of fire:** train for the engagement that happens at 20 m, not the qualification range.
- **Equipment operator training:** effects of heat and humidity, efficient operation within environmental limits, preventive maintenance per technical manual/lubrication order.
- **CBRN, air defense, fratricide:** train all of them in jungle conditions — the environment degrades each one.

## Fratricide Reduction (Appendix D)

- Short fields of fire, limited visibility, enemy close, friendly units close, nobody able to see either — fratricide is a constant risk.
- Countermeasures: identification, coordination, control measures. **Positive identification before firing** is the only thing standing between a patrol and its own sister unit.
