# Jungle Navigation, Movement, and Obstacles

Distilled from MCTP 12-10C (ATP 3-90.98), Appendix A–C (Sep 2020).

## Dead Reckoning — the Primary Jungle Method

- Although slower, dead reckoning is the most effective navigation method in the jungle. Terrain analysis, constant compass use, and an accurate pace count are essential.
- The thick canopy may degrade GPS. When it does, compass + map + pace count are all that remain.
- In jungle without significant terrain features, troops rely on compass and pace count to dead reckon to the objective.
- Where rivers, streams, or mountains exist, use **attack points** — identifiable terrain features adjacent to the objective — to locate it.
- **Resection:** locate position by grid azimuths to at least two well-defined map locations (three or more for greater accuracy). The fallback when a patrol is uncertain of its location.

## Pace Count

- Often the best method for measuring distance traveled. Each Soldier/Marine makes a **personal pace table**, practiced over different terrain types.
- At least **two** Soldiers/Marines in each patrol should be compass-qualified; **three or four** should keep pace count.
- The artillery fire support team chief keeps an accurate fix on location, as should platoon forward observers — the fire support chief is a navigation asset for the company commander.

## Daylight Movement Rates (Table A-1)

Maximum distance coverable in one hour of daylight. The normal error is **overestimating** distance traveled.

| Terrain | Maximum distance (meters/hour) |
|---|---|
| Tropical rainforest | up to 1,000 |
| Deciduous forest, secondary jungle, tall grass | 500 |
| Swamps | 100–500 |
| Rice paddies (wet) | 800 |
| Rice paddies (dry) | 2,000 |
| Plantations | 2,000 |
| Trails | up to 3,000 |

Planning rule: a 5 km movement through secondary jungle is a **ten-hour** movement, not a one-hour movement. Rate varies with troop condition, load, potential enemy contact, and jungle growth.

## Negotiating Jungle Obstacles (Appendix B)

- Rivers, swamps, steep banks, fallen trees, dense thickets, cultivated areas.
- Crossing methods, rope work where needed, crossing as a team with security. A river crossing in the jungle is a tactical operation, not a hike.

## Individual Combat Load (Appendix C)

- The load is what a Soldier/Marine can actually carry through the jungle at the movement rates above, in heat and humidity, day after day.
- The load that cannot be carried through secondary jungle at 500 m/hr is not a load — it is a liability.
