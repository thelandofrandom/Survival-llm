# Jungle Environment and Health

Distilled from MCTP 12-10C (ATP 3-90.98), Chapter 1 (Sep 2020).

## Jungle Types

| Type | Character |
|---|---|
| Primary jungle | Old growth, undisturbed; tall trees, thin ground undergrowth (canopy blocks light). Tropical rain forest or deciduous forest. |
| Secondary jungle | Previously cleared for farming/logging; younger, shorter trees, much thicker undergrowth. Harder to move through than primary. |
| Savanna | Natural grassland, few trees. Open by jungle standards; still a jungle environment for heat, disease, navigation. |

Common land features: swamps, savannas, cultivated areas. Large rivers are the highways — and the obstacles.

## Wildlife Hazards

- **Mosquitoes — the most dangerous tropical insects.** Malaria is the largest health threat; also chikungunya, dengue, yellow fever; heartworm larvae in military working dogs.
- **Flies** — carry and spread disease; can cause lesions and infections.
- **Wasps and bees** — rarely attack unless nests are disturbed. If disturbed: leave the area, reassemble at the last rally point; stings need medical attention.
- **Centipedes, spiders, scorpions** — painful stings; prefer dark places. Shake out sleep systems; check clothing and boots before dressing. Spider bites can cause necrotic tissue and secondary infection.
- **Ants** — dangerous to troops prone, sleeping, or injured and unable to move. Place wounded troops in an ant-free area.
- **Snakes** — stay alert, avoid disturbing. Treat all snakebites as venomous (TC 4-02.1). Antivenin is species-specific; no universal antivenin. Victims may need urgent medical evacuation. Units need an established snakebite procedure *before* it happens.
- **Leeches** (common in Southwest Pacific and Southeast Asia) — brush off before they bite. Once attached, do **not** pull off forcibly (part may remain in skin). They release if touched with insect repellent, a moist piece of tobacco, or similar. **Leech straps** around the lower legs prevent them crawling up.

## Malaria Discipline

1. Take prescribed antimalarial prophylactic medication.
2. Use insect repellent.
3. Wear permethrin-impregnated clothing covering as much of the body as possible.
4. Use nets or screens at every opportunity.
5. Avoid the worst-infested areas when possible.

Mosquitoes are most prevalent **early at night and just before dawn**. Malaria is more common in populated areas than in uninhabited jungle — be especially cautious around villages. Malaria discipline is a command responsibility.

## Heat Injury and Acclimatization

- Jungle heat injury is worse than the desert's: humidity prevents sweat evaporating, so cooling fails even at moderate temperatures.
- **Heat rash** — dots/tiny pimples. **Heat exhaustion** — most common; generally no organ damage. **Heat stroke** — most dangerous; heat-balance mechanism fails.
- **Risk factors:** lack of acclimatization, dehydration, alcohol, prior heat injury (any heat stroke, or more than two episodes of heat exhaustion).
- **Acclimatization:** ~**one week of daily heat exposure**; unusually susceptible personnel need more. Leaders must plan for troops who cannot fully acclimatize before operating.
- **Dehydration nullifies the benefits of heat acclimatization and physical fitness.** Leaders monitor for heat distress and adjust work/rest and water by the WBGT index (see the desert skill's Table 1-1).

## Water Purification

- Never consume water not yet treated by water purification specialists or medics.
- Specialists/medics disinfect with **chlorine** to a level prescribed by the command surgeon — kills organisms present and protects against later contamination.
- Heat reduces chlorine's disinfecting power: above **100°F (38°C)**, measure and maintain safe chlorine levels in storage containers.
- Personal consumption from suspect sources: **iodine purification tablets** (ATP 4-25.12).
- Use purified water for hand washing and food preparation as well as drinking.

## Equipment in the Jungle

Heat and humidity degrade nearly all equipment: metal corrodes, wood swells and rots, optics fog, electronics fail, ammunition deteriorates, lubricants wash out. Preventive maintenance per the equipment technical manual and lubrication order for hot-climate operations — daily, because a day's skipped maintenance is a week's corrosion.
