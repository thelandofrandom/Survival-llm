## Malaria — the Handbook's Priority Disease

- *"Of all the diseases that afflict mankind, many parasites, especially malaria, cause the highest morbidity and mortality worldwide."*
- Signature: repeated bouts of chills and fever. Vector: Anopheles mosquito.
- Field rules: **fever in the tropics is malaria until proven otherwise.** Malaria in a non-immune patient is a medical emergency. Fever + altered consciousness or jaundice = evacuate immediately.
- Prevention (durable): nets, repellent, treated clothing, chemoprophylaxis per **current** guidance — not the 1982 regimens in this book.

## Diarrheal Disease

| Disease | Signature | Field response |
|---|---|---|
| Amebic dysentery (Entamoeba histolytica) | Bloody diarrhea, cramps, fever | Rehydrate; evacuate — needs specific diagnosis |
| Bacillary dysentery | Bloody diarrhea, high fever, severe cramps | Rehydrate; consider bacillary vs. amebic first |
| Cholera (Vibrio cholerae) | Profuse watery "rice-water" diarrhea, rapid dehydration | **Fluid replacement is the treatment**; oral rehydration if the gut works, IV if not; evacuate |
| Food poisoning / gastroenteritis | Nausea, vomiting, diarrhea after food, often affects a group | Rehydrate, rest, watch for dehydration |
| Giardiasis | Prolonged watery diarrhea, cramps, bloating after suspect water | Rehydrate; needs diagnosis and current treatment |

**The durable lesson:** in diarrheal disease, rehydration is the treatment. Oral rehydration (clean water + salt + sugar) saves lives. A patient who cannot keep fluids down or shows severe dehydration (dry mouth, no urine, sunken eyes, confusion, weak rapid pulse) needs IV fluids and evacuation.

## Other Key Infections (Recognition Only)

- **Typhoid:** prolonged stepwise fever, headache, abdominal pain, rose spots; fecal-oral transmission; evacuate.
- **Hepatitis:** jaundice, dark urine, fatigue, right-upper-quadrant pain; fecal-oral (A/E) or blood/body-fluid (B/C) transmission; rest and evacuate.
- **Dengue:** sudden high fever, severe headache, pain behind the eyes, muscle/joint pain ("breakbone fever"); mosquito-borne; watch for bleeding.
- **Yellow fever:** fever, jaundice, bleeding; mosquito-borne; evacuate immediately.
- **Rabies:** any bite from an unknown animal — wash the wound thoroughly, evacuate for rabies evaluation. Once symptomatic, rabies is almost always fatal.
- **Tetanus:** muscle rigidity, lockjaw, spasms after a dirty wound; prevention is prophylaxis — every dirty wound gets tetanus consideration.
- **Plague, typhus, leptospirosis:** fever with exposure history (fleas, lice, contaminated water); evacuate — these are not field-managed diseases.

## Vector Control and Prevention

- Barrier + repellent + net: clothing tucked in and treated, repellent on exposed skin, bed nets at night.
- Handbook field expedients (historical): kerosene on neck, wrists, boot tops against chiggers/mites; lindane dust or sulfa powder against mites, chiggers, lice when repellents are unavailable. Modern repellents (DEET, picaridin, permethrin-treated clothing) replace these.
- Environmental control: drain standing water, clear vegetation near camp, control rodents and their fleas.
