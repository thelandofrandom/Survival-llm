# Operations — MCWP 3-35.6 (Chapter 3)

## Mobility is the key

Desert Storm was won by wide and rapid flanking movements, like Rommel's and Montgomery's in North Africa. Trafficability is generally good — but not always: salt marshes are NO-GO in the rainy season; sand bogs down traffic and exhausts foot movement; steep dune slopes and rocky mountains can be NO-GO for vehicles; wadis cross-compartment terrain, with steep unconsolidated banks, becoming torrents in rain and leading to NO-GO mud lake beds; rock quarries and mining areas, often unmarked on maps, affect mobility (satellite imagery identified them in Kuwait). Sharp angular debris punctures tires in rocky terrain. Given ample fuel, water, and resources, units can go around obstacles.

## Detection and night movement

Movement is easily detected by sand and dust signatures — in an engagement this may obscure the unit from direct fire, but surprise may be lost. **Moving at night is the logical choice.** Reconnaissance must be tempered with deception: extensive reconnaissance in one area can alert the enemy to intended operations there, and reconnaissance may itself serve as deception to draw attention from the real objective.

## Offensive operations

Concentrate overwhelming combat power — mass in time and space: rapidly concentrate at a given time and place, then disperse just as rapidly to avoid offering a lucrative target. Concentration means placing an overwhelming concentration of fires on the enemy, not necessarily massing men and vehicles. Mutual support matters as much as in temperate climates, but over large distances it means the capability to maneuver in support without disrupting the scheme of maneuver. Movement for concentration should take place at night or in limited visibility whenever possible; deception "cannot be overemphasized" where concealment is negligible. The attacker must suppress enemy weapon and surveillance systems; attack helicopters and high-performance aircraft are extremely useful for maneuvering firepower quickly over a large battlefield, so suppression of enemy air defense has high priority, as does destruction of enemy antitank capability — no target with a long-range antitank capability should be disregarded. In featureless terrain, shock, overwhelm, and destroy demand accurate reconnaissance (actual positions from false ones) and excellent navigation.

## Defensive operations

The defender's objective is to stop and destroy the attacking force by direct and indirect fires, obstacles, and counterattacks — the same logic in reverse.
