## Immediate Actions (in order)

1. Assess the immediate situation. **Think before you act.**
2. Protect yourself from NBC hazards (Chapter IX).
3. Seek a concealed site.
4. Assess medical condition; treat as necessary (Chapter V).
5. Sanitize your uniform of potentially compromising information.
6. Sanitize the area; hide equipment you are leaving.
7. Apply personal camouflage.
8. Move away from the concealed site — zigzag pattern recommended.
9. Use terrain to advantage: communication and concealment.
10. Find a hole-up site.

## Hole-Up Site Routine

- Reassess the situation; treat injuries, then inventory equipment.
- Review the plan of action; establish priorities.
- Determine current location.
- Improve camouflage.
- Focus thoughts on the task at hand.
- Execute the plan of action. Stay flexible.

## Concealment — Site Selection

A place of concealment must provide:
- Adequate concealment, ground and air.
- Safe distance from enemy positions and lines of communication (LOC).
- Listening and observation points.
- Multiple avenues of escape.
- Protection from the environment.
- Possible communications/signaling opportunities.

Stay alert, maintain security, drink water.

## Evasion Movement

- Travel slowly and deliberately. Do not leave evidence of travel.
- Noise and light discipline at all times.
- Stay away from LOC.
- Stop, look, listen, and smell; take appropriate action.
- Move from one concealed area to another.
- Use evasion movement techniques: move at night or in bad weather when possible, use covered routes, never move in a straight line toward the destination.

## Navigation

| Method | How |
|---|---|
| Map and compass | Primary when available; terrain association is the most reliable method with a map |
| Stick-shadow | Stick upright, mark shadow tip, wait 15 min, mark new tip — line between marks runs roughly east–west (first mark is west) |
| Sun | Rises east, sets west; shadow moves predictably through the day |
| Stars | North Star (northern hemisphere); Southern Cross (southern hemisphere) |
| Pace count / time–distance | Track distance actually traveled; never trust a single indicator |

**Navigation discipline:** move on a deliberate azimuth; check it often; record distance; note landmarks. Confirm direction by two methods when possible.
