# Sustainment: Medical, Protection, Water, Food — MCRP 3-02H

## Medical Self-Aid (Chapter V)

- You are your own medic. Assess, treat, keep moving.
- Bleeding: direct pressure and dressings; keep the casualty warm, flat, and still for shock.
- Fractures: improvised splints; splint the joint above and below.
- **Medical care serves movement.** A treatment that keeps the evader moving beats a perfect treatment that keeps him in place. Decide early whether an injury can be managed on the move or requires a hole-up.
- Foot care: treat blisters and hot spots early — a foot injury ends evasion faster than hunger.
- Improvised goggles prevent cold injury to eyes in bright snow or sand.

## Rules for Avoiding Illness

- Purify all water obtained from natural sources by using iodine tablets, bleach, or boiling for 5 minutes.
- Locate latrines 200 feet from water and away from shelter.
- Wash hands before preparing food or water.
- Clean all eating utensils after each meal.

## Personal Protection (Chapter VI)

- **Shelter:** protection from the environment first, concealment second. Lean-to, debris hut, snow cave, or natural overhang — and never a shelter visible from the air. A dry evader with a poor shelter outlasts a wet evader with a good plan.
- **Clothing:** layers, added or removed with activity. Avoid overdressing and overheating — sweat is the enemy in cold weather. Take special care of feet: change socks regularly, dry them when possible.
- **Fire:** warmth, purification, cooking, morale — and a signal to the enemy. Small fires, shielded from observation, dry wood, little smoke. Extinguish completely; scatter ashes.
- **Movement discipline:** maintain a realistic pace; take rest stops; go around obstacles, not over or through them; pack equipment to prevent loss, damage, imbalance, and injury.

## Water (Chapter VII)

- **Requirement:** water is the first survival priority. Days without food; hours without water in the heat.
- **Procurement, in order of preference:** rain, streams/springs, lakes, dew, improvised sources.
- **Solar still:** pit covered with clear plastic, container in the center, stone depressing the plastic above the container; sunlight evaporates ground moisture, condensation drips into the container. Slow, small yield — a supplement, not a supply.
- **Vegetation bag:** clear plastic bag tied over leafy vegetation collects transpired water.
- **Green bamboo:** water held in its sections.
- **Preparation:** filter cloudy water first (sediment shields organisms from chemical treatment), then **purify by iodine tablets, bleach, or boiling for 5 minutes**. Boiling is the most reliable.
- **Storage:** clean, covered containers; never near latrines or waste.

## Food (Chapter VIII)

- **Food is the lowest survival priority.** Water, shelter, and evasion come first. A healthy man can go days without food and still move.
- **Snares:** work while unattended. Simple loop snare, material that will not break under the strain; figure-8 (locking) loop if wire is used. Set on runs and trails where animals actually travel.
- **Fishing:** improvised hooks, lines, traps. Snares and fishing are the evader's methods because they work while he rests and hides.
- **Plant foods:** use the evasion chart to identify edible plants — the chart is the authority, not memory.
- **Universal edibility test (last resort only):** selection criteria first, then staged testing — touch to skin, touch to lip, small taste, small swallow, wait hours between stages, no other new food during the test. A plant failing any stage is not eaten. **Never eat a plant you cannot positively identify when any alternative exists.**
- **Illness rules:** the same four rules as above — purify water, latrines 200 feet from water, wash hands, clean utensils. Food poisoning in evasion is a mission-ending event.

## Induced Conditions (Chapter IX)

- **NBC hazards:** recognition, protection, first aid. The evader's rule is avoidance — detect, avoid, decontaminate if exposed. NBC protection is one of the immediate actions, before seeking concealment.
- **Heat, cold, altitude:** recognize the condition, protect against it with what you have, adjust the plan (slower movement, more water, better shelter, different route) rather than pushing through and becoming a casualty.
