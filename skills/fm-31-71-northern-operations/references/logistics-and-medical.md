# Logistics and Medical — FM 31-71

## Logistics

Unusual weather and terrain make supply, evacuation, transportation, and services more difficult and more time-consuming. Time and space factors vary with terrain, climate, and season; more time must be allowed for movement of supplies and troops because of the environment. **Distance is measured in time rather than space.** Commanders must issue orders early to allow adequate time for subordinates to move supplies and equipment. Logistical support planning is required in great detail, even for small unit operations. Adequate support must provide survival and comfort as well as combat resupply: food, fuel, clothing, sleeping gear, tentage, mountain gear, winter equipment, repair parts, and ammunition or nuclear weapons as appropriate. All means of transportation — aircraft, boats, wheeled and tracked vehicles, and individual means — must be considered in movement of supplies and equipment.

The capacity of the combat service support units to provide adequate logistical support may be the determining factor in evaluating the feasibility of a planned operation. The commander must be ready to alter the plan when environmental stresses make it impossible for the combat service support units to support it. Extended frontage, extreme weather conditions, and extended periods of darkness all weigh on logistical planning.

## Organization for Logistic Support

Support organization sites are selected for shelter, water, fuel, and protection from the weather and the enemy; storage must protect supplies from freezing, moisture, and loss; distribution must use every available means of transport, with aircraft and low ground pressure vehicles carrying the load where roads do not exist. Rear area security planning must account for the ease with which a mobile enemy can move through supposedly impassable ground on skis or by air. Low visibility operations complicate every logistical movement — convoys in whiteout are as vulnerable as patrols.

## Maintenance

General maintenance, vehicle operation and maintenance, and service are all harder in the north. Cold thickens lubricants, weakens batteries, and reduces personnel efficiency; maintenance shelters are essential and often unavailable. Vehicle operation in extreme cold requires warm-up procedures, special lubricants, and constant attention to fuel, batteries, and tires. Service — the supply of fuel, water, and rations — must be planned for the cold: water trailers must be kept in heated areas or provided with heater units to prevent freezing.

## Supply

Requirements planning, mobility planning, and distribution planning are the three legs of northern supply. Requirements must include the cold-weather items that temperate operations do not need — fuel for heating as well as for vehicles, sleeping gear, tentage, mountain gear, winter equipment. Mobility planning must match the transport to the ground and the season. Distribution must assume that routes will be closed by weather and that alternate means (air, boat, man-carry) will be needed.

## Medical Support

**Medical units.** Division-level health services should be available in the brigade combat trains to provide backup medical support to the aid station. This element must be equipped with appropriate radios to communicate with air ambulances, and vehicles with mobility equal to the units supported. It should be provided with an X-ray unit and film processing equipment to eliminate the necessity of evacuating all sprains as possible fractures. The size of the task force and the type of combat operation influence bed requirements and the type of hospital units best suited to support the force. Hospitals should be housed in tents, frame type, sectional unless better housing is available; tents, GP Medium, with liner, may be used when necessary. Flooring is considered mandatory. Water trailers should be kept in heated areas or provided with heater units to prevent freezing.

**Evacuation.** Small units operating as independent task force elements should establish a relatively short holding period for patients — **1 to 3 days** at unit level, subject to change by the unit surgeon — because adverse environmental conditions make extensive definitive care over an extended period exceedingly difficult. Unit medical support must maintain mobility equal to units being supported. The general nature of northern terrain makes surface evacuation of patients difficult in winter and virtually impossible in summer; the lack of adequate roadnets and the necessity of moving supplies over the same route greatly restrict patient evacuation. **The most practical means of patient movement is by helicopter.** The task force surgeon normally will be provided with adequate air ambulance support for evacuation within the task force area; air medical evacuation is the primary means outside the task force area, and aircraft resupplying the task force can carry patients on the return trip.

**Walking wounded.** Evacuation of walking wounded becomes a problem where there are no prominent terrain features for personnel to follow to the battalion aid station. Snowstorms, fog, and clouds further hamper movement by limiting visibility to a few feet. If walking wounded must be evacuated by foot, they must move in groups of no less than a specified minimum — never alone — with a guide who knows the route.

## Transportation and Traffic Control

Ports, rail, highway, and air transportation — all with northern modifications. Ports freeze; rail is limited to the few lines that exist; highways are few and weather-dependent; air transportation is the most flexible but the most weather-limited. Traffic control must account for the fact that the same route carries both supply and evacuation, and that a blocked route in the north has no alternate.

## Personnel

Selection of personnel for northern operations, replacements, morale, discipline, prisoners of war, refugees. Morale in the long northern night is a command function, sustained by routine, purpose, and the visible competence of leaders. Discipline in the north is largely self-discipline — the environment punishes lapses that a temperate climate would forgive. Chaplain support and religious services are planned as part of personnel support, not as an afterthought.
