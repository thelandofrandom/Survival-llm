# Arctic Environment — ATP 3-90.96

Distilled from ATP 3-90.96, Chapter 1 and Appendixes B–C (24 February 2025). Paraphrase with short quotes only; full text in `full-text.md`.

## Defining Arctic operations

- The Arctic is defined by latitude, climate, and conditions together: extreme cold, prolonged darkness and prolonged daylight, permafrost, sea ice, snow and ice terrain, vast distances, thin infrastructure.
- Arctic operations differ from ordinary cold-weather operations by the combination of all of these at once.
- Strategic significance: sea routes, resources, great-power competition — the reason the Army must be able to operate there, not merely survive there.

## Terrain

- Tundra, taiga (boreal forest), mountains, glaciers, muskeg, rivers, lakes, sea ice.
- Permafrost: digging difficult, drainage poor. Muskeg swallows vehicles in thaw. Rivers and lakes become highways when frozen, obstacles when thawing.
- **Snow depth and density are the most critical parameters for mobility**, followed by strength and layering (stratigraphy) of the snowpack.
- Terrain analysis must account for season: the same ground is a different battlefield in January, April, and July.

## Weather

- Extreme cold, high winds, blowing/drifting snow, ground blizzards, whiteout, icing.
- **Ground blizzard:** blizzard-like conditions without precipitation. Strong winds move unpacked snow forcefully through the air, severely limit visibility, can last for days, can lead to whiteout. Common in the Arctic. An attacker or defender with the wind at their back gains an advantage; the force facing the wind fights blind.
- Weather changes rapidly; forecasting is limited by sparse observation networks. Plan for weather you cannot predict precisely.

## Seasons

- Winter (dark, cold, frozen); spring thaw; summer (long daylight, thawed ground, insects, wet); autumn freeze-up.
- **Transition seasons — freeze-up and spring thaw — are the most dangerous for movement:** ice unreliable, ground impassable, conditions change daily.
- **Ice thickness is a commander's critical information requirement** — it determines safe travel over frozen water. Pay particular attention during thaw and freeze. Failure to determine safe ice thickness can result in vehicles and personnel breaking through.

## Wildlife

- Bears, moose, wolves, and others: a threat to personnel, a hazard to camps and food storage, a factor in movement and bivouac site selection.

## Windchill (Tables 1-4 and 1-5)

- Windchill dramatically amplifies cold. Vehicle movement, free-fall parachuting, and helicopter operations all create wind; any wind can exponentially increase windchill.
- Table 1-4: windchill chart (air temperature vs. wind speed in mph). Table 1-5: time to cheek frostbite in minutes.

| Frostbite danger | Time to frostbite, dry exposed cheek skin |
|---|---|
| Slight danger | Greater than 120 minutes |
| Increased danger | Less than 45 minutes |
| Great danger | Less than 5 minutes |

- Frostbite does not occur when ambient air is above freezing. Trench foot and immersion foot may occur at any point on the chart.
- Table 1-5 data are based on dry cheek skin of the most susceptible five percent of personnel. Wet skin or exposed extremities (fingers) can significantly decrease the time.
- Worked examples from Table 1-5: at −20°F with 25 mph wind, cheek frostbite in about **7 minutes**; at −30°F with 15 mph wind, about **7 minutes**; at −50°F with 40 mph wind, about **2 minutes**; at 0°F with 5 mph wind, greater than 120 minutes.

## Ice (Appendix C)

- Safe ice thickness must be calculated for travel and for aircraft (including ice runways).
- Ice thickness is only one factor in bearing capacity — temperature, quality, and loading matter too.
- Factors: ice thickness, temperature, quality. Check during freeze-up and thaw above all.
- Seaport access can be limited by sea ice; ice thickness considerations apply to aircraft and ski landing areas (ski landing area control officer elements).

## Mobility (Appendix B)

- Snow depth, density, strength, and layering govern vehicle, ski, and snowshoe mobility.
- Route selection must account for snowpack, slope, vegetation, and ice.
- Over-snow vehicles, skis, and snowshoes are mission equipment, not accessories.
