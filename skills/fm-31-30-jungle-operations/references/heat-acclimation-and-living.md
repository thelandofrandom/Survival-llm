# Heat, Acclimation, and Living in the Jungle — FM 31-30

## Acclimation

Acclimation — adaptation to work in heat with maximum efficiency and least discomfort — is the first training objective. Its characteristics:

- Begins the first day of arrival in the wet tropics; well developed by the fourth day.
- As it continues, sweating increases and begins more readily, but salt loss becomes less.
- Physical exercise speeds the process by inducing profuse sweating.
- The body remains acclimated for one to two weeks after departure from the wet tropical environment.
- Troops from southern mid-latitudes acclimate more easily than troops from farther north; surface transport arrival is easier than air arrival.
- Some soldiers may never become acclimated, "but, fortunately, the percentage is very small."

Normal body temperature is 98–99°F. The body sheds heat by conduction, radiation, and evaporation of sweat; evaporation becomes the most important means when radiation cannot keep up. An unacclimated man becomes irritable, sluggish, unable to sleep, and substandard in performance.

## Water

- **6 to 15 canteens of water a day** during normal operations, depending on sweat loss; **up to 25 canteens per day** at very high temperatures.
- "Water must not be rationed in training."
- Jungle water sources are ample, but troops must not drink from them without purifying the water.

## Heat Disorders

| Disorder | Cause | Symptoms | Treatment |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dehydration | Water not replaced as lost | Sluggish, listless | Replace water |
| Heat exhaustion | Excessive water loss | Headache, confusion, dizziness, drowsiness, weakness, incoordination, loss of appetite, vomiting, visual disturbances, rapid weak pulse, cool wet skin | Cool shaded rest, elevate feet, salt solution: **two salt tablets, well crushed, dissolved in a canteen of cool water** |
| Heat cramps | Excessive salt loss | Severe, painful muscle cramps | Same as heat exhaustion; prevent with **two salt tablets per canteen, not to exceed six tablets per day** |
| Heat stroke | Breakdown of heat regulation | Hot, red, dry skin; no sweating; temperature **106–110°F** | Cool immediately — creek or stream, pour on canteens of water, fan, ice if available; lower temperature to at least 102°F; evacuate at once |

## Prevention Rules

1. Drink plenty of water.
2. Become acclimated.
3. Use extra salt with food and water.
4. Eat the heavy meal during the cool of the day — food is the most important source of salt.
5. Dress properly: one loose layer, fatigue jacket outside the trousers, no undershirt. Tightly fitted uniforms saturate with sweat and hinder evaporation.

## Living in the Jungle

- The soldier must be conditioned to the jungle before commitment to combat, or he will be occupied solely with his surroundings and give little attention to the mission.
- Jungle operations require independent action by small units; each soldier must care for his own needs.
- He usually has to manpack his essentials — learn what is required, keep it to a minimum, use it well.
- Disease is constant: malaria (nets and anti-malaria medicines prescribed), dysentery, filariasis.
- Personal hygiene and sanitation are combat duties, not comforts.

**Caution:** the salt-tablet regimen (two per canteen, max six per day) is 1965 practice. Check it against current medical guidance before use.
