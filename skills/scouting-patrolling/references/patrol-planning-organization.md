# Patrol Planning and Organization (MCTP 3-01A, Chapters 1–2)

## Chapter outline

Ch. 1 Planning: definition, purpose, intelligence, types of patrols, movement techniques, tenets, planning considerations, unit commander duties, patrol leader duties, COC coordination. Ch. 2 Patrol Organization: organization, elements and teams, enablers, key leader responsibilities.

## The intelligence cycle

Direction → Collection → Processing → Dissemination. The patrol is the collection step. The patrol leader must know the intelligence requirements (the specific questions to answer) before planning anything else. A patrol that returns with interesting information but not the information the commander needed has failed.

## Tenets of patrolling

1. Detailed planning
2. Productive rehearsals (actions on the objective, actions on contact, passage of lines — until automatic)
3. Thorough reconnaissance (route, objective, enemy — maps, imagery, prior patrols)
4. Positive control (leader knows where every element is at all times)
5. All-around security (360 degrees, at all times, including halts and patrol bases)
6. Every Marine a collector

## Troop-leading steps (patrol leader duties)

| Step | Actions |
|---|---|
| 1. Begin planning | Receive mission; issue warning order; tentative plan; start movement of personnel/equipment |
| 2. Arrange reconnaissance | Coordinate map, imagery, and human reconnaissance of route and objective |
| 3. Make reconnaissance | Personally reconnoiter when possible, or best available substitute |
| 4. Complete the plan | Finalize plan including PACE contingencies (Primary, Alternate, Contingency, Emergency) |
| 5. Issue the order | Five-paragraph order: situation, mission, execution, administration/logistics, command/signal |
| 6. Supervise | Inspect personnel/equipment, conduct rehearsals, verify readiness — continues throughout |

## Unit commander's duties — checklist

- [ ] Determine patrol requirements
- [ ] Assign the unit
- [ ] Provide adequate planning time
- [ ] Provide the patrol leader all available information
- [ ] Provide special skills and equipment
- [ ] Provide miscellaneous support (transport, resupply, comms)
- [ ] Supervise the plan and preparations
- [ ] Debrief the patrol on return (the intelligence dies if not extracted promptly)

## Patrol organization

| Element | Role |
|---|---|
| Headquarters | Patrol leader, assistant, radio operator, corpsman |
| Security | Protects the patrol and the objective |
| Assault | Conducts the action on the objective |
| Support | Fire support from a support-by-fire position |
| Reconnaissance | Scouts the route and objective |

Teams (2–4 Marines) are the smallest building blocks, each with a team leader. Chain: team leader → element leader → patrol leader. Enablers: organic (corpsman, radio operator, machine gunner) and supporting (engineer, interpreter, dog team, air controller, fire support observer) — request supporting enablers early; an enabler who arrives at departure time is a liability.

## COC coordination

- Watch officer tracks the patrol; COC provides manual and technical control (radio checks, position reports, fire support).
- Agree in advance: reporting intervals, check-in procedures, overdue-patrol procedure.
- Information flows both ways: patrol reports position/status/findings; COC provides enemy, friendly, and support updates.
- A patrol that cannot communicate is on its own — plan for that.
