# Patrol Types, Movement, and Actions on Contact (MCTP 3-01A, Chapters 3–5)

## Chapter outline

Ch. 3 Types of Patrols — Ch. 4 Movement to and from the Objective Area — Ch. 5 Establishing and Operating a Patrol Base.

## Patrol types

| Type | Purpose | Key rule |
|---|---|---|
| Raid | Surprise attack on a fixed objective; destroy/capture, then withdraw | Surprise, speed, violence of action, planned withdrawal |
| Ambush | Surprise attack from concealment on a moving/halted enemy | Good killing zone, surprise, coordinated fires, rehearsed withdrawal; never linger |
| Security | Protect a unit/installation; detect enemy reconnaissance; early warning | Perimeter patrols, observation posts, sweeps of dead space |
| Contact | Establish contact with a friendly unit; exchange information | Recognition signals, link-up procedures, comms |
| Zone recon | Recon a defined area (disposition, terrain, routes) | Stealth; avoid detection and decisive engagement |
| Area recon | Recon a specific location (hill, village, crossing) | Answer the commander's specific questions |
| Route recon | Recon a route for movement suitability | Condition, obstacles, danger areas, enemy presence |

Reconnaissance patrols operate by stealth. If forced to fight, the mission has usually failed — the enemy knows you are there.

## Ambush — planning checklist

- [ ] Killing zone selected: enemy will be in it; you can see and shoot into it; he cannot easily escape
- [ ] Trigger defined (specific point, signal, or command that initiates the ambush)
- [ ] Assault element's route into the killing zone rehearsed
- [ ] Security elements cover flanks and withdrawal route
- [ ] Withdrawal route and rally point set
- [ ] Execute: occupy quietly, all-around security, wait, initiate on the trigger with maximum violence, assault through to confirm kills and collect intelligence, withdraw on the planned route

## Movement formations

| Formation | Best for | Weakness |
|---|---|---|
| Column | Speed, control | Vulnerable from flanks; slow to deploy into line |
| Wedge | Open terrain, all-around security (default) | — |
| Line | Maximum firepower forward; assault | Exposed flanks |
| Echelon | Protecting an exposed flank | One-sided security |
| Vee | Firepower forward and to flanks | — |

## Control measures

- **Checkpoints**: prominent terrain features along the route for position confirmation.
- **Rally points**: initial (near departure), en route (along route), objective (near objective), re-entry (near friendly lines). Every member knows every rally point and the actions at each. If scattered, move to the nearest rally point and wait the prescribed time; senior member present takes charge if the leader does not arrive.

## Danger areas — actions

1. Halt in a covered position; observe.
2. Send a reconnaissance element across; establish security on the far side.
3. Cross the rest of the patrol in small groups or on line, quickly, at the widest point.
4. IED threat: scan for indicators (disturbed earth, wires, debris, dead animals, absence of people); use a cleared lane; keep spacing; never step where you cannot see the ground; never bunch up.
5. Water: cross at the shallowest, narrowest point with least current; security on the far bank first.

## Passage of lines

- Coordinate in advance: time, location, numbers, recognition signals, password/countersign.
- Depart through a designated passage point with a friendly guide.
- On re-entry: approach slowly, make contact by agreed signal, wait to be recognized.
- Never surprise a friendly position. Fratricide risk is highest on re-entry after a raid or ambush, when the patrol is stressed and moving fast.

## Actions on contact — immediate action drills

| Contact | Reaction |
|---|---|
| General contact | Return fire, seek cover, report, maneuver (pre-rehearsed drill; first seconds decide) |
| Ambush (in killing zone) | Return fire, throw smoke, assault through the ambush — running out is what the killing zone is designed to kill |
| Ambush (outside killing zone) | Maneuver to support the engaged element; attack ambushers from the flank |
| IED discovered | Halt, security, mark, report, bypass at safe distance. Do not approach or touch. Cover 360° — the triggerman is nearby |
| IED attack | Expect a follow-on ambush. Cover, return fire, security, treat casualties, report. Do not rush the blast site (secondary device) |
| Air attack | Disperse, cover, go still. Do not fire unless ordered and a hit is realistic |
| Enemy drone | Cover and concealment, go still, minimize emissions, report. If a drone sees you, artillery or ambush follows |

## Patrol base — priorities of work (in order)

1. Security (perimeter, all-around, observation posts)
2. Communications
3. Hygiene
4. Water
5. Food
6. Rest
7. Equipment maintenance

Occupy in sequence (security first); exit in reverse, quietly, leaving no sign of occupation.
