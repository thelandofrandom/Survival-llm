## The scout as a hunter

- The scout thinks like a hunter: study the prey's habits, routes, and signs; study the ground; be patient; move quietly; watch before acting; act only when the moment is right.
- Methodology: understand the environment → find the sign → read the sign → predict the quarry's behavior → position to observe or intercept.
- The incident timeline reconstructs events: what happened, when, in what order — built from sign, observation, and the population.
- Traits: tactical patience (wait hours for minutes of observation); tactical cunning (deception, misdirection); deception (make the enemy see what you want); Good Shepherd philosophy (protect the population and the patrol; the population protects the scout).
- Interlocking fields of observation and reporting: position so observation overlaps with no seams or gaps; two scouts reporting the same thing from different angles is confirmation, not redundancy.

## The human eye

| Condition | Vision |
|---|---|
| Daylight | Sharp, color-capable |
| Night | Coarse, monochrome, slow to adapt — 30 minutes to full dark adaptation; one flash of white light destroys it |
| Night technique | Off-center vision: look slightly to the side of an object; rod cells (low-light detectors) are concentrated away from the retina's center |

The eye is drawn to movement, contrast, and things that break a pattern. Biological motion (a walking human) is recognized almost instantly. The eye adapts to a static scene and stops noticing it — keep scanning, relieve observers regularly.

## Why objects are seen — the six factors that break camouflage

1. **Shape** — an outline that does not match the surroundings
2. **Shadow** — cast by the object or by the observer's position
3. **Silhouette** — the object outlined against a lighter background (skyline, horizon)
4. **Surface** — shine or reflection (skin, metal, glass, wet surfaces)
5. **Spacing** — regular intervals (a line of men, evenly spaced equipment)
6. **Movement** — the single most visible factor

The scout looks for these in the enemy and eliminates them in himself.

## Observation techniques

- **Hasty search:** quick scan of a wide area for obvious targets or movement.
- **Detailed search:** slow, overlapping scan of a specific area, sector by sector.
- **Maintaining observation:** relieve observers, keep the log current, never leave a sector unwatched.
- **Limited visibility:** night vision/thermal when available; sound as a cue; off-center vision; scan slowly; accept slower, less certain observation.

## Recording and reporting

- **Observation log:** time, location, what was seen, observer — recorded as it happens. Memory alone is not evidence.
- **SALUTE spot report:** Size, Activity, Location, Unit/Uniform, Time, Equipment.
- **Sketches and cameras** support the report. Report promptly — intelligence that arrives late is history.

## Profiling — the six domains

| Domain | What to observe |
|---|---|
| Biometric cues | Physical characteristics |
| Kinesics | Body movement and posture |
| Proxemics | Use of space and distance |
| Geographics | Where people are and where they go |
| Iconography | Symbols, clothing, markings |
| Atmospherics | The mood of a place — tension, normalcy, fear |

Heuristics (mental shortcuts) enable fast decisions: identify the anomaly → assess context and relevance → decide. Threat indicators: smuggling behavior, unusually high situational awareness, mimicry/mirroring (copying others to blend in), illustrators and emblems (gestures and symbols signaling affiliation). Human responses to fear (fight, flight, freeze, compliance) are predictable and observable.

Suicide attack risk reduction: distance, barriers, observation, control of access. Be aware of insider threats and green-on-blue triggers.

## Rapport

Components: trust, respect, shared interest. Universal similarities: family, food, work, children. Behaviors that expedite rapport: patience, listening, honesty, consistency. A scout with rapport gets information; one without gets silence — or a warning to the enemy.

## Fieldcraft

- **Camouflage:** break the outline, match the background, eliminate shine and shadow.
- **Cover** (protection from fire — solid objects that stop bullets) vs. **concealment** (protection from observation — anything that hides you, even if it does not stop bullets). Know which you have.
- **Individual movement:** low crawl, high crawl, rush; use movement aids and clothing suited to the ground.
- **Limited visibility:** sound discipline matters more than visual camouflage. Move slowly, avoid noise, use sound to navigate and detect the enemy, plot threat positions by sound and touch.

## Range estimation

- **Mental estimation:** use known sizes (a man is ~1.8 m tall; a doorway ~2 m) and the appearance of objects at known distances.
- **Terrain study:** estimate from map and imagery before moving.
- **Good visibility:** bracketing (estimate too far, then too near, split the difference); the width-of-a-finger method; compare against known distances.
