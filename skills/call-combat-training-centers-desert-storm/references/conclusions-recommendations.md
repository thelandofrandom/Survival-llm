# Conclusions, Recommendations, and Appendix A — ARI Technical Report 970 (1993)

Distilled from Keene and Halpin, *How Well Did the Combat Training Centers Prepare Units for Combat?*, ARI Technical Report 970, January 1993.

## The Verdict on the CTCs

23% said the CTCs were harder than combat — "NTC rotations are harder than this war." 17% reported no surprises. 36% named specific changes: long-range communications and transport, host-nation support, OPFOR tactics, EPW numbers, and fratricide — "CMTC doesn't teach us that we kill more of our own than the enemy does."

The CTCs proved their worth: realism and intensity, low casualties, morale, weapons confidence, and teaching units to "think on their feet."

## Conclusions

The report's negative themes: the long rapid advance; communications and supply failures; the EPW burden; desert maintenance; fratricide, IFF, and navigation; unexploded ordnance; morale, sleep, intelligence, and NBC.

## Recommendations

- **Communications and navigation are equipment-acquisition problems** — including a GPS procurement contingency.
- **Logistics and EPW handling need contingency plans and analysis** — a potential "show stopper" for future operations.
- **Training solutions** for UXO handling, IFF, NBC, and sleep/rest discipline.
- **Reserve/Active integration — "One Army"** — through education.

## Appendix A — Respondents With No CTC Experience

481 respondents had no CTC rotation. Their top categories: Methods 89, Morale 73, Comms 70, Logistics 67, Maintenance 53, Mobility 35, Training 35, Weapons 27, POW 23, Casualties 18, Enemy 12, Hazards 7. Unique themes: "No one followed doctrine"; Reserve/Active friction — "Poor attitude of Active army towards USAR/NG troops"; too-old Reservists; joint-service friction — the Air Force "made their own rules"; cold-weather-only training; Scuds; mass casualties from a Scud rear-area attack; chemical casualty care not trained.

## How to Read This Report

This is questionnaire data — self-reported surprises from 1991, not verified lessons learned. The value is in the pattern: the same surprises named independently by hundreds of respondents, across ranks and units, point to real gaps between CTC training and the Desert Storm fight. The recommendations are the authors' — equipment acquisition for comms and navigation, contingency planning for logistics and EPWs, training fixes for UXO, IFF, NBC, and sleep — not findings of fact.
