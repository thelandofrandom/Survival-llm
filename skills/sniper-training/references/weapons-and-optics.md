# Weapons and Optics — FM 23-10

## M24 Sniper Weapon System

- 7.62-mm, bolt-action, magazine-fed, dedicated sniper rifle.
- Muzzle velocity: **2,794 fps** (M118). Maximum effective range: **800 m**; effective hits to **1,000 m** are the qualification confidence challenge.
- Length: 44.8 in overall; barrel 21.7 in (heavy contour).
- Issued with a deployment kit (operator tools and repair parts) and TM 9-1005-306-10, which the sniper must know.
- Designed for the M3A telescope or metallic iron sights (backup only). Scope removal/replacement shifts zero by less than 1/2 MOA — but leave the scope on the rifle.
- PMCS before every mission: inspect, then clean/lubricate as needed. In garrison, inspect often even when the rifle is infrequently used.

## M3A Scope

- Fixed-power day optic. Elevation dial marked 1–10: 1 = 100 m, 3 = 300 m, 7 = 700 m, etc.
- Each elevation click = **1 MOA**. Windage dial: 1 MOA per click.
- Zero on a known-distance range, preferably 900 m long, with bull's-eye-type targets (200-yard targets, NSN SR1-6920-00-900-8204).
- Record the zero in the sniper data book. Re-record whenever ammunition type or lot changes.

## Ammunition — M118 Special Ball

- 173-grain boat-tailed bullet; gilding-metal jacket, lead-antimony core; uncolored tip.
- Cartridge base stamped with year of manufacture and a circle with vertical and horizontal lines.
- Match-grade, not standard 7.62-mm ball. Lot-to-lot variation is real — log the lot in the data book.
- Planning figure: **100 rounds M118** per mission, plus M9 pistol and M9 bayonet.

## M21 Sniper Weapon System (Appendix B)

- Semi-automatic 7.62-mm system (accurized M14 with scope) — the M24's predecessor as the Army's primary sniper rifle.
- Faster follow-up shots than the bolt-action M24; slightly less inherent precision.
- Zeroing and maintenance procedures differ from the M24 — do not interchange them.

## Night Vision — AN/PVS-4

- Portable, battery-operated electro-optical sight; hand-held for observation or weapon-mounted for precision fire at night.
- Lets the observer detect and resolve distant targets in darkness; extends the team to continuous operations.
- Modern intensifiers/thermals replace it; the employment logic (team asset, trained with, managed like the day optic) is unchanged.

## Care, Maintenance, Malfunctions

- Maintenance = inspection, repair, cleaning, lubrication. Inspection reveals the need for the rest.
- **Salt water is the worst case:** extreme, rapid corrosion. During exposure, check and clean as often as possible, even if only lubricating.
- New barrels: formal break-in procedure (increases barrel life and accuracy, reduces cleaning need).
- Ammunition in direct sunlight: powder burn rate rises, muzzle velocity rises, point of impact rises. Keep ammo shaded and temperature-stable.
- Malfunctions not correctable by the sniper: turn the complete weapon system in to maintenance/supply for return to the contractor (Table 2-1 lists common malfunctions and corrections; it is not exhaustive).

## Pre-Mission Equipment Checklist

- [ ] M24 inspected (PMCS complete), scope mounted, zero verified and logged
- [ ] Iron sights present as backup; deployment kit packed
- [ ] 100 rounds M118 (lot number logged), M9 pistol, M9 bayonet
- [ ] M49 observation telescope; AN/PVS-4 with batteries
- [ ] Sniper data book, observation logs, range cards (blank and prepared)
- [ ] Man-portable secure radio; ghillie suit garnished with local vegetation
- [ ] Cleaning kit and lubricant; ammunition shaded from direct sun
