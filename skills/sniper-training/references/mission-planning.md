## METT-T Planning Factors

- **Mission:** what the team must do and what success looks like.
- **Enemy:** composition, disposition, capabilities, patterns.
- **Terrain and weather:** routes, hides, fields of fire, light, wind, temperature.
- **Troops available:** the two-man team, attached support, the SEO.
- **Time available:** planning, rehearsal, movement, execution, extraction — all budgeted.
- Arms, ammunition, and equipment are METT-T dependent. Planning figure: 100 rounds M118, M9 pistol, M9 bayonet.

## The Three Phases

**Insertion** — how the team gets in (foot, vehicle, helicopter), what support it needs, and the route to the hide. Planned and rehearsed as its own problem.

**Execution** — the firing position, the target set, engagement criteria, and the observation/reporting task. The team knows in advance what it may engage and what it must report.

**Extraction** — the route out, rally points, recovery plan, and the compromise drill. The SEO coordinates air support for all three phases. Never plan a mission you cannot extract from.

## The Sniper Employment Officer (SEO)

- The staff officer who employs the team: coordinates insertion, execution, and extraction; arranges air and fire support; deconflicts the team with friendly maneuver.
- The team briefs back to the SEO/commander — the plan is heard, not assumed.

## Communications

- Requirement: rapid, reliable, secure interchange of information.
- Team carries a man-portable radio with secure communications to the units involved in the mission.
- Discipline: short transmissions, prearranged brevity, awareness that every transmission can be direction-found.
- Reporting formats: SALT spot reports (Size, Activity, Location, Time) and the standard intelligence report formats (STANAG 2020 operational situation report; STANAG 2022 intelligence report).

## Qualification (Chapter 9)

- Firing Tables No. 1 and No. 2; scorecard at Figure 9-13.
- M24 maximum effective range is 800 m; effective hits at 1,000 m are the confidence challenge.
- To qualify, the sniper must meet the tables' standards — not merely shoot well on a calm day.

## Sustainment Training Program

Repetitive training in long-range marksmanship and fieldcraft — skills decay in weeks:

- [ ] Known-distance and unknown-distance firing
- [ ] Range-estimation drills (all five methods, scored against confirmed range)
- [ ] Observation exercises, including Kim's game
- [ ] Stalking lanes (judged on detection, not speed)
- [ ] Tracking lanes
- [ ] Night firing with AN/PVS-4
- [ ] Team drills: hide occupation, sniper/observer handover, compromise drill
- [ ] Data-book review after every session

## Pre-Mission Checklist

- [ ] Mission received and METT-T analysis complete
- [ ] Target priorities and engagement criteria confirmed
- [ ] Insertion, execution, and extraction planned; rally points set
- [ ] Coordination with SEO: fires, air support, recovery
- [ ] Communications check (secure radio, brevity, reporting formats)
- [ ] Equipment drawn and inspected (see weapons-and-optics.md checklist)
- [ ] Rehearsal conducted; brief-back delivered
