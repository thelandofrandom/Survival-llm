# Planning, Offensive, and Defensive Operations (MCRP 3-10A.4, Chapters 2–4)

## Chapter outline

Ch. 2 Planning: preparation, troop-leading steps, command and control planning. Ch. 3 Offensive Operations: purpose, characteristics, fundamentals, squad in the offense, limited visibility, infiltration, fortified positions. Ch. 4 Defensive Operations: purpose, characteristics, types, occupation, priorities of work, fighting positions, squad fire plan.

## Troop-leading steps

1. Begin planning — receive the mission, issue a warning order, tentative plan.
2. Arrange for reconnaissance — map, imagery, ground reconnaissance.
3. Make reconnaissance — personally when possible.
4. Complete the plan — finalize, including PACE contingencies.
5. Issue the order — five-paragraph order (situation, mission, execution, administration/logistics, command/signal).
6. Supervise — inspect, rehearse, verify readiness.

## Command and control planning

- **PACE:** Primary, Alternate, Contingency, Emergency plans for communications and movement.
- **Attachments/detachments:** who is attached, what they bring.
- **Fire support planning:** what fires are available, how to call them, no-fire areas, marking the squad's position.
- **Force protection:** security, camouflage, dispersion.

## Offense — the cycle

| Phase | What happens |
|---|---|
| Preparation | Study the objective; select the approach; designate assault and support elements; coordinate fires; rehearse on similar terrain, same time of day, same equipment |
| Conduct | Movement to the objective; assault (support suppresses, assault closes, breaches, clears); final assault (violent close-in fight) |
| Consolidation and reorganization | All-around security; redistribute ammunition; treat/evacuate casualties; replace lost leaders; prepare for counterattack; report |
| Exploitation | If the enemy is breaking, push forward, keep pressure on, prevent reorganization |
| Pursuit | Continue the attack against a fleeing enemy — fast, aggressive, decisive |

- **Limited visibility attacks:** same principles, tighter control — closer spacing, more frequent halts, compass and pace count, strict light/noise discipline; rehearse in the dark.
- **Infiltration:** move through or around the enemy to attack from an unexpected direction; stealth, route selection, control measures; bypass rather than engage prematurely.
- **Fortified positions:** breach obstacles (engineers, demolitions, manual breaching); attack bunkers (suppress, breach, clear — never enter from the front); assault trench systems (trench by trench, grenades and fire teams in sequence).

## Defense — priorities of work (in order)

1. Security
2. Clear fields of fire
3. Prepare fighting positions (foxhole → improved → overhead cover)
4. Emplace obstacles and mines
5. Establish communications
6. Stockpile ammunition, water, medical supplies

The defense is never "finished" — it is continuously improved until the enemy attacks or the squad moves.

## Fighting positions

- One- or two-man foxhole, improved with overhead cover, camouflaged.
- Primary and alternate sector of fire, grenade sump, withdrawal route.
- Range card for each position: range and direction to prominent terrain features — fires delivered from prepared data, not guessing.

## Squad fire plan

- Sectors of fire, principal directions of fire, final protective fires (a wall of fire across the enemy's most likely approach).
- Fire plan sketch: a drawing of the position showing sectors, ranges, and target reference points.
- The fire plan coordinates every weapon in the squad.
