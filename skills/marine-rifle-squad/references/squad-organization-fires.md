# Squad Organization and Fires (MCRP 3-10A.4, Chapter 1)

## Chapter outline

Mission and Purpose — Warfighting Philosophy — Organization — Preparation for Combat — Application of Fire — Movement and Maneuver — Actions on Contact — Fighter Leader.

## Organization — 13 Marines

| Position | Count | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Squad leader | 1 | Commands; responsible for everything |
| Fire team leader | 3 | Controls a fire team; fire discipline; typically near the automatic rifleman |
| Grenadier | 3 | M320/M203 40 mm grenade launcher + rifle; point targets, suppression, marking |
| Automatic rifleman | 3 | Base of fire — sustained, accurate automatic fire (M27 IAR in current organization) |
| Rifleman | 3 | Additional firepower, security, maneuver mass |
| Designated marksman | 1 | Precision fire at longer ranges; additional situational awareness; position set by the squad leader |

Senior fire team leader is the squad leader's second in command. The fire team (4 Marines) is the building block — it trains, moves, and fights together.

## Fire commands

Elements, in order: alert → direction → description of target → range → target assignment → command to fire. Subsequent fire commands adjust fire (shift, add/drop, cease) without repeating the whole command.

## Geometries of fire

| Geometry | Description | Effectiveness |
|---|---|---|
| Frontal fire | Directly at the front of the target | Least effective against a prepared enemy; last resort |
| Flanking fire | At the side of the target | Effective; exposes the enemy's weaker aspect |
| Enfilade fire | Along the long axis of the target | Most effective — every round that misses one target may hit another |
| Defilade | Target protected by terrain from some directions | Limits your options; find the direction that is not defiladed |

## Characteristics of fire

- **Trajectory:** flat for rifles at short range, plunging at long range.
- **Beaten zone:** the area where rounds strike the ground.
- **Cone of fire:** the spread of rounds from a weapon.
- **Danger space:** the distance over which a round remains dangerous.
- **Grazing fire:** fire that stays close to the ground over its whole trajectory — the most lethal form against an advancing enemy.

## Effects of fires

| Effect | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Suppression | Enemy keeps his head down and stops shooting — the most common and most important effect at squad level |
| Neutralization | Enemy temporarily ineffective |
| Destruction | Enemy killed or captured |

A suppressed enemy cannot shoot back, which lets the maneuver element move. Assaulting fire teams must recognize lulls in enemy incoming fire from effective suppression and advance into them.

## Unit fires and weapons to target match

- **Distributed fire:** spread across the target. **Concentrated fire:** on a single point. Fire teams shift fire between threats as the situation changes.
- Match the weapon to the target: rifles against personnel; automatic weapons for suppression; grenade launchers against point targets and positions; designated marksman against high-value or distant targets.

## Range determination

Methods: appearance of objects (a man is recognizable at certain distances), known distances, bracketing, optics and laser rangefinders when available. In the defense, ranges are recorded on range cards and the fire plan sketch so fires can be delivered quickly and accurately at night or in bad weather.

## Movement and maneuver

- **Base unit:** the element around which the squad maneuvers — usually a fire team that stays oriented on the objective while the others move; provides the reference for formations and the base of fire.
- **Fire team formations:** wedge (default), column, line, echelon, skirmishers.
- **Squad formations:** column, line, wedge, vee, echelon, file — built from the fire teams.
- **Changing formations:** by command and signal; rehearsed so the squad can do it under fire without confusion.

## Actions on contact

- **Fire and movement:** one element fires (base of fire) while another moves in alternating bounds; moving element advances in short rushes (3–5 seconds, 5–10 meters) while the firing element suppresses.
- **Fire and maneuver:** one element fixes the enemy with fire while another maneuvers to a position of advantage (flank, rear) and assaults.
