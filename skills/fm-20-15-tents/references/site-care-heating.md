# Site, Care, Heating — FM 20-15 (Chapters 4–10, ¶¶61–85)

## Tent Site (Chapter 4)

- **Selection (¶61):** shelter from wind/storms (never open field or exposed ridge; cold wind — closed end into the wind); high ground (drier); tough grass turf; shade free of underbrush in hot weather; never under dead trees or large dead branches; above the high-water mark by water; never in canyons or dry creek beds in mountains (flash torrents); never at the base of a cliff (avalanche/rockfall); level ground free of roots and rocks, extending ~1 ft beyond the walls for the ditch.
- **Snow (¶62):** prod for concealed crevasses with an ice/ski pole; pack snow hard or shovel to firm snow; doorstep dug 1 ft below the surface; leave space between tent and snow wall for shoveling drift; wall not high if snow drifts; horizontal platform on slopes; consider prevailing wind.
- **Trenching (¶63):** when in doubt, always dig. Exceptions: very sandy soil, or a mound sloping all ways. Always on heavy soil, clay, flat rock. Ring ditch just outside the pins, straight-cut (not V-shaped), dirt cast away from the tent (it rots canvas). Depth 4–5 in., shallowest places not over 3 in., sloped to flow to an outlet trench at the lowest point. Divert water from higher ground before it reaches the tent.

## Care of Tentage (Chapter 5, ¶¶64–69)

- **Treatment (¶64):** duck treated for fire, water, weather, mildew; dark olive-drab, practically lightproof; treatment adds 40–50% weight. Field re-treatment: Compound Coloring Type 1 Color C Green (51-C-1598) or Compound Re-treating Water/Weather/Mildew Resistant (51-C-1608) — waxes, mildew inhibitors, pigments in volatile inflammable solvent. Enemies: weather, fire, mildew, carelessness.
- **Rain (¶65a):** wet canvas and rope shrink and can tear a tent completely in two. Keep eave/corner lines loose — ~18-in. free swing at mid-line — or put blocks under upright poles and remove them when it rains.
- **Wind (¶65b):** tighten all lines at once, close door flaps, secure walls to foot-stop pins, lace corner lacings, guy ropes tight.
- **Fire (¶66):** fire-resistant chars slowly but burns. Use spark arresters; clean screen arresters regularly; fire out before removing arrester; improvise from fine-mesh wire screen if needed. Extinguishers: water or water plus harmless antifreeze only — acids/solvents ruin fabric.
- **Mildew (¶67):** ruins a good tent in 3 days. Never roll up wet (even dew-damp); dry seams, edges (bottom edge especially), and sod cloth; never roll pins/poles with the tent; inspect roofs under trees; keep clean. Storage: hang in midday sun or dry indoors, suspended, ventilated; dunnage of cured 2×4 lumber, 4 in. off hard/wood floors, 8 in. off earthen floors; dunnage between courses in damp air; bag near moisture-letting openings; never bag until dry and brushed; visible mildew → open air, preferably sun; issue oldest first; keep ~20 ft from shed sides/ends; segregate infected tentage. Re-treat on dry brushed tentage, brush in, dry ≥2 hours until solvent odor gone, outdoors away from flame, no smoking.
- **Carelessness (¶68) / repair (¶69):** follow procedures; never drag a tent; pack pins/poles separately; transport only covered (bag or saved osnaburg/burlap); inspect seams, bindings, lines, strain points. Cement patches: No. 1 (3 in.) for holes/rips to 1½ in.; No. 2 (4⅜ in.) to 2⅞ in.; No. 3 (6¼ in.) to 4¾ in.; ≥¾-in. overlap; two coats, first tacky (~5 min), roll together. Hand sewing: No. 15 sailmaker's needle, No. 5 ply cotton twine, beeswax, palm, 12.29-oz duck; stitch any hole over 4¼ in., overlap ≥1 in. plus ½ in. turn-under, sailmaker's stitch from top right. Larger damage → repair shop.

## Heating, Lines, Pins, Poles (Chapters 6–9, ¶¶70–80)

- **Heating (¶¶70–74):** spark arresters and shields around stovepipe openings; train personnel; stovepipe openings double as ventilation. Individual shelters have no issued heater; expedients: brush fire over the site for 1–2 hours then clear coals and pitch (warm dry ground for hours); 5–6-in. stones heated 2–3 hours, carried in with forked sticks or buried in a hole; one-burner cooking stove; solidified alcohol cans; gasoline lantern (candle lanterns also help). Shields/hoods: paint off the tent (paint removes waterproofing) — clean with naphtha/gasoline, primer class 101, dry 12 hours, lusterless olive-drab enamel class 204.
- **Lines (¶¶75–77):** store dry, loose coils off the floor on grating or pegs, air circulating, clean first, out of sun; dry wet rope hung in open air, not touching ground; wash dirty rope, dry thoroughly; keep from acids/alkalis, drying oils, paint, animal excreta.
- **Pins/poles (¶¶78–80):** poles stamped with kind (e.g., "Upright lower section, for 12 ft. 0 in. pole") — verify; inspect for cracks, splits, joiner, spindles; block uprights on soft ground; sink poles 2–4 in.

## Miscellaneous (Chapter 10, ¶¶81–85)

- **Flies:** auxiliary roof; usable with large/small wall, storage, squad M-1942, hospital ward tents (no longer issued for storage, small/large wall, squad). Tight on the ridge pole, or two ridge poles for an air gap when staying put; guyed to the same double-notched pins; may project as a porch or stand alone over supplies/cooking. A fly is a wind trap — a waterproof tent without one is often preferable.
- **Sod cloths:** 8–9-in. canvas strip along wall bottoms, laid flat inside and weighted with clean dry material; keeps out insects, animals, wind, cold. Leaves/earth banking is a poor substitute and rots canvas.
- **Ground cloths/floors:** generally not issued (trodden ground is more sanitary); exceptions — mountain tent built-in floor, maintenance shelter's six cloths. Wooden floors for semi-permanent camps: exact tent size; wet and dry a new tent before measuring (it shrinks a few inches).
- **Ventilation:** a closed tent is less permeable than a house with windows shut. Use built-in ventilators, stovepipe openings, doors, roll-up walls; command post tent has four windows. Never depend on air at the bottom — with the sod cloth weighted, almost none enters.
