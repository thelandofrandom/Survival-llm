# Soldier Load Tables — ATP 3-21.18

Source: ATP 3-21.18, Chapter 3 (Soldier Load). Distilled from the manual; see `full-text.md` for the verbatim source.

## Load Categories

| Load | Average weight | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| Fighting load | 60–80 lb (ideally ~60 lb) | Everything worn or carried to maneuver on, close with, and destroy the enemy in direct-fire contact |
| Approach march load | 80–100 lb (ideally ~80 lb) | Fighting load plus additional essential equipment (assault pack or rucksack); **must not exceed 100 lb** |
| Emergency approach march load | 100–125 lb | Only when necessary; limit march distances and provide recovery time afterward |

## Fighting Load — Possible Items (Table 3-1)

The fighting load is the sum of everything worn or carried. Representative items and weights from the manual's table include the uniform complete (OCP, T-shirt, socks, boots, belt — about 7.8 lb), body armor (a large improved outer tactical vest weighs about 35 lb; a medium about 30 lb), weapon and ammunition, helmet, and mission-essential equipment. The total is what matters, not the individual items — leaders weigh the soldier, not the packing list.

## Approach March Load — Possible Items (Table 3-2)

The approach march load adds the fighting load (about 68.9 lb in the manual's example) plus items such as the assault pack or rucksack, additional ammunition, water, food, cold-weather or wet-weather gear, and mission equipment. Items not needed in the fighting load but required for the mission go here — and are dropped in a secure location before contact.

## Load Tailoring Procedure

1. **Commander's guidance:** what the mission requires, what the enemy and terrain demand, what can be left behind.
2. **Estimate of the situation:** METT-TC (mission, enemy, terrain and weather, troops and support available, time available, civil considerations, informational considerations).
3. **Decisions to reduce or offset risk:** transfer load to vehicles, pack animals, or host-nation support; leave items behind; or accept the risk and march heavy — deliberately, with eyes open.
4. **Calculating load requirements:** weigh the load, don't estimate it. A pound on paper is not a pound on the back.
5. **Unit load (packing list):** standardized so every soldier carries the same essentials and leaders can check by inspection.
6. **Movement of supplies and equipment:** resupply operations, soldier load transfer (rotating crew-served weapons and other heavy loads between soldiers during the march), and contract, host-nation, and local-national support.

## Load Discipline Rules

- Every pound matters — pounds add up and pounds impact performance.
- Leaders ensure soldiers carry extra clothing including socks, and that combat lifesavers carry extra foot powder, gauze, adhesive tape, and foot-care items.
- During the march, adjust and refit loads at halts; switch crew-served weapons and heavy loads between soldiers.
- The approach march load is dropped before contact — the soldier fights in the fighting load, not the rucksack.
- An emergency approach march load is a deliberate, time-limited exception, not a planning standard.
