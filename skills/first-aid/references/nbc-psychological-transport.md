# NBC First Aid, Psychological Reactions, Rescue and Transport

Source: FM 4-25.11, Chapter 7 (First Aid in a Nuclear, Biological, and Chemical Environment), Chapter 8 (First Aid for Psychological Reactions), Appendix B (Rescue and Transportation Procedures).

## Chapter outlines

- Ch. 7: First-aid materials; classification of chemical and biological agents; masking; first aid for chemical attack; nerve agents; blister, choking, blood, incapacitating agents; incendiaries
- Ch. 8: Psychological reactions — causes, signs, first aid, return to duty
- Appendix B: Rescue and transportation — carries, improvised litters, evacuation

## NBC environment — the order of operations

1. **Protect yourself first.** Mask and protective clothing before touching a casualty — a contaminated casualty contaminates the rescuer.
2. Remove the casualty from the contaminated area.
3. Decontaminate skin with water and soap before treating wounds.
4. Then treat injuries in the normal priority order.

## Chemical agents

| Agent type | Signs | First aid |
|---|---|---|
| Nerve agents (sarin, VX) | Pinpoint pupils, runny nose, drooling, difficulty breathing, convulsions | Atropine and 2-PAM autoinjectors (Mark I kit), repeated as directed; evacuate |
| Blister agents (mustard) | Reddening and blisters hours after exposure | Flush with water; do not break blisters; cover |
| Choking agents (phosgene) | Coughing, choking, fluid in lungs | Rest; keep warm; evacuate; oxygen if available |
| Blood agents (cyanide) | Rapid collapse, gasping | Fresh air; artificial respiration; evacuate |
| Incapacitating agents | Confusion, inability to function | Keep safe and warm; evacuate |
| Incendiaries | Burns | Treat as burns; cool and cover |

Conditions for masking without order: sudden unexplained symptoms in several people, dead animals or insects, unusual liquid or vapor, an alarm — mask first, ask questions later.

## Biological and nuclear

- Biological agents: symptoms appear hours to days after exposure. First aid is supportive; evacuation is the priority.
- Nuclear: treat burns and wounds first; decontaminate (remove clothing, wash skin); radiation injury itself has no field first aid beyond decontamination and evacuation.

## Psychological reactions

Combat and disaster produce stress reactions — shaking, confusion, inability to function, withdrawal, panic. These are normal responses to abnormal stress, not cowardice. First aid: keep the casualty safe, warm, and fed; give simple purposeful tasks; reassure; stay with them; do not leave them alone. Most recover rapidly with rest, food, and reassurance and can return to duty. Those who do not improve need evacuation. (Current doctrine: ATP 4-02.11, Ch. 20 — combat and operational stress control; see the `tccc-casualty-response` skill.)

## Rescue and transportation (Appendix B)

- **Improvised litter:** two poles with a poncho, blanket, or shirts lashed between them.
- **Carries:** fireman's carry (one rescuer, conscious or unconscious casualty), two-person carry, pack-strap carry — chosen by the casualty's injuries and the terrain.
- Rules for every move: move the casualty as a unit; protect the spine; never move a casualty farther or rougher than the situation demands; rest the carrying team on a schedule.
