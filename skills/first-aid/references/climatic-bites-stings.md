# Climatic Injuries, Bites, and Stings

Source: FM 4-25.11, Chapter 5 (First Aid for Climatic Injuries) and Chapter 6 (First Aid for Bites and Stings).

## Chapter outlines

- Ch. 5: Heat injuries; cold injuries
- Ch. 6: Types of snakes; snakebites; human or animal bites; marine animals; insect bites and stings; first aid for bites and stings

## Heat injuries — progressive

| Injury | Signs | Treatment |
|---|---|---|
| Heat cramps | Muscle cramps (legs, abdomen), sweating | Rest in shade; sip water with salt |
| Heat exhaustion | Heavy sweating, weakness, headache, nausea, dizziness, cool clammy skin | Rest in shade; loosen clothing; cool with water and airflow; sip fluids |
| Heatstroke (emergency) | **Hot, dry skin — sweating stops**; confusion, seizures, unconsciousness | **Cool immediately and aggressively** — immerse in water if possible, or pour water over the body and fan; evacuate urgently; nothing by mouth if unconscious |

Heatstroke can kill in under an hour and occurs faster in the unacclimatized, dehydrated, or heavily working. Prevention: acclimatize gradually, drink on schedule, rest in shade, wear loose light clothing.

## Cold injuries

| Injury | Signs | Treatment |
|---|---|---|
| Frostbite | Pale, waxy, numb skin (fingers, toes, nose, ears) | Rewarm in warm (not hot) water only if refreezing is impossible; never rub; never use dry heat |
| Hypothermia | Shivering → confusion, clumsiness, slurred speech → shivering stops | Rewarm gradually; insulation, warm sweet liquids, body heat; never alcohol |
| Trench/immersion foot | Prolonged wet cold; pale, cold, numb feet that swell and blister when rewarmed | Prevention matters most: dry socks, foot movement, change socks |
| Snow blindness | Severe eye pain and tearing after sun on snow | Rest eyes in darkness; cool damp cloth; improvise slit goggles from cardboard or bark |

## Snakebites

- Identify the snake only if it can be done safely — never risk a second bite.
- Pit vipers (rattlesnake, copperhead, cottonmouth): severe local pain, swelling, bruising.
- Coral snake: little local pain but dangerous systemic effects (numbness, difficulty breathing).
- First aid: keep the casualty calm and still; keep the bitten limb at heart level or slightly below; remove rings and watches before swelling starts; immobilize with a splint.
- **Do NOT:** cut the wound, suck out venom, apply a tourniquet, apply ice.
- Evacuate — antivenin is a hospital treatment.

## Human and animal bites

Clean thoroughly with soap and clean water; cover with a dressing; evacuate for evaluation — human bites and animal bites (rabies risk) need medical care and often antibiotics.

## Marine animals

- Jellyfish: rinse with salt water (not fresh); remove tentacles with a stick or gloved hand; vinegar if available.
- Stingray: immerse in hot water (as hot as tolerable) to break down venom.
- Stonefish, cone shells: evacuate urgently.

## Insects, ticks, anaphylaxis

- Remove stingers by scraping (not squeezing — squeezing injects more venom). Apply cold.
- **Anaphylaxis** (difficulty breathing, swelling of face and throat, rapid pulse, collapse): immediate evacuation; epinephrine if available.
- Ticks: grasp close to the skin with tweezers, pull straight out; clean the bite; watch for spreading rash or fever in the following weeks.
