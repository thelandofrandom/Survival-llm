# Specific Injuries and Fractures

Source: FM 4-25.11, Chapter 3 (First Aid for Specific Injuries) and Chapter 4 (First Aid for Fractures).

## Chapter outlines

- Ch. 3: Head, neck, and facial injuries; chest wounds; abdominal wounds; burns; dressings and bandages (shoulder, elbow, hand, leg, knee, foot)
- Ch. 4: Kinds of fractures; signs and symptoms; purposes of immobilizing; splints, padding, bandages, slings, swathes; splinting procedures; upper and lower extremity, jaw, collarbone, shoulder, spinal, and neck fractures

## Head, neck, and facial injuries

- Suspect a spine injury with any head injury, fall from height, or unconscious casualty. Do not move; stabilize head and neck in the position found.
- Scalp bleeds heavily — control with direct pressure; a small cut can look catastrophic.
- Do not press on a depressed skull fracture or a penetrated eye.
- Clear blood from the mouth so the casualty does not choke.
- Deterioration signs: increasing drowsiness, repeated vomiting, unequal pupils — evacuate urgently.

## Chest wounds

- **Sucking chest wound:** air hissing/bubbling through a hole in the chest wall. Seal immediately with airtight material (plastic, foil, dressing wrapper) taped on **three sides** so air can escape but not enter. Place the casualty on the injured side so the good lung is uppermost. If breathing worsens after sealing, lift a corner to let trapped air out.

## Abdominal wounds

- Do not push protruding organs back in. Cover with a clean moist dressing, then a dry dressing over it. Keep the casualty flat with knees slightly bent. Give nothing by mouth. Internal abdominal bleeding cannot be stopped in the field — evacuate fast.

## Burns and the Rule of Nines

Cool with clean water; cover with a clean dry dressing; do not break blisters; do not apply grease, butter, or ointments. Treat for shock — a burn casualty loses fluid fast.

| Body part | % of body surface |
|---|---|
| Head and neck | 9% |
| Each arm | 9% |
| Each leg | 18% |
| Front of torso | 18% |
| Back of torso | 18% |
| Perineum | 1% |

Urgent evacuation: burns over 20% of body surface; burns of face, hands, feet, or genitals; any burn with breathing difficulty.

## Bandaging principles

Snug enough to hold the dressing and control bleeding; loose enough to preserve circulation. Check circulation below the bandage (color, warmth, feeling) after applying and every 15 minutes. The manual details shoulder, elbow, hand, leg, knee, and foot bandages — the pattern is the same: anchor, cover, secure, check.

## Fractures

**Kinds:** closed (skin intact) or open (bone through skin). **Signs:** deformity, swelling, pain on movement, tenderness, inability to use the limb, crepitus (grating), limb looks wrong. Treat every suspected fracture as a fracture.

**Splinting procedure:**
1. Remove jewelry; loosen clothing over the injury.
2. Splint the fracture **as found** — do not straighten it.
3. Immobilize the joint above and below the fracture.
4. Pad the splint (clothing, leaves, moss).
5. Tie snugly but not tight — check circulation below the splint.
6. Open fracture: cover the wound with a clean dressing first; do not push bone back in.

**Improvised splints:** sticks, boards, rolled newspaper, rifle, folded blanket. Slings from shirt, belt, or bandage; swathes to bind an arm to the chest.

**Special fractures:**
- Jaw: bandage under the chin; casualty leaning forward so blood drains from the mouth.
- Collarbone/shoulder: sling and swathe.
- Spine: do not move unless the scene is unsafe; if you must move, log-roll as a unit with several people, spine straight; rigid board/litter ideal.
- Neck: stabilize in position found; improvise a collar from a rolled towel or clothing; evacuate flat.
