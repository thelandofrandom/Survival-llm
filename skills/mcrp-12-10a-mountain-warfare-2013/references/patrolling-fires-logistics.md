# Patrolling, Fires, Logistics — MCRP 12-10A.1 (Chapters 10–12)

## Mountain patrolling (Chapter 10)

Mountainous terrain forces decentralized operations by small units. Covers patrol organization, planning, and execution in mountains — patrol bases, movement discipline, and individual load, including carrying purification tablets for natural water sources. Patrolling in mountains is slower, more exposed, and more dependent on the leader's route and weather judgment than on flat ground; it applies Chapters 3 and 4 to a tactical patrol.

## Fires (Chapter 11)

Weapons employment in mountains and cold weather: lubricants and other liquids may freeze or become thick or gummy; cold affects weapons, ammunition, and optics; the chapter covers weapons considerations, fire support, and the effects of altitude and cold on ballistics and equipment.

## Logistics and sustainment (Chapter 12)

"Logistic planning is good leadership" — failures in logistic planning "can be lethal in a mountainous/cold weather environment." Leaders constantly ask: what does the unit need, and how do supplies and equipment reach it to achieve the mission tomorrow, next week, next month? Predetermined supply routes; transport by air, helicopter, motor vehicles, and animal packing.

**Air delivery:** fixed-wing airdrop where surface resupply is not feasible — loads as heavy as **10,000 pounds** delivered an average of **150 meters** from the target from altitudes as high as **25,000 feet**. **Joint Precision Airdrop System (JPADS):** GPS-guided parachute systems in **750-, 2,000-, and 10,000-pound** versions, each a canopy plus an airborne guidance unit.
