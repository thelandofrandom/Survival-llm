# TM 21-210 Improvised Munitions Handbook

## Purpose

TM 21-210, *Improvised Munitions Handbook* (Headquarters, Department of the Army, 1969, 256 pages; cover imprint reads TM 31-210) — a handbook prepared by Frankford Arsenal, Philadelphia, Pennsylvania, for Special Forces and guerrilla troops. Its purpose, stated in the introduction, is to increase the potential of Special Forces and guerrilla troops by describing in detail the manufacture of munitions from seemingly innocuous locally available materials, for use in unconventional warfare operations where it may be impossible or unwise to use conventional military munitions as tools in certain missions. The manual covers methods for fabricating explosives, detonators, propellants, shaped charges, small arms, mortars, incendiaries, delays, switches, and similar items from indigenous materials. It is organized in seven sections: I — Explosives and Propellants (including igniters); II — Mines and Grenades; III — Small Arms Weapons and Ammunition; IV — Mortars and Rockets; V — Incendiary Devices; VI — Fuses, Detonators and Delay Mechanisms; VII — Miscellaneous. A reference section at the end gives data on standard explosives (mercury fulminate, lead styphnate, lead azide, nitrostarch, nitroglycerin, commercial dynamite, military dynamite, blasting gelatin, Composition B, Composition C4, ammonium nitrate) and on armor materials (thicknesses of materials required to defeat 7.62-mm and 12.70-mm fire).

**Historic status — read this first.** This is a 1969 handbook, marked "For Official Use Only" in its original printing, written for trained Special Forces personnel in unconventional warfare. It is a historical document. Its chemistry, improvised weapons, and field-expedient methods are of that era; many of its procedures are inherently dangerous, and some of its materials and techniques have been superseded by later doctrine and by modern safety practice. This skill distills the manual's scope, inventory, and stated safety rules — it does not reproduce the manual's step-by-step fabrication procedures in the distilled text. The complete verbatim text, including every procedure exactly as printed, is in `references/full-text.md` for reference. Nothing in this skill is a recommendation to build any of these items.

**The manual's own framing.** Manufactured, precision devices almost always will be more effective, more reliable, and easier to use than improvised ones, the introduction states — but shelf items will not be available for certain operations for security or logistical reasons, so the operator must rely on materials he can buy in a drug or paint store, find in a junk pile, or scrounge from military stocks. Many ingredients and materials are so commonplace or innocuous they can be carried without arousing suspicion; the completed item is often more easily concealed or camouflaged; and the field-expedient item can be tailored for the intended target, giving an advantage in flexibility and versatility over the standard item. The items were conceived in-house or obtained from other publications or personnel engaged in munitions or special-warfare work, and the manual contains simple explanations and illustrations to permit construction by personnel not normally familiar with making and handling munitions.

## 1. Safety and Reliability — The Manual's Own Rules

**1.1. Evaluation.** Each item was evaluated both theoretically and experimentally to assure safety and reliability. A large number of items were discarded because of inherent hazards or unreliable performance. Safety warnings are prominently inserted in the procedures where they apply — but the manual emphasizes that safety is a matter of attitude: "men who are alert, who think out a situation, and who take correct precautions have fewer accidents than the careless and indifferent." Work must be planned and instructions followed to the letter; all work should be done in a neat and orderly manner.

**1.2. Energy concentrations.** In the manufacture of explosives, detonators, propellants, and incendiaries, equipment must be kept clean and energy concentrations must be avoided: sparks, friction, impact, hot objects, flame, chemical reactions, and excessive pressure.

**1.3. Test before use.** The items were found effective in most environments; however, samples should be made and tested remotely prior to actual use to assure proper performance. Chemical items should be used as soon as possible after preparation and kept free of moisture, dirt, and the above energy concentrations. Special care should be taken in any attempt at substitution or use of items for purposes other than that specified or intended.

**1.4. Recurring cautions in the procedures.** The manual's cautions, repeated across the items, include: do not boil mixtures that must stay wet; do not overheat containers or they may shatter; remove mixtures from heat as soon as granules are dry; sulfuric and nitric acids burn skin and destroy clothing — wash spills away with large quantities of water and do not inhale the fumes (sulfuric acid fumes are dangerous); nitrobenzene is toxic — do not inhale fumes; do not inhale fumes from carbon tetrachloride mixtures; keep primary-explosive solutions away from flame; handle dry primary explosives with great care — do not scrape or handle roughly, they are shock and flame sensitive, and store in capped containers; methyl nitrate and similar liquid explosives are shock sensitive and must not contact skin; and electrical arrangements in the manual may employ voltages that are dangerous. These cautions are stated in the procedures themselves and are summarized here as the manual states them — they are not a complete safety program, and the manual's own rule is to follow each procedure to the letter. → `references/explosives-and-propellants.md`

## 2. Section I — Explosives and Propellants

**2.1. Scope.** Explosives and propellants made from locally available materials, with materials, sources, procedures, and "how to use" for each item. The section includes:

- **Plastic explosive filler** — potassium chlorate and petroleum jelly (Vaseline); detonated with a commercial #8 or any military blasting cap.
- **Potassium nitrate** — extraction/preparation from fertilizer and other sources.
- **Improvised black powder** — potassium nitrate, charcoal, and sulfur; the manual cautions not to boil the mixture and to be sure all mixture stays wet during preparation.
- **Nitric acid** — preparation by distillation; cautions on acid burns and fumes.
- **Initiator for dust explosions** — a device to initiate dust/air explosions.
- **Fertilizer explosive** — ammonium nitrate fertilizer-based explosive.
- **Carbon tet explosive** — carbon tetrachloride-based; cautions on dangerous fumes.
- **Fertilizer AN-AL explosive** — ammonium nitrate and aluminum.
- **"Red or white powder" propellant** — a propellant for small-arms and mortar use.
- **Nitric acid/nitrobenzene ("Hellhoffite") explosive** — cautions on acid burns and nitrobenzene toxicity.
- **Optimized process for cellulose/acid explosives** — nitrated cellulose.
- **Methyl nitrate dynamite** — shock-sensitive liquid explosive; cautions on handling and skin contact.
- **Urea nitrate explosive** — urea and nitric acid.
- **Preparation of copper sulfate (pentahydrate)** — a precursor for other items.
- **Reclamation of RDX from C-4** — recovering RDX from Composition C-4.
- **Lead picrate, picric acid from aspirin, double salts, sodium chlorate, mercury fulminate, sodium chlorate and sugar or aluminum explosive** — primary explosives and initiators, with repeated cautions that at several stages the mixture is a primary explosive: keep away from flame, handle dry explosive with great care, do not scrape, store in capped containers. Mercury fulminate and lead picrate are primary (initiating) explosives — shock and flame sensitive by nature.

**2.2. What the section teaches as doctrine.** The common thread is precursor chemistry from drug-store, paint-store, and fertilizer sources: nitrates (potassium nitrate, ammonium nitrate, urea nitrate, sodium chlorate) as oxidizers; petroleum jelly, sugar, aluminum, and carbon as fuels; acids (nitric, sulfuric) for nitration; and primary explosives (mercury fulminate, lead picrate, lead styphnate, lead azide) as initiators. Every item lists material required, source, procedure, and how to use; the manual's own cautions are embedded in the procedures. → `references/explosives-and-propellants.md`

## 3. Section II — Mines and Grenades

**3.1. Scope.** Improvised mines, grenades, and shaped charges:

- **Pipe hand grenade** — a pipe-body fragmentation grenade with a blasting-cap fuze.
- **Nail grenade** — a grenade body packed with nails as fragmentation.
- **Wine bottle cone charge** — a shaped charge using a wine bottle as the cone liner form.
- **Grenade–tin can land mine** — a grenade rigged in a tin can as a pressure or trip device.
- **Mortar scrap mine** — a mine using mortar shell scrap.
- **Coke bottle shaped charge** — a shaped charge in a soft-drink bottle.
- **Cylindrical cavity shaped charge** — a shaped charge with a cylindrical cavity liner.
- **Funnel shaped charge** — a shaped charge using a funnel as the liner.
- **Linear shaped charge** — a line-shaped charge for cutting.

**3.2. Doctrine.** The section's items are all built around the same principles: a container, an explosive filler from Section I, a fragmentation or liner element, and a fuze or firing device from Section VI. The shaped-charge items exploit the Munroe effect — a lined cavity that focuses the explosive energy into a jet — using bottles, funnels, and cylinders as expedient liners. → `references/mines-grenades-small-arms.md`

## 4. Section III — Small Arms Weapons and Ammunition

**4.1. Scope.** Improvised firearms built from pipe and scrap, with safety checks:

- **Pipe pistol for 9-mm ammunition** — pipe barrel and breech, with a safety check: test-fire the pistol before hand firing; instructions for operation.
- **Shotgun (12-gauge pipe shotgun)** — safety check: test-fire before hand firing; instructions for operation.
- **Rifle cartridge / pipe rifle** — improvised rifle with safety check: test-fire before hand firing.
- **Reusable primer** — fabrication of a reusable primer for cartridges.
- **Pipe pistol for .45 caliber, .38 caliber, and .22 caliber (long or short) ammunition** — caliber-specific pipe pistols, each with test-fire safety checks.
- **Low signature system** — a suppressed/low-signature improvised weapon system.

**4.2. Doctrine.** Every firearm in the section carries the same rule: **safety check — test fire before hand firing**. The weapons are single-shot, pipe-based, and built from standard pipe fittings; the manual gives material lists, procedures, and operating instructions for each. Ammunition is covered by the reusable primer and cartridge procedures and by the propellant items in Section I. → `references/mines-grenades-small-arms.md`

## 5. Section IV — Mortars and Rockets

**5.1. Scope.** Improvised indirect-fire weapons:

- **Recoilless launcher** — a launcher for improvised rockets or grenades.
- **Shotgun grenade launcher** — a shotgun-based grenade launcher.
- **Grenade launcher (57-mm cardboard container)** — a launcher built from a 57-mm ammunition cardboard container.
- **Fire bottle launcher** — a launcher for incendiary fire bottles.
- **Grenade launchers** — additional launcher variants.
- **80-mm mortar projectile launcher** — a mortar tube and projectile with a range table (desired range, maximum height, angle of elevation, required charge — number of increments — and the range the mortar will reach), covering ranges in yards with corresponding tube elevations and propellant increments.

**5.2. Doctrine.** The section pairs improvised launch tubes with the propellants of Section I and the grenades and incendiaries of Sections II and V. The 80-mm mortar's range table is the manual's only tabulated firing data for an improvised weapon: range is set by tube elevation and the number of propellant increments. → `references/mines-grenades-small-arms.md`

## 6. Section V — Incendiary Devices

**6.1. Scope.** Fire bottles, gelled flame fuels, and incendiary munitions:

- **Chemical fire bottle** — a chemically initiated fire bottle.
- **Igniter from book matches** — a match-based igniter.
- **Mechanically initiated fire bottle** — a fire bottle with a mechanical initiator; the manual warns not to remove the safety stick until ready to throw the fire bottle.
- **Gelled flame fuels** — lye systems, lye-alcohol systems, soap-alcohol systems, egg systems, latex systems, and animal blood systems; gelled fuels are made by mixing gasoline with a gelling agent (lye, soap, egg, latex, or blood) and are used in fire bottles and flame devices. Several procedures specify using an aluminum container.
- **Acid delay incendiary** — an incendiary with an acid-based delay; the manual marks the handling "extremely important."
- **Improvised white flare, improvised yellow flare** — pyrotechnic flares from local chemicals.
- **Improvised iron oxide** — preparation of iron oxide (a thermite component).
- **Improvised white smoke munition, improvised black smoke munition** — smoke-producing munitions.

**6.2. Doctrine.** Gelled flame fuels are the section's core: gasoline gelled with a common agent sticks to the target and burns longer than liquid fuel. The fire-bottle items pair a gelled or liquid fuel with a chemical or mechanical igniter and a delay; the acid delay incendiary uses the time for acid to eat through a barrier as its delay. → `references/incendiaries-fuses-delays-switches.md`

## 7. Section VI — Fuses, Detonators, and Delay Mechanisms

**7.1. Scope.** The initiation train — how to set off the items in Sections I–V:

- **Electric bulb initiator** — a light-bulb filament as an electric initiator.
- **Fuse igniter from book matches** — a match-head igniter for fuse.
- **Delay igniter from cigarette** — a cigarette as a time-delay igniter.
- **Watch delay timer** — a wristwatch as a delay timer.
- **Dried seed timer** — a seed-swelling delay timer.
- **Fast burning fuse and slow burning fuse** — improvised time fuses.
- **Clothespin time delay switch** — a clothespin as an electrical time-delay switch.
- **Can-liquid time delay** — a liquid-level time delay.
- **Short-term and long-term time delays for grenades** — delay fuzes for grenades.
- **Mousetrap switch, pull-loop switch, knife switch, altimeter switch** — improvised firing switches: mousetrap (spring-driven), pull-loop (trip), knife (manual), altimeter (pressure/altitude — for aircraft or elevation change).
- **Improvised scale, rope grenade launching technique** — a field-expedient scale for weighing charges and a rope technique for launching grenades.

**7.2. Doctrine.** The section's principle is that any measurable process can be a delay: a burning cigarette, a swelling seed, a draining liquid, a watch movement, or a clothespin held by a dissolving or burning element. Switches are the triggers: pressure, pull, release, tilt, altitude, or manual action closing an electrical circuit or releasing a spring. The firing train is blasting cap → booster → main charge, and the manual's initiators (electric bulb, match igniter) are the means of firing the cap. → `references/incendiaries-fuses-delays-switches.md`

## 8. Section VII — Miscellaneous

**8.1. Scope.** Supporting equipment and reference data:

- **Bicycle generator power source** — a bicycle-driven generator as an electrical power source for firing circuits.
- **Automobile generator power source** — a vehicle generator as a power source.
- **Improvised battery (short lasting) and improvised battery (2-hour duration)** — chemical batteries from local materials, with materials, common sources, procedures, and how to use.
- **Armor materials** — a table of thicknesses of materials required to defeat 7.62-mm and 12.70-mm fire.
- **Reference data on standard explosives** — mercury fulminate, lead styphnate, lead azide, nitrostarch, nitroglycerin, commercial dynamite, military dynamite, blasting gelatin, Composition B, Composition C4, and ammonium nitrate.

**8.2. Doctrine.** The section closes the loop: the electrical items (generators, batteries) power the firing switches and initiators of Section VI, and the armor table tells the operator what his improvised weapons can and cannot defeat. → `references/incendiaries-fuses-delays-switches.md`

## Output Contract

When answering a question from TM 21-210, give the item's purpose and the section it belongs to first, then the materials class and the manual's stated safety cautions, then where the full procedure lives (full-text.md). Never reproduce a step-by-step fabrication procedure in a distilled answer; point to the verbatim text. Always state the manual's historic (1969) status and its intended audience (trained Special Forces personnel). Never present an improvised munition as safe, reliable, or recommended — the manual itself says manufactured devices are almost always more effective, more reliable, and easier to use.

## Operating Rules

1. **Distill, don't reproduce.** The distilled skill names items, materials classes, and safety rules; step-by-step procedures live only in the verbatim full-text file.
2. **Safety is the manual's own rule.** Follow instructions to the letter; avoid sparks, friction, impact, hot objects, flame, chemical reactions, and excessive pressure; test samples remotely before use.
3. **Primary explosives are shock and flame sensitive.** Handle dry primary explosives with great care; do not scrape; store capped; keep away from flame.
4. **Acids burn; fumes kill.** Wash acid spills with large quantities of water; do not inhale fumes; nitrobenzene is toxic.
5. **Test fire before hand firing.** Every improvised firearm in the manual carries this rule.
6. **Historic document.** 1969, for trained personnel, in unconventional warfare; not a recommendation and not current doctrine.
