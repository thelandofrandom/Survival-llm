# Explosives and Propellants — TM 21-210 (Section I)

## Scope

Section I covers explosives and propellants (including igniters) fabricated from locally available materials. Each item in the manual gives material required, source, procedure, and how to use. This reference lists the items, their materials classes, and the manual's stated safety cautions. Step-by-step procedures are in `references/full-text.md` only.

## Doctrine — Precursor Chemistry

The section's common thread is oxidizer-plus-fuel chemistry from common sources: nitrates (potassium nitrate, ammonium nitrate, urea nitrate, sodium chlorate) as oxidizers; petroleum jelly, sugar, aluminum, and carbon as fuels; acids (nitric, sulfuric) for nitration; and primary explosives (mercury fulminate, lead picrate, lead styphnate, lead azide) as initiators for the firing train (blasting cap → booster → main charge).

## The Manual's Safety Rules (Section I and Introduction)

- Each item was evaluated theoretically and experimentally for safety and reliability; many items were discarded for inherent hazards or unreliable performance.
- Safety is a matter of attitude: alert personnel who think out a situation and take correct precautions have fewer accidents than the careless and indifferent. Work must be planned and instructions followed to the letter; all work in a neat and orderly manner.
- In manufacturing explosives, detonators, propellants, and incendiaries, keep equipment clean and avoid energy concentrations: sparks, friction, impact, hot objects, flame, chemical reactions, and excessive pressure.
- Make samples and test remotely prior to actual use to assure proper performance.
- Use chemical items as soon as possible after preparation; keep them free of moisture, dirt, and energy concentrations.
- Take special care in any attempt at substitution or use of items for purposes other than specified or intended.
- Recurring cautions: do not boil mixtures that must stay wet; do not overheat containers or they may shatter; remove mixtures from heat as soon as granules are dry; sulfuric and nitric acids burn skin and destroy clothing — wash spills with large quantities of water and do not inhale fumes; nitrobenzene is toxic; carbon tetrachloride fumes are dangerous; keep primary-explosive solutions away from flame; handle dry primary explosives with great care, do not scrape or handle roughly, store in capped containers.
