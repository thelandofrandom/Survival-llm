# Night Offensive Operations — FM 31-36 (TEST)

## What Night Combat Looks Like

Combat at night or under reduced visibility is characterized by:

- A reduced ability to place aimed fire on the enemy.
- A corresponding increase in the importance of close combat, volume of fire, and the fires of fixed weapons laid on definite targets or target areas.
- Difficulty in movement.
- Difficulty in maintaining control, direction, and contact.

Organizing forces at night is no different than in daytime — but the environment punishes every lapse in control.

## Purposes of the Night Attack

- Hasten accomplishment of the mission.
- Continue an action begun but not finished in daylight.
- Exploit a success made during daylight.
- Retain the initiative.
- Keep continual pressure on enemy forces, avoid loss of contact, and thereby prevent reorganization of his forces, reestablishment of his communications, and preparation of defensive positions.
- Avoid heavy losses by crossing at night terrain that predominately favors the defense.
- Achieve surprise.
- Fix enemy reserves or cause their premature commitment.
- Compensate for an enemy with air or ground superiority.

## The Nine Prerequisites

As a general rule, the following are necessary for successful night operations:

1. Detailed planning and preparation.
2. Detailed information of terrain, enemy, weather conditions, and illumination levels.
3. Secrecy and surprise.
4. Detailed orders and simplified schemes of maneuver.
5. Easily identifiable objectives.
6. Requirements for close control.
7. Special recognition and reporting procedures, particularly where secrecy is to be preserved.
8. Logistical support.
9. Deception.

Surprise and secrecy are attained more easily at night than in daylight, but achieving them requires stricter adherence to secrecy — concealed routes of approach and deception measures are used as much as possible.

## Maneuver

- Complicated maneuvers are avoided.
- Company and platoon objectives are delineated so they are recognizable at night with a medium-range night observation device.
- The battalion or task force commander employs enough combat forces forward to enable assaulting units to clear assigned objectives in the initial assault.
- If the objective is deep or the mission requires immediate continuation, the attack should begin early at night — breach initial defenses with dismounted forces, then exploit with highly mechanized forces during darkness.
- Where pressure is relentless, rested infantry units are committed around or through units in contact at night to preclude the enemy from gaining the initiative.

## Fire Support

- Fire support plans for night attacks are similar to daylight attacks; illumination is provided per the night visibility plan.
- When partial or no illumination is provided, special restrictions govern the movement of weapons and the initiation and shifting of supporting fires, controlled with night viewing devices.
- When the attack is made to achieve surprise, the commander announces the conditions under which supporting weapons will fire.
- If the attack is discovered after the assault is launched, supporting fires go on the objective, beyond it, or on its flanks, as appropriate.

## The Night Attack by Arm

Part Three covers the night attack by airborne forces, airmobile infantry forces, tank-mechanized infantry task forces, and armored cavalry units — each with the same planning prerequisites applied to its own mobility and firepower, and each emphasizing that night operating aids are what make the attack feasible at battalion level and below.
