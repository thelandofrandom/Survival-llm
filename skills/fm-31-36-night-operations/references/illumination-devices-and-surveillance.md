# Illumination, Devices, and Surveillance — FM 31-36 (TEST)

## The Toolkit (Chapter 3)

- **Planning** for night vision employment.
- **Battlefield illumination:** artificial light to support night operations, planned per the night visibility plan.
- **Night vision devices:** passive (image intensification, no artificial illumination) and active (used with infrared, pink, or visible illumination).
- **Sound ranging:** locating enemy weapons and activity by sound.
- **Anti-intrusion devices:** sensors and alarms for perimeter and approach security.
- **Radars:** ground surveillance radar for detecting movement at night.
- **Aerial surveillance:** air observation and reconnaissance at night.
- **Signal intelligence:** intercept and direction-finding support to night operations.

## The Planning Rule

The principal requirement is to produce a night vision advantage for the unit conducting the operation — near-daylight speed and accuracy — and to coordinate all night vision aids (passive devices, radar, incendiary and other illumination, aerial surveillance) under the commander's concept.

The use of night operating aids improves some tactical capabilities more than others; devices should be employed to best facilitate the individual task or unit mission. As the limitations imposed by the environment are reduced or removed by night vision devices or battlefield illumination, night attacks more closely resemble daylight attacks — and as a minimum, the control measures of daytime tactical operations are also employed at night.

## Equipment Appendices

Separate appendices describe each major item of night vision equipment and devices in use in 1968. All of them are obsolete as equipment and should be read as history, not as a catalog. The manual itself warns that new and improved night operating aids are continually being developed and that commanders must adapt tactics as capabilities and availability change.
