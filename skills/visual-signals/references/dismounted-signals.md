# Dismounted Hand and Arm Signals — TC 3-21.60, Chapter 1

## General rules (para 1-1)

- **Single arrowhead** in the illustration: the signal is not continuously repeated, but may be repeated at intervals until acknowledged or the desired action is executed.
- **Double arrowhead:** repeated continuously until acknowledged or the action is taken.
- Signals are illustrated as normally seen by the viewer; some are shown in oblique, right-angle, or overhead views for clarity.
- Leaders of dismounted units use these signals to control individuals, teams, and squads (para 1-2).

## Formations (paras 1-3 to 1-14, Figures 1-1 to 1-12)

| Signal | Movement (para) |
|---|---|
| Disperse (1-3) | Extend either arm horizontally from the shoulder; wave repeatedly to the front and to the side in a sweeping motion, palm toward ground |
| Assemble or rally (1-4) | Raise the arm vertically overhead, palm to the front; wave in large, horizontal circles. Normally followed by pointing to the assembly/rally site |
| Join me / follow me / come forward (1-5) | Point toward the person(s)/unit(s); beckon by holding the arm horizontally to the front, palm up, motioning toward the body |
| Increase speed / double time / rush (1-6) | Raise the fist to the shoulder; thrust the fist upward to the full extent of the arm and back to shoulder level, several times rapidly |
| Wedge (1-7) | Arms extended downward and to the sides at 45° below the horizontal, palms to the front. Alternate: non-firing hand, index finger and pinky to the ground, other fingers curled |
| Vee (1-8) | Arms raised and extended 45° above the horizontal. Alternate: non-firing hand, index and pinky fingers up, other fingers curled |
| Line (1-9) | Arms extended parallel to the ground |
| Echelon left (1-10) | Right arm raised 45° above the shoulder; left arm 45° below the horizontal, pointing toward the ground |
| Echelon right (1-11) | Left arm raised 45° above the shoulder; right arm 45° below the horizontal, pointing toward the ground |
| Staggered column (1-12) | Upper arms parallel to the ground, forearms perpendicular; raise arms so they are fully extended above the head. Repeat |
| Column (1-13) | Raise and extend the arm overhead; move it to the right and left; continue until the formation is executed. Alternate: non-firing hand touching the rim of the headgear directly in front of the face, fingers extended and joined |
| Herringbone (1-14) | Arms parallel to the ground; forearms bent perpendicular. Repeat |

## Patrolling signals (paras 1-16 to 1-38, Figures 1-13 to 1-35)

| Signal | Movement (para) |
|---|---|
| Contact left (1-16) | Extend the left arm parallel to the ground; bend the arm until the forearm is perpendicular. Repeat |
| Contact right (1-17) | Same with the right arm. Repeat |
| Air attack (1-18) | Bend the arms at a 45° angle; forearms crossed. Repeat |
| CBRN attack (1-19) | Extend the arms and fists; bend the arms to the shoulders. Repeat |
| Fix bayonets (1-20) | Simulate the movement of the right hand removing the bayonet from the scabbard and fixing it on the rifle |
| Enemy in sight (1-21) | Hold the rifle in the ready position at shoulder level; point the rifle at the enemy. Alternate: non-firing hand, index finger at the enemy, thumb pointing down, other fingers curled |
| Quick time (1-22) | Arm extended horizontally sideward, palm to the front; wave the arm slightly downward several times, arm straight, never above the horizontal |
| Take cover (1-23) | Arm at a 45° angle from the side, above the horizontal, palm down; then lower the arm to the side |
| Map check (1-24) | Point at the palm of one hand with the index finger of the other hand |
| Halt (1-25) | Raise the hand to head level, fingers extended and joined |
| Take a knee (1-26) | Following halt: lower the hand from the halt position to waist level, palm facing down, fingers extended and joined |
| Move to the prone (1-27) | Following halt or take a knee: lower the arm past waist level, palm down, then move the forearm from waist level to below waist level repeatedly; palm toward ground, fingers extended and joined |
| Pace count (1-28) | Tap the heel of the boot repeatedly with an open hand |
| RTO forward (1-29) | Raise the hand to the ear with the thumb and little finger extended |
| Head count (1-30) | Tap the back of the helmet repeatedly with an open hand |
| Danger area (1-31) | Draw the right hand, palm down, across the neck in a throat-cutting motion from left to right |
| Freeze (1-32) | Raise the fist to head level |
| Move the platoon leader to the front (1-33) | Non-firing hand, index finger to the rim of the headgear |
| Move the platoon sergeant to the front (1-34) | Non-firing hand, grab the collar (simulate if no collar) |
| Move the squad leader forward (1-35) | Non-firing hand, finger on the upper arm; the number of fingers is the squad number (two fingers = second squad leader) |
| Number signals (1-36) | All numbers with the non-firing hand; numbers greater than nine are given as individual digits (27 = 2, then 7) |
| Stop, look, listen, smell — SLLS (1-37) | Raise the open palm of the non-firing hand to ear level |
| Message acknowledged (1-38) | Hold the fist out, thumb up |

**Common confusions to drill:** halt vs. take a knee vs. move to the prone is a single descending chain (head → waist → below waist); take cover (arm 45° above horizontal, palm down, then lowered) vs. slow down (arm horizontal, palm down, waved slightly downward) are similar movements in different contexts; danger area (throat-cutting) vs. stop engines (throat-cutting) are the same motion in different domains — context is the disambiguator.

## Crew-served weapons signals (paras 1-39 to 1-44, Figures 1-36 to 1-40)

Crew-served weapons must communicate in environments where visual signals are the best means of transmitting information.

| Signal | Movement (para) |
|---|---|
| Commence firing / change rate of fire (1-40) | Extend the arm in front of the body, palm down; move it through a wide horizontal arc several times. For machine guns, giving the signal again and moving the arm faster means the next higher rate of fire; moving it slower means slow the rate. Primarily for direct-fire weapons |
| Change direction or elevation (1-41) | Move the hand and arm in the new direction; indicate the amount by the number of fingers shown — each finger = 1 mil or 1 meter of change. Extend the hand repeatedly to total the change. Example: UP EIGHT meters = arm upward with five fingers showing, then arm upward again with three fingers showing |
| Move over / shift fire (1-42) | Raise the hand on the side toward the new direction and move it across the body to the opposite shoulder, palm to the front; then swing the arm in a horizontal arc, extending the arm and hand to point in the new direction. For slight changes, move the hand from the final position to the desired direction |
| Cease firing (1-43) | Raise the hand in front of the forehead, palm to the front; swing the hand and forearm up and down several times in front of the face |
| Out of action (1-44) | Strike the fist of one hand several times in rapid succession against the palm of the other hand |

## Weapon control rule

Unless otherwise depicted, the individual weapon has been removed from the figures for clarity. Soldiers maintain positive control of their weapon at all times. If the proper signal cannot be performed while maintaining positive control, use an alternate signal with the non-firing hand. If both hands are needed, move the weapon to the hang or sling-arms position.
