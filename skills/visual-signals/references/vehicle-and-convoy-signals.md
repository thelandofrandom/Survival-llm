# Vehicle, Convoy, and Flag Signals — TC 3-21.60, Chapter 2

## General (para 2-1)

Signals illustrated from the hatch or the top of a tactical vehicle are for illustration only or to be given while the vehicle is stationary. Per AR 385-10, personnel will not expose more than their head and shoulders (name-tag defilade) except when actively engaging targets with the vehicle-mounted weapons systems.

## Mechanized movement techniques (paras 2-2 to 2-23, Figures 2-1 to 2-21)

| Signal | Movement (para) |
|---|---|
| Wedge (2-3) | Arms extended downward and to the sides at a 45° angle below the horizontal, palms to the front |
| Vee (2-4) | Arms raised and extended 45° above the horizontal |
| Line (2-5) | Arms extended parallel to the ground |
| Coil (2-6) | One arm raised above the head and rotated in a small circle |
| Echelon left (2-7) | Right arm raised 45° above the shoulder; left arm 45° below the horizontal, pointing toward the ground |
| Echelon right (2-8) | Left arm raised 45° above the shoulder; right arm 45° below the horizontal, pointing toward the ground |
| Staggered column (2-9) | Upper arms parallel to the ground, forearms perpendicular; raise arms so they are fully extended above the head. Repeat |
| Column (2-10) | Raise and extend the arm overhead; move it to the right and left; continue until the formation is executed. Alternate: non-firing hand touching the rim of the headgear directly in front of the face, fingers extended and joined |
| Herringbone (2-11) | Arms parallel to the ground; forearms bent perpendicular. Repeat |
| Traveling (2-12) | Arm extended overhead and swung in a circle from the shoulder |
| Traveling overwatch (2-13) | Both arms extended and raised up and down |
| Bounding overwatch (2-14) | One arm at a 45° angle; bend the arm and tap the helmet. Repeat |
| Fire (2-15) | Drop the arm sharply from the vertical position (usually from the "are you ready" position) to the side. To fire a single weapon, point at that weapon with the arm extended, then drop the arm sharply to the side. Usually a fire command for indirect-fire weapons |
| Move to the left (2-16) | Extend the arm to the left and raise it up and down |
| Move to the right (2-17) | Extend the arm to the right and raise it up and down |
| Advance / move out / follow me (2-18) | Face the direction of movement; hold the arm extended to the rear; swing the arm overhead and forward in the direction of movement (hold at the horizontal), palm down |
| Dismount (2-19) | Arms extended, two or three movements up and down, hands open toward ground |
| Stop (2-20) | Hands clasped together, palms facing, at chin level |
| Button up (2-21) | Both hands, one on top of the other, palms down, on top of the helmet; arms back and in the same plane as the body. Unbutton: give the button-up signal, then separate the hands, moving them to each side in a slicing motion; repeat |
| Open up (2-22) | Arms extended overhead, palms inward, then slowly lowered to a horizontal position |
| Close up (2-23) | Both arms parallel to the ground, palms uppermost, then moved upward and inward toward the head |

## Guiding drivers and crews (paras 2-24 to 2-39, Figures 2-22 to 2-36)

Hand-and-arm and light signals used to guide and direct vehicles. Flashlights are used at night; **blue filters should be used whenever possible to preserve the driver's night vision**. Chemical lights can also be used and have less effect on the driver's night vision.

| Signal | Movement — day / night (para) |
|---|---|
| Attention (2-25) | Extend the arm sideways, slightly above horizontal; palm to the front; wave the arm to and from the head several times |
| I am ready / are you ready? (2-26) | Extend the arm toward the person being signaled; then raise the arm slightly above horizontal, palm outward |
| Mount (2-27) | Two or three upward movements with the open hand, palm facing upward |
| Disregard previous command / remain in place (2-28) | Raise both arms and cross wrists above the head, palms facing out |
| I do not understand (2-29) | Raise both arms sideward to the horizontal; bend both arms at the elbows and place both hands across the face, palms to the front |
| Start engine / prepare to move (2-30) | Day: simulate cranking engines by moving the arm, with the fist, in a circular motion at waist level. Night: move a light to describe a horizontal figure 8 in a vertical plane in front of the body |
| Halt or stop (2-31) | Day: raise the hand upward to the full extent of the arm, palm to the front, hold until understood. Night: move a light horizontally back and forth several times across the path of approaching traffic. Same signal stops engines |
| Increase speed (2-32) | Day: raise the fist to shoulder level; thrust the fist upward to the full extent of the arm and back to shoulder level rapidly several times. Night: move a light vertically several times in front of the body |
| Right or left turn (2-33) | Day: extend the arm horizontally to the side, palm outward. Night: rotate a light to describe a circle 12 to 18 inches in diameter in the direction of the turn |
| Slow down (2-34) | Day: extend the arm horizontally sideward, palm facing downward; wave the arm slightly downward several times, keeping the arm straight; do not move the arm above horizontal |
| Move forward (2-35) | Move the hands and forearms backward and forward, palms toward the chest |
| Move in reverse — stationary vehicles (2-36) | Day: face the vehicle(s), raise the hands to shoulder level, palms to the front; move the hands forward and backward. Night: hold a light at shoulder level; blink it several times toward the vehicle(s) |
| Close distance between vehicles and stop (2-37) | Face the vehicle(s); extend the forearms to the front, palms inward and shoulder-width apart; bring the palms together as the vehicle(s) approaches. The vehicle(s) must stop when the palms come together |
| Stop engines (2-38) | Extend the arm parallel to the ground, hand open; move the arm across the body in a throat-cutting action |
| Neutral steer — track vehicles (2-39) | Cross the wrists at the throat; point the index finger in the direction of steer; make a fist of the other hand |

## Traffic control (paras 2-40 to 2-45, Figures 2-37 to 2-41)

Used by authorized officials (civilian and military police, personnel at traffic control points). At night, given with a flashlight or a lighted wand.

| Signal | Movement (para) |
|---|---|
| Left and right traffic stop (2-41) | Stand facing traffic with the arms raised, palms open, in the same plane as the shoulders |
| Front traffic stop (2-42) | Stand facing traffic with the arm raised, palm open |
| Rear traffic stop (2-43) | Stand with the back to traffic, the arm raised, palm open; rotate the upper body so the palm faces traffic |
| Traffic from the right, GO (2-44) | Stand with the right side facing traffic, left arm extended, palm open; right arm parallel to the ground, bent, palm at shoulder level |
| Traffic from the left, GO (2-45) | Stand with the left side facing traffic, right arm extended, palm open; left arm parallel to the ground, palm at shoulder level |

## Convoy control (paras 2-46 to 2-51, Figures 2-42 to 2-45)

- Signals are given by the convoy commander out of the **passenger-side window** or relayed to personnel operating a mounted weapons system. **The driver is the last resort** for relaying hand-and-arm signals to the convoy, for safety (para 2-47).
- **Open up / increase distance between vehicles (2-48):** extend the left arm horizontally to the side, palm to the front, then move the arm downward to a 45° angle below horizontal. Repeat several times.
- **Close up / decrease distance between vehicles (2-49):** extend the left arm sideward to the horizontal, palm up, and raise it to the vertical. Repeat several times.
- **Pass and keep going (2-50):** extend the left arm horizontally to the side, palm to the front, and describe large circles to the front by rotating the arm clockwise from the elbow.
- **Move in reverse (2-51):** face the unit being signaled and raise the hand to shoulder level in front of the body, palm to the front; extend the arm forward to its full extent in a pushing motion, keeping the palm to the front.

## Flag signals (paras 2-52 to 2-56, Figures 2-46 to 2-56)

- Each combat vehicle is equipped with a flag set: **one red, one yellow, one green flag**. Flag signals may be given with a single flag or a combination of two or three flags, according to a prearranged code. When understood, flag signals are repeated and executed at once.
- **Uses:** mark vehicle positions (a quartering party marks assembly-area positions); identify disabled vehicles; warn friendly elements of an advancing enemy (an OP flags the platoon to move to its fighting positions); control movement as an extension of hand-and-arm signals when distances between vehicles become too great.
- **Colors used alone:**
  - **Red — DANGER, or ENEMY IN SIGHT.**
  - **Green — ALL CLEAR, READY, or UNDERSTOOD.**
  - **Yellow — DISREGARD, or VEHICLE OUT OF ACTION.**
- In limited visibility, flashlights with colored filters or colored chemical lights may be substituted for flags.
- **Firing-range flags:** signal flags are used on firing ranges for tanks or fighting vehicles to indicate the status of the range and the status of the individual vehicle. **A red flag at the control point indicates that firing may be conducted; a green flag indicates that it may not.**
