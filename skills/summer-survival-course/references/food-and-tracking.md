# Food and Tracking — MWTC Summer Survival (2002), Chapters 8–13

## Traps and snares (Chapter 8)

- **Snaring is the most effective means to take game** in any environment; snares are easier and faster to build than traps. Success = location, presentation, construction.
- **Kill methods:** strangle (snare around the neck), crush (deadfall), spring-pole family.
- **Snare construction:** wire beats rope/cord; a locking device is imperative (secures the snare so it cannot slip open); support with a split stick so it fires correctly (locking device next to the split, or at 12 o'clock for a weighted snare); anchor or attach to a drag; load so it fires quickly; size the loop to the intended animal.
- **Traps:** deadfalls crush the animal; more time and material than snares. Set on game trails, near water and food — location comes from reading sign, not guessing.

## Uses of game (Chapter 9)

- Field dressing and butchering; cooking meats thoroughly (game carries parasites heat kills); specific parts — hides for shelter/cordage, bones for tools, sinew for binding, fat for calories and fire. A kill you cannot preserve is wasted — cook and eat, or smoke and dry, promptly.

## Expedient tools, weapons, equipment (Chapter 10)

- Improvise cutting tools, cordage, containers, weapons from stone, metal, plant fiber, sinew, bark, hide. Design rule: multipurpose and durable, built from what the environment offers. One thing done well beats five done badly (except in the kit, where weight forces multipurpose choices).

## Foraging — plants and insects (Chapter 11)

- **Edibility:** ~300,000 classified plants; a plant must be edible, fairly abundant, and distinguishable from any poisonous relative. Usually one part is edible: stalk, fruit, or nut.
- **Avoid:** mushrooms/fungi (toxic tasteless peptides); umbrella-shaped flowers (poison water hemlock family); legumes — beans, peas, seeds in pods (absorb minerals, e.g. selenium); 3-leafed and whorled-leaf patterns (Lupinas and other poisonous plants); all bulbs as a general rule (tulips, death camas); white and yellow berries (almost all poisonous; ~half of red berries poisonous); milky sap; shiny leaves; skin irritants (poison ivy).
- **Unknown plant prep:** boil one part (leaves, flowers, stems, or roots) in a canteen cup of water before testing. Heat destroys many toxins or they are water-soluble — but some are exceptions. Never prepare plants from the avoid list. Insects are a parallel protein source.

## Survival fishing (Chapter 12)

- Fish are the most reliable mountain animal protein; gear is light and lives in the kit. Improvised lines and poles, trotlines and gill nets (550 cord for net construction), bait from insects and grubs, reading water (where fish hold in current, depth, cover). A set line fishes while you work other priorities.

## Tracking (Chapter 13)

- Read footprints, scat, browse, trails, beds, feeding sign. Three uses: find game (where to set traps/snares), find water (trails converge on it), find people — or avoid them (counter-tracking: know what sign you leave). Set where the sign says animals go, not where you hope they go.
