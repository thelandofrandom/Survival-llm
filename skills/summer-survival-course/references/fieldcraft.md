# Fieldcraft — MWTC Summer Survival (2002), Chapters 5–7

## Core values and mountain leadership (Chapter 5)

- Leadership challenges peculiar to mountain operations: altitude, cold, steep terrain, isolation degrade judgment before they degrade the body.
- Core values applied to the practical problem: organize activity, sustain morale from internal cohesiveness, decide with incomplete information, keep the group on the priorities (shelter, fire, water, signaling in the first 24 hours).

## Signaling and recovery (Chapter 6)

- **Methods:** pyrotechnics (star clusters, smoke grenades — in snow, smoke grenades need flotation), mirror, lights (flashlight, strobe, chemlight), smoke from a signaling fire, ground-to-air panels, improvised signals.
- Signaling is a first-24-hours priority — rescue cannot happen if no one knows where you are.
- **Recovery:** make yourself findable; follow the recovery force's instructions exactly; do not leave a signaled position without a clear trail and message. Covers immediate actions, rally points, and signaling discipline.

## Survival navigation (Chapter 7)

- **Daytime:** sun and shadow as compass (with seasonal/latitude corrections), terrain association, prominent features as handrails and backstops.
- **Nighttime:** stars (including the North Star), moon; move slowly, check often — night movement in mountains is how ankles and lives are lost.
- **Techniques:** dead reckoning with pace count and time; natural direction indicators (wind-shaped trees, snowmelt patterns, animal movement); cardinal rule — know where your shelter is from every point you reach so you can always get back. Second-24-hours trap work doubles as navigation training.
