# Medicine, Weather, Evasion — MWTC Summer Survival (2002), Chapters 14–16 and Appendices

## Survival medicine (Chapter 14)

- **Five ways the body loses heat:** radiation (nude man loses ~60% of total body heat by radiation); conduction (contact with cold ground/snow/rock — insulate with mat or pack); convection (moving air or water never equalizes, loss continues); evaporation (1 quart of sweat in an hour of hard work carries away ~600 calories); respiration (inhaled air warmed and saturated, then exhaled — convection + evaporation inside the body).
- **Responses:** to heat — vessels dilate, then sweating. To cold — vessels constrict (blood to core: brain, heart, lungs, liver, kidneys; fingers/toes go cold), then shivering.
- **Hypothermia:** core temperature 95°F or less; the number-one killer in a survival situation; most cases occur at 30–50°F, not extreme cold. Contributing factors: ambient temperature, wind chill (improperly clothed only), wet clothing, cold-water immersion, improper clothing, exhaustion, alcohol/nicotine/drugs, injuries (immobility, major bleeding, major burns, head trauma). Number-one sign: altered mental status — confusion, slurred speech, strange behavior, irritability, impaired judgment. Worsening: loss of consciousness; severe victims may appear dead. Prevention: stay dry, fed, out of the wind; buddy system checks. Treatment: rewarm gently and gradually; never give alcohol; handle gently (a cold heart is irritable and can arrest).
- **Frostbite:** actual freezing of tissues; entirely preventable — keep extremities warm, dry, moving; no direct high heat on frozen tissue. Chapter also covers heat injuries and dehydration (see Chapter 3). For current clinical detail, defer to the TCCC/first-aid skills.

## Mountain weather (Chapter 15)

- Pressure: read the trend, not the absolute value — falling pressure means deteriorating weather. Mountain effects: orographic lift, valley winds, afternoon thunderstorms. Plan movement and shelter around the afternoon thunderstorm pattern; never be on high ground or in a drainage when weather turns.
