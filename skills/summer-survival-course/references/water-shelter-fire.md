# Water, Shelter, Fire — MWTC Summer Survival (2002), Chapters 3–4

## Water (Chapter 3)

- **Over-drink method:** thirst is not a reliable gauge; drink plenty whenever water is available, particularly when eating. Needs rise with fever, fear/anxiety, evaporation (poor shelter, bad clothing), rationing itself, and overwork.
- **Dehydration:** 5% body-fluid loss — thirst, irritability, nausea, weakness; 10% — dizziness, headache, inability to walk, tingling limbs; 15% — dim vision, painful urination, swollen tongue, deafness, numb skin; over 15% — can be fatal.
- **Incidental water:** dew (rags/tufts of grass tied around ankles, walk dewy grass before sunrise, wring out, repeat); rainfall (collect in a clean container — generally safe without disinfecting; inverted poncho works; always be ready to collect at a moment's notice).
- **Hazardous fluids — do not drink:** seawater (salt concentration forces the body to spend fluid eliminating it; kidneys fail); alcohol (dehydrates, clouds judgment; super-cooled liquid can frostbite the throat); blood (salty, a food — costs fluid to digest); urine (waste returned to the body, costs more fluid to process).
- **Water quality:** pathogens are the concern — virus, cysts, bacteria, parasites. Some resist chemicals, some pass improvised filters, some survive extreme cold; pathogens generally do not live in snow and ice. Three safety levels; disinfection is most desired — boil in the metal container where possible.

## Shelter and fire (Chapter 4)

- **Site:** near firewood, water, signaling position; ventilated (unventilated shelters accumulate carbon monoxide and smoke); protected from wind, falling limbs, runoff.
- **Shelters:** poncho shelter (easiest — cord + water-repellent material); lean-to (forested areas, limited cordage); double lean-to (2–5 people); hollow logs (cleaned/dug out, enhanced with ponchos/tarps). Insulate from the ground — conduction steals heat through contact with cold ground, snow, or rock; a mat or pack is survival, not comfort.
- **Fire lays:** fire pit and Dakota hole. The Dakota hole (hole + separate air-draft tunnel) is the tactical lay — less smoke, less light, less spark, concentrated heat for cooking. No fire is 100% tactical.
- **Fire building:** double handful of tinder + an extra double handful stored dry for morning (dew moistens tinder); tinder fine and dry, sticks no larger than a toothpick's diameter; kindling ignited by tinder — softwoods burn hot and fast, hardwoods slower with less smoke and a good coal bed. Signaling fires are built to make smoke; cooking/heating fires are not.
- **Ignition:** matches, lighter, flint and steel (sparks onto prepared tinder; some with magnesium), magnifying glass on bright sunny days, bow drill and fire plow (friction). Bow-drill troubleshooting: no smoke — drill and fire board same wood or wrong wood; smoke but no ember — U-shaped notch not cut into the center of the depression.
