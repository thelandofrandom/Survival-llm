# Medical, Water, Food, Induced Conditions — FM 21-76-1

## Medical Self-Aid (Chapter V)

- Assess medical condition; treat as necessary.
- Priorities: airway and breathing → severe bleeding (direct pressure) → shock (warm, flat, legs elevated).
- Treat yourself first — you cannot help anyone else if incapacitated.
- Keep wounds clean and covered; improvise splints and dressings.
- **Prevention is the treatment:** stay hydrated, warm and dry, feet sound, eat what you can, avoid injury.
- Treat diarrhea and dysentery early — they accelerate dehydration and are among the most dangerous field illnesses. Boil all water.

## Personal Protection (Chapter VI)

- Establish priorities and work them in order.
- Shelter, clothing, fire: protect against cold, heat, sun, wind, rain, insects.
- Improvise goggles against snow blindness and dust (slit-goggles from any opaque material).
- Protect eyes, head, and feet first — feet carry you out.
- Cold: layer, stay dry, insulate from the ground. Heat: shade by day, move at night, cover the body, drink on schedule. Sun: cloth, mud, or charcoal on exposed skin.
- Insects: clothing as a barrier, smoke and mud as improvised repellent, keep sleeping areas clear of standing water; check daily for ticks and leeches.

## Water (Chapter VII)

- **Need:** 2–3 liters/day at moderate temperature and exertion; more in heat, cold, activity, illness.
- **Sources:** animal trails downhill; birds at dawn and dusk; green vegetation; insects near water. Rain, dew, transpiration bags, solar stills, green bamboo, vines (cut high first, then low — avoid milky or bitter sap), banana stumps.
- **Purification:** tablets (follow directions); 5 drops 2% iodine per quart clear water, 10 drops if cloudy or cold, wait 30 minutes; boiling — rolling boil 1 minute at sea level, longer at altitude. Filter muddy water first (settling or sand/charcoal/cloth) — filtration only clears, it does not purify.
- Never drink urine, alcohol, or seawater.

## Food (Chapter VIII)

**Snares** (work while unattended):
- Place on trails to water, feeding, and bedding areas, and at the mouth of dens.
- Use materials that will not break under strain.
- With wire, use a figure-8 (locking) loop — once tightened it locks, preventing reopening and escape.
- Check regularly; a dead animal left too long is lost to scavengers and spoilage.

**Fishing:** line and hooks from the kit, improvised hooks, traps, spears.

**Plant edibility test:**
- Only test abundant plants — each part (roots, leaves, stems, bark) takes **more than 24 hours** to test.
- Test only **one part of one plant** at a time.
- Even after testing safe, eat in moderation — large portions on an empty stomach may cause diarrhea, nausea, or cramps.
- **Avoid:** milky sap (dandelion has milky sap but is safe and easily recognizable); spines, fine hairs, thorns (prickly pear and thistles are exceptions; bracken fern fiddleheads also violate this guideline); mushrooms and fungus; umbrella-shaped flowers (hemlock).
- Steps: skin contact → lip → tongue → chew-and-spit → small swallow (wait 8 hours, eat nothing else) → measured portion (wait 8+ hours). Any ill effects at any step: stop, do not eat that plant.

## Induced Conditions (Chapter IX)

**Nuclear:**
- Radiation protection depends on **time of exposure, distance from the source, and shielding**.
- FIND PROTECTIVE SHELTER IMMEDIATELY. Gather survival equipment (time permitting). Avoid detection and capture.
- Seek existing shelter that may be improved; if none, dig a trench or foxhole.
- The first hours after a detonation are the most dangerous — stay sheltered until the fallout threat passes or movement is ordered.

**Biological:**
- Avoid areas of obvious contamination, dead animals, unusual disease.
- Protect airway and skin; wash with water and soap when available; boil all water; cook all food thoroughly.
- Report symptoms early — early treatment is the only treatment that works reliably.

**Chemical:**
- Move upwind and uphill; avoid low ground and depressions where agents settle.
- Remove contaminated clothing; wash exposed skin with water.
- Do not eat, drink, or smoke in a contaminated area.
- Use any available protective equipment; seek decontamination at the earliest opportunity.
