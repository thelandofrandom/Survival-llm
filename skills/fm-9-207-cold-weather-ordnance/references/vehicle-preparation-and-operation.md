# Vehicle Preparation and Operation — FM 9-207

## Preparation for Operation

**Special considerations.** Before operating vehicles, crews should review the appropriate operator manuals for cold weather operations. These manuals all include a section subtitled "Operations Under Unusual Conditions." Operators must know other basic skills, such as working with tire chains and slave-starting. Soldiers must maintain equipment in the best mechanical condition to withstand the added difficulties and prevent failures during sub-zero operations. Commanders must place special emphasis on maintenance inspections. Placing equipment in proper mechanical condition before the onset of cold weather requires time for necessary and careful disassembly, repair, cleaning, and reassembly. Low temperatures must be anticipated far enough in advance to permit completion of winterization. Refer to pertinent operator and unit maintenance technical manuals (TMs) for operation, lubrication, preventive maintenance checks and services (PMCS), and maintenance under unusual conditions. Operators must be very cautious when using equipment that has been inactive for a long time. For example, if lubricants congeal in various components, parts can fail.

**Before-operations checks.** Chapter 2, Section I gives the before-operations checks for chassis and body components — the checks that must be performed before the vehicle is started, including inspection of tires (see the tire pressure guidance in the SKILL.md), hoses, belts, and body closures, and verification that winterization equipment is installed and working. Perform the checks in accordance with the operator's TM; the manual's checklists are the minimum, not the whole of the TM's PMCS.

## Power Train

**Engines.** Chapter 2, Section II covers engine preparation — oil (see the OEA guidance in the SKILL.md), coolant and antifreeze (arctic-type antifreeze protects to −90 °F; do not add water or any other type of antifreeze), fuel (arctic fuels for proper atomization), and the engine's cold-weather starting aids. An engine that fails to reach normal operating temperature or overheats must be reported to maintenance personnel for correction — failure to do so may result in serious damage to the engine.

**Transmissions, transfer cases, differentials, and final drives.** Chapter 2, Section II covers preparation of transmissions, transfer cases, differentials, and final drives — the correct seasonal lubricants per the lubrication order (LO), and the warning that if vehicles come into theater with temperate-grade automatic transmission fluids and become cold-soaked, seals are likely to blow when transmissions are engaged. When authorized, stationary engagement of hydromechanical transmissions can be used to facilitate engine warming.

**Cooling systems.** Chapter 2, Section II covers cooling system preparation — the arctic-type antifreeze (protects to −90 °F; do not add water or any other type of antifreeze), the requirement that antifreeze compounds be kept clean (use only containers and water that are free from dirt, rust, and oil), and the inspection of hoses, belts, and the radiator for cold damage. Chapter 3, Section VII covers cooling system starting considerations and maintenance during operation.

**Fuel systems.** Chapter 2, Section II covers fuel system preparation — arctic fuels, fuel filters, and the grounding requirement: fuel flowing over a surface generates static, which can cause a spark unless means are provided to ground the electricity. A metallic contact between the dispensing tank and the container being filled must be provided to ensure an effective ground. This hazard is particularly great in cold, dry air. When vehicles, generators, and POL containers are brought into warm storage from the cold, the fuel tanks/containers should only be filled **three quarters full** — expansion of the cold POL products could cause spillage and create a serious fire hazard. Chapter 3, Section VI covers fuel storage and maintenance during operation.

**Universal and slip joints.** Chapter 2, Section II covers universal and slip joints — lubrication per the LO and inspection for cold damage before operation.

## Electrical System

**Special concerns.** Chapter 2, Section III covers the electrical system's special concerns in the cold — battery capacity loss (see the SKILL.md battery section), thickened lubricants in starter motors and generators, and the brittleness of wiring and connectors. **System preparations:** prepare the electrical system per the operator's TM — batteries fully charged (recharge if specific gravity is less than 1.1250), battery preheater used if equipped, terminals clean and tight, and wiring inspected for cracks in insulation. **Water should never be added to a cold battery; add it when the battery is warm and charging, or it will not mix — freezing could result due to reduced specific gravity.** Add water to a battery only when the vehicle is to be immediately operated for **30 minutes or more**.

## Auxiliary Equipment

**Air compressors.** Chapter 2, Section IV covers air compressors — drain water from air filters and reservoirs after every **eight hours** of continuous operation. Water left in the system freezes and blocks the air supply.

**Auxiliary engines and generators.** Chapter 2, Section IV covers auxiliary engines and generators — winterize and inspect them as thoroughly as the main engine; generators develop a higher failure rate at sub-zero temperatures, attributable to outdated LOs, exposed batteries, and other causes.

**Power takeoff assemblies.** Chapter 2, Section IV and Chapter 3, Section VIII cover power takeoff assemblies — lubrication per the LO and inspection before and during operation.

## Operating and Idling

Operation of the main engine or auxiliary generator engine when a vehicle is stationary exposes the crew to possible carbon monoxide poisoning. The possibility is greatly increased when hatch doors are closed. To minimize this hazard, position the vehicle wherever possible so that wind will carry fumes away from the crew compartment; then turn on the turret ventilation blower. Be sure engine compartment bulkhead doors are secured before operating engines. **When there is no outside air movement, personnel must dismount for at least 10 minutes each hour** (tactical situation permitting). Passenger and crew compartments of wheeled and tracked carriers must be inspected and tested in accordance with **Technical Bulletin (TB) MED 269** at regular intervals. An engine that fails to reach normal operating temperature or overheats must be reported to maintenance personnel for correction.

## Starting Procedures

**Starting without assistance.** Chapter 3, Section I gives the cold-starting procedures for engines that will start on their own power — follow the operator's TM, allowing for reduced battery output (at 15 °F only 50 percent of normal current; at −30 °F only a little over 10 percent; at −40 °F and below practically zero) and thickened lubricants.

**Ether.** **Ether should be injected into the engine air intake system only while the engine is being turned over. Otherwise, flashback may occur.** Inject only during cranking, never into a stopped engine.

**Using auxiliary power (slave) receptacle.** Ensure that the slave cable is connected **positive to positive and negative to negative**. Batteries are checked prior to slave starting. If the dead vehicle has a master switch, it **must be off while connecting the slave cable**. See the manual's Figure 1-6 for the proper jump-starting procedure.

**Towing to start engine.** **Towing a vehicle to start its engine will only be done in an extreme emergency.** Use a tow bar when towing. Use tow chains only as a last resort. The operator's manual must be checked prior to tow-starting any vehicle. Some vehicles cannot be started by towing; if attempts are made to tow-start some equipment, severe transmission damage may result. **Vehicles should never be started by pushing.**

## Operating and Maintenance Procedures During Operation

**Chassis and body components.** Chapter 3, Section II covers operational considerations for chassis and body components — tires (inflate 10 PSI over normal for winter operations; a tire at 40 PSI indoors drops to 25 PSI at −50 °F outside), suspension, and body closures in the cold.

**Power train.** Chapter 3, Section III covers operational considerations for the power train during operation — monitor temperatures, listen for abnormal noises from cold-stiffened components, and report any engine that fails to reach normal operating temperature or overheats.

**Engine lubrication system.** Chapter 3, Section IV covers the engine lubrication system during operation and its maintenance — **oil consumption is much higher when using OEA; engines may run out of oil before the next maintenance check. Check frequently.** Incomplete combustion of fuels in a cold engine, and piston rings not sealing tightly until the engine reaches operating temperatures, causes rapid fuel dilution of the oil. During prolonged periods of extreme cold weather, it may be necessary to take oil samples more frequently for equipment registered in the Army Oil Analysis Program (AOAP).

**Electrical system.** Chapter 3, Section V covers the electrical system during operation — starting procedures, during operation, and maintenance. Keep batteries warm and charged; test state of charge every three days in constant cold weather.

**Fuel system.** Chapter 3, Section VI covers the fuel system — fuel storage and maintenance. Keep fuel containers grounded during dispensing; fill tanks only three quarters full when bringing vehicles, generators, and POL containers into warm storage from the cold.

**Cooling system.** Chapter 3, Section VII covers the cooling system — starting considerations and maintenance. Arctic-type antifreeze protects to −90 °F; do not add water or any other type of antifreeze.

**Auxiliary equipment.** Chapter 3, Section VIII covers auxiliary equipment — operational considerations and power takeoff assemblies. Drain water from air filters and reservoirs after every eight hours of continuous operation.

Chapter 2 covers chassis and body components, the power train (engines; transmissions, transfer cases, differentials, and final drives; cooling systems; fuel systems; universal and slip joints), the electrical system, and auxiliary equipment (air compressors, auxiliary engines and generators, power takeoff assemblies).

Chapter 3, Sections II–VIII cover operating and maintenance procedures for chassis and body components, the power train, the engine lubrication system, the electrical system (starting procedures, during operation, maintenance), the fuel system (fuel storage and maintenance), the cooling system (starting considerations and maintenance), and auxiliary equipment including power takeoff assemblies.

Source: FM 9-207/TO 36-1-40, Ch. 2 (Preparation for Operation) and Ch. 3 (Operating and Maintenance Procedures), Headquarters, Department of the Army and Department of the Air Force, 20 March 1998.
