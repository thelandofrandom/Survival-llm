# Task Force Operations — JP 3-50.21

Distilled from JP 3-50.21, Chapters II and V and Appendixes A–H (23 March 1998). Paraphrase with short quotes only; full text in `full-text.md`.

## The CSAR task force (CSARTF)

- A tailored package built for one mission: recovery aircraft, escort, command and control, suppression, and support, sized to the threat (low/medium/high) and the objective area.
- Two jobs at once: recover the isolated person and protect the recovery force while doing it.
- Typical compositions are in Figure II-5; the catalog of CSAR-capable assets is in Appendix G.
- Size and complexity scale with the mission: a low-threat recovery may be a single helicopter with minimal escort; a high-threat recovery is a full task force.

## Airborne mission commander (AMC)

- The airborne commander of the CSAR effort.
- Controls the task force in the air; coordinates all participating aircraft; maintains communications with the JSRC/RCC and the OSC; manages altitude deconfliction and airspace; makes real-time decisions on search, threat response, and abort.
- Positioned on airborne alert as close to hostile areas as possible; orbit in FRAGOs or SPINS; may move the orbit as the situation requires.
- Normally high altitude (threat dictates); needs radio LOS with the OSC and HF or SATCOM with the JSRC.
- Must be proactive and aggressive in taking control of the situation; keeps OSC/CSARTF/RCC informed of threats, aborts, fades, and EW information.
- AMC-capable aircraft must be prepared to assume AMC duties in a combat environment.
- **Appendix A — AMC CSAR Checklist:** notification, assessment, planning, launch, search control, recovery control, egress, conclusion.

## Rescue combat air patrol (RESCAP)

- Fighter aircraft assigned to protect the recovery force and the isolated person from air and surface threats.
- Holds air superiority over the objective area for the duration of the recovery; engages threats; deters enemy interference.

## Forward air controller, airborne (FAC(A))

- Controls close air support and strike aircraft supporting the recovery.
- Marks targets and coordinates fires so suppression lands where it helps — and nowhere near the survivor or the helicopters.

## On-scene commander (OSC)

- The commander at the recovery scene — often the first aircraft or ground element to arrive; the role can pass from a witness aircrew to the arriving task force's designated OSC.
- Locates and authenticates the survivor; controls aircraft in the objective area; coordinates the pickup; reports to the AMC.
- **Appendix B — OSC Checklist:** locate, authenticate, control, recover, report.

## Helicopter recovery force

- The helicopters that make the pickup, with crews, pararescuemen or rescue specialists, and equipment (hoists, ropes, litters, medical gear).
- Procedures: approach, hover, hoist or landing pickup, casualty handling, egress — all under the OSC's direction and the AMC's control.

## RESCORT — rescue escort

- Escort specifically for the recovery aircraft: flies with, ahead of, or above the helicopters to suppress threats en route and in the objective area.
- **Types:** fixed-wing RESCORT; helicopter gunship RESCORT; helicopter RESCORT tactics.
- **Hover cover:** an escort aircraft holds overhead while the recovery helicopter hovers for the pickup — the few minutes of maximum vulnerability.
- **Night vision goggle/device escort:** RESCORT adapted to night operations.
- Concepts cover positioning, weapons employment, communications, and handoff between en route escort and objective-area cover.

## Organization and planning (Chapter II)

- **JSRC:** responsibilities and functions in peacetime (Figure II-1: SOPs, plans, training) vs. combat (Figure II-2: threat matrices, SPINS, incident monitoring, coordination).
- **Staffing:** trained, experienced personnel who know CSAR plans and each component's force capabilities.
- **Communications:** interoperable facilities (Figure II-4); see Appendix C checklist (frequencies, call signs, authentication procedures, reporting formats, backup communications).
- **Documentation:** CSAR appendix to Annex C, SPINS, ISOPREPs, EPAs, incident files.
- **Physical requirements:** communications, plotting/display, space for continuous operations.
- **Logistics planning:** sustainment of the JSRC and deployed CSAR forces.
- **Coordination:** JSRC ↔ RCCs ↔ Joint Air Operations Center ↔ component staffs ↔ adjacent JSRCs.
