# Survival Medicine

Source: ATP 3-50.21, Chapter 2 (Survival Medicine).

## Chapter outline

- Survival medicine vs. traditional medicine — remote, improvised care; delayed evacuation
- Four fundamentals: prevention, recognition, mitigation, treatment
- Lifesaving steps: airway, bleeding, shock, fractures, burns
- Survival medicine properties (medicinal plants)
- Poisonous plants

## Lifesaving steps — in order

1. **Airway and breathing.** Open the airway. Use the jaw-thrust method if a neck injury is suspected; head-tilt/chin-lift otherwise. Clear the mouth of debris, blood, or vomit. If breathing has stopped, begin rescue breathing.
2. **Bleeding.** Direct pressure first; add a pressure dressing; use pressure points (brachial in the upper arm, femoral in the groin) if pressure fails. A tourniquet is a last resort for an extremity: band at least 1.5 inches wide, windlass tightened until bleeding stops, time of application marked on the casualty, never loosened in the field.
3. **Shock.** Signs: weak rapid pulse, cool clammy skin, pale or gray skin, thirst, nausea, restlessness, shallow breathing. Treatment: lay the casualty flat, elevate legs 6–12 inches unless head, chest, or abdominal injury forbids it; keep warm with insulation from the ground; loosen tight clothing; give nothing by mouth if surgery may follow.
4. **Fractures.** Splint as found — do not straighten. Immobilize the joint above and below the break. Pad the splint. Check circulation beyond the splint (color, warmth, feeling) after application.
5. **Burns.** Cool with clean water; cover with a clean dry dressing; do not break blisters; treat for shock; a burn victim may lose up to 5 quarts of water per day — fluids matter as much as the dressing.

## Heat and cold injuries

| Injury | Signs | Field treatment |
|---|---|---|
| Heat cramps | Muscle cramps, sweating | Rest in shade, sip water with salt |
| Heat exhaustion | Heavy sweating, weakness, nausea, headache | Rest in shade, cool with water and airflow, sip fluids |
| Heatstroke (emergency) | Hot dry skin, confusion, loss of consciousness | Cool immediately — water, shade, airflow, fanning; evacuate |
| Hypothermia | Shivering, then confusion, clumsiness, slurred speech; shivering eventually stops | Rewarm gradually — insulation, warm sweet liquids, body heat; never alcohol; never rub frostbitten tissue |
| Frostbite | Pale, waxy, numb skin | Rewarm in warm (not hot) water only if refreezing is impossible; do not rub; protect from further cold |

## Medicinal plants — examples from the manual

- **Willow bark** — contains salicin; chew bark or brew as tea for pain and fever.
- **Garlic, onion** — antiseptic; apply crushed to wounds.
- **Astringent plants (oak bark, blackberry root)** — teas for diarrhea.
- Rules: positively identify before use; test a small amount first; never use an unknown plant as medicine.

## Poisonous plants — danger signs

Milky sap; almond scent; spines or fine hairs; umbrella-shaped flower clusters; beans or bulbs; seeds inside pods. **Avoid all mushrooms.** If poisoning is suspected: induce vomiting only if the casualty is conscious and it is within the first hour (and the plant is not corrosive); keep the casualty warm and still; save a sample of the plant for identification.

## Prevention checklist (daily)

- Drink on schedule, not just when thirsty — thirst is late and weakens in cold.
- Keep feet dry; change socks; treat hot spots before they become blisters.
- Eat what you can; do not fast through a survival situation.
- Protect skin from sun, wind, insects, and cold.
- Rest on a schedule; exhaustion causes the mistakes that cause injuries.
