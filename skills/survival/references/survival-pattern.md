## The SURVIVAL acronym (Figure 1-1)

| Letter | Meaning | In practice |
|---|---|---|
| S | Size up the situation | Surroundings, physical condition, equipment, time, weather |
| U | Undue haste makes waste | Stop, think, then act; panic burns energy and creates errors |
| R | Remember where you are | Orient yourself; know where safety, water, and danger lie |
| V | Vanquish fear and panic | Fear is normal; training and action are the antidote |
| I | Improvise | Every object has a second use |
| V | Value living | The will to live is a decision, renewed daily |
| A | Act like the natives | Local people have already solved this environment |
| L | Learn basic skills | Skills practiced in training become confidence in isolation |

## Stressors and reactions

The body responds to a stressor with fight, flight, or freeze: stored fuel is released, breathing rate rises, muscles tense. These reactions cannot be sustained long. Signs of distress: difficulty making decisions, forgetfulness, low energy, constant worrying, carelessness, trouble getting along with others, withdrawal, angry outbursts, hiding from responsibilities, thoughts of death or suicide.

Common survival reactions: fear, anxiety, anger, frustration, guilt, depression, loneliness. Countermeasures: establish a routine, set small achievable goals, keep busy with survival tasks, maintain a realistic recovery plan, conserve energy, and rest on a schedule.

## The survival pattern — priority order

1. **Survival medicine** — top priority in every environment.
2. **Protection** — fire, shelter, clothing (order shifts: in cold, fire first; in heat, shade and water first).
3. **Sustenance** — water, then food.
4. **Navigation** — movement toward recovery once the above are handled.

## Stay or move — decision checklist

Stay when: rescuers know your location or last known position; you are near a route, crash site, or landmark; you are injured; moving would consume more than it gains; water/shelter/food are available where you are.

Move when: the location is dangerous (flooding, enemy, fire); water, food, or shelter cannot be found locally; you know a definite route to safety; staying guarantees deterioration.

Before moving: leave a record of direction and intent (note, stone arrow, marked trail); mark your trail as you go; travel in daylight when possible; rest on a schedule.
