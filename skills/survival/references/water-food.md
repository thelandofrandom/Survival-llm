# Water — Requirements, Sources, Collection, Purification

Source: ATP 3-50.21, Chapter 3 (Water).

## Chapter outline

- 3-1–3-4: Hydration considerations — requirements, losses, dehydration
- 3-5 onward: Water sources and indicators; procuring water; purification

## Daily water requirements

| Condition | Requirement |
|---|---|
| 68°F, limited activity | 2–3 quarts/day |
| Normal daily losses | ~1.4 qt urine, ~1.0 qt sweat, ~0.2 qt fecal |
| High heat (sweat loss) | Up to 3.5 quarts per hour |
| Burn victim | Up to 5 quarts/day additional loss |
| Illness (vomiting/diarrhea) | Losses increase sharply — fluids are the treatment |

Thirst is a late indicator. In cold weather the thirst response weakens, so cold-weather dehydration is common. Drink on a schedule.

## Rules

- Treat **all** surface water as contaminated (possible exception: very high mountain streams/springs in uninhabited areas).
- Never drink urine or alcohol.
- Never drink stagnant water from natural depressions.

## Water indicators

- Animal trails converging downhill; birds circling water at dawn and dusk.
- Green vegetation in dry country; swarms of insects (bees, mosquitoes stay near water).
- Damp ground, seeps at the base of cliffs, and dry stream beds (dig at the outside of a bend).

## Collection methods

| Method | Notes |
|---|---|
| Rain catcher / bamboo catcher | Set out any container, poncho, or broad leaves before rain |
| Transpiration bag | Clear plastic bag tied over a leafy branch in sun; collects evaporated water |
| Solar still | Pit with container, clear plastic cover weighted with a stone; low yield — build several |
| Dew collection | Wipe dew from grass/metal with cloth at dawn, wring into container |
| Green bamboo | Cut a section; water collects in the joints |
| Vines | Cut high first, then low; let drip into container; reject milky or bitter sap |
| Banana stump | Cut the trunk, hollow a bowl; sap collects overnight |
| Condensation trap | Plastic over vegetation in a pit |

Always secure a container first — improvised from plastic bags or any non-porous material.

## Purification table

| Method | Procedure | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Boiling (safest) | Rolling boil **1 minute** at sea level; **3 minutes above 8,000 ft** | Kills all waterborne pathogens including Cryptosporidium |
| Iodine, 2% tincture | **5 drops per quart**; 10 drops if cloudy or cold; wait 30 min | 1 oz bottle is standard kit item |
| Povidone iodine, 10% | **2 drops per quart** | Civilian 2% solution needs 10 drops per quart |
| Bleach (sodium hypochlorite) | Per label/tablet directions | Does not reliably kill Cryptosporidium |
| Filtration | Cloth/sand filter first to remove sediment | Clear water purifies far more reliably; filter, then purify |

## Dehydration — signs and response

Early: dark urine, headache, fatigue, dizziness. Late: confusion, rapid pulse, no urine, sunken eyes. Response: rest in shade, sip water steadily (small amounts, often), add salt if available. Do not gulp large volumes at once if vomiting.
# Food — Edibility, Plants, Traps, Fishing

Source: ATP 3-50.21, Chapter 4 (Food).

## Chapter outline

- Food considerations; basic cooking and preservation; preserving food
- Plants; most common global plants to procure
- Killing devices (snares, deadfalls, traps); butchering mammals; preparing fish
- Insects and other food sources

## Priorities

Water matters more than food for the first days. Once water is secured: plants first (lowest energy cost and risk), then traps and fishing (passive — they work while you do other tasks), then active hunting (highest energy cost). Preserve what you catch: smoke, dry, or salt meat; cook everything thoroughly.

## Universal Edibility Test — 24 hours, one plant at a time

1. **Inspect.** Reject plants with milky sap, almond smell, spines or fine hairs, umbrella-shaped flower clusters, beans, bulbs, or seeds in pods. Avoid all mushrooms.
2. **Smell.** Crush a small part; reject if it smells of almonds or peaches.
3. **Skin test.** Rub on the inside of the elbow/wrist; wait 15 minutes; reject if it itches, burns, or reddens.
4. **Lip test.** Touch to the lips; wait 15 minutes.
5. **Tongue test.** Hold a small piece on the tongue; wait 15 minutes; do not swallow.
6. **Chew and spit.** Chew a small piece, spit out; wait 15 minutes.
7. **Swallow a small amount; wait 8 hours; eat nothing else.**
8. **Eat a larger portion; wait another 8 hours** before declaring it safe.

If any step produces discomfort, discard the plant and do not test another plant that day.

## Common global edible plants (from the manual)

| Plant | Notes |
|---|---|
| Cattail | Roots, shoots, and pollen heads; starch-rich root |
| Dandelion | Leaves, roots, flowers; cook leaves to reduce bitterness |
| Plantain | Leaves and seeds; common in temperate zones |
| Wild onion/garlic | Positive ID by smell is essential; avoid look-alikes without onion scent |
| Clover | Leaves and flowers; eat cooked |
| Acorns | Leach in water to remove bitter tannins before eating; then roast or grind |
| Nettle | Cook to remove sting; nutritious green |

General rules: cook leaves to reduce bitterness and toxins; eat roots and tubers cooked; never eat a large quantity of any new plant at once.

## Traps and snares

| Device | Use |
|---|---|
| Simple loop snare | Game trails, runways |
| Apache foot snare | Trail trigger that snares the foot |
| Drag noose | Allows animal to move a short distance, tiring itself |
| Twitch-up snare | Spring pole lifts the animal off the ground |
| Treadle spring snare | Pressure plate trigger |
| Deadfall (figure-4, Paiute) | Crushing weight over bait |
| Bow trap | Spring-driven striker |
| Bottle trap / fish trap | Funnel design; fish enter, cannot exit |
| Gill net / mist net | Passive capture of fish or birds |

Set snares on game trails, at burrow entrances, and near water. Check at dawn and dusk. Camouflage with local materials; avoid leaving human scent (handle with gloves or smoke). Set many — a single snare is a lottery ticket, not a plan.

## Fishing

Improvise hooks from bone, thorn, or bent wire; make gill nets and bottle traps; build weirs across narrow streams. Fish are most active at dawn and dusk. Butchering: bleed, skin, gut, and cook promptly; save fat; boil bones for broth; never eat raw freshwater fish or meat.

## Insects and other protein

Grasshoppers, crickets, grubs, and ants are safe when cooked and rich in protein. Remove legs, wings, and stingers first. Avoid brightly colored insects, hairy caterpillars, and anything that stings or bites.

## Cooking and preservation

- Boiling is the safest cooking method; it also purifies the water used.
- Roast on a spit or on hot rocks; bake in a pit oven (hot stones, food wrapped in leaves, covered with earth).
- Preserve meat by smoking (thin strips over a low smoky fire), drying in sun and wind, or salting.
- Cook all meat thoroughly; never eat raw freshwater fish, pork, or bear.
