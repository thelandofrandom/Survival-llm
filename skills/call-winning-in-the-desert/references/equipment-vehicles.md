# Equipment, Vehicles, and Saudi Lessons — CALL: Winning in the Desert (1990)

Distilled from CALL: *Winning in the Desert*, DTIC ADA446544, August 1990 — the environmental effects on equipment chapter and the Desert Operations: Saudi Arabia Lessons Learned chapter.

## Environmental Effects on Equipment

**Sand and dust are the greatest danger to equipment.** Use minimum lubrication — weapons may be better dry; use muzzle covers; protect optics from pitting; clean air filters daily (a filter is useless in 3 days); change oil about twice as often, 20W-50.

**Batteries:** specific gravity 1.200–1.225, electrolyte 1.2085–1.2185 (TM 9-6140-100-12).

**Cooling:** a gauge reading 10–20° hotter than normal is expected; 180° is average, 200° means monitor; if overheating, shift to neutral and run at ~1,200 RPM; a drip per second is 7 gallons in 24 hours; don't remove hood side panels while running.

**Static electricity:** ground tankers and vehicles; a helicopter hook must touch ground first.

**Expansion:** tires inflated at night may burst by day; don't overfill fuel tanks.

## Desert Operations: Saudi Arabia Lessons Learned (SANG, 1982)

From the Saudi Arabian National Guard program, 1982:

- **Tires:** flats ran 40% per 3-day exercise. Recover a stuck vehicle by letting out about a quarter of the air, sand ladders, the rocking method — a winch is best. Driver skill matters more than vehicle type.
- **Vehicle survival kit:** 13 items, including 5 gallons of water per occupant.
- **Observation:** from a 300-meter hill you can see 20–30 km but miss intermediate ridges — recon routes; few hard roads exist.
- **TOW:** the dust signature is not serious.
- **Repair parts:** stock +50%.
- **Batteries:** go dead after 5–10 days idle.
- **Cooling:** systems calcify; clean filters daily.
- **Weapons lubrication debate:** U.S. standard in storage, light in use.
- **WD-1 wire** dry-rots — use shielded cable.
- **Eye protection:** wear it.
- **Liquids:** force them — Arabs drink sweet hot tea.
- **Bad water** causes gallstones and urinary disorders.
- **Morale:** fresh fruit and ice cream are morale items.
- **Jet lag** takes 2–3 days — ideally 1 day per time zone.
- **Respiratory disorders** are common.
- **Water:** nonpotable saline water is a theater problem; a unit needs **at least 7 gallons of water per man per 24 hours**.
- **Fuel contamination:** Petromin is the only flushing facility; POL storage has no sampling, gauging, or strapping.
- **Ammo:** consumption runs up and ballistics are erratic in heat — store ammo double-shaded and about 1 meter below the floor to keep it under 100°F.
- **Desert fog** brings zero visibility; optics condense near the Gulf and Red Sea.
- **Dispersion:** bunching is visible 10 km from the air.
