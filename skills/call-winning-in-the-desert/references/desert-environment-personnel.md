# Desert Environment and Personnel — CALL: Winning in the Desert (1990)

Distilled from CALL: *Winning in the Desert*, DTIC ADA446544, August 1990 — the Saudi Arabia geography and climate chapter, the desert environment chapter, and the environmental effects on personnel chapter.

## Geography and Climate

**Saudi Arabia** covers about 2,217,949 sq km. Three great deserts dominate: the Nafud in the north, the Ad Dahna in the center, and the Rub Al Khali (the Empty Quarter) in the south, over 500,000 sq km. The Hijaz mountains along the west run 2,000–6,000 ft; the Asir range in the southwest reaches 9,000 ft. Temperatures reach 130°F inland, with day-night swings up to 70°F. The highest recorded desert temperature is 136°F; tank interiors reached ~160°F in the Sahara in World War II.

**Iraq** covers about 435,000 sq km in four regions, with ~90% of its rain falling November–April.

## Desert Types and Driving

Three desert types: mountain, rocky plateau, and sandy dune. Dunes run over 1,000 ft high and 10–15 miles long. Driving: night and early morning are best; sand build-up throws tracks off; recon a dune crossing on foot first; watch for crust that breaks under weight, thorn punctures, and rock wear on tires and undercarriages.

**Lost or stranded:** use the buddy system — never fewer than two vehicles; stay with the vehicle; a raised hood or trunk means help is needed; a white cloth on the antenna; signal with a mirror or oil smoke; make a shadow-stick compass (15 minutes to read direction).

## Water

Water is ~75% of body fluid. A 2-quart loss (2.5% of body weight) costs ~25% of efficiency; a 15% loss is usually fatal. Leaders must force drinking — **2 quarts per hour** when active, small sips often. "YOU CAN'T DRINK TOO MUCH WATER!" Best water temperature is 50–60°F. Plastic cans keep water good 72 hours vs. 24 hours for metal; change canteen water at least every 24 hours; trailer water lasts 5 days in shade — change it if it exceeds 92°F, when bacteria cause diarrhea.

**Salt:** MRE salt is enough up to 4 quarts of water per day; salt tablets (2 per 1-quart canteen, dissolved) only under medical direction.

## Heat, Sun, Acclimation

Acclimation takes 2 weeks of progressive exposure. Sunburn: 5 minutes the first day, plus 5 minutes a day; the sun is as dangerous on a cloudy day. Heat illness runs cramps, exhaustion, stroke — and a heat-stroke victim creeps away to hide, so watch for missing men.

## Sanitation and Disease

**Field sanitation.** Garbage at least 100 ft from water; burial at least 30 yards from the kitchen; garbage pits 4×4×4 ft for 100 men for 1 day, filled to no more than 1 ft from the top. Latrines at least 100 yards from mess and water; close when waste is within 1 ft of the surface, mound 1 ft high and spray 2 ft on each side, mark "Closed Latrine" with the date.

**Diseases to expect:** plague, typhus, malaria, dengue, dysentery, cholera, typhoid.

## Clothing and Personal Equipment

Lightweight camouflage, not fatigue green; long sleeves and trousers tucked into boots; no jungle boots (sand gets in); sweater and field jacket for cold nights; wool and cotton scarves; sleeping bag; chapstick, sunburn ointment, salt tablets, foot powder, insect repellent, sunglasses, 2-quart canteens, goggles.
