# Offense and Defense Checklists — ATP 3-21.50

Source: ATP 3-21.50, Chapters 3, 4, and 5. Distilled from the manual; see `full-text.md` for the verbatim source.

## Offense Planning Checklist

1. **Reconnaissance first.** Route, enemy position, fields of fire, weather window, exfiltration route — known before committing.
2. **Rehearse on similar terrain.** Terrain the unit has not trained on will defeat the plan.
3. **Stockpile at the assault position.** Ammunition, water, warming supplies — resupply during the attack will be slow or impossible.
4. **Choose the form.** Envelopment is preferred in mountains (attack flanks and rear through terrain the enemy cannot cover). Infiltration suits acclimatized troops with advanced skills. Penetration is the last resort against a strong point.
5. **Move at night or in poor visibility** when possible; use covered approaches (dead ground, draws, reverse slopes).
6. **Assault the last covered position, then cross the final open ground fast and violently.** Control the crest.
7. **Consolidate immediately** after the attack: reorganize, redistribute ammunition, treat casualties, prepare for the counterattack — which will usually come from higher ground not yet taken.

## Defense Planning Checklist

1. **Site for the approaches.** Fields of fire down the avenues of approach (passes, saddles, valley floors) — the enemy can only come through a few places.
2. **Build positions in depth.** Primary, alternate, supplementary, and subsequent battle positions; a position that cannot be shifted when found will be destroyed.
3. **Overhead cover.** Rock protects from direct fire, not plunging fire or indirect fire — dig in and cover.
4. **Covered routes between positions** so soldiers can move without being observed.
5. **Choose the defensive form:**
   - **Area defense:** platoon battle positions in depth, mutually supporting.
   - **Linear obstacle:** use the river, ridgeline, or cliff as the defense; cover the crossing points.
   - **Perimeter defense:** all-around security for isolated or surrounded units; central reserve.
   - **Reverse-slope defense:** positions on the sheltered side of a crest, out of enemy observation and direct fire. The enemy crests blind and is engaged at close range. Requires strict fire discipline — you cannot see the enemy until he is very close.
6. **Engage at maximum effective range** on the approaches; break up formations with indirect fire on canalized routes; destroy at close range in the killing area you chose.
7. **Reorganize after repelling an attack** — the next one will come from a different direction.

## Observation Post (OP) Procedures

- Site for observation of the avenues of approach, with a covered route to and from the main position.
- Plan the **withdrawal route before occupation**. An OP that cannot withdraw is a sacrifice, not a sensor.
- Equip for extended occupation: water, warming gear, communications, and a means to mark the position for friendly forces.
- Report by exception and on schedule; maintain radio discipline — a transmitting OP is a located OP.

## Combat Outpost Procedures

- Larger and stronger than an OP: designed to **fight**, not just observe.
- Mission: delay and disrupt the enemy, force early deployment, buy the main body time to prepare.
- Must be strong enough to survive until the main body is ready, with a planned withdrawal.
- Site for mutual support with the main defense and for covered withdrawal.

## Tunnels and Caves

- Offer superb protection from weather and indirect fire; used for shelter, storage, and fighting positions.
- **Always maintain a second exit.** Never occupy a cave you have not fully reconnoitered — a blocked entrance turns shelter into a trap.
- Ventilate for cooking and heating; watch for carbon monoxide.

## Base Operations

- Site for defense, water, and access to routes.
- All-around security; ability to sustain the unit through weather that may cut resupply for days.
- Keep the base small and mobile enough to displace if the enemy finds it or the weather turns.
