# Landing and Air Movement — FM 90-4

## The Landing Plan

The landing plan must support the ground tactical plan. The AATF is most vulnerable during landing; it lands with tactical integrity, prepared to fight in any direction. **An alternate LZ is designated for each primary LZ.**

### LZ Criteria

- **Location** relative to the objective.
- **Capacity** — enough room for the aircraft in the lift.
- **Alternates** — an alternate LZ for each primary LZ.
- **Enemy disposition and capabilities** in the vicinity.
- **Cover and concealment** near the LZ.
- **Obstacles** — LZs free of obstacles; when attacking, land on the **enemy side** of obstacles.
- **Identification from the air** — chemical lights, preferably **infrared for NVG**.
- **Approach and departure routes** that avoid flank exposure.
- **Weather** at the LZ.

### Single LZ vs Multiple LZs

**Single LZ advantages:** concentration of force, ease of control, concentrated fires and security, fewer routes, centralized resupply, less planning.

**Multiple LZ advantages:** avoids presenting a lucrative target, rapid dispersal, the enemy must fight in multiple directions, less congestion, harder for the enemy to gauge force size.

### LZ Designation and Landing Formations

LZs are designated by **letter or code word, not the same number as the objective**. Landing formations are chosen so the force lands ready to employ fire and movement; select a **SAFE landing area close to concealment**, not a picture-perfect one. The first lift lands ready to fight.

### Fires to Support the Landing

The initial assault is often conducted **without preparatory fires**, to preserve surprise. When preparations are used, they are planned to be **intense, short, and high-volume, ending just before the first lift lands**. Deception false preparations may be used. A long preparation loses surprise, and ordnance can crater the LZ, blow down trees, and obscure it.

## The Air Movement Plan

Based on the ground tactical and landing plans.

- **Flight routes** are chosen for tactical and technical factors; routes are designated by **color or number**.
- **Flight corridor:** **200–300 m either side of the route and 500 ft above and below** it.
- **Flight axis:** a route with width.
- **Expedient flight routes** are defined by checkpoints.
- **Air control points (ACPs):** points where the route changes direction.
- **En route formations** and **terrain flight modes**, including **nap-of-the-earth (NOE)** flight, are specified.
- **Supporting fires** are planned along the route.

### Timing

**Timing basis: the first aircraft of the first lift touching down (H-hour).** In the manual's example, **SP time = H-10** (8-minute route plus 2 minutes from release point to LZ) and **lift-off = H-12** (including 2 minutes from PZ to SP). Times are recorded in the **air movement table**.

### Communications En Route

**Short radio reports** keep the net clear — for example, "One, One King" means the first serial of lift 1.
