# Loading, PZ/LZ Operations, and Execution — FM 90-4

## The Loading Plan

**PZ selection:** multiple PZs avoid concentration of aircraft and troops; a PZ should be sized to accommodate all supporting aircraft at once if possible; proximity to the troops; masked from enemy observation; usable as-is or with limited preparation.

**PZ control officer (PZCO):** organizes and controls the PZ, forms the control group, uses **two radio frequencies** (movement/loading and aviation), plans fires and security, clears obstacles, and marks the PZ. PZs are named by **color**; markers show where aircraft land, matching the landing formation.

**Movement to the PZ** is scheduled so that only the troops to be loaded and the helicopter arrive together; a **staging area** is used if the assembly area is distant.

**Airloading table:** assigns each man and each major item of equipment and supplies to a specific aircraft (**chalk**). It is the accountability tool and the loading manifest; a copy is left with a designated person.

**Load planning:** **tactical cross-loading** — do not put all leaders or all crew-served weapons in one chalk; maintain unit integrity where possible; provide communication between chalks if possible without aircraft radios. **Bump plan:** who and what is left behind if aircraft are unavailable, and in what priority. **Slingload and external loads** follow FM 55-450-1; internal loads, allowable gross weight, and the manifest are part of the plan.

## Execution — Combat Operations (Chapter 4)

### Hasty Attack

Movement to contact, a modified deliberate plan, exploitation of success, or attack from the defense. Tentative PZs, LZs, and routes are pre-identified; orders are brief; the operation relies on SOPs and training. Suppressive fires are used, with **priority to suppression of enemy ADA**. Smoke may screen aircraft but **must not obscure the LZ**.

### Deliberate Attack

A detailed plan, additional assets, changed task organization, detailed intelligence. Objectives are normally in the **enemy rear or flank/rear**; the force bypasses main defenses to destroy artillery, command posts, logistics, and communications, or to secure key terrain.
