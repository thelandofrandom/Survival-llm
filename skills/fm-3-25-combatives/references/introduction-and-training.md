# Introduction and Training — FM 3-25 (2002; text identical to FM 3-25.150), Chapters 1–2

## Introduction (Chapter 1)

- **Definition:** hand-to-hand combat is an engagement between two or more persons in an empty-handed struggle or with handheld weapons (knives, sticks, rifles with bayonets). Matters because projectile weapons may be lost, broken, or fail to fire, and intermingled forces make firearms/grenades impractical.
- **Purposes:** silent elimination when required; any soldier may face unarmed confrontation. Side benefits: strength, flexibility, balance, cardiorespiratory fitness, courage, confidence, self-discipline, esprit de corps.
- **Principles (1-3):** physical balance (feet shoulder-width, knees flexed, low center of gravity; exploit opponent's balance); mental balance (fear/anger must not overcome concentration); position (whole body off the line of attack, usually 45 degrees); timing (too soon = anticipated, too late = struck). Reframed around the ground fight: principles serve gaining a dominant body position.
- **Safety (1-4):** controlled progression, protective equipment, trained supervision before live training against resistance.

## Training (Chapter 2)

- **Train the trainer:** instructors fit, highly proficient in demonstration and application; safety is the trainer's first responsibility; no technique taught live until taught by the numbers.
- **Unit training:** basic/one-station training builds the foundation; sustainment program keeps it alive; designed to run anywhere with little preparation.
- **Training areas:** mass formations; matted room; constructed pit for throws/falls; bayonet assault course for grading under near-combat demands.
- **Teaching techniques:** prescribed warm-ups and stretches; crawl-walk-run; demonstrations; execution by the numbers; execution at combat speed; drills; training pads and protective equipment. Sequence matters: automaticity by the numbers first, combat speed second.
