# Takedowns, Strikes, Weapons — FM 3-25 (2002; text identical to FM 3-25.150), Chapters 5–7

## Takedowns and throws (Chapter 5)

- **Breakfalls (5-1):** first — a soldier who cannot fall safely cannot be thrown in training; the thrower must stay on his feet and in control.
- **Close the distance, achieve the clinch (5-2):** the bridge from striking to grappling range; whoever controls the clinch controls the takedown, and the takedown decides who is on top.
- **Throw and takedown techniques (5-3):** hip throws, shoulder throws, leg trips and sweeps — whole-body movements driven by hips and legs.
- **Defending against headlocks (5-4):** the headlock is often the entry to a throw; defending it is defending the takedown.
- **Takedowns against a wall (5-5):** walls remove space to sprawl or circle, so techniques change.
- **Double leg attack (5-6), single leg attacks (5-7):** level change, penetration step, drive through, finish to a dominant position.
- **Attacks from the rear (5-8):** safest place to attack from, hardest to defend — why the back mount tops the hierarchy.

## Strikes (Chapter 6)

- **Arm strikes (6-1):** punches, elbows, knife-hand, heel-palm, hammer-fist; preference for natural weapons over the fragile fist.
- **Punching combinations (6-2):** one strike opens the target, the next hits it.
- **Kicks (6-3):** low kicks to knee, shin, groin; high kicks discouraged (balance sacrificed for a visible target).
- **Transition between ranges (6-4):** move deliberately between striking, clinching, grappling; transitions trained as their own skill.

## Handheld weapons (Chapter 7)

- **Angles of attack (7-1):** numbered angles — common language for attack, defense, counter; drilled by the numbers first.
- **Rifle with fixed bayonet (7-2):** thrust, slash, butt-stroke, parry as instinctive patterns; close, control, finish.
- **Bayonet/knife (7-3), knife-against-rifle sequence (7-4):** knife as primary weapon; knife vs. longer weapon is a desperate case.
- **Advanced weapons techniques (7-5):** integrated weapons work at combat speed against resistance.
- **Field-expedient weapons:** entrenching tool (7-6), three-foot stick (7-7), six-foot pole (7-8). Same angles and vital targets apply; the tool extends reach and multiplies force. (The 1992 edition's three-foot rope is dropped here.)
