# TCCC Phases and MARCH-PAWS

Source: ATP 4-02.11, Chapter 3 (Fundamentals of Tactical Combat Casualty Care).

## Chapter outline

- TCCC overview; service-specific skills; TCCC training
- TCCC phases: Care Under Fire, Tactical Field Care, Tactical Evacuation Care
- TCCC clinical practice guidelines (CoTCCC — the living standard behind this manual)
- MARCH-PAWS assessment; documentation (DD Form 1380); 9-line MEDEVAC request procedure

## The three phases

| Phase | Situation | Care |
|---|---|---|
| Care Under Fire (CUF) | Still under effective enemy fire | Return fire; move casualty to cover; hasty tourniquet "high and tight" on life-threatening extremity bleeding — nothing else |
| Tactical Field Care (TFC) | Relative cover, no longer under effective fire | Full MARCH-PAWS assessment; bleeding control, airway, breathing, circulation, hypothermia, pain, antibiotics, wounds, splinting |
| Tactical Evacuation Care (TACEVAC) | During movement to a medical treatment facility | Continue and reassess all TFC care; monitor; prepare for handover (MIST, 9-line) |

## MARCH-PAWS

| Letter | Meaning | Chapter |
|---|---|---|
| M | Massive hemorrhage — the top priority | Ch. 4 |
| A | Airway | Ch. 5 |
| R | Respiration (breathing) | Ch. 6 |
| C | Circulation | Ch. 7 |
| H | Hypothermia and head injury | Ch. 8 |
| P | Pain | Ch. 9 |
| A | Antibiotics | Ch. 9 |
| W | Wounds | Ch. 9, 14 |
| S | Splinting | Ch. 13 |

Rule from the manual: do not treat a secondary injury before completing MARCH. A splinted fracture means nothing if the casualty bled out while you splinted it. Life-threatening injuries first, every time.

## Tier 1 and Tier 2 skills

- **Tier 1 skills:** every service member — tourniquet application, wound packing, pressure dressings, airway positioning, chest seal application, hypothermia prevention, documentation on the TCCC Card.
- **Tier 2 skills:** combat lifesavers and medics — needle decompression, surgical airway, blood-product administration, advanced splinting, casualty monitoring and evacuation preparation.
- Training targets the gaps: bleeding control including junctional and noncompressible hemorrhage, airway, and evacuation procedures.

## Documentation

All care is documented on the DD Form 1380 (TCCC Card), secured to the casualty. The card is the MIST report for the receiving facility. The manual notes its content is accurate as of 23 March 2026; CoTCCC clinical practice guidelines are the authoritative updates after that date.
