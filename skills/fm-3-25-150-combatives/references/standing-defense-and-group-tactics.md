# Standing Defense and Group Tactics — FM 3-25.150 (2002), Chapters 8–9 and Appendices

## Standing defense (Chapter 8)

### Unarmed opponent (Section I)

- **Defense against chokes (8-1):** tuck the chin, protect the neck, turn into the choke, attack the choking arm's leverage, break the base. Counter starts the instant the choke is felt.
- **Defense against bear hugs (8-2):** free the arms, drop weight, attack balance, turn the hug into a takedown or escape.

### Armed opponent (Section II)

- **General rule (8-3):** gain a weapon or create distance first; engagement is the last resort.
- **Defense against a knife (8-4):** control the weapon arm, never the blade; move off the line of attack; attack the arm's structure.
- **Unarmed defense vs. rifle with fixed bayonet (8-5):** get inside the weapon's effective arc, control the rifle, strike vital targets.
- Throughout: unarmed against a weapon is a desperate position — techniques are damage control, not parity.

## Group tactics (Chapter 9)

### Lethal force scenarios (Section I)

- **Range (9-1), control (9-2), finishing (9-3):** control range, control the opponent's body, finish, move to the next threat. Do not get tied to one opponent while another closes.

### Restrictive force scenarios (Section II)

- **Two against one (9-4), three against two (9-5), parity (9-6), one against two (9-7), two against three (9-8).**
- With the advantage: split and isolate. At parity: pair off and finish fast. Outnumbered: keep moving, use one opponent as a shield against the other, break contact the moment a finish is not available.
- One against two: make it two one-against-one fights in sequence, never one one-against-two fight.

## Appendices

- **Appendix A — Situational training:** scenario-based training in mission context (sentry scenarios, detainee handling, restrictive-force situations). Bridge between drill field and fight.
- **Appendix B — Competitions:** rules, scoring, refereeing as a training tool — competition is the principal motivational device and the closest safe approximation to a fully resistant opponent. Origin of the formal program the 2017 manual expands.
