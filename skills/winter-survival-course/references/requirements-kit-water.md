# Requirements, Kit, Water — MWTC Winter Survival (2002), Chapters 1–3

## Requirements (Chapter 1)

- **SURVIVAL acronym:** Size up situation/surroundings/physical condition/equipment; Undue haste makes waste; Remember where you are; Vanquish fear and panic; Improvise; Value living; Act like the natives; Learn basic skills.
- **Stressors:** injury, illness, death; uncertainty; environment; hunger/thirst; fatigue; isolation.
- **Priorities of work:** First 24 hours — shelter, fire, water, signaling (order by enemy/weather/terrain/time/resources). Second 24 hours — tools and weapons, traps and snares, pathguards. Remainder — continuously improve until rescue.
- **Group survival:** organizing activity; morale from internal cohesiveness. In winter the first test is usually the shelter build before dark.

## Kit (Chapter 2)

- Six components, layered with most important on the body, multipurpose items preferred: fire starting, water procurement, food procurement, signaling, first aid, shelter. Winter emphasis: fire-starting and shelter items rank highest; the metal boiling container is essential because most winter water is melted snow/ice. Redundancy matters (matches fail wet and cold).

## Water (Chapter 3)

- **Over-drink method:** thirst is unreliable; cold suppresses thirst, so winter dehydration is silent — respiration and sweat under layers lose water without a thirst signal.
- **Dehydration:** 5% — thirst, irritability, nausea, weakness; 10% — dizziness, headache, inability to walk, tingling limbs; 15% — dim vision, painful urination, swollen tongue, deafness, numb skin; over 15% — can be fatal.
- **Snow:** melt for potable water; costs more fuel; uncontaminated snow needs no disinfection. **Ice:** melt for potable water; preferable to snow (more water per volume) but needs disinfection. **Sea ice:** loses salinity over time; rounded corners and bluish color identify it; gray ice still holds salt.
- **Rules:** never melt snow or ice inside your mouth (removes body heat, invites cold injury); on the move, use body heat (container against the body); do not waste fuel melting snow/ice when a stream is available; when melting by fire, start with a little water in the container and use a hot rock to speed it.
- **Hazardous fluids:** seawater, alcohol (dehydrates, clouds judgment; super-cooled liquid can frostbite the throat), blood, urine.
