# Food and Tracking — MWTC Winter Survival (2002), Chapters 8–11, 13

## Traps and snares (Chapter 8)

- Snaring is the most effective means to take game: location, presentation, construction. Wire snares with locking device, split-stick support, anchor or drag, quick firing, loop sized to the animal. Deadfalls crush where snares cannot be set. In winter, set on trails animals must use — food and water are concentrated, so trails are predictable.

## Uses of game (Chapter 9)

- Butchering, thorough cooking (parasites), specific parts — hides, bones, sinew, fat. In winter, fat is the highest-value part: calories are heat.

## Expedient tools and foraging (Chapters 10–11)

- Improvise tools, cordage, containers, weapons from the environment; multipurpose and durable.
- Foraging avoid list (unchanged from summer): mushrooms/fungi, umbrella flowers, legumes, 3-leafed/whorled patterns, bulbs, white/yellow berries, milky sap, shiny leaves, skin irritants. Boil any unknown plant before testing. In winter, foraging is marginal — plan on traps, snares, and fishing, not plants.

## Winter tracking (Chapter 13)

- Snow is the best tracking medium: every track is fresh, dated by snowfall, readable. Read snow sign for game location (trap/snare placement), water, and people — and counter-tracking, because your own tracks in snow confess where you went.
- Tracks age visibly (edges soften, snow fills in); judging a trail's age is the winter tracker's core skill.
