# Fieldcraft — MWTC Winter Survival (2002), Chapters 5–7

## Leadership (Chapter 5)

- Altitude, cold, steep terrain, isolation degrade judgment before the body. Keep the group on the priorities; decide with incomplete information. Most common winter leadership failure: delaying the shelter decision until too dark and too cold to build well.

## Signaling and recovery (Chapter 6)

- Pyrotechnics (star clusters, smoke grenades — in snowpack, smoke grenades need flotation or they sink out of sight), mirror, lights (flashlight, strobe, chemlight), signaling fires, ground-to-air panels.
- Snow is a high-contrast background — dark panels, trampled patterns, and smoke show well against it.
- Recovery: be findable; follow the recovery force's instructions; do not abandon a signaled position without a trail and message.

## Navigation (Chapter 7)

- Daytime: sun, shadows, terrain association. Nighttime: stars, moon; slow movement, frequent checks.
- Dead reckoning with pace count; natural direction indicators. Winter problems: whiteout destroys terrain association, tracks fill in behind you, compass hard to read with gloves and blowing snow.
