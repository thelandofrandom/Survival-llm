# Expedient Snowshoes — MWTC Winter Survival (2002), Chapter 12

## Why

- Without over-the-snow mobility, movement becomes difficult and dangerous: clothing gets wet, and "post holing" (sinking knee-deep or more every step) consumes energy vital in a survival situation. Increasing surface area under the body is the whole idea.

## Advantages and disadvantages

- **Advantages:** mobility (floatation); limited maintenance; heavy loads relatively easy to carry/pull on gentle terrain; movement in confined areas relatively easy.
- **Disadvantages:** construction consumes a lot of material (~70 feet of 550 cord for one shoe); movement over moderate to steep slopes extremely difficult; thick forest and branches through snow difficult; green staves shrink as they dry — all lashing must be retied.

## Nomenclature

- **Tip** (front of frame), **tail** (rear), **crossbars** (three, lashed for reinforcement), **webbing** (cord latticework — floatation, releases snow when the shoe lifts), **window** (opening letting the boot toe pivot — without it the boot forces the tip into the snow), **frame** (resilient green saplings: willow, aspen), **binding** (8–10 foot cord attaching boot to shoe).

## Construction

- Three straight, resilient green staves: 5 feet long, 1–1.5 inches diameter; cut one into three 15-inch sections.
- Join the two 5-foot staves at tips and tails with a shear lash (the frame).
- Crossbars: first 12–14 inches from the tip, second 4–6 inches below the first, last 15 inches below the second — each secured with a square lash.
- Latticework webbing from the first crossbar toward the tip; second latticework from the second crossbar toward the tail. If cord/wire is limited, space the latticework out; interwoven branches or boughs increase floatation.
- Attach the boot with the cord binding system; secure all pigtails with a square knot and two overhand knots.
- **Stability is the most important characteristic** — snowshoes undergo great strain and pressure; material selection matters. Materials can vary: green saplings, aluminum aircraft skin and ribbed framing, cordage, wire, cargo strapping.

## Canadian emergency snowshoes

- Low-cordage alternative: five poles, 6 feet long (the individual's height), ¾ inch at the base tapering to ¼ inch at the tip, per shoe, with 10-inch cross-sticks. Excellent when saplings are available and cordage is limited.
