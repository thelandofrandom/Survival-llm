# Foundations and Combat Power — ATP 3-90.99 (2021), Chapters 2–3

## Foundations (Chapter 2)

- Desert operations span the range of military operations, from military engagement to major operations and campaigns. Influencing factors may originate beyond the area's physical boundaries.
- **Areas of operations:** close (where most subordinate units fight), deep (sets conditions for future close combat), support (facilitates the operation), rear (extends forward from the rear boundary), consolidation (requires a task-organized combined-arms unit for area security; where gains are made permanent). Commanders designate main and supporting efforts for clear priorities of support and resources; a shaping operation may be the main effort until the decisive operation begins.
- **Multi-domain extended desert battlefield:** air, land, maritime, space, and cyberspace domains all impact desert operations. Space provides information collection, early warning, environmental monitoring, satellite communications. The information environment moves in near-real time; cyberspace is a global domain within it; the electromagnetic spectrum is contested — open sandy/dune deserts especially favor electromagnetic operations. Advantages in cyberspace and the EMS come from synchronizing capabilities.
- **Fundamentals:** harsh environment impacts all echelons; vast spaces and no foliage are both challenge and opportunity (open ground favors combined-arms maneuver, but most deserts are too large to occupy or control completely). The twelve principles of joint operations apply — but physical control may not be possible everywhere, so plan for presence, influence, and control of key terrain (water, routes, passes). Many deserts hold critical resource-extraction infrastructure. Desert operations are resource-intensive — commanders plan to end them as quickly as possible.

## Combat power (Chapter 3) — eight elements

Combat power is the total means of destructive and/or disruptive force a unit can apply. The eight elements, each desert-applied:

1. **Leadership:** through mission command; ensure understanding of purpose and efficient employment. Desert factors — above all fatigue — degrade leadership performance.
2. **Information / information warfighting function:** every engagement needs complementary information operations; actions are more powerful than words. Open terrain facilitates sending, receiving, and collecting information — for both sides.
3. **Command and control:** unity of effort essential and harder across desert distances; MDMP (Army) and MCPP (Marine Corps) planning methodologies; IPB as systematic mission-variable analysis; targeting requires careful analysis to avoid fratricide (Appendix C).
4. **Movement and maneuver:** freer on open ground, harder through canalization, water, fuel, maintenance. Balance risk against mission requirements in the operational approach.
5. **Intelligence:** size and limited trafficability challenge collection; UAS and other reconnaissance fill gaps ground patrols cannot cover.
6. **Fires:** correction of artillery fires, especially larger pieces, complicated by heat, dust, wind, and difficulty observing impacts at long range in featureless terrain.
7. **Sustainment / logistics:** the dominant desert problem — water, fuel, maintenance over long distances. Sustainment determines how far and how long a force can operate; it is the limiting factor, not the tail.
8. **Protection / force protection:** dispersion, camouflage, cover in terrain offering little of any; protect water sources, lines of communication, and the force against an enemy with the same observation advantages.
