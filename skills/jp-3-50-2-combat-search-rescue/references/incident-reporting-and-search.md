# Incident Reporting and Search — JP 3-50.2

Distilled from JP 3-50.2, Chapter II (26 January 1996). Paraphrase with short quotes only; full text in `full-text.md`.

## Distress indicators

- Mayday.
- Nonreturn from a mission.
- Overdue contact.
- Emergency beacon transmission received.
- Sighting of an aircraft or vessel going down.
- Report of personnel isolated by enemy activity.
- Ground emergency codes from survivors of downed aircraft.

Note: the indicator may be picked up by noncomponent assets (e.g., AWACS) and relayed directly to the JSRC, producing multiple reports of the same incident. Follow procedures anyway.

## Incident sequence of events (summary)

1. Distress indicator received.
2. Requesting unit notifies its component RCC; RCC notifies the JSRC.
3. Component RCC becomes CSAR mission coordinator initially: reports to JSRC, initiates planning, gets intelligence briefing on area threat, designates an ISOPREP control point, obtains ISOPREP data and EPA from units, tasks subordinate CSAR-capable forces, coordinates with JSRC and requesting unit, requests additional forces through JSRC if needed.
4. JSRC coordinates JFC tasking of other component RCCs when a component cannot execute or needs support; coordinates nondedicated CSAR resources; coordinates SOF with J-3 and the special operations component; coordinates CSARTF development; coordinates with intelligence/SOF to alert evasion-and-recovery nets; alerts all forces in the area to report evidence of isolated personnel; determines whether current operations provide temporary air superiority near the isolated personnel.
5. JSRC assigns a CSAR mission coordinator and provides all available data.
6. Mission coordinator confirms the distress call and authentication data; assists in planning.
7. Isolated personnel confirm distress and authenticate.
8. Recovery forces evaluate probability of success and execute.
9. Intelligence personnel debrief recovered personnel (per JP 3-50.3).

## When the JSRC assumes mission coordination

- RCCs are not established.
- The JSRC receives the initial notification.
- The event is complex enough to require tasking several component commanders.
- The mission is beyond conventional CSAR forces and requires a SOF response.
- The responding RCC requests additional assistance and asks the JSRC to assume coordination.
- Current operations or nonavailability of CSAR-capable resources preclude the component commander from initiating or continuing a response.

## Reporting — what the distressed crew transmits

Call sign, nature of distress, type of aircraft or unit, position, course, speed, altitude, number of personnel, and the pilot's or unit commander's intentions. Call on the frequency of last contact or an established common frequency; transmit "in the blind" only when contact with known friendlies is impossible by any other means. Maintain the transmission long enough for a direction-finder plot where possible — balanced against enemy direction-finding risk. If time permits, turn IFF to emergency; aircrews may elect not to, to avoid highlighting the aircraft.

## Reporting — the chain

- **Individual/crew** → radio report as above; unit commander may designate an on-scene commander for immediate support.
- **Unit** → reports to component RCC; also reports its own CSAR capability and status of ongoing CSAR.
- **Component RCC** → forwards incident information to the JSRC by the quickest and most secure means (normally radio or telephone), followed up with applicable U.S. message text formats (USMTFs); relays requests for active CSAR support to the JSRC and adjacent RCCs.
- **JSRC** → receives reports on all CSAR incidents involving joint force resources; forwards to higher authority as directed; provides follow-up reports to higher authority, adjacent JSRCs, and component RCCs.

## Search methods

- **Electronic:** search radars (surface vessel, forward-looking airborne, side-looking airborne) and direction-finding/homing equipment. Limited first by the distressed person's sending capability (emergency locator beacon) and the searchers' receiving capability (homing adaptor), then by enemy activity.
- **Visual:** visual search, alone or combined with electronic search, using the area-designation methods below.
- **Maritime:** normally a combination of surface and air search and rescue units. Detailed maritime planning factors (aerospace drift, maritime drift, water temperature) are in JP 3-50 Vol. I and JP 3-50.1 Vol. II.
- Choice of method depends on environment (maritime, coastal, inland), enemy activity, weather, time of day, and available CSAR-capable resources. Every search is tailored to the incident.

## Search-area designation methods

| Method | Definition | Example from the manual |
|---|---|---|
| Boundary (maritime) | Square/rectangle oriented E-W or N-S, stated as two latitudes and two longitudes | D-7: 26N to 27N, 64W to 65W |
| Corner point (maritime) | Area (except circular) described by latitude/longitude of each corner in sequence | E-7: 23°15'N 74°35'W to 23°10'N 73°25'W to 22°20'N 73°25'W to 22°25'N 74°25'W to origin |
| Center point | Area defined by a center point and radius | Approximate location, radius search |
| Track line | Area along a known track or route | Last known along a route |
| Boundary (inland) | Area bounded by prominent geographic features stated in sequence | A-1: Highway 15 (S), Lake Merhaven (W), Runslip River (N), Bravado mountain range (E) |
| Corner point (inland) | Area described by geographic features of each corner in sequence | A-6: junction of Highways 15 and 26, Red River bridge, north end of Whitney Canal, summit of Eagle Mountain |

The inland boundary method is most often used for inland CSAR incidents.

## Recovery methods

- Helicopter recovery (workhorse: hoist, rope, landing pickup).
- Fixed-wing recovery.
- Conventional ground-force recovery.
- Special operations recovery.
- Naval vessel recovery.
- The airborne mission commander (AMC) controls the airborne effort and coordinates RESCAP, forward air controllers, and recovery aircraft (duties in Figure II-2). The on-scene commander runs the immediate recovery scene.
- Recovery is not normally initiated until the isolated person has been authenticated (see `isolated-personnel.md`).
