# Isolated Personnel — EPA, ISOPREP, Authentication — JP 3-50.2

Distilled from JP 3-50.2, Chapters I–II and Appendixes H and J (26 January 1996). Paraphrase with short quotes only; full text in `full-text.md`.

## Who counts as isolated personnel

Military or civilian personnel separated from their unit or organization in an environment requiring them to survive, evade, resist, or escape while awaiting recovery. This includes aircrew downed in hostile territory, cut-off ground personnel, and civilians such as Civil Reserve Air Fleet crew members and deployed technical representatives operating with U.S. forces.

## Responsibilities of isolated personnel

- Confirm distress and authenticate themselves to recovery forces.
- Follow their evasion plan of action (EPA).
- Use evasion aids, signaling devices, and emergency equipment as trained.
- Cooperate with recovery forces and follow the on-scene commander's instructions.
- Preserve authentication data: do not compromise code words, signals, or ISOPREP details. Compromised authentication data can turn a rescue into an ambush.
- After recovery, submit to intelligence debriefing in accordance with JP 3-50.3.

## Evasion Plan of Action (EPA) — Appendix H

- A pre-planned course of action for the individual (and unit) if isolated: intended routes, rally points, safe areas (SAFEs), signaling methods, and actions on contact with friendly or hostile forces.
- Unit EPAs and personal EPAs: component commanders ensure both are developed, maintained, updated regularly, and readily available to the JSRC through the RCC.
- Aircrew EPAs are normally mission-specific: developed and updated as requirements dictate.
- Planning factors for EPAs are extracted from the JSRC's theater evasion-and-recovery concept of operations and passed to subordinate units.
- Development guidance is in JP 3-50.3, *Joint Doctrine for Evasion and Recovery*.
- A good EPA is the difference between a survivor who moves with purpose toward recovery and one who wanders. It also tells the rescue force where to look, which routes the survivor will use, and what signals to expect.

## ISOPREP — Isolated Personnel Report, DD Form 1833 — Appendix J

- The primary means of authenticating isolated personnel.
- Contents: identity and personal data, physical description, photograph, fingerprints, and authentication data (code words and personal details only the individual would know).
- Component commanders must ensure ISOPREPs — including fingerprinting — are properly prepared and maintained for all personnel whose duties may place them in danger of isolation.
- An original may be required by the recovering unit. The capability to make quality copies (photo and fingerprints) may not exist at the deployment location — so originals matter and must be safeguarded.
- The component RCC maintains staff cognizance for preparing and maintaining ISOPREPs and designates an ISOPREP control point during an incident to obtain ISOPREP data and the EPA from units.
- Appendix J (Figures J-1 and J-2) gives the front and back of DD Form 1833 with instructions.

## Authentication — why it gates recovery

- Recovery forces evaluate probability of success and execute only after the isolated person has been authenticated.
- The CSAR mission coordinator confirms the distress call and the isolated personnel authentication data as one of the first steps after assignment.
- The JSRC/RCC holds the ISOPREP data; the isolated person provides the matching answers over the radio or by visual signal.
- Authentication protects both sides: it keeps the enemy from luring a rescue force into a trap with a false survivor, and it keeps the isolated person from walking into a hostile "rescue."
- If authentication fails or cannot be established, the mission does not proceed as a recovery — the situation is reassessed, the location is refined, and other means of confirming identity are used.

## What the rescue force needs from the survivor's paperwork

- Current ISOPREP on file and reachable by the JSRC/RCC (through the ISOPREP control point).
- Current EPA, so planners know intended movement, SAFE locations, and signals.
- Both must be updated regularly — an EPA written for a different mission area or a stale ISOPREP with an old photograph slows or blocks authentication.

## Checklist — before a high-risk mission

- [ ] ISOPREP current (DD Form 1833), photo and fingerprints on file, original available.
- [ ] Personal EPA current and mission-specific; unit EPA current.
- [ ] Authentication data memorized; code words and signals rehearsed.
- [ ] Evasion aids, signaling devices, and emergency radio checked and carried.
- [ ] Briefed on theater CSAR SPINS, frequencies, and procedures.
- [ ] Unit knows the reporting chain: RCC, then JSRC.
