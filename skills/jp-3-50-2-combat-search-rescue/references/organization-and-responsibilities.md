# Organization and Responsibilities — JP 3-50.2

Distilled from JP 3-50.2, Chapters I, III, and VI (26 January 1996). Paraphrase with short quotes only; full text in `full-text.md`.

## The chain

| Echelon | Owns | Key instrument |
|---|---|---|
| JFC | CSAR for the whole joint force, incl. civilians (CRAF crews, technical reps) | JSRC |
| Component commander | CSAR for own operations; mutual support to others | RCC (or Navy RCT / JFSOC JOC equivalent) |
| Unit commander | CSAR from inherent capability; mutual support to adjacent units | On-scene commander (OSC) when needed |
| Isolated person | Confirm distress, authenticate, follow EPA | ISOPREP, EPA, evasion aids |

## JSRC — Joint Search and Rescue Center

- A primary SAR facility, staffed by supervisory personnel, equipped to plan, coordinate, and execute joint SAR/CSAR in the joint force's area.
- Staffed jointly (two or more components) or multinationally; staffed equitably from each component, Coast Guard where practical.
- Normally created by tasking one component commander to designate their RCC to function also as the JSRC. That component must have C4I and surveillance capability to execute expeditiously, and the JFC gives it the authority to do so. The designated component commander names the JSRC Director.
- Standing JFCs keep a standing JSRC; contingency JTF commanders establish one in the earliest stages of forming the JTF. In a limited operation, the JSRC may sit on the JFC's own staff.
- Control of forces committed to a joint CSAR operation runs from the JFC through a designated component commander or through the JSRC.

## JSRC duties — peacetime

- Joint force CSAR SOPs and communications plans; reporting requirements for JSRC and RCCs.
- CSAR appendixes to Annex C (Operations) of OPLANs/CONPLANs/OPORDs, linked to casualty affairs, medical, repatriation, and mortuary affairs.
- Coordinate and deconflict component evasion-and-recovery plans; review for supportability.
- Integrated personnel recovery concept of operations; OJT and informal training; CSAR mission training exercises; peacetime SAR as realistic training.
- Transition plan to combat operations: augmentation personnel, additional communications, dedicated intelligence support (JIC/JISE).

## JSRC duties — combat

- CSAR threat decision matrix tailored to current threat analysis.
- SPINS in air tasking orders: theater CSAR and recovery guidance, concepts, procedures for all high-risk combatants.
- Alert components to isolated personnel locations; coordinate intelligence (national, theater JIC, host-nation, component) on location, status, and threat.
- Coordinate with PSYOP on influencing the local population favorably toward CSAR.
- Coordinate and deconflict mutual CSAR support (joint and multinational); monitor all CSAR incidents prosecuted by component RCCs.
- Maintain a database and file on each isolated person until recovery is complete; forward files and database to the Joint Services SERE Agency. Do not destroy.

## Component RCC duties

- Notify and coordinate with the JSRC on unilateral CSAR missions; coordinate CSAR support to/from other components.
- Coordinate augmentation personnel to the JSRC; develop component RCC procedures.
- Extract planning factors from the theater E&R concept of operations for subordinate units' evasion plans; coordinate unit and personal EPAs.
- Staff cognizance for ISOPREPs; CSAR and E&R appendixes to Annex C for component supporting plans.
- Forward isolated-personnel data (status, location) to the JSRC on request; maintain a file on each isolated person until recovery is complete.
- If no RCC is established, duties fall to another staff organization (normally operations), with a trained component search and rescue controller acting in the commander's name.

## Component commander — additional duties

- Ensure subordinate units and key personnel know joint CSAR doctrine, joint force CSAR SOPs, general TTP (JP 3-50.21), and area-specific TTP.
- Ensure unit and personal EPAs are developed, maintained, updated regularly, and available to the JSRC via the RCC. Aircrew EPAs are mission-specific.
- Ensure ISOPREPs (with fingerprinting) are prepared and maintained for all personnel at risk of isolation.
- Provide an equitable share of trained augmentation personnel to staff the JSRC when tasked.

## Unit commander duties

- Include CSAR planning in all operation planning; forward requirements beyond capability to the component RCC (forces, planning information, evasion aids).
- Ensure personnel are familiar with CSAR doctrine, SOPs, and TTP.
- On notification of a subordinate's distress, decide if immediate on-scene support is appropriate; may designate an OSC for escort, communications, or immediate needs.
- Report present CSAR capability and status of ongoing CSAR to higher headquarters.

## Command relationships (Chapter III)

- Figures III-1–III-3: JFC to Service components; JFC to functional components; variant where the SOF commander holds joint force CSAR responsibility.
- CSAR is tasked by mission type orders — the commander states mission and intent; the executor decides the how.
- Coordination runs through JSRC/RCCs on established CSAR communications channels. Adjacent JFCs coordinate mutual support through their JSRCs, exchanging liaison personnel temporarily when needed.
- Interoperability (Appendix G): common communications, procedures, equipment compatibility across Army, Navy, Marine Corps, Air Force, Coast Guard, and SOF. Figure III-4 gives typical voice circuits in a joint CSAR task force.

## Support requirements (Chapter VI)

- Personnel: trained, experienced JSRC/RCC staff, equitably drawn from components, plus augmentation.
- Facility and operating space: communications, intelligence access, plotting/display capability, and space for 24-hour operations.
- Service CSAR capabilities cataloged in Appendixes A–F (Army, Navy, Marine Corps, Air Force, Coast Guard, special operations component).
