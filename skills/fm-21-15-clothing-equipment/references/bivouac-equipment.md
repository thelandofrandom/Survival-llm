# Bivouac Equipment — FM 21-15

## What Is Issued

Except in extremely cold or extremely warm, moist climates — where the two-man mountain tent and the jungle hammock (FM 20-15) are issued — men in the field use the **shelter tent**, composed of two shelter halves. Woolen sleeping bags (one per individual) may be issued in lieu of blankets in temperate-climate theaters. In mosquito-infested areas, the mosquito bar is issued when authorized.

## The Shelter Tent

- The shelter half is issued on the basis of one per enlisted man, with one tent pole, five tent pins, and one guy line; two per officer, with two tent poles, ten tent pins, and two guy lines. The shelter half is also used as a fly.
- The tent is made of light, water-repellent cotton canvas and is constructed in two sections buttoned together to accommodate two men.
- The old-type tent has a **ridge height of 43 inches** and a single closure provided by a triangular piece of canvas sewed to one end; the new-type has a double closure with triangular pieces of canvas sewed to both ends.
- The tent may be ventilated by opening the closed end.

## Pitching

- Instruction in formal tent pitching is given so commanders can establish units quickly and men can be taught to pitch tents in a short time (see FM 22-5).
- Except at inspection, tents are pitched on ground that will make cover and concealment possible and contribute to the men's comfort.
- **Two men should be able to erect this tent in about 5 minutes.**
- Pairs of odd- and even-numbered men pitch tents together (1 and 2, 3 and 4, and so on):
  1. Each odd-numbered man marks the ground with his left heel where the front tent pole will go.
  2. At "Pitch tents," each man lays his rifle on the ground (muzzle to the front, barrel to the left, butt near the toe of his right foot), unslings his equipment, places his pack or roll two paces in front of him, and takes out his shelter half, pole, and pins.
  3. Each odd-numbered man drives a pin on his mark; the pair spreads the shelter halves with buttons to the center, the even-numbered man's half on the left, one half right side out and the other wrong side out so the rows of buttons may be inserted into the buttonholes (the letters "U.S." are stamped on the right side).
  4. The halves are buttoned together; the odd-numbered man slips his pole into the front eyelets and holds it upright; the even-numbered man pins down the front corners in line with the pins, drives a pin in front of the pole at a distance equivalent to a tent-pole length, loops the guy line over that pin, runs the other end through the loops of the shelter halves, and ties it, making sure the pole is vertical when the line is taut; then the rear pole is adjusted through the rear eyelets.

## Sleeping Bag and Mosquito Bar

The woolen sleeping bag, with its water-repellent case, and the mosquito bar are covered in paragraphs 17 and 18 of the manual. The sleeping bag is rolled with the shelter half as described in `packing-and-inspection.md` — the bag is fitted into its waterproof case, straps drawn through the slits in the case, zipper at the side, top of the bag along the squared end of the shelter half.
