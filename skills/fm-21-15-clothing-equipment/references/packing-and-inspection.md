# Packing and Inspection — FM 21-15

## The Carriers

Clothing and equipment are carried in duffel bags, canvas field bags, haversacks and pack carriers, rucksacks, jungle packs, field packs, and cargo and combat field packs. **The cargo and combat field pack is designed to supplant all other packs.**

## The Duffel Bag

A large, durable container, easily carried, in which the soldier places all clothing not carried in his pack.

- **Closing:** after packing, fold the flaps envelope-fashion, slip the eyelets over the U-hook, and secure with the snap hook on the carrying strap; a padlock, though not issued, may be used to safeguard the contents.
- **Carrying:** by the handle as a suitcase, or slung over the shoulder caddy-style.

## The Blanket Roll (Haversack M-1928)

1. Spread the shelter half on the ground or floor, buttons up, and convert it into a rectangle by folding in the triangular end (or both triangular ends, with the new-type shelter half).
2. Halve the length of the tent by folding at the center.
3. Fold the blanket to halve its width, fold again to one-fourth of its original width, bring the ends together, and place the blanket in the center of the folded shelter half.
4. Place clothing neatly in the folds of the blanket, spread out to prevent bunching.
5. Lay the tent pole at the folded end of the blanket, alternating the tent pins (or placing them together against the pole, heads and points alternated).
6. Fold the sides of the shelter half and the pole end over the blanket; make a **10-inch fold** at the other end; begin at the pole and roll tightly; insert the roll between the layers of the 10-inch fold, making an envelope fold.

## The Sleeping-Bag Roll

Lay the shelter half on the ground or floor; fit the sleeping bag into its waterproof case and draw the straps through the slits in the case; with the new-type shelter half, fold in one triangular end at the base of the triangle to match the old type; stretch the sleeping bag so the zipper is at the side and lay it in the center of the shelter half, the top of the bag along the squared end and the straps extended — then roll as with the blanket roll.

## Display of Basic Equipment for Formal Inspection

- Section IV prescribes the display: for the dismounted enlisted man with haversack and pack carrier, canvas field bag or rucksack, jungle pack, field pack, or cargo and combat field pack (paragraph 29); for mounted enlisted men or those similarly equipped (paragraph 30); and for formal inspection in a barracks (paragraph 31).
- If shelter tents are not pitched, the relative positions of basic articles are as shown in the manual's figures; special items not shown are added to the display without disturbing the relative positions of the basic articles.
- If shelter tents are pitched, the blanket sits in the position shown for the shelter half and blanket, with its rear edge on line with the entrance of the shelter tent.
- **Inspection in barracks:** the appearance of the enlisted man, the condition and arrangement of his clothing and equipment, and the cleanliness of his barracks are the principal considerations. Such inspections are usually held on Saturday mornings in most posts, camps, and stations; the details of preparation are laid down by the commanding officer.
- **The foot-locker** serves as the soldier's bureau and contains clothing and personal articles currently being used; **only clean clothing is kept in the foot-locker**, as dirty clothing tends to contaminate clean clothing. The lower part of the foot-locker is used for underwear, towels, and similar items; personal items and the contents of the foot-locker are arranged neatly.
