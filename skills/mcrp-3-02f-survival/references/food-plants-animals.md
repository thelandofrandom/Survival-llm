# Food, Plants, Animals — MCRP 3-02F

## Food Procurement (Chapter 8)

Food is the lowest immediate priority — a healthy person can survive weeks without it — but it sustains energy, warmth, and morale for movement and work. Fish, trap, snare, and gather.

**Snares:** work while unattended. Set on trails to water, feeding, and bedding areas, and at den mouths. With wire use a figure-8 (locking) loop — once tightened it locks and the animal cannot escape.

## Survival Use of Plants (Chapter 9)

Learn the edible plants of your area of operations **before** you deploy. Appendix B gives detailed descriptions and photographs of common edible plants; Appendix C covers tropical plants. Identification in the field is a training task, not an improvisation.

## Universal Edibility Test (UET)

**Rules:** test only one part of one plant at a time; each part takes more than 24 hours to test, so only test abundant plants; even after a food tests safe, eat in moderation.

**Steps (waiting period after each):**
1. Skin contact — crush and rub on the inside forearm; wait 15 minutes.
2. Lip contact — touch a small piece to the lip; wait a few minutes.
3. Tongue — hold briefly; do not swallow.
4. Chew and spit — chew a small amount, spit out; wait.
5. Small swallow — swallow a small amount; wait 8 hours; eat nothing else.
6. Measured portion — eat a small portion; wait 8+ hours. No ill effects (nausea, cramps, diarrhea, dizziness) means the part is edible in moderation.

**Avoid plants with:** milky sap (dandelion is a safe, recognizable exception); spines, fine hairs, or thorns (prickly pear and thistles are exceptions); mushrooms and fungus — avoid entirely; umbrella-shaped flower clusters (hemlock).

## Poisonous Plants (Chapter 10)

Danger signs: milky sap; almond scent; spines or fine hairs; umbrella-shaped flower clusters; beans or bulbs; seeds inside pods. Appendix C has photographs and descriptions. Never eat a plant you cannot positively identify. Never test an unknown plant by eating a full portion.

## Dangerous Animals (Chapter 11)

- Know the dangerous animals of the area before operating there (Appendix D: dangerous animals; Appendix E: snakes; Appendix F: venomous fish, toxic fish and mollusks).
- Prevention: watch where you put hands and feet; shake out clothing and bedding; avoid carrion and dens; use care turning over rocks and logs.
- **Snakebite:** keep the casualty calm and still; keep the bitten limb below heart level; remove constricting items; evacuate. Do not cut, suck, or apply a tourniquet.
- **Sea hazards:** avoid splashing and blood in the water; if a shark attacks, strike the eyes, gills, or nose.

## Field-Expedient Weapons, Tools, and Equipment (Chapter 12)

- Improvise clubs, spears, slings, and traps from available material.
- Fashion cutting and digging tools from stone, bone, and metal scrap.
- **The knife is the single most valuable field tool** — protect it, keep it sharp, use it for shelter construction, food preparation, and every other task.
- A weapon is included in the kit only if the situation dictates.
