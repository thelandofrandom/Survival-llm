# Survival Pattern and Kits — MCRP 3-02F

## The SURVIVAL Actions (Chapter 1)

- **S — Size up the situation.** In combat, find concealment first — security takes priority. Use hearing, smell, and sight: what is the enemy doing — advancing, holding, retreating? Size up your surroundings (every environment has a rhythm — animal and bird noises, insect sounds, enemy traffic, civilian movements; a break in the pattern warns you), your physical condition (check for overlooked wounds; give first aid; drink water; in cold or wet climates add clothing to prevent hypothermia), and your equipment (check what you have and its condition).
- **U — Undue haste makes waste.**
- **R — Remember where you are.**
- **V — Vanquish fear and panic.**
- **I — Improvise.**
- **V — Value living.**
- **A — Act like the natives.**
- **L — Learn basic skills.**

## Psychology of Survival (Chapter 2)

Stress is the body's reaction to pressure. Moderate stress sharpens performance; excess becomes distress — panic, forgetfulness, carelessness, angry outbursts, withdrawal, thoughts of death. Manage it with relaxation skills, time management, assertiveness, and cognitive restructuring. "The will to survive" is "the refusal to give up." Fear, anxiety, anger, frustration, guilt, depression, and loneliness are normal — expect them. Countermeasures: small achievable goals, a schedule, staying busy with survival tasks, hope through a realistic recovery plan.

## The Survival Pattern (Priority Order)

1. **Survival medicine** — always first.
2. **Protection** — fire, shelter, clothing.
3. **Sustenance** — water, then food.
4. **Navigation** — movement toward safety or recovery.

In cold, fire comes before shelter; in heat, water and shade come first. Reassess constantly.

## Survival Planning and Kits (Chapter 3)

- Planning is preparation; include survival considerations in mission planning; know your route and area; keep dental problems fixed and immunizations current.
- Standard kits (Appendix A): over-water survival kit, hot-climate survival kit, aviator survival vest.
- **Layering:** map and compass always on the body; less important items on load-bearing equipment; bulky items in the rucksack. Multi-use items only; never duplicate a function. Case: water-repellent or waterproof, easy to carry or attach, accepts variously sized components, durable.
- **Contents:** first aid items; water purification tablets or drops; fire-starting equipment; signaling items; food-procurement items; shelter items. Examples: lighter, metal match, waterproof matches; snare wire; signaling mirror; wrist compass; fish and snare line; fishhooks; candle; small hand lens; oxytetracycline tablets; solar blanket; surgical blades; butterfly sutures; condoms for water storage; Chap Stick; needle and thread; knife. Weapon only if the situation dictates.

## Basic Survival Medicine — Checklist (Chapter 4)

- **Water baseline:** 2–3 liters/day at 20°C (68°F), average exertion. More with heat, cold, activity, altitude, burns, illness.
- **Hygiene:** wash hands, keep feet dry and sound, protect against insects.
- **Lifesaving order:** airway and breathing → stop severe bleeding (direct pressure, pressure dressings) → treat shock (warm, flat, legs elevated unless head/chest/abdominal injury).
- **Fractures:** splint as found; do not straighten a deformed limb.
- **Heat injury:** cool in shade with water and airflow. **Cold injury:** warm gradually; never rub frostbitten tissue; never thaw a frozen limb if it may refreeze.
- **Rashes:** moist → keep dry; dry → keep moist; do not scratch; vinegar or tannic-acid compress (tea, boiled acorns, hardwood bark) for weeping rashes.
- **Field remedies** (use with caution): chlorine bleach, alcohol, vinegar, concentrated salt water, iodine for fungal infections.
