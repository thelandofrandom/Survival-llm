# Jungle Operations Framework — FM 31-35

## The Framework

"Standard and current combat principles must be applied to a jungle environment with emphasis placed on the modification to those principles that are necessitated by the environment." Jungle operations are characterized by restricted maneuver, slow tempo of operations, close combat under conditions of extremely limited visibility, and difficulty in providing logistical support. Patrolling, operations from forward positions, ambush, and other forms of close combat are common.

## Command and Control

- Centralized planning, decentralized execution.
- Mission-type orders giving maximum latitude to subordinates; the mission must be understood by all personnel.
- Movement to an objective may take longer than estimated, and the enemy may reinforce with a unit initially considered too far away.
- Never conclude that the enemy cannot attack through supposedly impenetrable areas or reinforce in time to affect the mission.

## Reconnaissance, Surveillance, and Security

Extensive patrolling is necessary because other collection sources are limited by poor observation from ground and air. The commander needs timely, accurate information on enemy location, strength, disposition, composition, and activities.

## Offense, Defense, Retrograde

- **Offense:** limited objectives, close control, patrols to develop the situation.
- **Defense:** all-round security, shortened frontages.
- **Retrograde:** a deliberate operation, not a withdrawal under pressure — the same movement and control problems apply in reverse, with security at every halt.

## Riverine Operations

Chapter 5, Section VII — new in this manual. Jungle riverine operations exploit the rivers that are often the only lines of communication through the jungle. Rivers are both highway and obstacle: they carry supply and troops, and they channelize movement into predictable routes that the enemy can watch and ambush. The manual treats the riverine environment (including the middle sector / central valley) with its own tactical considerations for movement, fire support, and base siting.

## Stability Operations

Chapter 5, Section VIII — also new. Stability operations are the population-centered work of village security, civic action, and counterinsurgency. The manual's framing: jungle operations in 1969 were inseparable from the political fight for the population, and influencing the population — offering assistance, especially medical assistance — is a combat task, not an afterthought.
