# Combat and Combat Support — FM 31-35

## The Combined-Arms Picture (Chapter 3)

Every arm in jungle conditions:

- **Infantry:** the decisive arm — the fight is won by dismounted infantry.
- **Armor:** limited by terrain; used against definitely located targets.
- **Artillery:** fires adjusted by sound where visibility prevents observation.
- **Air defense / air defense artillery; naval gunfire and tactical air support.**
- **Army aviation.**
- **CBR operations.**
- **Engineers.**
- **Communications:** a full section of its own — the canopy and terrain defeat line-of-sight radios, and wire is hard to lay and maintain. The answer is redundancy: multiple means, relay stations, strict communications discipline, with the expectation that units will fight for periods with no contact with higher headquarters.
- **Intelligence:** most information comes from patrolling; the enemy's ability to move through supposedly impenetrable terrain, live off the land, and choose the time and place of contact is the central intelligence problem.
- **Military police.**

The recurring theme: every arm works, but none works the way it works in open terrain, and fire support is hard to control — timing, communications, and observation all degrade in the jungle.

## Combat Service Support (Chapter 4)

- **Supply:** anticipate requirements well in advance; reconnoiter supply routes and water points continuously; develop alternates.
- **Maintenance, medical, transportation, personnel management.**
- **Headquarters:** site headquarters in an inconspicuous place, with provision for weapons, ammunition, and enemy contact; a reduced headquarters may be necessary where the jungle will not support a full one.
- **Waterways and air resupply** are primary means, not expedients. The wet season, which makes rivers navigable, can be operationally advantageous for supply and evacuation.
- **Medical:** field sanitation, personal hygiene, and disease prevention are the medical service's main jungle work; evacuation planning must account for the jungle's distances in time, not miles.
