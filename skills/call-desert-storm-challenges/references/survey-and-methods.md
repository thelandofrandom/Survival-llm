# Survey and Methods — ARI Research Report 1633 (1993)

Distilled from Halpin and Keene, *Desert Storm Challenges: An Overview of Desert Storm Survey Responses*, ARI Research Report 1633, January 1993.

## The Survey

10,000 surveys distributed (5,000+ in Saudi Arabia in April 1991, the rest in CONUS through July 1991); **2,463 usable returns**. Findings are on 7-point scales unless noted; percentages are of those who answered that item, not of the whole survey.

## The Respondents

- 1,864 officers (52% captain, 21% major), 478 NCOs including 111 sergeants major, 58 warrant officers.
- By form: 415 Combat Commander, 132 CSS Commander, 1,916 Staff.
- 55% at division level or lower; the rest at corps and echelons above corps. The sample covers 12 divisions and separate brigades and 62 corps and EAC elements.
- 43% had at least one NTC rotation, 36% JRTC, 39% CMTC, 39% BCTP.
- 400 had prior combat experience: 226 Vietnam, 47 Grenada, 117 Panama, 20 other including Lebanon and the Korean DMZ.

## What the Report Is — and Is Not

The report is explicitly **not lessons learned**: it "provides a description of data available from which lessons learned may be derived," with more detailed analyses deferred. It describes what 2,463 Desert Storm participants reported about the challenges of the war, organized by theme.

## Limitations

- All responses carry equal weight regardless of rank, position, or experience (hearsay risk).
- Some respondents filled the wrong form — combat commanders filled Staff forms and vice versa; one brigade commander returned both.
- No light/heavy, G-2/G-4, or brigade/EAC breakdowns.
- Opinions are not matched to actual battle experience.
- The n varies by item because not every respondent answered every question; a rating's strength depends on its respondent count.

## Companion Report

This is the companion to ARI Technical Report 970, *How Well Did the Combat Training Centers Prepare Units for Combat?* (see the `call-combat-training-centers-desert-storm` skill), which asked the same population what surprises their CTC experience had not prepared them for. The two reports share the survey: 10,000 questionnaires, 2,463 returns.
