# Open Water and Abandon Ship — MCRP 8-10B.6

## Survival Mindset

Water survival begins with the proper mindset. Any water- or air-based craft you board becomes a hazard when it causes you to deliberately or accidentally abandon it. Before embarking, learn about the craft:

- How many life preservers and lifeboats/rafts are aboard?
- Where are the life preservers and lifeboats/rafts located?
- What type of unit survival equipment is aboard?
- Are individual survival kits issued to each person aboard?
- How much food, water, and medicine do the survival kits contain? When was the last time the contents were inspected for proper quantities and shelf life expiration?
- Is there sufficient survival equipment available for the number of personnel?
- How many other personnel are there aboard, and where are they located?
- What are the egress procedures for the ship, boat, watercraft, amphibious assault vehicle, or aircraft?

## Abandoning Ship

When you embark on a Navy ship, you will receive instructions on how to abandon ship from Navy personnel. If given the order to abandon ship, report to your designated assembly area and put on a life preserver. **Do NOT inflate the life preserver until you are clear of the ship.** Torn life preservers will not inflate and inflated life preservers can block you, and those behind you, from exiting the ship. A flotation device that has been inflated may also burst if you jump from a significant height.

**Do NOT remove your clothing, boots, or shoes before abandoning ship.** Your trousers and blouse may be the only flotation devices available if your life preserver is faulty or becomes damaged, and your clothes can provide some insulation from the cold water. The soft cover is lightweight and prevents skin burns by protecting your head from direct sunlight.

### Jettisoning equipment

Equipment should be kept properly packed and waterproofed in case you have to abandon ship. When entering the water from a height of **15 feet (4.6 meters) or less** and equipped with a proper flotation device or a waterproofed pack, you may maintain an essential, small survival kit. Entering the water from a height greater than 15 feet wearing your pack, gas mask, vest, or rifle could cause injury upon impact. If you are forced to abandon ship, your sole mission is to survive. Upon impact with the water, the helmet will "cup" air inside of it. The chin strap may also create a "hanging effect" as you submerge from the force of the fall. **Therefore, you should remove your helmet and gas mask before abandoning ship.**

### Abandon ship technique

1. Place your hands on their opposite shoulders, forming a criss-cross pattern.
2. Step to the edge of the ship's deck. If you are not on the top level, look up to ensure there are no other personnel attempting to enter the water from higher levels and that there is no falling debris. Check the water below for debris or survivors. If the water is not clear, move to another location.
3. **Do NOT hold your nose as you abandon ship.** The force of impact could jar your arm and hand and cause you to break your nose.
4. Step off the side of the ship with a smooth, **30-inch stride. Do NOT dive off the ship. Do NOT look down at the water. Look straight ahead.** Looking down at the water can render you unconscious or cause injuries upon impact.
5. Bring your trailing leg forward during the fall. Cross your trailing leg behind your leading leg.
6. Keep your head parallel to the water's surface until hitting the water.
7. Remain in the abandon ship position until your descent into the water has almost stopped. The weight imbalances in your body may cause you to be in a "J" shape under the water. Once your downward motion has ceased, your feet may be parallel with the ocean bottom or you may be nearly inverted with your feet over your head. Pause briefly and allow the natural buoyancy of your torso to bring your body to a nearly upright position.
8. Floating debris can be hazardous. Swim upward, extending one arm (hand shaped as a fist) upward to feel for obstructions. If you encounter debris, try to push it away or surface in a different location.
9. Swim away from the ship. **Do NOT look back at the ship.** Looking back could slow your movement away from the area. Your objective is to leave the area as quickly as possible because equipment and debris may be falling from or spilling out of the ship; additional casualties can result if individuals abandoning the ship fall on top of swimmers already in the water; and swimmers close to the sinking ship may get pulled underneath the water by the suctioning effect of the ship as it goes under.

## Surface Burning Oil Swim

After you have abandoned ship, rise to the surface — but fuel from sinking ships or downed aircraft will float on the surface of the water. You must move clear of the floating fuel by swimming away from the ship or aircraft as soon as possible. Either swim **upwind (into the wind)** of the ship/aircraft or swim **against the current**. Either method allows you to move away from the fuel and the wind/current will push the fuel past you. To properly execute a surface burning oil swim:

1. Extend your arms overhead as far as possible.
2. Wave your arms back and forth vigorously to splash a hole while moving upward.
3. Splash as long as possible to push burning fuel away from the surfacing area.
4. Use your arms and hands to sweep away fuel and debris.
5. Kick your legs in a constant breast stroke kick.
6. Extend your arms (palms outward) forward on the surface, arms shoulder-width apart.
7. Pull your hands in and back toward the chest.
8. Stop your hands in front of your face and rotate them so that your palms face forward (roughly halfway out of the water).
9. Sweep your arms forward to a full extension at the shoulder width. This splashes debris, oil, or burning liquids aside. To reduce the chance of fatigue, use two short splashes to the front to extend the path.
10. Repeat as necessary while swimming clear of the area.
