# Desert Operations — FM 31-25

## Principles and Fundamentals

- **Principles of war:** objective, offensive, mass, economy of force, maneuver, unity of command, security, surprise, and simplicity. They tend to reinforce one another but may conflict depending on circumstances, so the degree of application of any specific principle varies with the situation.
- **Fundamentals of combat:** employ the soldier effectively; use the environment to best advantage — commanders use every advantage afforded by terrain, air space, weather, and time.

## What the Desert Gives the Attacker

- Opportunities for imaginative and efficient employment of weapons.
- Reasonably unrestricted lines of communication essential to control decisive maneuver and its support.
- Many avenues of mounted and dismounted approach permitting application of combat power through mobility and maneuver.
- In offensive operations, the tactical advantages of retaining terrain are not normally exploitable, since there is generally a lack of key terrain — except in the vicinity of communication centers, logistical areas, port facilities, and mountain passes. Seizure of terrain dominating these critical objectives is usually important.

## Forms of Desert Operations (Chapter 2)

- **Offensive operations:** mobility and maneuver through the desert's many avenues of approach; seize terrain dominating communication centers, logistical areas, port facilities, and mountain passes.
- **Defensive operations:** the opposite problem from the jungle — little concealment and little cover, so the defense depends on dispersion, depth, and the ability to move, and on water and supply, which are the defender's vulnerability as much as the attacker's.
- **Retrograde operations:** withdrawal over open ground, with the same water, fuel, and control problems in reverse.
- **Airmobile operations:** helicopters exploit the desert's openness and the absence of roads.
- **Counterguerrilla operations:** the desert's openness aids detection but also aids the guerrilla's movement; security of water points and lines of communication is central.
- **Chemical, biological, radiological defense:** CBR operations in desert conditions, where wind and temperature govern the behavior of agents.

## Support (Chapter 3)

- **Combat support** and **combat service support** in the desert: water, fuel, and maintenance dominate.
- Water must be brought in and stored; sand destroys machinery; distances are measured in hours of driving over open ground.
- **Appendix B** gives maintenance techniques and procedures for desert conditions.

## Training (Chapter 4)

- **Individual training:** the desert environment, water discipline, heat-injury prevention, navigation in featureless terrain.
- **Unit training:** desert maneuver, with emphasis on the fundamentals of Chapter 2 — mobility, maneuver, and use of the environment — practiced before deployment.
- The manual's North Africa lesson: a force that learns the desert in contact pays for the lesson in heat casualties and lost vehicles.
