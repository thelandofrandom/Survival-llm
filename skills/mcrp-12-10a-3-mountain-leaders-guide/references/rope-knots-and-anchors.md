# Rope Management, Knots, and Anchors

Distilled from MCRP 12-10A.3, Chapter 4 (U.S. Marine Corps, Apr 2018).

## Rope Care and Inspection

- Inspect before every use: cuts, abrasion, heat glazing, soft spots, chemical or grit contamination.
- Keep a **rope log**: serial number, history of use, falls taken, inspections. Retire rope on condition, not on a calendar.
- A rope that has held a factor-2 fall has done its job once and may not do it again.

## Coiling

- **Mountain coil** — for carrying.
- **Butterfly coil** — for rapid deployment.
- A badly coiled rope is a tangled rope; a tangled rope at the top of a cliff is a mission failure.

## Mountaineering Knots — Four Classes

| Class | Purpose | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| I — End-of-the-rope | Stopper; keep rope from running through a device | Figure-eight stopper, overhand stopper |
| II — Anchor | Tie rope to the anchor; a life hangs from these | Figure-eight on a bight, bowline and variants |
| III — Middle-of-the-rope | Clip in mid-party; build anchors | Butterfly / alpine butterfly |
| IV — Special | Ascend, descend, haul, rescue | Prusik, Klemheist and other friction hitches |

- **Knot strength:** every knot weakens a rope. Know the approximate strength loss for the knots you use and test to the manual's standards.
- A knot that is not **dressed and set** is not a finished knot. Tie it, dress it, set it, back it up.

## Anchor Types

- **Natural anchors** (preferred where they exist): trees, rock horns, boulders, chockstones — strong, simple, fast.
- **Artificial anchors**: pitons, nuts, cams, bolts — placed where nature gives nothing.
- **Unidirectional anchors:** loaded in one direction only.
- **Multidirectional anchors:** loaded from several directions. The system must match the load.

## Anchor Systems

- **Single-point construction:** fast, simple, single point of failure.
- **Multi-point construction:** distributes load, provides redundancy.
- Standard systems: in-line two-point anchor; picket hold fast (snow/soft ground); deadman (buried anchor for snow); equalized rope anchor; equalized cordelette anchor; web-o-lette method; American triangle; self-equalizing cordelette anchor.
- **Carabiners and slings:** locking carabiners at the anchor and anywhere failure would be catastrophic. Gates closed and locked. Keep rope off sharp edges — pad or reroute.

## Principles for Evaluating an Anchor System

Every anchor must be:

1. **Strong** — each piece solid.
2. **Redundant** — no single point of failure.
3. **Equalized** — load shared across pieces.
4. **Non-extending** — if one piece fails, the system does not shock-load the rest.

Evaluate every anchor against all four before anyone commits weight to it.
