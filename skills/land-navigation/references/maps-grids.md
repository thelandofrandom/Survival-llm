## Map scales

| Scale | 1 cm on map | 1 inch on map | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1:25,000 | 250 m | ~0.4 mi | Detailed tactical navigation |
| 1:50,000 | 500 m | ~0.8 mi | Standard military map of choice |
| 1:100,000 | 1,000 m | ~1.6 mi | Larger area, less detail |

## Marginal information — read the margin first

Sheet name and number, series and edition, declination diagram (true north, grid north, magnetic north and the angles between them), scale bar, contour interval, grid zone designation, datum notes. A map used with the wrong declination or datum puts you in the wrong place with great confidence.

## Colors and symbols

- Black: man-made features (buildings, roads, boundaries)
- Brown: relief (contour lines)
- Blue: water
- Green: vegetation
- Red: major roads and populated areas
- Purple: updates

## Contour lines and relief

Contour lines join points of equal elevation. The **contour interval** (vertical distance between adjacent lines) is printed in the margin — commonly 10 or 20 meters at 1:50,000. Closely spaced lines = steep ground; widely spaced = gentle. A V pointing uphill marks a valley or drainage; a V pointing downhill marks a ridge or spur. Index contours (every fifth line, thicker) carry elevation numbers. A saddle is the low point between two hills and the high point between two valleys — the natural crossing point.

## UTM and MGRS

The world is divided into 6°-wide UTM zones; between 84°N and 80°S, each zone is covered by 100,000-meter squares identified by two letters. A coordinate is read **right, then up** — easting first, then northing. Parts: grid zone designation (e.g., 16S), 100,000-meter square letters (e.g., GJ), numbers (easting + northing).

## Grid precision

| Digits per half | Total digits | Precision |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | 8 | 1,000 m |
| 5 | 10 | 100 m |
| 6 | 12 | 10 m |

Standard field reporting: **8-digit grid (10-meter precision)**; 6-digit (100 m) when the exact point does not matter. Always state the precision — a 6-digit and an 8-digit grid for the same place look alike and are not interchangeable.

## The G-M angle

Grid north and magnetic north differ by the **G-M angle**, shown on the declination diagram. Conversion rule (per the margin notes on each sheet): **add the G-M angle when converting grid azimuth to magnetic azimuth if it is easterly; subtract if it is westerly** — or follow the diagram's printed rule, which is authoritative for that sheet. Declination changes over time; check the diagram's date. A 1° error is about 17 meters per kilometer walked.
