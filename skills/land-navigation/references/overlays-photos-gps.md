# Overlays, Aerial Photographs, GPS, and Training

Source: FM 3-25.26, Chapters 1 (Training Strategy), 7 (Overlays), 8 (Aerial Photographs), 14 (Unit Sustainment), Appendices A–K.

## Training strategy (Ch. 1)

Crawl-walk-run: classroom (map reading) → practical exercises (pace count, resection, intersection) → day courses → night courses and adverse conditions. Land navigation is a perishable skill — it decays without practice.

## Overlays (Ch. 7)

A transparent sheet laid over the map carrying operational graphics — boundaries, routes, objectives, control measures — registered to the map by at least two grid ticks. Overlays keep the base map clean and let graphics be shared, updated, and removed without damaging the map.

## Aerial photographs (Ch. 8)

Photographs show the ground as it is, not as the mapmaker drew it: they reveal vegetation, water, and structures the map may miss. The manual covers:

- Types of photographs and film; numbering and titling information
- Scale determination; indexing; orienting the photograph
- Point designation grid; identification of features; stereovision (viewing overlapping photos in 3D to judge relief)
- A photo and a map used together are better than either alone. A photo is a snapshot; a map is a survey — each goes out of date in different ways.

## GPS (Appendices J and K)

The Global Positioning System gives position, speed, and direction anywhere on Earth — when it works. The manual's doctrine: **GPS is a tool, not a crutch.** Batteries die, signals are blocked by terrain and canopy, and GPS can be jammed or denied. Use GPS to confirm map-and-compass navigation, never to replace it, and keep map-and-compass skills current so a dead GPS is an inconvenience, not an emergency. The Precision Lightweight GPS Receiver (PLGR) appendix covers the military receiver; the principles apply to any receiver, including a phone.

## Unit sustainment (Ch. 14)

Sustainment is scheduled practice: regular navigation exercises, night courses, and testing. A unit that does not practice navigation loses it — the skill is perishable at both the individual and the unit level.
