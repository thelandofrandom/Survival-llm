# Distribution, Film Strips, Recognition Slides — FM 21-7 (Section III, ¶¶8–15; ¶¶25–27)

## Distribution (Section III)

- **General (¶8):** Chief Signal Officer distributes films, strips, slides, and equipment in the continental U.S. to AGF/ASF units through service-command film libraries. Oversea theaters/departments/bases/defense commands may establish film and equipment exchanges (War Dept. Memo 850-44, 12 Aug 1944). AAF production/distribution is the Commanding General, AAF (AAF Regs 50-19, 65-1, 95-5).
- **Libraries (¶9):** central library/exchange at each service command HQ — stocked with all approved films, temporary loans to all organizations (requests state number, title, size 16-mm/35-mm). Sublibraries at troop concentrations stock constant-need films; occasional films come on loan from central. Auxiliary libraries serve specialized schools/units. Reception center libraries stock a basic-training set in order: TF 21-2067 Introduction to the Army; 8-1238 Sex Hygiene (or 8-2060 Pick Up); 8-155 Personal Hygiene; 21-2048 Military Courtesy; 11-235 Articles of War; 19-2034 A.W.O.L. and Desertion; 8-2047 First Aid for Battle Injuries; 8-2049 First Aid for Non-Battle Injuries; 7-248 Instruction of the Soldier, Dismounted, Without Arms; 30-2033 Sucker Bait.
- **Circuits (¶10):** geographically grouped activities booked on one circuit; route sheet assigns play dates; copy pasted in the film can; each installation forwards the print to the next location on the shipping date. Posters and booking notices mailed ~2 weeks ahead. "Why We Fight," G.I. Movie Weekly, "Brief for Invasion," "War Speeds Up" distributed by circuit.
- **Supply (¶¶11–12):** Signal Corps Photographic Center supplies only AGF/ASF-approved films; no commercial films without approval — the approved list is this manual. Film summaries reach libraries ~40 days before release; sublibraries request prints via the central library, which consolidates the service command order to the Photographic Center, 35-11 35th Ave., Long Island City, NY. Combat Bulletins, some Film Bulletins, and miscellaneous releases cannot be pre-notified; initial distribution is set at the Center. Reorders: sublibrary → central library. Stock/use reports periodically; inventory on Monthly Film Library Report SAU-12. Film strips: advance notice ~20 days; projectors one per battalion or higher HQ; units may retain strips as long as needed.
- **Obsolete/damaged (¶13):** obsolete prints withdrawn at once, unwound from reels, disposed per AR 380-5 ¶32b (15 Mar 1944); reels/cans returned to central library. Minor damage spliced in the sublibrary; severe damage exchanged at central library.
- **Slides/equipment (¶¶14–15):** slides via Photographic Center → central libraries → sublibraries, bases of issue per ¶26; oversea only on request. Projection equipment by requisition through Signal channels (ASF Circular 258; T/A 20-2 domestic; T/O&E 11-500 oversea).

## Film Strips (¶25)

AAF strips (FS 1-4 Lathe; 1-5 Milling Machine; 1-6 Planers; 1-7 Shaper; 1-8 Aerodynamics; 1-9 Classification of Engine Types; 1-11 Forced Landings; 1-12 Using an Aircraft Machine Gun; 1-14 Browning Aircraft Machine Gun, .30 Cal. M2) and the general SFS series — list runs pages 62–210 of the manual, each entry with number, year, and a subject note.

## Recognition Film Slides (¶26)

2×2-in. slides; each aircraft set includes silhouettes and flight views from various angles. Bases of issue: Set 1 (U.S. Army aircraft) — 1 per 3,000 men other than AAA; Set 2 (U.S. Army/Navy, British, Russian, German, Italian, Japanese aircraft) — 1 per AAA battalion; Set 3 (RAF, German, Italian) — staging areas to ETO/North Africa, 1 per 3,000 men other than AAA; Set 4 (U.S. Navy and Japanese) — staging areas to Alaska/Hawaii/Southwest Pacific, 1 per 3,000 men other than AAA; armored vehicle Sets 1 (U.S.) and 2 (British, Russian, German, Italian, Japanese); naval vessel Set 1 for coast artillery harbor defense (2 sets Coast Artillery School; 5 sets Coast Artillery RTC). Armored examples: T303 Light Tank M5/M5A1 "Gen. Stuart"; T307 Medium Tank M3; T313 Medium Tank M4; T320 105-mm Howitzer Motor Carriage M8; T321 3-in. Gun Motor Carriage M10.

## Phonograph Records (¶27)

Three double-faced 12-in. 78 rpm records, six 5-minute dramatized Radio Transmission Security Violations: (1) Ex-Sergeant; (2) Hawaiian Islands; (3) West Coast Air Base; (4) Bombing Mission; (5) South Sea Island; (6) Secret Flight. One incident every day or two during code practice/radio procedure — never all six at once; playable on any phonograph or over code-table headphone circuits. To interested units on request.
