# Using Training Films — FM 21-7 (Sections I–II, ¶¶1–7)

## Definitions (¶2)

- **Training Film (TF):** approved War Department doctrine; specific topic; definite time and place in training; mostly 16-mm and 35-mm.
- **Film Bulletin (FB):** new developments, not necessarily approved doctrine; for officers and enlisted men; 16-mm and 35-mm.
- **Combat Bulletin (CB):** latest significant combat scenes from all fronts, with commentary; a weekly picture of the war.
- **Miscellaneous Film:** special productions; includes adopted films from other services whose doctrine may not fully conform.
- **War Information Film (OF):** background, history, progress of the war; allies and enemies.
- **Film Strip:** still pictures on 35-mm strip, used with lectures/demonstrations; some with sound recordings.
- **Recognition Film Slide:** 2×2-in. slides — silhouettes and views from various angles — for aircraft, armored vehicle, and naval vessel recognition.
- **Film Library:** depository for equipment and films; loan service to units. **Distribution Division:** Signal Corps Photographic Center, Long Island City, NY; supplies films/strips/slides/literature and supervises libraries and oversea exchanges.

## Purpose and Advantages (¶¶3–4)

Films present subjects vividly, interestingly, accurately; they supplement but never supplant the instructor. Alone they have limited value; used by a resourceful instructor under sound principles they are invaluable. Improper use — unrelated films for a prolonged period in a hot room, with no indication of what to look for — teaches little, and without follow-up examinations, discussions, or applicatory exercises the material is not learned well. Advantages: standardized instruction; emphasis on fundamentals; demonstrations brought to the troops; repeatable without repeating field labor; every man sees all phases, with close-ups; expert troops and instructors demonstrate; animation, slow motion, time lapse, miniature and micro-photography show hidden action.

## The Five-Step Method (¶5)

1. **Integrate** in the training program — orient, build attitudes/morale, instruct in details; proficiency comes from doing, after seeing how.
2. **Preview** — study the film in advance; no film is perfect; pick key points, items to omit, obsolete portions needing explanation; plan intro and follow-up; decide whether to split the showing.
3. **Introduce** — what it is about, why shown, battle importance, key points, relation to earlier training/duties; or questions/problems the film will solve; or a demonstration.
4. **Show** — instructor stays with the group, ready to adjust; do not show too long; break long films into parts; may turn off sound and commentate (e.g., over obsolete portions) but commentary must conform to approved doctrine.
5. **Follow up** — immediate practice where possible (gas-mask film → gas-mask drill; map film → map reading); otherwise oral discussion, oral/written quizzes on key points (Quiz Cards for yes/no), or demonstrations (e.g., good/bad camouflage exhibits).

**Film References (¶5f):** published for training films and certain film bulletins released/revised after 1 July 1944 — synopsis, use and place in training, suggested intro, follow-up activities, correlated materials, suggested quiz, special notes. Replace the former Film Digests; distributed by The Adjutant General to film libraries.

## Required Viewing (¶¶5g–6)

- **"Fighting Men" series** (every combat soldier; not too early in training): TF 21-1007 Snafu; 21-1018 Keep It Clean; 21-1019 Crack That Tank; 21-1020 How To Get Killed In One Easy Lesson; 21-1021 Wise Guy; 21-1024 Kill or Be Killed; 21-1026 On Your Toes; 21-1027 Latrinograms; 21-1028 Heroes; 21-1029 On Your Own; 21-1375 Time Out; 21-2014 Baptism of Fire; 21-2015 Secret Weapon; 21-2056 By Your Command.
- **Required for all personnel:** TF 30-1315 Postal Censorship; War Information Films OF 1 Prelude to War, OF 2 The Nazis Strike, OF 3 Divide and Conquer, OF 4 The Battle of Britain, OF 5 The Battle of Russia, OF 16 Know Your Ally Britain, OF (RF) 51 The Negro Soldier.

## Film Strips (¶7)

Preview and study first; Illustrated Instructor's References exist for some (marked "Reference" in ¶26). Continuous-development strips: build the lesson around them like a film. Chart-like strips: use the strip or selected frames for a specific lesson phase. Most effective with small groups; presentable in partially darkened rooms or outdoors with a shadow box and current (TM 21-250).
