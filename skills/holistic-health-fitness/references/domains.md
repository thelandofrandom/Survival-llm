# The Five Domains in Depth — FM 7-22

## Nutritional Readiness

**Foundational nutrition:** a healthy eating pattern of nutrient-dense whole foods — vegetables from all subgroups (dark green, red and orange, legumes, starchy); fruits, especially whole fruits; lean protein; healthy fats; complex carbohydrates. The pattern matters more than any single food or supplement.

**Body composition:** managed through the eating pattern and training load together — not crash dieting, which destroys performance and readiness.

**Operational nutrition and event fueling:**
- Fuel **before**: carbohydrate for glycogen.
- Fuel **during** prolonged activity: carbohydrate and fluids.
- Recover **after**: carbohydrate plus protein, fluids, electrolytes. The first hour after hard work is when the body is most receptive to refueling — post-event recovery nutrition is part of the event, not an afterthought.

**Environment:** heat, cold, and altitude all change nutrition and hydration needs. **Hydration is the constant:** poor hydration degrades performance, raises resting heart rate, and impairs cognition long before it produces thirst.

**Therapeutic nutrition:** injuries and medical conditions change nutritional needs; manage them with medical guidance, not guesswork.

## Mental Readiness

| Capability | What it covers |
|---|---|
| Cognitive | Attention, working memory, decision-making under stress |
| Emotional | Recognizing and regulating emotion, managing stress |
| Interpersonal | Communication, teamwork, leadership under pressure |

**Training:** deliberate practice under stress — scenario training, controlled exposure to pressure, after-action review of decisions, and stress-management techniques (controlled breathing, self-talk, goal-setting, visualization). The goal: a Soldier who can think clearly when the body is exhausted and the situation is ambiguous.

## Spiritual Readiness

- **What it is:** meaning, purpose, and connection — the "why" that sustains a Soldier through hardship.
- **General practices:** reflection, values clarification, service, community.
- **Religious practices:** worship, prayer, study, dietary and clothing practices — for those who hold them.
- **Roles:** commanders create the conditions (time, access, respect); the Chaplain Corps (unit ministry team, garrison chaplain's office) leads development and provides resources; the individual owns it.
- **Doctrine:** free exercise and religious liberty, the Establishment Clause, and pluralism are addressed directly — spiritual readiness is inclusive by doctrine, and assessment of it is the individual's own.

## Sleep Readiness

- **Standard:** most Soldiers need **7 to 9 hours of sleep every 24 hours** to maximize health and sustain performance.
- **Continuum, not threshold:** more sleep always produces improved performance. There is no clear cutoff below which effectiveness is compromised and above which it is sustained.
- **Sleep continuity** (unbroken sleep) matters as much as duration.
- **Promoting healthy sleep:** dark, cool, quiet environment; consistent pre-sleep routine (wind-down, no screens or stimulants late); consistent bed and wake times.
- **Leadership's role:** set conditions, lead by example, educate and encourage, prioritize and plan. Sleep-deprived performance is measurably equivalent to intoxication.
- **Planning for insufficient sleep:** bank sleep before a known deficit (sleep extension); use naps strategically (short naps restore alertness); manage sleep debt on return (recovery sleep works); use caffeine timed correctly, light exposure, and activity to sustain readiness.
- **When to seek expert help:** persistent insomnia, loud snoring with witnessed apneas, excessive daytime sleepiness, or sleep problems that do not resolve with the basics — medical issues, not discipline issues.

## Special Conditioning (Chapter 12)

- **Rehabilitation and Reconditioning Program (RCP):** rehabilitation (return to function, under medical supervision) → reconditioning (return to full duty and fitness).
- **Rule:** better to press on too slowly than too soon — re-injury costs months, patience costs weeks.
- **Pregnancy and postpartum physical training:** progressive, supervised, individualized — with dedicated sessions, exercises, and education blocks.

## Coaching and Schedules (Chapters 13–14)

- **H2F coaching:** building readiness through holistic health, proactive health, physical exercise, surroundings, personal development, nutrition, recharge, family/friends/colleagues, spirit, and the power of the mind. The coach translates doctrine into the individual Soldier's week.
- **Schedules:** complete H2F training schedules for every phase — Future Soldier Program, Basic Combat Training, Fitness Training Unit ACFT Improvement, Advanced Individual Training / One Station Unit Training, first unit of assignment, sustaining phase (Build 1, Build 2, Peak 1, Peak 2, Combat, Transition), Reserve Component, and remotely located Soldiers. The schedules are the doctrine made into a calendar.
