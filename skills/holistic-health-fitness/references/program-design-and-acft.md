# Program Design and the ACFT — FM 7-22

## Soldier Assessment

Program design starts with assessment: movement screening, strength and endurance testing, body composition — and sleep, nutrition, and stress status. A program for a Soldier sleeping 5 hours a night is a different program from one for a Soldier sleeping 8.

## Components of Physical Fitness

- **Structural requirements:** body composition, bone and joint health.
- **Physiological capacity:** strength, power, endurance, VO2 max.
- **Physical components:** the trainable qualities, addressed in proportion to the Soldier's job demands.

## Training Session Structure

1. **Preparation** — warm-up, movement preparation.
2. **Strength** — power before strength.
3. **Endurance** — strength before endurance; skill work before fatigue.
4. **Recovery** — cool-down.

Heavy days and light days are planned, not accidental.

## Karvonen Formula (corrected in Change 2)

Target HR = ((HRmax − HRrest) × %intensity) + HRrest, where HRmax ≈ 220 − age.

**Worked example:** 25-year-old, resting HR 60, training at 70% intensity.
HRmax ≈ 195; HR reserve = 195 − 60 = 135; 70% × 135 = 94.5; target HR ≈ 155 bpm.

**Resting heart rate as a readiness signal:** daily spikes above the normal range may indicate overtraining or the need to reduce intensity. Poor sleep, nutrition, hydration, and stress control all raise resting heart rate.

## The ACFT — Six Events (FM 7-22, 2020 edition)

In order:
1. **3-repetition maximum deadlift** — maximal strength.
2. **Standing power throw** — explosive power (phosphagen pathway).
3. **Hand-release push-up** — muscular endurance.
4. **Sprint-drag-carry** — anaerobic capacity and loaded movement.
5. **Leg tuck** — core and grip endurance.
6. **2-mile run** — aerobic endurance (oxidative pathway).

**The test is the assessment, not the program.** Training only the test events produces a Soldier who can pass a test and fail a mission.

> Note: later Army policy replaced the leg tuck with the plank. This manual's edition uses the leg tuck.

## Ruck Marching

- Trained progressively: load, distance, and pace increase over weeks — never all at once.
- Principles of physical readiness apply: precision (correct movement and load), progression (gradual overload), integration (all qualities together).

## Army Water Survival Training

- Progresses from basic survival swimmer to combat survival swimmer.
- Safety is the controlling condition for all water training.
