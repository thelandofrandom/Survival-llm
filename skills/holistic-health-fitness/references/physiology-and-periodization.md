# Physiology and Periodization — FM 7-22

## Energy Pathways

| Pathway | Duration | Intensity | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| Phosphagen (phosphocreatine) | 5–10 seconds | Very high power | 3–5 s sprints, standing power throw, max lifts |
| Glycolytic | Tens of seconds to minutes | High | Repeated hard efforts, 400-m runs |
| Oxidative phosphorylation | Minutes to hours | Moderate | Long runs, ruck marching, sustained work |

- Phosphagen depletes rapidly and needs long rest to re-energize; without enough rest, ATP loss degrades performance.
- Glycolytic work produces lactate; training improves the ability to sustain hard efforts.
- Oxidative work runs on glycogen; when glycogen runs out, performance collapses ("hitting the wall"). Avoid depleting glycogen before prolonged aerobic activity — e.g., the anaerobic sprint-drag-carry before the 2-mile run.

## VO2 Max

- The highest amount of oxygen usable during maximal aerobic effort — the ceiling of endurance performance.
- Trainable: training raises it, detraining lowers it. The single best predictor of aerobic readiness.

## Anatomy and Adaptation

| System | Adaptation rule |
|---|---|
| Bone | Adapts to load (Wolff's law): progressive loading strengthens it; sudden overload breaks it |
| Muscle | Adapts to the training imposed: heavy loads → strength; high reps → endurance; power work → explosiveness |
| Nervous system | First adapter — early strength gains are mostly neural (better recruitment, coordination); technique and practice matter as much as load |
| Neuroendocrine | Testosterone, cortisol, growth hormone respond to training stress and recovery; chronic under-recovery suppresses the anabolic response |
| Cardiorespiratory | Adaptation is stroke volume and the muscles' ability to extract oxygen |

**Adaptation rule:** the body adapts to the stress imposed — and only to that stress. Specificity is absolute. Progressive overload drives adaptation; sudden jumps drive injury.

## Training Cycles

| Cycle | Length | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Microcycle | ~1 week | Basic building block |
| Mesocycle | Several microcycles (2–6 weeks) | Focused on one quality |
| Macrocycle | Months to a season | Full arc from base to peak |

## Integrated Periodization — The Phases

| Phase | Character |
|---|---|
| Base | General physical preparedness, movement competency, aerobic foundation; high volume, moderate intensity |
| Build | Specific qualities for the mission — strength, power, loaded movement; intensity rises, volume falls |
| Peak 1 | Sharpening — highest intensity, lowest volume, timed to a test or deployment milestone |
| Combat / Peak 2 | Sustainment under operational demand; maintain what Base and Build created |
| Recovery | Planned load reduction so the body supercompensates — adaptation happens in recovery, not in training |

**Planning rule:** every training week has a purpose inside a mesocycle, and every mesocycle has a purpose inside the macrocycle. Training without a phase is exercise; training with a phase is preparation.
