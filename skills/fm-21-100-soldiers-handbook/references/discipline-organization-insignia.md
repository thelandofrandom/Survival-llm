# Discipline, Organization, Insignia — FM 21-100 (Chapters 1–4, ¶¶1–53)

## General Information (Chapter 1)

- **Group life (¶¶1–3):** fit into the squad, mess, and barracks; the Army is free citizens chosen from a free people, and the soldier upholds its reputation.
- **NCOs, officers, civilians (¶¶4–14):** prompt obedience through proper channels; courtesy to civilians, who judge the whole Army by the conduct of its members in public.
- **Military obligations and the Articles of War (¶¶15–19):** obedience to lawful orders, loyalty, readiness; the Articles of War — the military law of 1940, later replaced by the Uniform Code of Military Justice (1951) — including absence without leave and desertion.
- **Post and station activities (¶¶20–25):** reveille, mess, duty assignments, the daily round.

## Discipline and Courtesy (Chapter 2, ¶¶26–33)

- **Discipline (¶¶26–29):** prompt, willing obedience formed by habit — on time, equipment ready, the job done without being told twice.
- **Courtesy (¶¶30–33):** saluting, addressing officers by rank, attention when addressed, courtesies to the flag and the national anthem. Courtesy is the visible sign discipline is working, and the first thing civilians notice.

## Insignia (Chapter 3, ¶¶34–44)

- **Arms and services (¶¶34–40):** insignia identifying branch — infantry, cavalry, artillery, engineers, signal, medical, quartermaster, ordnance, chemical warfare, air corps, and others.
- **Officers and NCOs (¶¶41–44):** rank insignia, so the soldier can recognize rank and branch at a glance.

## Organization (Chapter 4, ¶¶45–53)

Squad (the smallest fighting unit) → platoon → company → battalion → regiment → brigade → division → corps → army. The chapter explains what each arm and service does, so the soldier understands where his unit sits in the whole.
