# Fieldcraft: Guard, Marches, Camps, Compass, Maps, Security — FM 21-100 (Chapters 10–13, ¶¶185–236)

## Interior Guard Duty (Chapter 10, ¶¶185–193)

Guard duty is the soldier's trust. Know the general and special orders, the challenge and countersign, sentinel duties, and relief procedures. Sleeping on post or leaving it is a grave offense. A guard who does not know his orders is not a guard.

## Marches, Camps, Bivouacs (Chapter 11, ¶¶194–202)

- **Marching is the daily job (¶194):** "Battles take place at indefinite intervals, but marches occur daily."
- **Evening before (¶195):** canteen clean and filled; kit checked (towel, soap, toothbrush, comb, mirror, needles, thread, safety pins, spare buttons); pack straps and belt adjusted; two pairs of woolen socks without holes or mends; boots comfortable, in good repair, well broken in — never new boots on a march. Clear trash before dark; leave the camp site better than found.
- **Morning (¶195):** reveille, toilet, roll and pack, breakfast, wash and pack mess kit, fall in fully equipped.
- **Conduct (¶197):** drink plentifully before the start, then sparingly (the manual's 3–4-hour rule is superseded — modern doctrine is drink before, during, and after in small quantities; see FM 21-18). Small pebble in the mouth keeps it moist. Only commander-approved water; sweets increase thirst; salt or salt tablets replace salts lost in sweat. No profanity toward civilians; no entering private property or taking fruit/vegetables — serious offenses.
- **Halts (¶197):** 15 minutes at the end of the first 45 minutes; then 10 minutes each hour. Attend to equipment, animals, and vehicles promptly. Rest fully — stretch out at full length on dry ground, or sit with a back rest. Never sit or lie on wet ground. Dig a small pit for calls of nature and refill it at once.
- **Road discipline (¶198):** keep to the side directed (usually right, left free for traffic); never straggle across the road — motor units may pass at 60 mph. At halts, clear the road to the side marched on.
- **Camps (¶¶199–202):** ideal site — water, wood, level dry well-drained ground, concealment, sanitation. Shelter tent; bed hollowed for the hips with straw, grass, leaves, or branches, sleeping on the raincoat against damp. Ditch the tent as soon as it is up, even for one night; air bedding in the sun daily; ventilate at night. Mounted units have their own routines for horses and picket lines.

## Compasses and Maps (Chapter 12, ¶¶203–216)

- **Prismatic compass (¶¶203–207):** dial-needle graduated 0–360°, 0 north, 90 east, 180 south, 270 west. Magnetic azimuth = compass reading of a line toward the object. Day: eyepiece and cover raised, dial released, object lined up through eyepiece slit and hair-line, read through the eyepiece. Night: set the radiolite marker on the index ring to the required azimuth (e.g., 55°), clamp, turn until the dial-needle points to the marker; on a dark night, send a comrade to the limit of visibility and direct him by compass, then move up and repeat.
- **Maps (¶¶208–216):** a map is the earth's surface on paper, drawn to scale (1 inch = 1 mile; scale by bar line, words/figures, or representative fraction, e.g., RF 1/63,360). Orient the map, orient yourself, read relief and the conventional signs of FM 21-30, and put positions on the map.

## Security and Protection (Chapter 13, ¶¶217–236)

- **Individuals (¶¶221–228):** conduct, sentinels, messengers, connecting files between units, antiaircraft security (dispersion, concealment), antitank security (warning, cover), protection against gas.
- **Small units (¶¶229–236):** scouts (smallest security detachment), patrols, security at halt, on the march (advance, flank, and rear guards), while defending, and while attacking. Somebody always watches every direction the enemy could come from, and somebody is always ready to give the alarm.
