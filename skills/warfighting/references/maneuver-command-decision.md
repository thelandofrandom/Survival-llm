## Maneuver warfare — the doctrine in one paragraph

Maneuver warfare seeks to shatter the enemy's cohesion through a series of rapid, focused, and unexpected actions that create a turbulent and rapidly deteriorating situation with which the enemy cannot cope. It is enemy-oriented, favors indirect approaches, relies on speed and tempo, accepts risk and uncertainty, decentralizes decision-making, and seeks disruption over destruction. A unit cut off, confused, and leaderless is defeated even if it has taken few casualties.

## The OODA loop

John Boyd's cycle: **Observe — Orient — Decide — Act**. Victory goes to the side that cycles faster and more accurately.

| Step | What it is | Where it fails |
|---|---|---|
| Observe | Collect information about self, enemy, environment | Sensors, reporting, denial |
| Orient | Fuse observation with experience, culture, training, analysis into understanding | The most common failure point — wrong mental model |
| Decide | Choose a course of action | Hesitation, waiting for certainty |
| Act | Execute; the action generates new observations | Slow execution, micromanagement |

Rules: make decisions at the lowest capable level; favor timely over perfect; a decision at 70% information now beats one at 95% too late; build organizations that act on decisions without further approval.

## Commander's intent — template

A good intent statement contains:
1. **Task** — what the unit must do.
2. **Purpose** — why it matters, in the context of the higher mission.
3. **End state** — what success looks like when the mission is complete.

Test: can a subordinate two levels down state the intent when communications are dead and the plan is obsolete? If not, the order has failed.

## Mission tactics — how to write orders

- Tell subordinates *what* to do and *why*. Leave the *how* to them.
- Orders short, clear, focused on purpose rather than method.
- A subordinate who understands intent can adapt when the situation changes; one who only knows the task cannot.
- Tolerate subordinate initiative even when their solution differs from yours. Mistakes from initiative are preferable to paralysis from waiting.

## Main effort

- At any time, one unit or effort is the main effort: the focus of combat power, the point where the commander seeks the decision.
- All other efforts support it, directly or indirectly.
- The main effort shifts as the situation changes; resources must shift with it.
- A force with no main effort disperses its power and achieves nothing decisive.

## Surfaces and gaps

| | Surface | Gap |
|---|---|---|
| Definition | Enemy strength — prepared, dangerous | Enemy weakness — vulnerable, unprepared, absent |
| Forms | Fortified position, massed fires, strong unit | Unguarded flank, seam between units, repositioning moment, broken will |
| Action | Avoid or bypass | Penetrate and exploit to collapse the enemy from within |

Surfaces and gaps are dynamic: a gap closed becomes a surface; a new gap opens elsewhere. Constant reconnaissance and aggressive exploitation are how gaps are found and used.

## Shaping the action — checklist

- [ ] Reconnaissance identifies enemy vulnerabilities and friendly vulnerabilities
- [ ] Deception misleads the enemy about time, place, or intent
- [ ] Fires suppress or fix the enemy where he is strong
- [ ] Maneuver positions forces to exploit the gap
- [ ] Information operations shape the enemy's perception
- [ ] The decisive engagement is entered on favorable terms — often decided before the assault begins

## Combined arms — the dilemma principle

Integration of infantry, armor, artillery, aviation, engineers, and logistics so each complements and protects the others. The point is not merely having different weapons present; it is coordinating them in time and space so the enemy faces a dilemma: defend against one threat and he is exposed to another. The MAGTF exists to make combined arms routine at the lowest practical level.
