## The nature of war — distilled

| Element | Meaning | Practical consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Friction | Everything simple becomes difficult; countless small incidents compound | Plans must be simple, robust, tolerant of error |
| Uncertainty | Fog: intelligence is incomplete, often wrong, sometimes deceptive | Act through uncertainty; do not wait for certainty |
| Fluidity | Continuous flow; each episode merges into the next | Advantage goes to the fastest adapter |
| Disorder | Order degrades as it descends toward the fighting | Operate effectively inside disorder; impose more on the enemy |
| Complexity | Countless interacting elements; 2nd/3rd-order effects dominate | Think in systems, not single causes |
| Human dimension | War is fought by people, decided by will | Morale, cohesion, leadership usually decide |
| Violence and danger | Organized violence is the environment, not an accident | Discipline, training, cohesion exist to let people function while afraid |
| Physical / moral / mental | Tangible means, psychological will, intellectual judgment | A complete plan addresses all three |
| Nature vs. character | Nature is constant; character (weapons, tactics) changes | New technology never abolishes friction |

## Levels of war

| Level | What happens there | Typical products |
|---|---|---|
| Strategic | National objectives; all instruments of power | Policy, national strategy, military strategy |
| Operational | Campaigns and major operations in a theater | Campaign plans, theater objectives |
| Tactical | Battles and engagements | Orders, fire plans, maneuver |

Rule: always know which level you are on, and what your actions mean for the levels above and below. Tactical brilliance cannot redeem bad strategy; bad tactics can wreck good strategy.

## Attrition vs. maneuver — comparison

| | Attrition | Maneuver |
|---|---|---|
| Target | Enemy's material means | Enemy's cohesion, command, will |
| Method | Destroy forces, wear down logistics | Strike vulnerabilities, exploit gaps, create unmanageable situations |
| Cost | High; favors the side with more resources | Low relative to effect; demands judgment and speed |
| Defeat looks like | Enemy has nothing left to fight with | Enemy collapses though much of his force remains |
| Risk | Low uncertainty, high cost | Higher uncertainty, decisive payoff |

## Centers of gravity and critical vulnerabilities — procedure

1. Identify the enemy center of gravity: the source of power (force, leader, alliance, public will, logistics) on which his ability to resist depends.
2. Find critical vulnerabilities: weaknesses that expose the center of gravity — gaps in defense, fragile supply, rigid command, seams between units.
3. Focus effort on the vulnerability, not the strength. Attacking strength head-on is attrition.
4. Reassess continuously: centers of gravity shift as the campaign develops.

## Speed, focus, surprise, boldness — checklist

- [ ] Speed: is our decision-action cycle faster than the enemy's? Where is the bottleneck?
- [ ] Focus: is combat power concentrated at the decisive point and time? What is the main effort?
- [ ] Surprise: are we striking where the enemy is unprepared — physically, mentally, organizationally?
- [ ] Boldness: are we accepting risk for decisive results, or defaulting to caution?
- [ ] Initiative: are we acting while the enemy reacts, or the reverse?
