# FM 21-78 Resistance and Escape

## Purpose

FM 21-78, *Resistance and Escape* (Headquarters, Department of the Army, 15 June 1989) — the Army field manual on resistance to exploitation and escape from captivity, successor in subject to FM 21-77 (1958). It covers the history and legal background of prisoner-of-war treatment, the Code of Conduct, the physical and psychological effects of captivity, organizing in captivity, exploitation, propaganda, indoctrination, interrogation, resistance techniques, POW camp communication, escape and rescue, and detention in peacetime — including terrorist captivity and hostage situations.

**TOC only — the vault hosts only the table of contents; no body text exists in this source.** The vault PDF (4 pages, 647 words) is the table of contents of the classified 1989 manual, posted by Jeff Schmidt, author of *Disciplined Minds*. No chapter body text is available in this source, so this skill describes what the manual covers, chapter by chapter, from its table of contents — it does not and cannot reproduce the manual's doctrine, procedures, or guidance. Do not treat the chapter titles below as the manual's content. For actual resistance, escape, and SERE doctrine, use the `sere` skill (AFH 10-644) and `survival-evasion-recovery` (MCRP 3-02H), and the historic `fm-21-77-evasion-escape` skill for the 1958 predecessor's full text.

**Source quality note.** The vault PDF is a fax-like scan and the extraction is heavily garbled in places — page headers in the OCR read "FM 21-79" and "UNCLASSIFIED," which are OCR artifacts, not source facts. The table of contents below is reconstructed from the readable portions; a few section numbers in Chapter 11 are uncertain where the OCR is illegible. See `references/table-of-contents.md` for the full listing and `references/scope-and-limitations.md` for what is and is not known.

## What the Manual Covers — Chapter by Chapter

- **Preface (v) and Introduction (vi)** — the manual's framing and scope.
- **Chapter 1 — Historical Background.** Ancient and medieval treatment of prisoners; humanitarian standards of the Renaissance; the American Revolution; the Civil War; international agreements; recent treatment of prisoners of war. The historical arc behind the Code of Conduct.
- **Chapter 2 — The Code of Conduct.** The Defense Review Committee and the current articles of the Code.
- **Chapter 3 — Effects of Captivity.** Political pressures and world opinion; cultural aspects; stages of captivity; physical and psychological effects.
- **Chapter 4 — Organizing in Captivity.** Individual organization, group organization, and general guidance for PW organization.
- **Chapter 5 — Exploitation.** Prisoner exploitation; increased use of PWs; history and change; terminology; means; preparatory phases; coercive manipulation; exploitation approaches; collaboration; disciplinary punishment and torture.
- **Chapter 6 — Propaganda.** The process and power of propaganda; its targets; methods of obtaining it; its forms; U.S. guidance for news media exposure.
- **Chapter 7 — Indoctrination.** The process, approaches and forms, objectives, program, and tools of indoctrination.
- **Chapter 8 — Interrogation.** The process; the interrogator; tactical interrogation; phases; methods; the interrogator's handicaps.
- **Chapter 9 — Resistance to Exploitation.** Authorized communication; resistance posture; resistance techniques; other issues.
- **Chapter 10 — POW Camp Communication.** Principles, methods, organizational concepts, and communication beyond the camp.
- **Chapter 11 — Escape and Rescue.** Deciding to escape; organizing for escape; determining the method; planning for after escape; preparing for escape; ensuring security in escape planning; discussing historical issues and escape; surviving a rescue.
- **Chapter 12 — Detention in Peacetime.** Wartime vs. peacetime detention; goals; background; terrorist captivity guidance; recommendations for hostage situations; peacetime detention by a government; news media; things to remember.
- **Appendices.** A — Reading Material; B — Extract of DoD Directive 1300.7 (December 1984); C — Extracted Articles of the Geneva Convention on the Treatment of POWs; D — Extracted Articles of the UCMJ; E — Release Policy. Plus a Glossary, References, and Index.
