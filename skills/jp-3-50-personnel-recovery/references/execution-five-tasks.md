# Execution — The Five Tasks — JP 3-50

## Report

Actions required to provide notification that personnel are or may have become isolated.

- **Distress notification:** how an isolated person signals distress.
- **Notification methods and procedures:** the means and the routing of the report.
- **Notification responses:** what the receiving organization does when it gets the report — and what it does when the report is ambiguous or incomplete.
- **Speed matters:** every hour of delay in reporting is an hour of delay in locating, and the isolated person's situation usually gets worse, not better.

## Locate

The effort to find and confirm the identity of isolated personnel.

- **Determine and maintain location:** the search effort, and keeping the location current once found.
- **Protracted recovery cases:** when the isolated person cannot be found quickly — the effort continues, and the case is managed, not forgotten.
- **Extended searches:** widening the search when the initial effort fails.
- **Authenticate:** confirm that the person found is the person reported. The enemy has used false distress signals and false recoveries; the locate task is not complete until identity is confirmed.

## Support

Support to both the isolated person and the isolated person's next of kin.

- **Support to isolated personnel:** resupply, communications, medical advice, reassurance — anything that sustains them until recovery.
- **Support to families:** a command responsibility from the moment of notification. Families are told what is known, what is being done, and what to expect — and protected from misinformation and exploitation.

## Recover

The coordinated actions and efforts of commanders and staffs, recovery forces, and isolated personnel.

- **Recovery methods:** the means by which isolated personnel are brought back — air, ground, sea, or a combination.
- **Personnel recovery task force:** the task-organized force built for a specific recovery mission.
- **Recovery force elements:** the units and capabilities that make up the force.
- **Recovery vehicles and forces:** the platforms and the personnel that execute the mission.
- **Isolated personnel responsibilities prior to recovery:** authenticate; report the situation (enemy activity, site characteristics, party size, medical situation, signal devices available); prepare the site; follow the recovery force's instructions.
- **Physical custody:** the moment the recovery force takes custody of the isolated person — the point at which the recover task ends and reintegration begins.

## Reintegrate

Begins when the report notification is received and there is a possibility the recovered person will enter the reintegration process — planned from the start, not improvised after recovery.

- **Process:** a deliberate, staged process — immediate medical care and debriefing, then a structured return to duty or to normal life.
- **Challenges:** physical, psychological, and informational — including what the recovered person was exposed to and what they may have disclosed.
- **Follow-up:** continues long after the headlines stop.
- **Administrative:** the paperwork of recovery — accountability, status, and records.
- **Medical:** care for injuries and illness, including care that only becomes apparent after recovery.
- **Support to families and designated individuals:** families are part of reintegration, not bystanders to it.
