# PR System and Execution Tasks — AFDP 3-50

Distilled from AFDP 3-50, Chapters 1, 2, and 5 (26 September 2025). Paraphrase with short quotes only; full text in `full-text.md`.

## Definition

Personnel recovery (PR) is "the sum of military, diplomatic, and civil efforts to prepare for and execute the recovery and reintegration of isolated personnel (IP)" (JP 3-50). The Air Force is the only service specifically directed to conduct PR on a global scale (DoDD 5100.01). Rescue forces should be ready to launch and recover any IP, regardless of Service, any time, any place.

## Why PR matters

- Sustains morale, cohesion, and capability of forces.
- Successful recovery is a force multiplier — personnel return to fight again.
- Preserves critical combat resources.
- Denies adversaries the intelligence and propaganda value of captured personnel.
- Failure to prepare creates the perception the U.S. cannot protect its people, eroding political and public support and diminishing credibility, influence, and access.

## The three groups

1. **Commanders and staffs** — manage PR programs and missions; link force protection programs and PR.
2. **Recovery forces** — deliberately trained PR forces plus augmenting assets.
3. **Individuals at risk** — Airmen trained for potential isolation and recovery; high-risk personnel get SERE school, ISOPREP, and EPA review.

## Who is at risk

- Combatants and noncombatants. Terrorists, insurgents, criminal networks, pirates, non-state actors in urban or ill-defined battlefields.
- Missing-personnel categories (DoDI 2310.05): missing in action, interned or detained (foreign country), captured, beleaguered, besieged against a person's will; extends to hostage taking, kidnapping, abduction.
- Scale: one person to an entire organized unit. All personnel have the potential to become isolated.

## Command and control

| Element | Role |
|---|---|
| JPRC (Joint Personnel Recovery Center) | Joint-force level: coordinates/tasks PR support across components; locates IP if unknown; recommends and coordinates force tasking; monitors component-initiated missions |
| PRCC (personnel recovery coordination cell) | Component level: PR mission coordinator on incident report; initiates planning; tasks/coordinates subordinate PR-capable units; sends SAR incident and situation reports to JPRC |
| AOC / wing operations centers | Direct communication links essential, especially when a CSARTF forms |
| JSRC (legacy, JP 3-50.2) | Predecessor joint center; JPRC is the current joint construct |

- A component initiating a PR mission independently must notify the JPRC through its PRCC for coordination and deconfliction.
- PR command relationships and authorities are set in planning, before the event.
- PR forces should influence ROE development early for maximum flexibility; law of war and ROE govern use of force during CSAR and treatment/release of captured or detained persons.

## The five execution tasks

### 1. Report
- Awareness and notification. Rapid, accurate notification is essential.
- IP attempts contact with friendly forces per PR SPINS in the ATO (threat permitting).
- PRCC assumes mission-coordinator duties, initiates planning, reports to JPRC.

### 2. Locate
- Methods: theater electronic surveillance, reconnaissance, C2 aircraft, space assets, wingman reports, counterintelligence support to force protection, visual and electronic search by trained PR forces.
- Even with precise coordinates, authenticate IP identity before support/recovery.
- Authentication methods: CSAR code words, letters, numbers, visual signals, IP report data. Theater-specific procedures in directives, OPLANs, PR SPINS.
- Authentication must not be compromised — rescue assets are extremely vulnerable during execution.

### 3. Support
Five objectives: situational awareness; protection; two-way communications; morale-building support; aerial resupply (including aerial escort to a supply cache or more secure area).
- May include suppression of enemy threats to the IP (precluding capture, disrupting adversary response).
- CROs, pararescuemen, and equipment may be pre-positioned to support IP until recovery.
- Family members may receive physical and psychological assistance.
- Support continues during captivity (including MIA) until the PR event is resolved.

### 4. Recover
- PRCC assesses quickly, determines feasibility, disseminates data to participating units.
- Once feasible: task units to plan or launch from alert. JFC or designated PR supported commander issues the execution order; PR CONOPS or SPINS direct launch/execution authority.
- **Immediate:** from ATO-directed ground or airborne alert; forces forward-located or loitering to cut flight time and air refueling needs; may be embedded in existing airborne missions.
- **Deliberate:** when immediate response is not possible (environmental, political, threat reasons); may create a recovery task force from single-component, joint, multinational, or multi-agency assets.
- **Hold:** a mission is never closed due to risk or inability to locate the IP, but may be placed on hold when information required to execute does not meet the commander's execution criteria (e.g., location, intelligence).
- CSAR is the preferred recovery mechanism; the method for PR in denied/hostile environments. Not conducted for humanitarian assistance, civil SAR, or casualty evacuation.
- Three CSAR components: C2, recovery forces, IP.

### 5. Reintegrate
- Reintroduce the IP into society; return the member to service.
- Medical care and well-being through decompression; debriefings gather intelligence and SERE information.
- Begins when the recovery force relinquishes physical control of the IP to a designated team member/organization in the theater reintegration process.
- Theater reintegration in two phases under CCDR guidance; Phase III may be long-term Service responsibility with no specific end date.
- Non-DoD American citizens recovered by DoD PR teams are reintegrated through a Department of State process unless DoD specialized capabilities are specifically required, requested, and approved.

## Recovery forces and the CSARTF

- Recovery forces: personnel who organize, train, and perform PR operations, plus augmentation forces.
- **CSARTF:** tailored task force for denied/contested environments; two elements — PR assets and augmenting assets (designated aircraft, specially trained personnel, specific positions).
- Protects the IP and the recovery force package before, during, and on egress from small arms, surface-to-air, air-to-air, and air-to-ground threats.
- Composition varies with threats en route and near the IP; adjusts during recovery as threats and IP status change. PRCC requests augmentation from other components through the JPRC.
- **Vertical lift:** rescue helicopters — long-range, low-level, day/night, marginal weather, hostile environments. Usually multi-ship for mutual support; single ship possible by threat/asset availability.
- All aircraft under the air component commander's C2 should be prepared to assist recovery operations.
- **Dynamic rescue:** operations that adapt in real time — forces re-tasked and re-composed during execution.
