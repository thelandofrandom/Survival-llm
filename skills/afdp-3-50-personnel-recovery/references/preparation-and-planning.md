# Preparation and Planning — AFDP 3-50

Distilled from AFDP 3-50, Chapters 3 and 4 (26 September 2025). Paraphrase with short quotes only; full text in `full-text.md`.

## Preparation — three areas, three groups

| Area | What it covers |
|---|---|
| Policy and doctrine | Reading and applying PR policy/doctrine; linking force protection programs and PR |
| Education and training | SERE and Code of Conduct training; PR exercise participation (e.g., Red Flag Rescue); OJT |
| Equipment | Organizing, training, and equipping PR forces (e.g., rescue squadrons) and others who may be called on |

Focused on: commanders and staffs; recovery forces; individuals at risk of isolation. Preparation is ongoing in peace and conflict. Because PR events do not lend themselves to extensive prior planning, preparation done long before the event shortens decision and execution.

## Commanders and staffs

- DoD policy requires commanders to link force protection programs and PR to preserve the force.
- Highest levels: manage and synchronize PR. Lower levels: coordinate and execute PR.
- Failure to prepare creates the perception the U.S. cannot protect its people — eroding political/public support, credibility, influence, and access.

## Forces

- **Dedicated:** specifically organized, trained, and equipped to conduct PR.
- **Designated/capable (non-dedicated):** capabilities can be applied to effect or support recovery; may be tasked or designated to support or recover personnel as a primary or additional mission.
- **Recognized capability:** not primarily organized/trained/equipped for PR, but has a recognized capability to recover personnel.
- All three categories matter to the planner.

## Individuals at risk — and the ACE gap

- High-risk Airmen receive in-depth preparation: SERE school, ISOPREP, EPA review.
- **Agile Combat Employment (ACE)** widens the at-risk population: Airmen who traditionally stayed "inside the wire" at main operating bases now move between forward operating sites and contingency locations, where isolating events are more likely — in land or maritime areas that may be more permissive, but where Airmen are still IP if they must survive, evade, resist, or escape.
- That in-depth preparation is not normally afforded to all Airmen supporting ACE. Plan for them explicitly.

## Adaptation

- Lessons learned from exercises and operational PR events, saved in JLLIS and validated via PR events, debriefings, exercises, wargames, and other activities, feed continuous improvement.
- Historical data are vital. Example exercise: Red Flag Rescue (USAF, joint, coalition PR training).

## Planning — where it lives

- PR planning is part of contingency and crisis planning, using the joint planning process for air (JPPA): detailed mission analysis, course-of-action development, wargaming.
- Product: the PR appendix — **Appendix 5 to Annex C** of a JFC's campaign plan or the air component's joint air operations plan (JAOP), built from a completed PR mission analysis.
- Options may be diplomatic, civil, or military; the bulk of military planning is the military option, with support to the other two as tasked.

## Planning — information required for launch authority

- Location of IP; authentication; threat; weather; terrain assessment; evaluation of safe passage corridors; air refueling capabilities.
- Best practice: co-locate all PR planning activities; direct communication with the AOC, JPRC, PRCC, and wing operations centers.

## Planning — across the competition continuum

- Cooperation → competition below armed conflict → armed conflict.
- Lower end: PR supports engagement, security cooperation, deterrence in chief-of-mission (COM) environments.
- Escalation: Title 10 authorities clarify; DoD leads.
- Planners identify limitations, threats, and additional capabilities (including partner nations) at each stage, and translate strategy and tactics to operational objectives.

## Planning — force flow and designations

- Identify forces needed; phase them into the operational area; identify resources, delivery date, employment location; support with TPFDD.
- Force flow must include sufficient recovery forces commensurate with risk to force and risk of isolation.
- PR designation coding on the TPFDD: **dedicated, designated, or capable** — lets planners extract PR data and schedule recovery-force deployments against the flow of other forces.

## Planning — other considerations

- Consider host-nation, other Service/functional component, and multinational capabilities.
- Update Appendix 5 to Annex C of the JAOP as plans change.
- Operational contract support requirements in the JAOP's Annex W.
- Know the law of war and applicable ROE; influence ROE development early for maximum flexibility in recovering IP.
