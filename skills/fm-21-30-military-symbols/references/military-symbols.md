# Military Symbols — FM 21-30 (Section IV, ¶¶7–9)

## Color (¶7a)

Friendly forces and objects in friendly-controlled territory: **blue**. Enemy: **red**. Exceptions: areas to be covered by friendly fire or gas are blue even though generally in enemy territory; gassed areas are always red wherever located.

## Unit Designations — Position Is Meaning (¶7b)

- **Within** the symbol: serial numbers of armies (spelled out) and corps (Roman numerals), for troop units considered collectively.
- **Right**, Arabic numerals: serial numbers of divisions, brigades, regiments, separate battalions, separate companies, or similar separate units — followed by an abbreviated explanation of the unit's size.
- **Left**, Arabic numerals: battalion numbers within a regiment.
- **Left**, capital letters: company, troop, or battery letters. Units designated by name (e.g., Antitank Company, Headquarters and Service Troop) use the proper abbreviation on the left.
- **Right**: abbreviations for explanatory purposes (Section VI). Abbreviations show what a single symbol cannot.
- **Under** the symbol: caliber (37-mm, 155-mm, 12-in., .50-in.); the unit of measure is often omitted.

## Echelon Marks (¶8c)

Marks placed above the basic symbol: squad (one tick), section (two), platoon (three), company/troop/battery/flight I, battalion/squadron II, regiment/group III, brigade/wing X, division/air force XX, corps XXX, army XXXX, corps area/department/section of communications zone OOO, communications zone OOOO, General Headquarters GHQ. The same marks ride on boundary and line symbols to show the echelon a line belongs to. A soldier is a small circle; automatic rifleman, assistant leader, leader, and squad leader are variations on it.

## Basic Symbols (¶8)

- **(a) Purpose/activity:** military post or station; command post/headquarters (staff ends at the location); troop unit; airdrome; airship hangar, mooring mast; airport, advanced landing field; autogiro; ammunition point; arsenal (incl. gas-generating); balloon ascension point, balloon bed, barrage balloon ascension point; barrage; demolitions; depot/supply point (incl. temporary depot in combat zone); debarkation/embarkation point; dugout (isolated, in trench system, gas-proof); entanglement (wire, concealed); gas areas (to be avoided, blanketed by smoke with time effective, probably affected by gas cylinder cloud, to be gassed nonpersistent); general hospital; laboratory/experimental station/proving ground; leader gear; message center; mines (individual, chemical land mine, contact mines showing number per line, controlled mines in 19-mine groups).
- **(b) Arms and services:** branch symbols placed inside the unit frame — infantry, cavalry, field artillery, coast artillery, engineers, signal, medical, quartermaster, ordnance, chemical warfare, air corps, armored force, military police, and others.
- **(d) Boundaries and lines:** aviation limits of radius of action / zone of reconnaissance (bombardment, army, corps, air force, observation, pursuit); front line; main line of resistance; regimental reserve line; outpost line; line of departure; line of communication; straggler line; prisoner-of-war enclosure; limit of wheeled traffic by day/night; line beyond which vehicle lights are prohibited; limiting point.
- **(e) Weapons:** automatic rifle (dotted when emplacement unoccupied); machine gun (arrow = principal direction of fire; alone = water-cooled .30-cal.; antiaircraft and antitank variants; single gun; two-gun section with sectors of fire and final protective line); gun and gun battery; howitzer or mortar (open when emplacement unoccupied); 4.2-in. chemical mortar; Livens projector; chemical land mine.
- **(f) Hasty sketches:** simplified area-occupied symbols, brush, cultivated land, stream, woods; suggested road classes (hard surface; graded and improved; graded, not improved; poor road; trail).

## Application (¶9)

Worked combinations show how symbols and abbreviations build complete designations — Air Corps (3d Air Force, 701st Air Base Group, 901st Transport Squadron, 88th Reconnaissance Squadron, 701st Bombardment Wing Light, 532d Pursuit Group Interceptor, Second Army Aviation), Armored Force (Headquarters Company 1st Armored Division, its MP and transportation platoons, 1st Reconnaissance Battalion and its rifle company and weapons platoon), and infantry, artillery, engineer, and service equivalents. The manual notes these are only a few possible combinations, many more complete than necessary: often a unit's number alone, to those familiar with it, indicates its activity.

## Caveat

The drawn symbols do not survive plain-text extraction. This reference records what each symbol denotes and how it combines; for the exact drawn form, consult the PDF.
