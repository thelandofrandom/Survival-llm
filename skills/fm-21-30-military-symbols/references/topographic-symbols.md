# Topographic and Air Navigation Symbols — FM 21-30 (Sections II–III)

## General Topographic Symbols (Section II, ¶¶3–4)

**Characteristics (¶3).** Symbols resemble the objects they represent and vary in size with map scale. On small-scale maps they reduce to their most elementary form and do not conform to scale; at larger scale they assume the shape and scaled size of the object. Colors are those of a four-color map; when fewer colors are used, symbols in the unused colors are executed in black ink.

**Lettering carries meaning.** Vertical Roman = civil divisions. Slant Roman (italic) = natural water features. Vertical Gothic = natural land features. Slant Gothic = works of man. Hastily prepared military maps and sketches may vary considerably from these types, and their marginal data will be far less complete.

**The list (¶4) covers, in order:** works and structures (canal, ditch, open aqueduct, abandoned canal, buried aqueduct or water pipe, aqueduct or canal in tunnel, canal lock); roads by surface (good motor, poor motor or private, hard impervious surfaces, other surface improvements, good and poor pack trails and footpaths) with U.S. and state route markers and the date of road information in the margin on tactical maps; railroads (any kind on small-scale maps; single track, double track, narrow gauge, abandoned, electric passenger-only on large-scale maps); boundaries and survey marks; hydrography; vegetation (woods, brush, cultivated land); and relief.

**Authority.** Sections II, III, and V were approved by the Federal Board of Maps and Surveys and are prescribed for use on all United States maps — not only military ones.

## Air Navigation Symbols (Section III, ¶¶5–6)

A separate list for air navigation maps only: airfields and landing grounds (including advanced landing fields), airdromes, airship hangars and mooring masts, radio and navigational aids, and obstruction/hazard markings. The selection and emphasis differ from ground maps because a pilot reads a chart at speed. As with Section II, the symbols are pictographic drawings; the extracted text preserves the labels, not the drawings — see the PDF for exact forms.

## Using These Sections

- Read lettering type before reading the word: it tells you whether the feature is civil, water, land, or man-made.
- On a small-scale map, do not measure a symbol as if it were to scale — it is an elementary mark, not a footprint.
- Any symbol not in the authorized lists may be used only if it does not conflict with an authorized symbol and is explained in a legend on the map.
