# Recon Missions — Reference (MCRP 2-10A.6, Chapters 6 and 7)

Distilled from MCRP 2-10A.6 *Ground Reconnaissance Operations* (USMC, Apr 2018). Paraphrased; short quotes only.

## Chapter outline

- Ch. 6 — Reconnaissance operations: ground reconnaissance types, amphibious, riverine, surveillance, combat assessment, battlespace shaping, hunter-killer, urban, raids, special missions.
- Ch. 7 — Methods of maneuver: waterborne, helicopterborne, airborne, dismounted, mounted, specialized ground operations.

## Mission taxonomy

| Mission | Question it answers | Depth of detail |
|---|---|---|
| Area reconnaissance | Everything about a specific bounded area: enemy, terrain, obstacles | High — detailed examination |
| Zone reconnaissance | Is the enemy in this larger area, and where? | Medium — coverage over detail |
| Route reconnaissance | Is this route trafficable, and what is on it? | High for the route itself; report is a product the maneuver commander moves on |
| Force-oriented reconnaissance | What is this specific enemy force doing? | Focused on the force, not the ground |
| Bridge reconnaissance | Condition, capacity, and enemy control of a bridge | Specialized; a chokepoint problem |
| Leader's reconnaissance | What does the objective/route actually look like? | Short, fast, high-value; the leader goes personally |
| Amphibious / underwater reconnaissance | Beach, hydrography, obstacles for a landing | Advance force, pre-assault, post-assault phases |
| Riverine reconnaissance | Enemy, terrain, and waterways in river/littoral areas | Own planning and environmental factors |
| Surveillance | Continuous observation of a named area of interest | Duration over action; the OP/hide site is the mission |

## Surveillance and hide-site procedures

1. **Selection:** a site that observes the named area of interest, with cover, concealment, and an escape route. Urban hide sites add the problem of blending into the built environment.
2. **Occupation:** move in during limited visibility; establish the OP and the hide site as separate but mutually supporting positions — the OP observes, the hide site sustains and communicates.
3. **Security:** the OP generates Level I intelligence reports and relays them to the hide site; the hide site holds the radio and the bulk of the team. Neither position is compromised by the other's routine.
4. **Reporting:** on schedule, in the agreed format, via communication windows only.
5. **Link-up and dissemination:** plan how the team links up with follow-on forces and how its reporting reaches the commander while the mission is still running.
6. **Contingencies:** compromise plan, emergency exfiltration route, and the decision point for abandoning the site.

## Urban terrain — KOCOA evaluation

| Factor | What to assess |
|---|---|
| Key terrain | Buildings, intersections, rooftops, and infrastructure that dominate movement |
| Observation and fields of fire | Lines of sight down streets and across open areas; dead space behind structures |
| Cover and concealment | Building construction (what stops bullets vs. what hides movement); rubble and basements |
| Obstacles | Rubble, barricades, canals, walls; obstacles canalize urban movement absolutely |
| Avenues of approach | Streets, alleys, subterranean routes, rooftops — and their intercity distribution of building types |

Urban considerations: industrial areas and residential sprawl shape movement differently; sources of information about the urban area include maps, imagery, and the population; individual equipment (including climbing and breaching gear) matters more in cities; communications degrade inside concrete and steel; hide sites and OPs need camouflage discipline against a population that lives next door.

## Amphibious reconnaissance phases

| Phase | Reconnaissance task |
|---|---|
| Advance force operations | Early, clandestine beach and hydrographic reconnaissance before the main force arrives |
| Pre-assault operations | Final beach, obstacle, and enemy confirmation immediately before the assault |
| Post-assault operations | Reconnaissance in support of the force ashore; expansion of the lodgment |

Planning considerations: surf, tides, beach gradients, obstacles (natural and man-made), and the coordination of reconnaissance with the landing plan.

## Combat assessment

- Definition: determining the effect of fires on a target — did the strike achieve the intended effect?
- Report damage types and levels using the framework in Appendix G (damage assessments for specific targets).
- Combat assessment is a reconnaissance product: the team that observes the strike is the only source that can confirm its effect.

## Battlespace shaping and hunter-killer

- **Battlespace shaping:** missions that prepare the environment for follow-on operations — deception, target development, and shaping fires. Mission planning factors include the shaping objective, the target set, and the hand-off to the main force.
- **Hunter-killer:** a reconnaissance element paired with a strike element. Principles: the reconnaissance element finds and fixes; the killer kills. The reconnaissance element must not become the killer — its survival depends on remaining undetected after the strike.

## Insertion methods — quick reference

| Method | Key facts |
|---|---|
| CRRC (Combat Rubber Reconnaissance Craft) | Crew responsibilities, tactical loads, sea conditions, surf limitations, distance, navigation; training qualifications required |
| Combat swimming | Scout swimmers; qualification and capability certification; limited loads and distances |
| Helocast | Jump from helicopter into water; safety personnel qualifications and training requirements are extensive |
| Combatant diving | Open- and closed-circuit; limited-visibility, contaminated-water, altitude, and cold-weather diving; submarine debarkation and withdrawal |
| Helicopterborne | SPIES, fast rope, rappelling; LZ selection and marking; pickup and landing zones |
| Airborne | Military free-fall, multi-mission parachute system, tandem offset resupply, low-level static line; DZ surveys; special-purpose DZs (rough terrain, open sea, high altitude, cold weather) |
| Dismounted | Foot movement; planning, contingencies, camouflage; stay-behind operations |
| Mounted | Speed, firepower, sustainment — at the cost of signature and terrain limits; vehicle prep and inspections |
| Specialized | Military mountaineering, over-the-snow mobility, pack animals |

## Five-phase mission structure (Ch. 5)

1. **Planning** — arrange for reconnaissance, make reconnaissance, complete the plan, issue the order, supervise.
2. **Insertion and infiltration** — insertion is delivery; infiltration is movement to the objective. Planned and rehearsed separately.
3. **Execution — actions on the objective** — observe, report, and act only as tasked.
4. **Exfiltration and extraction** — the team's own movement out, or recovery by another asset. Planned before insertion, with alternates.
5. **Recovery** — debrief and RAIDERREP. The debrief is where the mission's intelligence value is captured.
