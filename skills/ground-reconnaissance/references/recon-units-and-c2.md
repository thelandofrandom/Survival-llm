# Recon Units and C2 — Reference (MCRP 2-10A.6, Chapters 2 and 3)

Distilled from MCRP 2-10A.6 *Ground Reconnaissance Operations* (USMC, Apr 2018). Paraphrased; short quotes only.

## Chapter outline

- Ch. 2 — Units and organizations: characteristics, missions, organization, concept of employment, sustainment.
- Ch. 3 — Command and control: employment, command/support relationships, SARCC, COC, ROC, FSCC, staff, C4I.

## Unit table

| Unit | Level | Mission | Key traits |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reconnaissance Battalion | Division | Ground reconnaissance and surveillance for the division; limited offensive tasks | Companies → platoons → teams; operates forward and light |
| Reconnaissance Company | Battalion | Maneuver element; deploys platoons/teams on specific missions | Command, sustainment, subordinate organizations |
| Reconnaissance Platoon | Company | Basic maneuver element; independent action for limited periods | Stealth, mobility, endurance, communications; limited firepower and sustainment |
| Force Reconnaissance Company | MEF | Deep reconnaissance; deep insertion; limited direct action; special-operations support | Deepest ground recon asset; airborne, amphibious, subsurface capable |
| Force Reconnaissance Platoon | Company | Deep recon team | Same traits at greater depth and risk |
| Radio Reconnaissance Platoon (Radio Bn) | Battalion | SIGINT support to reconnaissance | Signals collection and direction finding |
| Ground Sensor Platoon (Intel Bn) | Battalion | Remote sensor employment | Unattended ground sensors |
| Light Armored Reconnaissance Battalion | Division/Regiment | Mounted reconnaissance | Speed, firepower, survivability — and a large signature |
| Scout Sniper Platoon (Infantry Bn) | Battalion | Surveillance and precision fires for the infantry battalion | Observation and long-range precision engagement |

## Joint / other-service assets available in support

Army Special Forces, Ranger Regiment, special operations aviation (rotary- and fixed-wing), SEAL teams, Special Warfare Combatant-craft Crewmen, special operations combat controllers, MARSOC. Ground reconnaissance is one node in a joint reconnaissance enterprise.

## Reconnaissance battalion corpsmen

- The corpsman is a combat multiplier and a sustainment necessity.
- Roles: casualty care under fire, prolonged field care during extended missions, medical training of the team, and medical planning for insertion and exfiltration.
- A reconnaissance team without medical capability cannot take a casualty and keep moving.

## C2 centers

| Center | Role |
|---|---|
| G-2/S-2 section | Owns the intelligence effort: develops requirements, tasks collection, fuses reporting |
| Intelligence Operations Center (IOC) | Staff fusion node |
| SARCC (Surveillance and Reconnaissance Coordination Center) | Coordination hub for all surveillance and reconnaissance in the MAGTF; deconflicts collection, tasks assets, ensures coverage |
| Senior SARCC (MAGTF/GCE) | Top-level coordination; liaison cell to supported units |
| Regimental SARCC | Regimental-level coordination |
| Battalion SARCC | Battalion-level coordination |
| ROC (Reconnaissance Operations Center) | The reconnaissance unit's own operations center: plans, controls, sustains deployed teams; maintains comms; passes reporting up |
| COC (Combat Operations Center) | Supported unit's operations center; receives reporting; coordinates fires and maneuver |
| FSCC (Fire Support Coordination Center) | Coordinates fires in support of reconnaissance — including the problem of firing for a team that must remain undetected |

## Command and support relationships

| Relationship | Meaning |
|---|---|
| General support | Supports the force as a whole; tasked by the higher HQ |
| Direct support | Dedicated to a specific supported unit; tasked by that unit within the higher HQ's priorities |
| Attached | Placed under another unit's command for a period; the gaining unit owns tasking and sustainment |

The relationship changes who tasks the team, who sustains it, and who owns its reporting. Confirm it before deployment.

## Key personnel (reconnaissance unit)

- **Unit commander:** owns the mission, the risk decision, and the reporting.
- **Executive officer:** runs the ROC and sustainment.
- **Staff:** operations, intelligence, logistics, communications — small, and every member does more than one job.
- **Subordinate commanders:** platoon and team leaders executing mission-type orders.
- **Liaison officers:** the link to the supported unit and the SARCC; without them, coordination fails.

## Reconnaissance push vs. pull

| Method | Character | When to use |
|---|---|---|
| Reconnaissance push | Conservative; confirms what the commander believes; fills specific gaps; main-force plan largely set | Enemy situation relatively known; plan needs refinement |
| Reconnaissance pull | Aggressive; reconnaissance drives the plan; main force follows where weakness and gaps are found | Enemy situation genuinely uncertain; opportunity-driven maneuver |

Decide before tasking — the choice changes risk tolerance, reporting priority, and tasking.
