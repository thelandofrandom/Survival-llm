# Overview and Classification — ATP 3-37.10 / MCRP 3-40D.13

## What a Base Camp Is

A base camp is an evolving, scalable facility that provides the full range of support services — billeting, dining, medical, power, water, waste, security — for a deployed force in support of contingency operations. It is planned from the start to grow, change standards, and eventually transfer or close.

## Operational Challenges

- The deployed force's size and composition change between planning and construction and will almost certainly change again over the camp's life.
- The two hardest estimates: the intended population (personnel, vehicles, and equipment on the camp at any one time) and the expected life span (from mission duration).
- False assumptions produce inadequate facilities or wasted construction. Commanders must tolerate ambiguity and mitigate risk through extensive planning and continuous coordination.

## Threat Levels (Table 1-1)

| Level | Threat | Capability required |
|---|---|---|
| I | Agents, saboteurs, sympathizers, terrorists, insider threats, civil disturbances | Organic base security forces |
| II | Small tactical units; irregular forces, which may include significant stand-off weapons threats | Base security forces plus a mobile security force (MSF) |
| III | Large tactical force operations, including airborne, heliborne, amphibious, infiltration, and major air operations | Base security forces, a mobile security force, and a tactical combat force (TCF) |

Threat levels are based on the activity, capability, and intent of enemy agents or forces.

## Classification by Duration (Table 1-2)

A contingency base camp operates 2–10 years or less; an enduring base camp operates more than 5 years. Facilities transition from contingency to enduring standards when appropriate, typically within a 6-month to 5-year period; the actual trigger is conditions, not the calendar.

| Phase | Construction standard | Expected duration |
|---|---|---|
| Contingency | Organic | Up to 90 days |
| Contingency | Initial | Up to 6 months |
| Contingency | Temporary | Up to 5 years |
| Contingency | Semipermanent | 2–10 years |
| Enduring | Permanent | 5 years or longer |

The combatant commander (CCDR), in coordination with Service components and the Services, specifies construction standards for facilities in the theater. Semipermanent facilities have a life expectancy of less than 10 years, extendable to 25 years with maintenance.

## Classification by Size

- Platoon: ~50
- Company: ~300
- Battalion / battalion landing team: ~1,000
- Brigade combat team / regimental combat team: ~3,000
- Support area: 6,000 or greater

Size drives land area, perimeter length, and every planning factor in Appendix E.

## Service Levels

- **Basic** — established at initial entry with organic capabilities and pre-positioned stock. Sustains operations for a minimum of 60 days. Rapid deployment and emplacement; facilities highly flexible and movable, built with minimal engineering effort.
- **Expanded** — improved for efficiency, expanded to sustain operations for a minimum of 180 days. Additional engineer effort above basic facility standards.
- **Enhanced** — surpasses expanded: optimal efficiency, sustains operations for an unspecified duration. Flexible, durable, nearly self-sustaining, primarily implemented through contracted support.

## Principles

- **Scalability** — grow and shrink with the force; plan for the population expected, not the population present.
- **Sustainability** — supportable over its expected life: power, water, waste, and supply keep pace with growth.
- **Standardization** — use standardized plans and designs (AFCS, JCMS, UFCs) so camps are buildable, maintainable, and interchangeable.
- **Survivability** — protect occupants through dispersion, hardening, overhead cover, and a defense matched to the threat level.

## Activities

Master planning, construction, operation and maintenance, protection, and sustainment/combat service support.
