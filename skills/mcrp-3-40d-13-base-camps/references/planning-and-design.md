# Planning and Design — ATP 3-37.10 / MCRP 3-40D.13

## Planning Principles

Plan early, plan with the supported commander, and plan for change. The base camp plan is part of the operation plan, not a separate engineering exercise. Planners work from the mission, the force to be supported, the threat, the terrain, and the expected duration — and revisit the plan as any of those change.

## Basing Strategy

Answers the big questions first: how many camps, where, how large, and for how long. Weighs mission requirements against threat, terrain, host-nation considerations, lines of communication, and the availability of existing facilities. Fewer, larger camps are more efficient to support but present a bigger target; more, smaller camps disperse risk but multiply the support and security bill.

## Planning Approach

Top down and ground up at the same time: higher headquarters sets the basing strategy and standards, while the engineer and the supported unit reconnoiter the site and feed ground truth back into the plan. The plan is a living document, updated as the site survey, the force list, and the threat picture mature.

## Master Planning

The long view: lays out the camp's ultimate footprint, zones, and infrastructure so today's construction does not block tomorrow's expansion. Covers land use, circulation, utilities, force protection, and expansion areas; approved by the commander who owns the camp. Appendix B gives the master-planning process and its relationship to the military decisionmaking process (MDMP) and the Marine Corps Planning Process (MCPP).

## Development Planning

Turns the master plan into buildable projects: sequences construction by priority, assigns resources, and sets standards for each facility. This is where the service level (basic, expanded, enhanced) and the construction standard (organic, initial, temporary, semipermanent, permanent) are matched to each facility and each phase.

## Funding Sources and Authority

- Components fund their participation in an operation with operations and maintenance (O&M) funds.
- Typical funding sources for contingency construction: O&M, military construction (MILCON), and separate acts of Congress.
- The judge advocate advises on current contingency construction limits and requirements.
- The funding source must be identified before construction starts, not after.

## Contingency Construction (10 USC 2804)

- The Secretary of Defense is authorized by 10 USC 2804 to execute contingency military construction projects.
- Joint Publication 3-34 provides joint contingency construction standards.
- The Army Facilities Components System (AFCS) designs help solve problems associated with contingency construction constrained by time, materials, and engineer capacity.
- The Joint Construction Management System (JCMS) is a database and reporting system used by military engineers for contingency construction activities, and a tool for base camp development, planning, and design.
