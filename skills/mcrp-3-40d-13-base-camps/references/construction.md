# Construction — ATP 3-37.10 / MCRP 3-40D.13

## Facilities and Infrastructure

A base camp is a system of facilities and infrastructure: billeting, dining, medical, headquarters and administration, maintenance, storage, power generation and distribution, water production and distribution, sewage, waste management, roads, airfields and helipads, force protection works, and communications. Appendix F gives design detail for each.

## Standards and Standardized Designs

Standardized designs come from the Army Facilities Components System (AFCS), the Joint Construction Management System (JCMS), and the Unified Facilities Criteria (UFCs). Using them is how a camp gets built on time with the materials and engineers actually available. Deviations are the exception and need justification.

## General Requirements

Every facility must meet the theater's construction standards, environmental, safety, and occupational health (ESOH) requirements, and the commander's force-protection standards. Health and safety are not traded away for speed.

## Project Approval

Construction projects are approved through the chain of command and, for larger efforts, an acquisition review board that validates the requirement, the standard, the funding, and the construction agent before work starts.

## Construction Management — Six Phases

1. **Initial project planning** — begins when a sponsoring entity originates, sponsors, and funds a construction project, or on receipt of a construction directive (operations order, fragmentary order, or warning order) for a design-build project.
2. **Preliminary construction planning** — begins on receipt of a construction directive for projects already designed; gives the engineering unit commander a quick overview, an assessment of unit capacity, and time to develop the commander's intent, assign primary personnel, and acquire plans and specifications.
3. **Detailed project planning** — provides a schedule for the entire construction project and an accurate estimate of tools, equipment, materials, and personnel for each construction activity, including a bill of materials, product and resource-leveling schedules, and quality control.
4. **Final planning and project turnover** — includes project confirmation briefings.
5. **Project execution and monitoring** — the direction, monitoring, and control of construction.
6. **Final project transfer and close-out** — turn-in of all tools and materials and formal transfer of the completed facility.

## Construction Plan, Priorities, and Surveying

Base camps are constructed in phases based on priorities and sound construction practices. Surveying proceeds in three stages: **reconnaissance** (a quick look to confirm the site will work), **preliminary location** (a closer survey to fix the layout), and **final location and construction layout** (staking the facilities to the ground).

## Materials

Use what is available locally first — local materials reduce the logistics burden — but verify quality and suitability. Imported materials are reserved for what local sources cannot provide. Material selection is driven by the construction standard, the expected duration, and the threat.

## Site Preparation

Clear the site (remove vegetation and obstacles), grub (remove roots and organic material), strip (remove topsoil), then cut and fill to grade, and provide drainage. Drainage is not an afterthought: standing water breeds disease, undermines foundations, and turns a camp into a swamp. Grade the site so water runs away from facilities.

## Construction Methods

In order of increasing permanence and effort:

- **Existing structures** — use what is already there (buildings, warehouses, airfields) when it meets the requirement; fastest and cheapest.
- **Tents** — the default for initial and temporary standards; fast to erect, flexible, and movable, but they degrade with long exposure to the elements and are less likely to be repackable and reusable the longer they are used. Tents used outside the United States are subject to host-nation considerations.
- **Pre-engineered metal or fabric structures** — faster than traditional construction, more durable than tents.
- **Modular or trailer units** — factory-built, delivered and emplaced; good for billeting, offices, and medical facilities.
- **Prefabricated structures** — components built off-site and assembled on-site.
- **Traditional construction** — wood, steel, or concrete masonry unit (CMU); slowest, most durable, used for semipermanent and permanent standards.

## Construction Means

- **Troop construction** — organic engineer units build with their own personnel and equipment.
- **Defense construction agents** — the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers (USACE) and the Naval Facilities Engineering Command (NAVFAC) execute larger or more complex construction.
- **Contracted construction** — civilian contractors build under contract; the primary means for enhanced services and enduring camps.
- **Horizontal and vertical projects** — horizontal construction is earthwork, roads, airfields, and utilities; vertical construction is buildings and structures. The two are planned and resourced separately and sequenced so horizontal work leads vertical work.
