# Mountain Weather — MCRP 12-10A.1

Source: MCRP 12-10A.1, Chapter 1 (Mountain Weather). Distilled from the manual; see `full-text.md` for the verbatim source.

## Temperature

- Temperature decreases with increasing altitude. Forecast at the elevation you will occupy, not the valley floor.
- Expect the summit to be far colder and far windier than the valley.
- In fair weather, air currents flow down streams and hillsides in the early morning and start flowing uphill as the day warms. Valley winds reverse with the sun.

## Wind Chill

- Wind chill is the effect of air temperature and wind speed on exposed skin.
- Formula: **Wind chill (°F) = 35.74 + 0.6215T − 35.75(V^0.16) + 0.4275T(V^0.16)**, where T = temperature (°F) and V = wind speed (mph).
- The chart's warning: **frostbite occurs in 15 minutes or less** at the coldest wind-chill values.
- Leaders check the wind-chill chart before exposing skin — and re-check when wind or temperature changes.

## Humidity and Dew Point

- Humidity is the amount of water vapor in the air. Warmer air holds more moisture.
- When air is saturated (100% relative humidity) and then cooled, water molecules join to create droplets — the temperature at which this happens is the saturation point, or dew point.
- Fog, cloud, and precipitation follow the dew point. Humidity matters because wet cold is far more dangerous than dry cold.

## Pressure and Altitude

- Air pressure decreases as altitude increases, measured in millibars or inches of mercury.
- A barometer is used for forecasting the weather and determining altitude. An altimeter measures altitude by measuring barometric pressure.
- A rising barometer generally means improving weather; a falling barometer means deteriorating weather. Track pressure trends, not just the current reading.

## Clouds

| Cloud type | What it means |
|---|---|
| Cirrus | Ice crystals, usually 20,000–35,000 ft, thin and feathery. Can build hundreds of miles ahead of a front — an early warning. |
| Cumulonimbus | Heavy, dark, towering, anvil-shaped. Thunderstorms — get off exposed ridges and summits before they build. |
| Cumulus | Fair-weather puffs; towering cumulus means convection is building. |
| Stratus / fog | Low cloud, poor visibility, navigation hazard. |

- Cloud type, height, and movement are the leader's forecast.
- Cumulonimbus over peaks in the afternoon is the classic mountain thunderstorm pattern — plan movement to be off high ground before it builds.

## Weather Decision Rules

1. Treat every forecast as a guess with a **6–12 hour horizon**. Re-evaluate at every halt.
2. Forecast at the elevation you will occupy, not the valley floor.
3. If the weather is turning, the route changes — the mountain does not negotiate.
4. Whiteout, fog, and spindrift can erase navigation in minutes — carry map, compass, altimeter, and GPS, and know how to use all four.
5. Lightning kills mountaineers who linger on high ground. Get off exposed ridges and summits before afternoon convection builds.
