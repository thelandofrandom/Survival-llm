# Desert Operations and Maneuver

Distilled from MCTP 12-10D (ATP 3-90.99), Chapters 2–6 (Apr 2021).

## Foundations (Chapter 2)

- Desert operations are defined by **distance, dispersion, and tempo**. The battlefield is wide, forces spread thin, and the side that can move and sustain itself across that distance wins.
- **Multi-domain extended desert battlefield:** cyberspace, space, and the electromagnetic spectrum are part of the battlefield, not an overlay. A desert force that loses its networks is blind across distances where blindness is fatal.
- **Fundamentals:** mobility (move or die — a static force is a target), dispersion (one strike must not cripple the force), security (openness makes surprise hard to achieve and hard to prevent), sustainment (water, fuel, ammunition over long lines), tempo (the side that sets the pace forces the other to react across punishing distances).

## Combat Power (Chapter 3)

Each warfighting function is degraded by the desert and must be deliberately compensated:

- **Leadership:** water, heat, and navigation discipline enforced by the leader's own example.
- **Information:** in open desert, information is the terrain — see further, understand faster.
- **Command and control:** long distances stretch C2; mission command (clear intent, decentralized execution) is a necessity, not a preference.
- **Movement and maneuver:** mobility is the decisive advantage; the force that can move and sustain movement chooses the fight.
- **Intelligence:** openness aids collection and defeats concealment; the problem is sorting the real from the mirage.
- **Fires:** long fields of fire and excellent observation favor fires; heat shimmer, dust, and distance degrade acquisition and damage assessment.
- **Sustainment:** water, fuel, ammunition over long, exposed lines of communication.
- **Protection:** dispersion, camouflage where there is no cover, and protection against the environment itself.

## Offensive Operations (Chapter 4)

- **Surprise is hard and valuable:** movement is observed, so surprise comes from speed, deception, and attacking across ground the enemy considers impassable.
- **Concentration by movement, not assembly:** forces dispersed for survival converge on the objective, strike, and disperse again.
- **Audacity matters more in the desert** — distances punish hesitation.
- **Favored forms of maneuver:** envelopment and turning movement — the wide, fast move around a flank the open ground permits and a linear defense cannot cover.
- **Frontal attack is a last resort:** it plays to the defender's long fields of fire.
- **Consolidate gains:** secure the water and the ground, reorganize, prepare for the counterattack across the same open ground.

## Defensive Operations (Chapter 5)

- The desert defense is a defense of **positions that dominate movement**, not of lines — there is no continuous line in the desert.
- Depth comes from dispersion and successive positions.
- **Reverse slope defense (Figure 5-1)** — the desert classic: position on the reverse slope, hidden from enemy observation and direct fire; the enemy crests the ridge into a killing zone at close range. Trades long fields of fire for surprise and protection; in a desert where the attacker can see everything, being unseen is worth more than seeing far.
- **Types:** area defense, mobile defense, retrograde. Always plan the transition to the offense — the desert punishes the force that sits still.

## Stability Operations (Chapter 6)

- Population in the desert is concentrated at water, roads, and towns — those are the decisive points.
- **SWEAT-MSO lines of effort:** sewer, water, electricity, academics, trash, medical, safety, other — the civil framework that makes stability stick.
- Stability is not the absence of combat; it is combat's continuation by other means. Warfighting function considerations and transitions apply as in offense and defense.
