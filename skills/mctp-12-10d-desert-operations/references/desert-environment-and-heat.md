# Desert Environment, Water, and Heat

Distilled from MCTP 12-10D (ATP 3-90.99), Chapter 1 (Apr 2021).

## Desert Classifications

| Type | Character | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Mountain desert | Scattered ranges, barren hills/mountains, flat basins; salt lakes | Great Salt Lake, Dead Sea |
| Rocky plateau desert | Extensive flat areas, solid/broken rock at or near surface; good trafficability, poor cover | National Training Center, Syrian Golan Heights |
| Polar desert | Arctic/Antarctic; icecap and glacier; widest temperature range on earth | Antarctica, Arctic Circle |
| Sandy / dune desert | Sand seas and dune fields; worst trafficability, worst dust | — |

Common denominator of all four: lack of significant annual precipitation.

## Temperature Extremes

- Highest known ambient temperature: **136°F (58°C)**. Internal tank temperatures in the Sahara in WWII approached **160°F (71°C)**.
- Winter in Siberian deserts and the Gobi: **−50°F (−45°C)**. Antarctic polar desert: **−128°F (−88°C)**.
- Inland Sinai day-to-night swing: up to **72°F**. Equip and dress for both extremes in the same 24 hours.

## Winds

- Near-hurricane force with dust and sand; visibility can drop to meters.
- Sahara *khamseen*: lasts days, spring and summer. Iran's "wind of 120 days": sand from the north at up to **75 mph**, almost constant.
- No one is buried alive by a sandstorm, but individuals are separated from their units — separation in the desert is a casualty.
- Dust raised by wheels, tracks, and marching troops betrays movement; wind drags the signature downwind for miles.

## Water Discipline

- Forecast water need before, during, and after the mission. When supply is limited, adjust the plan.
- **The conservation trap:** troops who recognize water's value may not drink enough. Enforce drinking — encourage troops to drink more than they think necessary, especially during acclimatization.
- At rest in high desert heat, a person may lose **½ qt/hr** sweating. In very high heat and low humidity, sweat evaporates so fast the skin looks dry — the person who looks dry is losing water fastest.
- Heavy sustained work at **80°F WBGT**: nearly **¼ gallon of water an hour**.
- **Water loss:** 2 qt (1.8 L) = 2.5% of body weight = **25% loss of efficiency**. 15% of body weight loss is usually fatal. ~75% of the body is fluid.
- **Hyponatremia warning:** over **3 gallons in a single day** may cause a potentially fatal electrolyte emergency. Enforce the table, not unlimited drinking.

## WBGT Work/Rest and Water Consumption Guide (Table 1-1)

Sustains performance and hydration for at least 4 hours. Individual needs vary ±¼ qt/hr. Rest = minimal activity, in shade if possible.

| Heat cat. | WBGT (°F) | Easy: work/rest, water | Moderate: work/rest, water | Hard: work/rest, water |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 78–81.9 | NL, ½ qt/hr | NL, ¾ qt/hr | 40/20, ¾ qt/hr |
| 2 (Green) | 82–84.9 | NL, ½ qt/hr | 50/10, ¾ qt/hr | 30/30, 1 qt/hr |
| 3 (Yellow) | 85–87.9 | NL, ¾ qt/hr | 40/20, ¾ qt/hr | 30/30, 1 qt/hr |
| 4 (Red) | 88–89.9 | NL, ¾ qt/hr | 30/30, ¾ qt/hr | 20/40, 1 qt/hr |
| 5 (Black) | > 90 | 50/10, 1 qt/hr | 20/40, 1 qt/hr | 10/50, 1 qt/hr |

- **Limits:** hourly intake ≤ **1½ qt**; daily intake ≤ **12 qt**.
- **Gear corrections:** body armor in humid climates adds **5°F** to WBGT; CBRN protective ensemble adds **10°F**. MOPP gear: +10°F. Apply corrections *before* setting the work/rest cycle.

## Heat Injury and Dehydration

- **Early dehydration:** loss of coordination, fatigue, inability to concentrate, headache. Thirst present but not overpowering — do not wait for overwhelming thirst.
- **Heat exhaustion:** most common heat injury; generally not associated with organ damage.
- **Heat stroke:** most dangerous; the body's heat-balance mechanism fails. Requires medical treatment and supervision.
- **Risk factors:** prior heat injury (any heat stroke, or more than two episodes of heat exhaustion), lack of acclimatization, conditions/drugs impairing sweating.
- **Acclimatization:** minimize physical activity during the first days of heat exposure; graded exposure over days.
- Dehydration risk is just as great in polar deserts as in hot ones. Chronic dehydration increases constipation, kidney stones, and other problems.
- Diarrheal disease compounds dehydration.

## Clothing and PPE

- **Remain fully clothed** in the desert: covering skin retains sweat (improves cooling) and prevents direct sun on skin. Stripping down increases water loss and sun injury.
- Flavored electrolyte additives improve palatability and encourage drinking. Avoid alcohol, caffeine, and other diuretics — they accelerate dehydration.
