# Foundations, Components, Proficiencies — FM 3-50

## Definition

Army personnel recovery is the military efforts taken to prepare for and execute the recovery and reintegration of isolated personnel. Army forces work with DOD and other U.S. Government organizations, and with multinational partners, to recover persons separated from their unit or in a situation where they must survive, evade, resist, or escape.

**Who is at risk:** Soldiers, Army civilians, contractors accompanying the force (CAAF), and other designated persons — in hostile or permissive environments.

## Foundations

- **Four functions:** preparation, planning, execution, adaptation.
- **Five execution tasks:** report, locate, support, recover, reintegrate.
- **Mission command:** commanders, supported by their staffs, exercise mission command of the PR tasks. Commanders are familiar with the tasks and prepared to execute them — PR is not delegated away and forgotten.

## Organizations

- At the joint force level, the PR organization links the joint force commander, the Joint Personnel Recovery Center (JPRC), and the component commanders.
- Inside the Army, the PR organizational structure is built from doctrine, policy, procedures, people, organizations, equipment, and information systems.
- At every echelon the structure is visible in two activities: maintaining the unit's link to the chain of command, and maintaining personnel recovery within the unit.
- Senior leaders establish and maintain the structure; commanders and staffs build a foundation for prevention of, preparation for, and response to isolating events.

## The Other Services (DOD Policy)

DOD policy directs the Services to develop a PR capability; each does it differently:
- **Marine Corps:** tactical recovery of aircraft and personnel (TRAP).
- **Navy:** PR to support surface, subsurface, and air assets.
- **Air Force:** combat search and rescue is a primary task; equips and trains for it.
- **Coast Guard:** PR to support surface and air assets.

## Focal Groups

The three entities most concerned with applying PR doctrinal principles:
1. **Commander and staff** — leadership and accountability at every echelon, from company and below through battalion and brigade to echelons above brigade.
2. **Unit or recovery force** — the force that executes the recovery.
3. **Isolated persons** — the individual whose actions (survival, evasion, authentication, reporting) make recovery possible.

**Commander and staff focal group actions:**
- Develop and disseminate general PR guidance; revise as conditions change. Subordinate leaders build their guidance on higher headquarters'.
- Produce **isolated Soldier guidance (ISG)** based on the PR guidance — more developed and refined than the commander's PR guidance, force-tailored by leaders at all echelons for each specific mission within the AO. When all three focal groups base their actions on the ISG, recovery is facilitated.
- Maintain personnel accountability at all times — accurate accountability and reporting narrow the focus of search operations and save time and lives.

## U.S. Government PR Options

The options include the diplomatic and civil efforts alongside the military methods. Partners typically represented at an embassy include USAID, the Department of Agriculture, the Central Intelligence Agency, the Department of Commerce, DOD, the Drug Enforcement Administration, the Department of Justice, the Department of Homeland Security, the Department of the Treasury, and the Federal Aviation Administration.

## The Five Tasks in Army Terms

- **Report** — most effective when conducted soon after the command confirms isolation.
- **Locate** — find and confirm the identity of isolated personnel.
- **Support** — support to the isolated person and to the next of kin.
- **Recover** — sometimes a subordinate task of another mission.
- **Reintegrate** — goal: return the isolated person to duty with minimum physical and psychological harm. Commanders force-tailor reintegration to individual needs. Initial actions secure recovered individuals, provide basic life support, and transport them. The reintegration team focuses on debriefing and ensures medical and behavioral health care before return to duty. The commander determines who has access to recovered persons; public affairs coordinates public statements. A seamless transition from recovery force to reintegration team facilitates an effective return to duty. The PR coordination staff element (PRCS) documents each isolating event and its aftermath. Government agencies or contract organizations participate in the reintegration of civilians.

## Shared PR Proficiencies (Figure 1-6)

Built on the training principles of ADRP 7-0. Army initial entry training introduces all Soldiers to the Warrior Ethos and basic combat skills; individual and collective training in units provides further instruction; SERE training and exercises contribute to individual preparation.

1. **Structure** — establish and maintain the PR organizational structure.
2. **Communicate** — the responsibility of all three focal groups. Integrated information systems and knowledge management move knowledge between headquarters and individuals. The quality of the information matters as much as the hardware: a command's ability to improve situational awareness about an isolated person directly correlates to a positive outcome. Communication is most important to the isolated person — they expect higher headquarters and recovery forces to have robust systems to identify and locate them, but that does not relieve the individual of the responsibility to know how to communicate with those organizations.
3. **Navigate** — determine position and move toward safety or a recovery site.
4. **Preserve life** — survival medicine, water, food, shelter, and protection.
5. **Endure hardship** — the psychological resilience to survive isolation, evasion, and captivity and return with honor.
