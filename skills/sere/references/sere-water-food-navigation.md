# SERE Water, Food, Navigation, and Travel

Source: AFH 10-644, Chapters 16 (Water), 17 (Food), 18 (Land Navigation), 19 (Land Travel and Evacuation Techniques), 20 (River Travel).

## Water (Ch. 16)

- Requirements: **~2 quarts/day** in cold climates, **5–6 quarts/day** with heat and exertion. Thirst is a late indicator.
- Sources and indicators: animal trails downhill, birds circling at dawn/dusk, green vegetation, insects near water.
- Collection: rain catchers, transpiration bags, solar stills (low yield — build several), dew, green bamboo, vines (cut high first, then low; reject milky or bitter sap), banana stumps.
- Purification: boiling is safest (**1 minute** at sea level, **3 minutes above 8,000 ft**); iodine 2% tincture, **5 drops per quart** (10 if cloudy or cold), wait 30 minutes. Treat all surface water as contaminated.

## Food (Ch. 17)

The most complete food chapter in the vault: plants by region, the Universal Edibility Test, traps and snares, fishing, hunting, butchering, cooking, preservation.

### Universal Edibility Test — 24 hours, one plant at a time

1. Inspect — reject milky sap, almond smell, spines, umbrella flower clusters, beans, bulbs, seeds in pods. Avoid all mushrooms.
2. Smell — crush; reject almond/peach smell.
3. Skin test — inside elbow/wrist, wait 15 minutes.
4. Lip test — wait 15 minutes.
5. Tongue test — hold, wait 15 minutes, do not swallow.
6. Chew and spit — wait 15 minutes.
7. Swallow a small amount — wait 8 hours, eat nothing else.
8. Eat a larger portion — wait another 8 hours.

### Priorities

Plants first (lowest cost and risk), traps and fishing second (passive — they work while you do other tasks), hunting last (highest energy cost). Preserve by smoking, drying, or salting; cook everything thoroughly; never eat raw freshwater fish or meat.

### Traps and snares

Simple loop, Apache foot snare, drag noose, twitch-up, treadle spring, deadfalls (figure-4, Paiute), bow trap, bottle and funnel fish traps, gill nets. Set on game trails, at burrow entrances, near water; check at dawn and dusk; set many.

## Land navigation (Ch. 18)

Map and compass, dead reckoning, terrain association, celestial navigation (Polaris, Southern Cross, sun, moon), shadow-stick and watch methods, pace count, field-expedient direction finding. The concise field version is the `land-navigation` skill (FM 3-25.26).

## Land travel and evacuation (Ch. 19)

- When evading: travel at night, rest by day; use covered routes; avoid ridgelines and open ground; cross danger areas quickly and at the narrowest point.
- Mark your trail for rescuers but not for the enemy.
- Evacuation of the injured: carries and improvised litters; move as a unit; protect the spine.

## River travel (Ch. 20)

- Crossing: slowest, shallowest section; cross at an angle downstream; stick as a third leg; unbuckle the pack; never tie yourself to a rope across a river.
- Rivers as routes: raft or improvised flotation where the river is the way out — but scout ahead for rapids, strainers, and falls.
