# SERE Mission, Psychology, and the Code of Conduct

Source: AFH 10-644, Chapters 1 (Mission), 2 (Conditions Affecting Survival), 3 (The IP's Needs), 4 (Psychological Aspects of SERE).

## Chapter outline

- Ch. 1: IP mission — "return to friendly control without giving aid or comfort to the enemy, to return early and in good physical and mental condition"; the three goals (maintain life, maintain honor, return)
- Ch. 2: Conditions affecting survival — environment, physical condition, equipment, threat; preparation and the evasion plan of action
- Ch. 3: The IP's needs in priority order — water, food, shelter/clothing, fire, health, security
- Ch. 4: Psychological aspects — stressors, reactions, captivity psychology, the will to survive

## The IP's mission and goals

| Goal | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Maintain life | Water, food, shelter, health — the survival skills |
| Maintain honor | The Code of Conduct; no aid or comfort to the enemy; faith with fellow prisoners |
| Return | Early, and in good physical and mental condition |

"Friendly control" is not only a combat situation: a pilot down in the Arctic at −40°F or a crew separated in 120°F desert is in a hostile environment even in peacetime.

## The IP's needs — priority order and numbers

1. Water — about **2 quarts/day** in cold climates, **5–6 quarts/day** with heat and exertion.
2. Food — energy and morale; plants first, traps second, hunting last.
3. Shelter and clothing — protection from the elements.
4. Fire — warmth, water purification, cooking, signaling.
5. Health — prevention, hygiene, foot care, rest.
6. Security — concealment and evasion.

## The Code of Conduct (six articles)

1. I am an American, fighting in the forces which guard my country and our way of life. I am prepared to give my life in their defense.
2. I will never surrender of my own free will. If in command, I will never surrender the members of my command while they still have the means to resist.
3. If I am captured, I will continue to resist by all means available. I will make every effort to escape and aid others to escape.
4. If I am captured, I will keep faith with my fellow prisoners. I will give no information or take part in any action which might be harmful to my comrades. If I am senior, I will take command. If not, I will obey the lawful orders of those appointed over me.
5. When questioned, should I become a prisoner of war, I am required to give name, rank, service number, and date of birth. I will evade answering further questions to the utmost of my ability.
6. I will never forget that I am an American, fighting for freedom, responsible for my actions, and dedicated to the principles which made my country free.

The Code is useful at all times and in all situations — moral obligations apply in peacetime as well as in wartime.

## Psychology — stressors and countermeasures

Stressors: pain, cold, heat, thirst, hunger, fatigue, boredom, loneliness, fear. Reactions: fear, anxiety, anger, guilt, depression, loneliness, withdrawal, panic. Countermeasures: establish a routine; set small achievable goals; keep busy with survival tasks; maintain a realistic recovery plan; conserve energy; rest on a schedule.

## Captivity

Maintain military bearing and discipline; organize with fellow prisoners; communicate; resist exploitation; give no information beyond name, rank, service number, and date of birth; never forget that POWs will not be forgotten. Interrogation resistance is built on preparation: know the Code, know what you will and will not say.
