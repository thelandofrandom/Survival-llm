## Distinguishing features of urbanized terrain

| Feature | Consequence |
|---|---|
| Three-dimensional battlefield (street, rooftop, basement/sewer) | Must clear up and down, not just across |
| Short ranges (≤50 m in streets, room distance inside) | Short-range weapons dominate; long-range weapons lose advantage |
| Cover and concealment everywhere — both sides | Defender hard to find and hit; attacker exposed in the street |
| Fragmented command and control | Radios blocked; small units fight semi-independently |
| High ammunition and casualty rates | Grenades, demolitions, medical support consumed fast |
| Civilian presence | Every fire mission and entry must account for noncombatants |
| Infrastructure as weapon and target | Water, power, sewage, comms can be denied or destroyed |
| Restricted maneuver | Streets canalize movement into predictable, ambushable routes |

## Phases of the urban attack

1. **Isolate** — cut the objective off from reinforcement and resupply; seize key terrain; block routes; establish a cordon.
2. **Assault** — deliberate attack on the objective, building by building.
3. **Exploit / consolidate** — secure ground taken; reorganize; prepare for counterattack or the next objective.

## Offense by echelon

| Echelon | Urban tasks |
|---|---|
| Battalion | Seize key objectives; infiltration; route security |
| Company | Attack within a built-up area; attack an outpost; seize a traffic circle or major intersection; seize key terrain; movement to contact |
| Platoon | Attack a building; move down a street; employ the reserve |

## Defense — key points

- Defend a built-up area to deny it, buy time, inflict casualties, or hold key terrain.
- Reasons *not* to defend: ties the force to fixed positions; invites isolation; risks being cut off; places civilians in the line of fire.
- Organize in depth: forward positions, main battle area, reserve.
- Battalion: defense in sector; delay in a built-up area; defense of a village.
- Company: defense of a village; delay; defense of a city block; defense of a traffic circle or key intersection.
- Platoon: defense of a strongpoint; defense against armor; armored ambush (let enemy armor enter a street, engage from flanks and above).

## Combat support — urban notes

| Support | Urban reality |
|---|---|
| Artillery | Degraded by building masking; high collateral risk |
| Mortars | More useful than artillery in the close fight (high angle, short range, responsive) |
| Naval gunfire | Available in coastal cities; same masking/collateral problems |
| Aviation | Constrained by target ID needs and rooftop small-arms / MANPADS threat |
| Armor | Direct fire support and breaching; vulnerable without infantry protection |
| Antiarmor weapons | Infantry's answer to enemy armor |
| Snipers | Disproportionately effective: one sniper can halt a street or dominate an intersection |
| Engineers | A maneuver arm in urban ops: breach, clear mines, build positions |
| Communications | Buildings block radios; plan relay, retrans, runners from the start |
| Sensors | Acoustic, seismic, thermal, unmanned aerial — find an enemy who is hard to see |

## Logistics — critical classes of supply

Class I (food and water), Class III (fuel), Class V (ammunition — especially grenades and demolitions), Class VIII (medical). Resupply moves in protected convoys or by air; forward stockpiles inside secured buildings. Health service support is forward and heavy: casualty collection points in buildings, evacuation routes through the city.

## Noncombatants — checklist

- [ ] Impact on fires and maneuver assessed
- [ ] Command authority and legal basis defined (law of war, ROE, mission)
- [ ] Health and welfare: food, water, medical care, shelter
- [ ] Law and order: prevent looting and violence
- [ ] Public affairs / media plan (the battle is fought on television too)
- [ ] Civil affairs and psychological operations: communicate with the population
- [ ] Refugee control: manage displaced civilians so they do not block operations or become casualties

## Restrictive conditions

- Rules of engagement are tightest in urban operations: positive ID before firing, disciplined fires, coordination with civilian authorities.
- The enemy will exploit the restrictions — using civilians as shields, firing from protected structures, counting on your restraint.
- MOOTW (peacekeeping, humanitarian assistance, counterterrorism, noncombatant evacuation) is the common urban context outside declared war.
