# Building Clearing and Urban Movement (MCRP 12-10B.1, Appendices A and C)

## Organization for urban combat

The rifle squad and fire team are reorganized for urban combat into assault, support, and security elements, with breaching and clearing teams designated. The organization is ad hoc and task-specific — a squad that clears buildings needs different teams than one that defends a strongpoint.

## Movement — the rules

- **Crossing a wall:** climb over quickly with overwatch; do not silhouette on top.
- **Observation around corners:** use a mirror, camera, or a quick "slice of the pie" look — lean out incrementally, weapon ready, never stepping into the open.
- **Movement past windows:** stay below the sill, move fast, do not silhouette.
- **Doorways:** never stand in a doorway — it is a fatal funnel, pre-aimed and pre-ranged. Pass through quickly or breach a wall instead.
- **Movement parallel to buildings:** hug the wall; use the building as cover.
- **Crossing open areas:** by rush, in small groups, with overwatch and smoke.
- **Movement inside a building:** along walls, through hallways and stairwells, always with a teammate covering.

## Entry techniques

| Technique | Notes |
|---|---|
| Upper levels / roof down | Preferred — the defender expects attack from below |
| Ladders | Quick access to upper floors and rooftops |
| Grappling hook | Scale walls when no ladder is available |
| Scaling walls | Slow, exposed — needs overwatch |
| Rappelling | Fast descent to a roof or upper floor |
| Lower levels / breaching the exterior wall | Best way in — a door is a known, defended point |
| Hand grenades | Clear a room before entry; thrower moves away from any wall that might be penetrated by fragments |

## Clearing techniques (Appendix A, Section V; Appendix C)

1. **Room, door open or closed, stacked positions** — team stacks on the door, enters on the breach/grenade, dominates the room, covers the corners.
2. **Room, door closed, split positions** — team splits on both sides of the door to cover both directions.
3. **Covering team members** — designated shooters cover the entry team from the hallway or an adjacent position.
4. **Room through a mousehole** — enter through a breached hole in the wall; same domination drill, different entry point.
5. **L-shaped hallway** — clear the near leg first, then pie off the far leg.
6. **T-shaped hallway** — clear each branch systematically; never expose yourself to two branches at once.
7. **Stairwell** — clear from above when possible; treat each landing as a room; never rush a stairwell blind.

## Common clearing principles

- Dominate the room on entry; cover the corners first.
- Never enter alone. Never cross in front of a teammate's muzzle.
- Never stop in the doorway.
- Clear from the top down: enter from the roof or an upper floor, push the defender downward, keep cleared floors clear.
- Post security on cleared areas; plan for casualties and prisoners.
- **Mouseholes** — holes breached between rooms or through walls with demolitions, so the attacker moves through walls rather than through defended doorways and hallways. The best way to enter a building is to breach the exterior wall.

## Firing positions

- **Hasty:** quickly prepared, using available cover (furniture, rubble, a low wall).
- **Prepared:** built up with sandbags, rubble, and reinforcement; loopholes (firing ports) cut in walls; protection against grenades and indirect fire; a withdrawal route planned. A position that cannot be abandoned is a trap.
- **Target acquisition:** urban targets are fleeting and close; scan windows, doorways, rooftops, and rubble.
- **Firefighting planning:** buildings burn; plan for fire, smoke, and evacuation.

## Street movement

- Move along one side of the street at a time, using buildings as cover.
- **Crossing an intersection** — the most dangerous point in urban movement: cross at the narrowest point, with overwatch and smoke, in small groups, by rush.
- **Reacting to enemy contact:** return fire, take cover in a building, report, maneuver. Do not stay in the street.

## Navigation and camouflage

- Navigate by street names, building numbers, and prominent features; compass and GPS are degraded among buildings.
- Camouflage: face paint, uniform, equipment; use shadows; match the urban background (concrete, brick, rubble), not the forest.
