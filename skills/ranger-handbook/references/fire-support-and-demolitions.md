## Chapter 3 — Fire support: outline

- Basic tasks and targeting: detect, deliver, assess, integrate.
- Interdiction: limit, divert, disrupt, delay, destroy, damage enemy surface potential.
- Risk estimate distance (RED): Table 3-3; danger close 600 m; RED is a combat number, minimum safe distance is a training number.
- Fire support overlay: sterile vs. nonsterile (Figures 3-1, 3-2); contents per Table 3-4.
- Close air support: immediate CAS request format; fixed-wing (Table 3-7), unmanned (Table 3-8), rotary-wing (Table 3-10) capabilities.
- Army attack aviation.

## The four basic tasks of fire support

1. **Detect** — find the target (observers, sensors, intelligence).
2. **Deliver** — engage with the right system and munition.
3. **Assess** — battle damage assessment; did the fires achieve the desired effect?
4. **Integrate** — the thread through the other three: fires synchronized with maneuver so friendly forces are never the target.

## Mortar and artillery capabilities (Tables 3-1, 3-2)

**How to use RED in the field:** if friendly troops are inside the RED for the system and range band being fired, the risk of friendly casualties exceeds 0.1% and the mission requires commander's acceptance — "danger close" is not a clearance, it is a warning that the observer's target location must be exact. Prone posture buys roughly 15–30 meters over standing. Shorter range bands are safer than max range for the same system.

## Fire support overlay

A **sterile** overlay shows only the fire support coordination measures that apply to the operation; a **nonsterile** overlay shows all graphics. Table 3-4 standardizes the overlay's contents so any observer can read any unit's overlay: boundaries, fire support coordination measures, targets, no-fire areas, restrictive fire areas/lines, and the observer's location. The target–trigger–location–observer–delivery system–attack guidance–communications checklist (Table 3-5) is the pre-fire sanity check: every fire mission needs a trigger (what event starts the fires), a location, an observer, a delivery system, attack guidance (what effect), and comms.

## Close air support

**Immediate CAS request format:**
- a. Observer identification.
- b. WARNORD — "request close air."
- c. Target description — type and number of targets, activity or movement, point or area target.
- d. Target location (grid), including elevation.
- e. Desired time on target.
- f. Desired effects on target.
- g. Final control.
- h. Remarks — friendly locations; wind direction and hazards; threats (air-defense artillery, small arms).

**Fixed-wing CAS (Table 3-7):** F-15E Strike Eagle (JDAM, LJDAM, LGB, CBU/WCMD, AGM-130/158, JSOW, 20-mm cannon; Link 16; FLIR/SAR/Sniper/LITENING; VHF-AM/FM, UHF, SATCOM); AV-8B Harrier II (LGB, AGM-65E, GP bombs, CBU, JDAM, 2.75-in rockets, 5-in Zuni); A-10C Thunderbolt II (LGB, AGM-65, GP bombs, CBU/WCMD, JDAM, 2.75-in rockets, 30-mm cannon); EC-130 (electronic warfare). **Unmanned systems (Table 3-8)** and **rotary-wing CAS (Table 3-10)** are tabulated the same way — aircraft, ordnance, sensors/markings, data link, frequency band, frequency hopping, COMSEC. **Army attack aviation** (section 3-18) covers AH-64 employment in support of the patrol. The FO's job in all of it: observer identification, exact target grid with elevation, and honest remarks about friendlies, wind, and threats.

**Low explosives** detonate at up to 1,300 ft/s — a pushing/shoving effect. **High explosives** detonate at 3,280–27,888 ft/s — a shattering effect. C-4 (M112) is the patrol's workhorse: moldable, stable, waterproof, cut to shape for cutting and breaching charges. Detonating cord (PETN core) is both a primer and a means of linking multiple charges to fire simultaneously.

## Initiating and firing systems

- **MDI (modernized demolition initiator):** blasting caps pre-attached to shock tube or detonating cord, fired with a fuze igniter. Five MDI cap types replace the legacy M6 electric and M7 nonelectric caps. Best practice is to prime with MDIs.
- **Shock tube:** a thin extruded-polymer tube with a thin layer of special explosive on the inner wall. It carries the initiation signal as a low-energy detonation wave — no electric current, so no stray-current or radio-frequency hazard. Cut with the technique in Figure 5-1 (square cut, no crushing).
- **M11:** MDI cap with an attached, movable J-hook for quick attachment to detonating cord. **M14:** high-strength MDI cap.
- **Dual initiating system (Figure 5-4):** two independent initiation paths to the same charge — if one fails, the other fires. **British J initiating system (Figure 5-5):** an alternative dual-path layout.
- **Firing system components (Figure 5-2):** initiator (fuze igniter/M81), transmission line (shock tube/det cord), blasting cap, main charge. Every component is checked before the charge is placed.
- **Demolitions effects simulator charge (Figures 5-6 to 5-9):** a training device — constructed system, dual-initiated variant, and a variant using an uli knot with 12–18 inches of detonating cord; the cardboard cutout dimensions are standardized so the simulator is built the same way every time.

## Demolitions safety

Safety is its own section (5-10), not a footnote. Working rules distilled from the chapter: prime with MDIs where available; build dual initiation for any charge that must fire; keep the firing system and the charge separate until the moment of emplacement; never crush or kink shock tube; verify every connection before arming; and treat a misfire as a live charge — wait, do not approach immediately, and re-approach only per unit SOP and TM 3-34.82. The M18A1 Claymore (carried in the patrol's basic load — 3 per M4 gunner per the load table in chapter 2) is a command-detonated directional mine, not a demolition charge, but it is emplaced by the same safety discipline.

**Grazing fire rule:** on level or uniformly sloping terrain, the M240B and M249 attain a maximum of 600 meters of grazing fire; the M2 attains 700 meters. Grazing fire is the defense's backbone — a beaten zone no more than a meter above the ground across the platoon's front.

**Classes of fire:**
- **Fixed** — one point, no traverse or elevation change.
- **Traversing** — distributed in width across a target.
- **Searching** — distributed in depth.
- **Traversing and searching** — both, for a deep and wide target.
- **Swinging traverse** — fired at the cyclic rate across a wide front; consumes a lot of ammunition and is used only when the target demands it.
- **Free gun** — the gunner engages targets of opportunity without a fixed pattern.

**Employment:** offense — the PL establishes a base-of-fire element (M240B/M249, plus MK19/M2 if available) to suppress while the assault element maneuvers; the MK19 and M2 are particularly effective at preventing lightly armored enemy vehicles from escaping the kill zone. Defense — guns sited for interlocking grazing fire across the front; tripod-mounted M2 and MK19 are highly accurate out to their maximum effective ranges. **Control** — the WSL designates the principal direction of fire and secondary sectors, shifts and corrects fires, alternates guns, and enforces rates of fire so barrels survive the fight.
