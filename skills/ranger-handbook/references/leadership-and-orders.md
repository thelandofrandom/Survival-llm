# Leadership and Orders — TC 3-21.76, Chapters 1–2 and 4

## Chapter 1 — Leadership: outline

- Leadership requirements model (Table 1-1): attributes (character, presence, intellect), competencies (leads, develops, achieves), levels (direct, organizational, situational), outcomes (mission success, sound decisions, fit units, healthy climates).
- Patrol roles and duties: PL, PSG, SL, WSL, TL, medic, RTO, FO — each with duties in planning, movement, halts, danger areas, actions on the objective, and the patrol base.
- Assumption of command (Table 1-2): inform, check security, check crew-served weapons, pinpoint location, coordinate/check equipment, check personnel status, issue FRAGORDs, reorganize, maintain noise/light discipline, continue patrol-base activities, reconnoiter, finalize plan.

## The leadership requirements model in detail

**Character** — Army values, empathy, warrior ethos, service ethos, discipline. **Presence** — military and professional bearing, fitness, confidence, resilience. **Intellect** — mental agility, sound judgment, innovation, interpersonal tact, expertise.

**Leads** — leads others; builds trust; extends influence beyond the chain of command; leads by example; communicates. **Develops** — creates a positive environment and fosters esprit de corps; prepares self; develops others; stewards the profession. **Achieves** — gets results.

**Levels of leadership:** direct (refine ability to apply competencies at a proficient level), organizational (apply competencies to increasingly complex situations), situational (adjust actions to complex and uncertain environments). **Special conditions:** formal (designated by rank or position), informal (initiative taken, special expertise applied), collective (synergy from multiple leaders aligned by purpose).

**Outcomes:** secured U.S. interests, mission success, sound decisions, expertly led organizations, stewardship of resources, stronger families, fit units, healthy climates, engaged Soldiers and civilians.

## Patrol roles — duty checklists

### Platoon leader (PL)
- Responsible for everything the patrol does or fails to do.
- Establishes the time schedule using reverse planning (execution, movement to the objective, planning/preparation).
- Takes initiative in the absence of orders; keeps higher HQ informed with periodic situation reports.
- Plans with PSG, SLs, TLs, FO, and attachment leaders; stays abreast via coordination with adjacent patrols and higher HQ.
- Positions and employs all assigned/attached crew-served weapons and supporting weapons.
- Commands through the SLs within the intent of the commander two levels up; conducts rehearsals; maintains a position to influence the most critical task (usually with the main effort).

### Platoon sergeant (PSG)
- Second in succession of command; issues OPORD paragraph 4 (Sustainment).
- Organizes and controls the patrol command post per SOP and METT-TC (I).
- Maintains patrol status of personnel, weapons, equipment; consolidates casualty reports (DA Form 1156); receives and orients replacements.
- Supervises the medic and aid-and-litter teams; ensures the CASEVAC plan is complete and executed.
- At danger areas: directs near-side security (usually trail squad/team), maintains accountability.
- On the objective: supervises the objective rally point, final preparation of Soldiers/weapons/equipment, consolidation and reorganization of ammunition and equipment, and the casualty collection point.
- In the patrol base: assists occupation, establishes/adjusts the perimeter, enforces security, camouflage, and noise discipline; assigns sectors of fire; ensures alert personnel and equipment readiness.
- Security plan contents: interlocking sectors of fire for crew-served weapons; claymores covering dead space; complete range cards and sector sketches; alert plan; evacuation plan; withdrawal plan; alternate patrol base; maintenance, hygiene, messing, water, and rest plans.

### Squad leader (SL)
- Responsible for what the squad does or fails to do; leads by example.
- Inspects weapons, clothing, equipment; completes casualty feeder cards; submits the LACE report (liquids, ammunition, casualties, equipment) to the PSG.
- During movement: rotates heavy equipment and shares difficult duties; maintains movement techniques, route, pace, and azimuth; prevents breaks in contact; ensures subordinate leaders disseminate information, assign sectors of fire, and check personnel.
- On the objective: ensures special equipment is ready; maintains positive control; positions key weapons; redistributes ammunition; reports status.
- In the patrol base: ensures occupation per plan; interlocking fires over the entire sector; sends out listening/observation posts per METT-TC (I); ensures priorities of work are accomplished; ensures personnel know the alert and evacuation plans and the locations of key leaders, OPs, and the alternate base.

### Weapons squad leader (WSL)
- Same duties as the SL, plus: controls the machine guns in support of the mission; advises the PL on employment.
- Supervises machine gun teams (priorities of work, range cards, fighting positions, fire plan understanding); supervises machine gun maintenance.
- Designates sectors of fire, principal direction of fire, and secondary sectors for all guns.
- Fire commands: shift fires; correct windage or elevation; alternate firing guns; control rates of fire and fire distribution.
- Knows locations of assault and security elements at all times to prevent fratricide; rotates loads (machine gunners tire first).

### Team leader (TL)
- Controls the fire team's movement and the rate and placement of fire; leads from the front; uses proper commands and signals.
- Maintains accountability of Rangers, weapons, equipment; enforces unit standards.
- WARNORD: assists in control of the squad. OPORD preparation: posts schedule changes and team duties, submits and picks up ammunition/supply requests, distributes ammunition and special equipment. OPORD issuance/rehearsal: monitors the squad, assists the SL during rehearsals, enforces rear security.
- In the objective rally point: supervises final preparation, controls personnel entering/departing, reorganizes the perimeter after the recon party departs, disseminates PIRs.
- In the patrol base: inspects the perimeter for interlocking sectors of fire; prepares the team sector sketch; enforces the priority of work.

### Medic
- Assists the PSG in directing aid-and-litter teams; monitors health and hygiene of the platoon.
- Treats casualties, conducts triage, assists CASEVACs under PSG control; provides technical expertise to combat lifesavers and supervises them.
- Requests Class VIII (medical materiel) through the PSG; ensures casualty feeder cards are correct and attached to each evacuated casualty.

### Radio operator (RTO)
- Establishes and maintains communications with higher HQ and within the patrol.
- Enters the net at the specified time; presets frequencies, COMSEC fills, net identifications; weatherproofs all communications equipment.
- Informs SL/PL of changes to call signs, frequencies, challenges/passwords, and number combinations from the automated net control device.
- En route recorder: tracks time after the assault begins; records all enemy contact in SALUTE format; reports operation schedules to higher HQ.

### Forward observer (FO)
- Works for the PL; locates targets and calls for/adjusts indirect fire (field artillery and mortars).
- Knows the terrain, tactical situation, mission, concept of operations, scheme of maneuver, and priority of fires.
- Selects targets from the company OPORD, PL guidance, and METT-TC (I) analysis; maintains the eight-digit grid coordinate of the current location at all times.

## Chapter 2 — Operations: outline

- Troop leading procedures (Table 2-1): 8 steps; 1/3 : 2/3 rule; WARNORD contents.
- Combat information: METT-TC (I) mission analysis; specified vs. implied tasks (Table 2-2); constraints (prescriptive/prohibitive); mission-essential tasks; gauge–evaluate–analyze–reduce framework (Figure 2-1, Table 2-6).
- Operation order: five-paragraph format; fragmentary order; annexes; coordination checklists; actions/purpose/operations/tasks; terrain model (Figure 2-2).

## TLP in detail

1. **Receive the mission** — OPORD or FRAGORD from higher.
2. **Issue a warning order** — type of operation; general location; initial timeline with critical times; reconnaissance to initiate; movement to initiate; planning/preparation instructions and timeline; information requirements; commander's critical information requirements.
3. **Make a tentative plan** — detailed mission analysis on METT-TC (I); higher intent two levels up (OPORD para 1b) and one level up (paras 2–3); specified and implied tasks; constraints; mission-essential tasks; courses of action; wargaming.
4. **Initiate movement** — begin movement necessary for the mission (recon, quartering party) while planning continues.
5. **Conduct reconnaissance** — leader's recon, map recon at minimum; confirm or deny planning assumptions.
6. **Complete the plan** — finalize after recon.
7. **Issue the operation order** — five paragraphs, briefed on the terrain model, with rehearsals.
8. **Supervise and refine** — spot-checks, rehearsals, and continuous refinement until execution.

**1/3 : 2/3 rule:** the leader uses one-third of available planning and preparation time; subordinates use two-thirds. The rule applies to planning and preparation only.

## METT-TC (I) — the mission variables

- **M**ission — task and purpose, commander's intent two levels up.
- **E**nemy — disposition, composition, strength, capabilities, most likely and most dangerous courses of action.
- **T**errain and weather — OAKOC: observation and fields of fire, avenues of approach, key terrain, obstacles, cover and concealment; weather effects on both sides.
- **T**roops and support available — own and attached forces, combat power, sustainment.
- **T**ime available — planning, preparation, movement, execution; reverse planning from the objective.
- **C**ivil considerations — ASCOPE: areas, structures, capabilities, organizations, people, events.
- **I**nformational considerations — what the enemy and the population know, and how information affects the mission.

## The five-paragraph OPORD

1. **Situation** — enemy forces; friendly forces; attachments and detachments; civil considerations.
2. **Mission** — who, what, when, where, why (task + purpose, in one sentence).
3. **Execution** — commander's intent; concept of operations (scheme of maneuver, scheme of fires); tasks to subordinate units; coordinating instructions.
4. **Sustainment** — logistics, personnel services, health service support (issued by the PSG at platoon level).
5. **Command and signal** — command (succession of command, location of key leaders) and signal (frequencies, call signs, challenges/passwords, pyrotechnics, reports).

**FRAGORD** — an abbreviated order that changes the existing OPORD; only the paragraphs that change are issued. **Annexes** carry the detail (fires, sustainment, signal) so the base order stays readable. **Coordination checklists** (section 2-36) are the pre-mission conversations with adjacent units, attachments, and supporting elements — done before the patrol steps off, not after contact.

## Terrain model

Built to scale on the ground (sand table, dirt, or a poncho with sticks and stones), the terrain model shows the objective, routes, rally points, danger areas, and the scheme of maneuver. The OPORD is briefed on it; every Ranger walks the model before the patrol moves. If the model is wrong, the plan is wrong — verify it against the map and the leader's recon.

## Chapter 4 — Communications: key points

- Radios: SINCGARS family for the patrol net; UHF/SATCOM for reach-back.
- Antennas: field-expedient antennas are cut from wire — a vertical element plus three ground-plane wires, each cut to the operating frequency. Antenna height and placement decide range more than transmitter power.
- Line-of-sight limits: the curvature of the Earth (Figure 4-1) caps VHF/UHF range; height of the antenna is the practical fix — get the antenna up.
- The RTO weatherproofs all comms equipment, presets frequencies/COMSEC fills/net IDs, and tracks time and contacts in SALUTE format during the mission.
