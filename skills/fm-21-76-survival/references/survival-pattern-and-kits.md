# Survival Pattern and Kits — FM 21-76

## The SURVIVAL Acronym

- **S — Size up the situation.** Surroundings, physical condition, equipment.
- **U — Undue haste makes waste.**
- **R — Remember where you are.**
- **V — Vanquish fear and panic.**
- **I — Improvise.**
- **V — Value living.**
- **A — Act like the natives.**
- **L — Learn basic skills.**

## Stress Management

Stress is the body's reaction to pressure. Moderate stress sharpens performance; excess becomes distress — panic, forgetfulness, carelessness, angry outbursts, withdrawal, thoughts of death. Manage it with relaxation skills, time management, assertiveness, and cognitive restructuring (control how you view the situation). "The will to survive" is "the refusal to give up."

## The Survival Pattern (Priority Order)

1. **Survival medicine** — always first.
2. **Protection** — fire, shelter, clothing.
3. **Sustenance** — water, then food.
4. **Navigation** — movement toward safety or recovery.

In cold, fire comes before shelter; in heat, water and shade come first. Reassess constantly — the priority that was right at noon may be wrong at midnight.

## Survival Planning

- Planning is preparation: realize something could happen, take steps.
- Include survival considerations in mission planning; know your route and area.
- Keep dental problems fixed and immunizations current.
- Know the standard kits (Appendix A): over-water survival kit, hot-climate survival kit, aviator survival vest. Even if you cannot access them, knowing their contents helps you build your own.

## Kit Layering

- **On the body:** map and compass — always.
- **Load-bearing equipment:** less important items.
- **Rucksack:** bulky items.
- Pick multi-use items; never duplicate a function.
- Case: water-repellent or waterproof, easy to carry or attach, accepts variously sized components, durable (Band-Aid box, first aid case, ammunition pouch).

## Kit Contents (FM 21-76, Chapter 3)

First aid items; water purification tablets or drops; fire-starting equipment; signaling items; food-procurement items; shelter items. Examples: lighter, metal match, waterproof matches; snare wire; signaling mirror; wrist compass; fish and snare line; fishhooks; candle; small hand lens; oxytetracycline tablets; solar blanket; surgical blades; butterfly sutures; condoms for water storage; Chap Stick; needle and thread; knife. Weapon only if the situation dictates.

## Basic Survival Medicine — Checklist

- **Water baseline:** 2–3 liters/day at 20°C (68°F), average exertion. More with heat, cold, activity, altitude, burns, illness.
- **Hygiene:** wash hands, keep feet dry and sound, protect against insects.
- **Lifesaving order:** airway and breathing → stop severe bleeding (direct pressure, pressure dressings) → treat shock (warm, flat, legs elevated unless head/chest/abdominal injury).
- **Fractures:** splint as found; do not straighten a deformed limb.
- **Heat injury:** cool in shade with water and airflow.
- **Cold injury:** warm gradually with insulation and warm liquids; never rub frostbitten tissue; never thaw a frozen limb if it may refreeze.
- **Rashes:** moist → keep dry; dry → keep moist; do not scratch; vinegar or tannic-acid compress (tea, boiled acorns, hardwood bark) for weeping rashes.
- **Field remedies** (use with caution): chlorine bleach, alcohol, vinegar, concentrated salt water, iodine for fungal infections.
