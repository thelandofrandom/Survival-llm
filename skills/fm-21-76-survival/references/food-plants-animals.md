# Food, Plants, Animals — FM 21-76

## Food Procurement

Food is the lowest immediate priority — a healthy person can survive weeks without it — but it sustains energy, warmth, and morale. Fish, trap, snare, and gather.

**Snares:** work while unattended. Set on trails to water, feeding, and bedding areas, and at den mouths. Simple loop snare: use material that will not break under strain; with wire use a figure-8 (locking) loop — once tightened it locks and the animal cannot escape.

## Universal Edibility Test (UET)

**Rules before testing:**
- Test only **one part of one plant at a time**.
- Each part takes **more than 24 hours** to test — only test abundant plants.
- Even after a food tests safe, eat in moderation: large portions on an empty stomach can cause diarrhea, nausea, or cramps.

**Steps (with a waiting period after each):**
1. **Skin contact** — crush a small amount and rub on the inside of the forearm; wait 15 minutes; look for rash or irritation.
2. **Lip contact** — touch a small piece to the lip; wait a few minutes.
3. **Tongue** — place on the tongue; hold briefly; do not swallow.
4. **Chew and spit** — chew a small amount and spit it out; wait.
5. **Small swallow** — swallow a small amount; wait 8 hours; eat nothing else during the wait.
6. **Measured portion** — eat a small portion; wait a further 8+ hours. If no ill effects (nausea, cramps, diarrhea, dizziness), the part is edible in moderation.

**Avoid plants with:** milky sap (dandelion is a safe, recognizable exception); spines, fine hairs, or thorns (prickly pear and thistles are exceptions); mushrooms and fungus — avoid entirely; umbrella-shaped flower clusters (hemlock).

## Poisonous Plants — Danger Signs

Milky sap; almond scent; spines or fine hairs; umbrella-shaped flower clusters; beans or bulbs; seeds inside pods. Never eat a plant you cannot positively identify. Never test an unknown plant by eating a full portion. Learn the edible plants of your area of operations **before** you deploy.

## Dangerous Animals

- Know the dangerous animals of the area before operating there.
- Prevention: watch where you put hands and feet; shake out clothing and bedding; avoid carrion and dens; do not handle unfamiliar animals.
- **Snakebite:** keep the casualty calm and still; keep the bitten limb below heart level; remove rings, watches, and constricting items; evacuate. Do **not** cut, suck, or apply a tourniquet.
- **Sea hazards (Chapter 16):** avoid splashing and blood in the water; if a shark attacks, strike the eyes, gills, or nose.

## Field-Expedient Weapons, Tools, and Equipment

- Improvise clubs, spears, slings, and traps from available material.
- Fashion cutting and digging tools from stone, bone, and metal scrap.
- **The knife is the single most valuable field tool** — protect it, keep it sharp, use it for shelter construction, food preparation, and every other task.
- A weapon is included in the kit only if the situation dictates.
