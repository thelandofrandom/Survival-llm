# Water, Fire, Shelter — FM 21-76

## Water Procurement

**Daily need:** 2–3 liters at 20°C (68°F), average exertion. Heat, cold, activity, altitude, burns, illness increase losses. Thirst is a late, unreliable indicator; in cold weather the thirst response weakens — drink on schedule.

**Finding water:** animal trails converging downhill; birds circling at dawn and dusk; green vegetation in dry country; bees and mosquitoes rarely range far from water. Dig in dry streambeds at the outside of bends; collect dew with cloth at dawn.

**Collecting water:** rain catchers; transpiration bags tied over leafy branches; solar stills (hole, container, clear plastic, stone weight — yields are small, build several); green bamboo sections; vines (cut high first, then low, let it drip — avoid milky or bitter sap); banana stumps; condensation traps. Always carry a container.

## Water Purification

| Method | Procedure |
|---|---|
| Boiling (safest) | Rolling boil 1 minute at sea level; +1 minute per additional 300 m altitude; or 10 minutes anywhere |
| Iodine, 2% tincture | 5 drops per quart clear water; 10 drops if cloudy or cold; wait 30 minutes |
| Purification tablets | Follow directions provided |
| Filtration (clearing only) | Settle 12 hours, or filter through sand, crushed rock, charcoal, cloth — still must purify after |
| Deodorizing | Charcoal from fire; stand 45 minutes |

Rainwater in clean containers or from plants is usually safe. Purify all lake, pond, swamp, spring, and stream water — especially near settlements or in the tropics. Never drink urine or seawater.

**Risks of untreated water:** dysentery (severe prolonged diarrhea, bloody stools, fever, weakness); cholera and typhoid; blood flukes in stagnant tropical water; leeches that attach in throat or nose.

## Firecraft

**Three materials, prepared before the first spark:** tinder (absolutely dry, ignites from a spark), kindling (small sticks), fuel (larger wood).

**Fire lays:**
- **Tepee:** tinder in the middle, kindling cone above — lights easily, burns hot.
- **Lean-to:** tinder under a leaning stick, kindling leaned against it — good in wind.
- **Cross-ditch:** tinder in the middle of a cross, kindling pyramid above, shallow ditch for airflow.

**Ignition (most reliable first):**
1. **Flint and steel** — most reliable direct-spark method; strike hard sharp-edged rock with steel onto tinder.
2. **Convex lens** — bright sunny days only; concentrate rays until tinder smolders, blow gently into flame.
3. **Battery and wire** — touch bare wires together next to tinder.
4. **Gunpowder** — a spark ignites powder; extremely careful.
5. **Friction (bow drill, fire plow, hand drill)** — works but demands dry wood, correct technique, practice. Learn in training, not in an emergency.

Carry at least two ignition methods (lighter + waterproof matches + metal match or flint). Keep fires small and feedable.

## Shelters

**Site selection:** high, dry ground; away from standing water and animal trails; near water and materials; not in a drainage or avalanche path; concealed from the enemy in hostile areas.

**Types:** poncho lean-to, poncho tent, natural-material shelters; snow cave and snow trench in cold weather (snow insulates); below-ground shade shelter in desert.

**Rules:** insulate from the ground (ground contact steals heat faster than air); low entrance away from wind; build just big enough — a shelter too large cannot be warmed.
