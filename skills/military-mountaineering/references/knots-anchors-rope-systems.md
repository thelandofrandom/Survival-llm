## Strength notes

- **Figure-eight family retains roughly 75–80% of rope strength** — the strongest commonly used knot family in the manual.
- Every knot weakens rope; the tighter the bend, the greater the loss. A knot you cannot verify by sight and feel gets retied.
- **Dress, set, inspect:** align strands neatly, pull tight, leave adequate tails, inspect before loading. A sloppy knot is a failed knot that has not been loaded yet.

## Rope and hardware

- **Kernmantle rope:** braided sheath over load-bearing core. Know diameter, length, and history.
- **Webbing:** tubular and flat; sewn slings for anchors and extensions.
- **Carabiners:** rated on the major axis only. Never load across the gate or on the minor axis; keep gates closed and locked under load.
- **Retire rope** that has held a factor-2 fall, shows core damage, soft/flat spots, cuts, or chemical/UV exposure.

## Rope inspection and care checklist

- [ ] Run the rope through your hands before and after every use; feel for soft spots, flat spots, cuts, sheath damage
- [ ] Inspect carabiners for gate function, cracks, and wear
- [ ] Coil properly (butterfly or mountaineer's coil); keep rope out of dirt, chemicals, exhaust
- [ ] Dry before storage; store cool, dark, dry
- [ ] Log falls and heavy loads on each rope

## Anchor decision matrix

| Anchor | When to use | Strength notes | Test |
|---|---|---|---|
| Large tree | Best natural anchor | Tensionless hitch ≈ full rope strength | Push/pull; check roots and trunk soundness |
| Massive boulder / protrusion | Solid natural anchor | Very strong if immovable | Try to move it; pad rope edges |
| Chockstone / tunnel / arch | Natural rock anchor | Strong if the stone cannot shift | Test by loading; check for cracks |
| Pitons | Cracked rock | Good if driven solidly | Bounce-test; a ringing piton is suspect |
| Bolts | Fixed protection | Most reliable artificial anchor | Requires drilling; not an on-the-move option |
| Chocks / nuts | Tapering cracks | Strong when seated correctly | Tug in the direction of load |
| Camming devices | Parallel-sided cracks | Strong; range-limited | Check cam angle and seating |
| Ice screws | Ice | Standard ice anchor; angle to the fall line | Test by loading; check ice quality |
| V-thread (Abalakov) | Ice | Surprisingly strong, retrievable | Two intersecting holes threaded with cord |
| Deadman (buried ski/pack/picket) | Snow/earth | Depends on burial depth and snow consolidation | Load-test before trusting |
| Snow bollard | Snow | Carved horseshoe trench | Needs consolidated snow; load-test |
| Picket holdfast | Snow/earth | Often used in series | Drive at an angle; test each picket |

## Anchor principles (SERENE/ERNEST in modern terms)

- **Solid:** each point individually sound.
- **Equalized:** load shared across points.
- **Redundant:** failure of any one point does not fail the anchor.
- **No extension:** if one point fails, the anchor does not shock-load the rest.

Single-point anchors are acceptable only when the point is bombproof (massive tree, huge boulder, solid rock tunnel). Otherwise build multipoint.

## Belay and rappel checklists

**Top-rope belay:**
- [ ] Anchor bombproof and redundant; belayer anchored or braced
- [ ] Rope through anchor, belay device or Munter rigged correctly
- [ ] Climber tied in (rerouted figure eight), harness doubled back
- [ ] Commands standardized and called every time: "On belay?" / "Belay on" / "Climbing" / "Climb on" / "Slack" / "Tension" / "Off belay" / "Belay off"

**Rappel:**
- [ ] Anchor checked; rappel device or Munter rigged; backup Prusik below device
- [ ] Gloves on, helmet on, loose clothing and hair secured
- [ ] Weight-test the system before committing
- [ ] The transition onto and off the rope is the most dangerous moment — check, then commit

**Lead climbing:**
- [ ] Leader places protection as they climb; clips rope to manage drag
- [ ] Belayer feeds slack smoothly, catches falls dynamically
- [ ] Fall factor awareness: a lead fall can be twice the distance to the last piece

## Hauling-system math

| System | Force advantage (theoretical) | Trade-off |
|---|---|---|
| 1:1 | 1× | Full force, full speed |
| 3:1 (Z-drag) | 3× | 3 m pulled per 1 m gained |
| 5:1 | 5× | 5 m pulled per 1 m gained |
| Compound (e.g., 9:1) | 9× | Very slow; for heavy loads only |

- Friction eats theoretical advantage: put a pulley at every bend.
- Progress capture: a Prusik on the haul line holds the load between pulls.
- Lowering: create friction (Munter, figure-eight device, brake bars) plus a hands-free backup — a friction hitch on the brake strand — so a slipped grip does not drop the load.
- Passing a knot through a hauling/lowering system is a rehearsed skill, not an improvisation.

## Crevasse rescue sequence (Ch. 14 drill)

1. **Arrest** the fall — team self-arrests and holds the load.
2. **Build an anchor** — snow/ice anchor, load-tested.
3. **Transfer the load** from the arresting climber to the anchor.
4. **Communicate** with the victim (voice, rope signals); assess injuries.
5. **Extract** — victim Prusiks out, or team hauls with a mechanical advantage system.
6. Rehearse on flat ground before it is needed on ice. Travel roped and spaced; cross crevasses perpendicular to their run; move in morning cold when snow bridges are strongest.
