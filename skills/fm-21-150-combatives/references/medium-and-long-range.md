# Medium- and Long-Range Combatives — FM 21-150 (1992), Chapters 4–5

## Vital targets (4-1) — body in three sections

- **High (head and neck — most dangerous):** top of head (weak cranial joints — unconsciousness, hemorrhage, death); forehead (whiplash, cerebral hemorrhage); temple (thin skull, artery and nerve near skin — concussion, massive hemorrhage if artery severs); eyes (jab: watering, blurred vision; forceful jab: blindness, gouging); ears (cupped-hand strike: ruptured eardrum, concussion); nose (thin bones break, extreme pain, watering); under nose (nerve center — great pain, watery eyes); jaw (break/dislocation; facial nerve pinched = one side paralyzed); chin (paralysis, concussion, unconsciousness — jawbone transmits force to brainstem cardiac/respiratory centers); back of ears and base of skull (moderate blow: unconsciousness by jarring).
- **Middle (torso):** solar plexus, ribs, kidneys — disrupt breathing, break structure, drop the guard for a high-section finish.
- **Low (legs):** knees, shins, insteps — destroy mobility and balance, the setup for a throw or finish.

## Striking principles (4-2) and short strikes (4-3)

- Strike *through* the target, not at it. Use natural weapons (elbow, knee, heel of hand, forehead) where the fist is fragile.
- Combine strikes: one strike opens a target, the next hits the opened target.
- Short strikes for medium range: elbow strikes (horizontal, vertical, rear), knee strikes, hand strikes (knife-hand, heel-palm, hammer-fist), head butts — do not forget head butts.

## Long range (Chapter 5)

- **Extended punches and kicks (5-1, 5-2):** power from hips and step, not the limb. Kicks target low (knee, shin, groin) — a high kick sacrifices balance for a target the opponent sees coming.
- **Angles of attack (5-4):** weapon attacks taught as numbered angles — a common language for attack, defense, and counter drilled by the numbers. Learn the angles before the techniques.
- **Defense vs. armed opponent (5-3, 5-5, 5-6):** first choice is always a weapon or distance. If forced: control the weapon arm (never the blade), move off the line at 45 degrees, attack the weapon arm's structure. Same logic vs. rifle with fixed bayonet: get inside the weapon's arc, control the rifle, strike vital targets.
- **Bayonet/knife (5-8, 5-9, 5-10):** thrust, slash, butt-stroke, parry as instinctive patterns of natural movement, drilled until thoughtless. Knife-against-knife sequence included.
- **Field-expedient weapons (5-11 to 5-14):** entrenching tool, three-foot stick, three-foot rope, six-foot pole. Same angles and vital targets apply — the tool extends reach and multiplies force. A soldier who understands angles and targets can fight with whatever is in his hand.
- **Advanced weapons techniques (5-7):** integrate everything at combat speed against a resisting opponent.
