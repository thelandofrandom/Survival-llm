# Close-Range Combatives — FM 21-150 (1992), Chapter 3

Close range: opponents have closed the gap so they can grab one another. Balance, leverage, timing, and body positioning decide it.

## Throws and takedowns (3-1)

- Attack balance: break posture, load the opponent's weight onto a fulcrum (hip, leg, shoulder), drive through with legs and hips — arms steer, hips do the work.
- Follow to the ground after the throw to maintain control; do not admire the result.

## Strangulation and choking (3-2, 3-3)

- Chokes render an opponent unconscious in seconds and silently — the close-range finisher and the core of sentry removal.
- **Blood chokes** (carotid compression) act faster than **air chokes** (windpipe compression) and are preferred.
- Apply with the whole body: arms lock the hold, legs and hips anchor it; the opponent's struggle tightens it.

## Counters to chokes (3-4)

- Tuck the chin, protect the neck with hands and shoulders, turn into the choke to relieve pressure, attack the choking arm's leverage, break the opponent's base before the hold sets.
- The counter must start the instant the choke is felt; once locked and committed, escape is far harder.
- Teach every choke with its counter — a soldier who knows only chokes is half-trained.

## Grappling (3-5)

- Win through superior body movement: position, leverage, balance.
- Pain compliance is a tool, not a plan — a determined or drugged enemy may fight through pain. Aim at control of the body, not at hurting him into submission.
