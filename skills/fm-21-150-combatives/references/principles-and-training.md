# Principles and Training — FM 21-150 (1992)

## Basic principles (Chapter 1)

- **Physical balance:** equilibrium, stable upright position; feet shoulder-width apart, knees flexed, center of gravity lowered. Two skills: keeping/regaining your own balance, and exploiting the opponent's balance.
- **Mental balance:** fear and anger must not overcome concentration or instinctive reaction.
- **Position:** move the whole body to a safe position where the attack cannot continue unless the enemy moves his whole body; move off the line of attack, usually at a 45-degree angle, toward or away from the opponent.
- **Timing:** move too soon and the enemy anticipates; too late and he strikes. Same for launching your attack.
- **Distance and range:** close (grabbing — throws, chokes, grappling), medium (touching — short punches, elbows, knees, head butts), long (extended punches/kicks, handheld weapons). Study in reverse order of encounter: survive the range you are in, then create the range you want.
- **Purpose of training:** silent elimination of the enemy when required; any soldier may face unarmed confrontation (mechanized/airborne/air-assault dispersion, low-intensity conflict, guerrilla conditions). Side benefits: strength, flexibility, balance, cardiorespiratory fitness, courage, confidence, self-discipline, esprit de corps.

## Training (Chapter 2)

- **Train the trainer:** professional instruction is the key; instructors must be fit, highly proficient in demonstration and application, confident, enthusiastic, technically expert.
- **Safety first:** safety precautions, protective equipment, and controlled progression precede live work.
- **Progression:** warm-ups and stretches → demonstration → execution by the numbers → drills → execution at combat speed (crawl, walk, run).
- **Stances and falls:** fighting stance is the base for everything; breakfalls are taught early — a soldier who cannot fall safely cannot be thrown in training.
- **Training areas:** mass formations; matted room for ground work; constructed pit for throws and falls; bayonet assault course for qualification under near-combat demands. Bayonet court precedes the assault course.
- **Foam pads:** let soldiers strike at full intent with controlled injury.
- **Sustainment:** once basic proficiency in Chapters 3–7 is reached, sustainment training runs anywhere with little preparation — drills need only a partner and ground.
