# Transitions and Sentry Removal — FM 21-150 (1992), Chapters 6–7 and Appendices

## Transition techniques (Chapter 6)

- When ammunition is exhausted or a weapon fails at small-arms/grenade range, the fluidity of battle may dictate hand-to-hand combat — and the fight moves between ranges whether planned or not.
- **Combat scenarios (6-1):** noncombatants may be present; firing would endanger them, so hands-on engagement may be required even with ammunition remaining. Going hands-on is a tactical judgment, not a failure.
- **Bridging the gap (6-2):** spheres of influence around each fighter contact in long range; interval gaps between them are the openings. Bridge the gap by moving through the interval at the moment the opponent's sphere is weakest — after his attack, during recovery, when his balance is committed.
- The transition is the most dangerous moment: between ranges, neither range's tools are fully available. Train transitions as their own skill.

## Sentry removal (Chapter 7)

- Purpose: gain access to an enemy location or escape confinement. Planning, rehearsal, execution are vital — a mission, not a technique.
- **Planning (7-1):** detailed schematic of the guarded area; known and suspected sentry locations marked. Learn guard-change schedule, post-checking schedule, meal times — best attack time is often soon after he has eaten, when his guard is lowered. Know his route, relief, communications, and what happens if he misses a check-in.
- **Rehearsals (7-2):** on a mock-up of the actual ground, in actual conditions (darkness, noise, footing).
- **Execution (7-3):** silent, fast, final — approach from the blind side, control body and weapon simultaneously, apply choke or strike, lower the body without noise.
- **Psychological aspects (7-4):** killing at arm's length must be addressed in training before the mission, not discovered during it.
- **Techniques (7-5):** Chapter 3 chokes plus approach and control methods for a sentry who does not know he is in a fight.

## Appendices

- **Appendix A — Pugil training:** prepares the soldier to use the rifle-bayonet confidently and aggressively against a thinking, moving, evading opponent who fights back and makes corrections. Equipment: pugil stick and protective gear. Pugil bouts bridge the bayonet court and the assault course.
- **Appendix B — Bayonet training:** court first (techniques), then the assault course (qualification under near-combat demands). Instructors grade all previously learned techniques on the course. The court teaches; the course tests.
