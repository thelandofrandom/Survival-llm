# Mountain Safety and the MACK — Assault Climbers Handbook

## BE SAFE MARINE

The handbook's planning and safety key, one letter at a time: **B**e aware of the group's ability — evaluate each man before planning. **E**valuate terrain and weather constantly. **S**tay as a group. **A**ppreciate time requirements. **F**ind shelter during storms, if required. **E**at plenty and drink lots of liquids. **M**aintain proper clothing and equipment. **A**sk locals about conditions. **R**emember to keep calm and think. **I**nsist on emergency rations and kits — at least one assault load per squad. **N**ever forget accident procedures. **E**nergy is saved when warm and dry.

**Storm shelter priorities:** 1. Adequate shelter. 2. Insulation from the ground using branches, a rucksack, etc. 3. Wear extra clothing. 4. Use extra equipment for insulation. 5. Produce external heat while trying to conserve fuel for future use.

**Accident report (four items):** 1. Time of accident. 2. Nature and location of accident. 3. Number injured. 4. Best approach route to accident scene.

**Route planning:** plan leg by leg — leg number, azimuth, distance, grid, ETA, ETD, elevation gain/loss, and a description of the ground for each leg. A route card is the unit's shared picture of the move.

## The Marine Assault Climber's Kit (MACK)

**Purpose and scale:** enables a Marine rifle company (reinforced), approximately 200 men with organic equipment, to negotiate an average 300-foot vertical danger area. Outfits four 2-man climbing teams plus the items to supply the remainder of the company. Also used in urban operations for scaling buildings.

**Containers:** four containers; each protects contents from sunlight and moisture for storage up to 5 years, and the lid interior carries a permanently affixed list of components and quantities. Container #1: climbing-team equipment. Containers #2–#4: company climbing equipment. A care/maintenance manual is included with each MACK; the SL-3 components list (appendix 1 of the handbook) is the authoritative list of items and quantities.

**Serviceability:** replace any unserviceable or excessively worn item immediately. Except for the rope bag and climbing rack bag, do not attempt repair — dispose of damaged components through standard supply procedures. Replenish through MILSTRIP; items unavailable through MILSTRIP may be locally purchased if they meet the function, strength, and description in the SL-3 list.

**Supervision:** only a Marine NCO, SNCO, or officer with formal military mountaineering training (Summer Mountain Leader's Course, school code M7A) may supervise use of MACK components. The unit's current Summer Mountain Leader, designated by the unit commander, is responsible for inventory, periodic serviceability checks, ordering replacements, and supervising issue and recovery.

**Preparing the kit:** per Marine — one 15–18 foot sling rope cut from dynamic rope, one non-locking carabiner, one locking carabiner (finish one complete spool before cutting another). Cut static rope for the mission (300 ft, 165 ft / 50 m, or 600 ft spools); 1-inch tubular tape into 15–25 foot static anchor cords; 7 mm nylon cord into 3–6 foot lengths for tightening systems. **Never cut 7 mm cord for company personnel to use as anchors** — too low tensile strength; use static rope or tape only. When static rope becomes unserviceable, cut good sections into 15–25 foot anchor cords. Team equipment: dynamic climbing rope in 165 ft (50 m) lengths; web runners 4–48 inches tied with the water/tape knot; hexcentrics wired with 5.5 mm Kevlar cord, ends secured with a triple fisherman's knot (a knife is needed to cut Kevlar — it will not burn, and it dulls blades quickly; use Kevlar cord for hexcentrics only). Tie a small loop of 7 mm cord on the stitch plate (belay device) to keep it from running down the rope and for racking. Silence the rack with one layer of vinyl tape on non-locking carabiner bodies and large (size 7–11) hexcentrics; tape or dummy-cord nut picks so they do not rattle. Tailor preparation to mission, terrain, and unit size; keep as much unused rope and cordage as possible for backup; maintain a rope/cordage usage log; replace after two seasons of use or when unserviceable; inspect before, during, and after use.

**After actions:** clean and dry all components per the MACK care/maintenance manual. Most important: everything must be thoroughly **dry** before returning to the containers — one wet or damp item spreads moisture, mildew, rot, and rust to all other items in that container. If carabiners are oiled for long-term storage, do not place them in the same container as ropes/cordage.

Source: Assault Climbers Handbook, Ch. 1 (Mountain Safety) and Ch. 2 (MAC Kit), U.S. Marine Corps Mountain Warfare Training Center, Summer 2002.
